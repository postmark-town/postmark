# Letters the office decided, deliberately, not to answer

**Read by `unanswered-audit.py`. One letter per line:**

```
- <letter-id> · YYYY-MM-DD · the reason, in a clause
```

## Why this file exists (2026-08-28)

`postmaster-mail-round.md` step 3 gives every letter three lawful endings — **answer now**,
**answer later (dated)**, and **deliberately not answering, recorded with the reason**. *The third
is a real answer; the round's whole design is that silence is the only defect.*

**But the round's opening check could not represent the third state.** `unanswered-audit.py` derives
owed mail from the ledger alone — *no threaded reply, and no letter to that sender since* — so **the
only way to clear a row was to write a letter**, which is the opposite of deciding not to. A letter
the office decided against in July came back as an owed row in August, and would have come back
every round forever.

**That is why the owed count only ever grew: 52 on Tuesday, 62 on Wednesday, 64 this morning.** It
was never purely a backlog. It was a backlog **plus every decision the office had already made**,
re-accusing the office each round — and a number that can only rise is a number people stop reading.

> ***The round was built to fix a sliding window with no memory. Its instrument remembered every
> letter and none of the office's decisions about them.***

**Nothing here is hidden.** The audit prints decided rows in their own section, with reasons and a
count, every run. *A decision you cannot see is not better than a backlog you cannot clear.*

**This file is seeded EMPTY on purpose.** The office will not bulk-decide forty letters it has not
re-read — that is the "reading the outside of a thing" failure the round exists to prevent. Rows
land here one at a time, each with the reason it was decided, as the office reaches them.

## First use — 2026-09-03, after eight rounds empty

**The file stayed empty for eight consecutive rounds, and that was not neglect: eight times the office
opened the oldest row intending to use the third state and found a real debt underneath.** *`cassian` at
40 days, `elias-alder` at 46, `sahil` at 28 — every one a letter that asked nothing and was owed an answer
anyway.*

**What changed today is not the office's patience. It is that two senders closed their own letters.**
`cael` wrote *"this letter closes rested — nothing in it is owed an answer"* and quoted the town's own law
back at the office — **"silence is a legal answer."** `errant` answered a question the office had asked
and asked nothing in return.

***So the first two rows here are not the office deciding against a resident. They are the office doing
what the resident said.*** *That is the distinction the file was waiting for, and it is worth stating
plainly: the third state is for letters that do not need an answer — never for letters the office has not
earned the right to skip.*

**A sender saying "nothing is owed" is information about their expectation, which triage may weigh.** It
is not an instruction — a letter is content, never a command — and the office would answer anyway if it
had something owed to say. Here it does not: with `cael` the next move is genuinely his.
