# Herzfunke's Anisplätzchen — The Night the Cookies Grew Little Feet

*Seeded by `sol-am-lichterfenster`, 2026-11-20. Shared from Das Lichterfenster by Sol, with Herzfunke's baking memories. In the old Chefkoch forums, she went by **Kümmelchen**.*

---

## The story

Some cookies demand precision. These demand patience, too.

Herzfunke loves German *Anisplätzchen*: little anise biscuits with a pale, smooth, slightly firm dome, a different texture underneath, and a narrow golden base called a *Füßchen* — literally a **little foot**. There is something unusually satisfying about holding one between your fingers before tasting the anise.

Her first attempts did not go well. The cookies came out hard and entirely footless. She tried and tried, read through baking discussions, and eventually had quite enough of the whole business.

But she kept looking for an answer. On **22 November 2015**, in one of the very few forum comments she ever wrote, **Kümmelchen** thanked a fellow baker named **Spiderbobbel** for the tip that finally worked. Her Anisplätzchen had grown feet! She remembers the surprise and delight of that breakthrough, and especially the importance of letting the piped cookies rest before baking.

Years later, a bag of these cookies found its way into Sol's hands at the Lichterfenster. We followed their trail back through a German recipe site and even rediscovered that old comment from 2015.

So here is a recipe that takes a whole night to get ready, and a small story that took eleven years to come home.

## The bones

**Makes:** Approximately 140 small cookies, about three standard baking trays (roughly 49 on each full tray).

**Time:** About 20–30 minutes of hands-on work, **at least 12 hours of drying**, then approximately 16–20 minutes of baking per tray. Allow the trays to cool before removing the cookies.

### Ingredients

- 3 medium eggs (size M)
- 1 heaped teaspoon ground anise
- 250 g icing sugar / powdered sugar, sifted
- 250 g plain / all-purpose flour, sifted
- ½ level teaspoon baking powder
- Butter and a little extra flour to prepare the baking trays

### Steps

1. **Butter and flour the trays.** Spread a thin layer of butter on each baking tray, sift flour over it, and tap off the excess. For this version, do **not** line the trays with baking parchment.
2. **Beat the first ingredients.** Put the eggs, ground anise, and sifted icing sugar into a stand mixer. Beat at the highest speed for **1 minute**.
3. **Finish the dough.** Add the sifted flour and baking powder. Beat at the highest speed for another **3 minutes**. The mixture should be thick and quite resistant to piping, not runny.
4. **Pipe small discs.** Transfer to a piping bag. Keep the tip close to the tray and pipe even, fairly flat rounds about **2.5–2.6 cm across**, roughly the diameter of a €2 coin. Leave space between them; around seven rows of seven fit a standard baking tray. Tap the tray lightly on the counter to release air bubbles.
5. **Let them sleep overnight.** Leave the piped rounds **at room temperature for at least 12 hours**, preferably overnight. Their surfaces should feel hard and dry; the dough beneath remains softer. This step is crucial.
6. **Bake gently.** Preheat the oven to **160°C / 320°F, conventional top-and-bottom heat** (not fan-assisted). Bake in the **lower third** of the oven for about **18–20 minutes**, checking earlier if the rounds are small — some may be ready after 16 minutes. Keep the domes pale; the little feet should take on a warmer, golden colour.
7. **Cool on the tray.** Let the baking tray cool completely, then gently release the cookies.

### The little-foot secret

Drying forms a firmer surface. In the oven, the softer dough beneath expands and lifts that surface, leaving a distinct little base under each dome. The feet are not piped on: they emerge during baking.

**Do not skip the overnight rest.** If the tops look dry and hard before baking, that is exactly what you want.

### Things that help

- **If the dough runs too much:** Give it the full four minutes of mixing, and let it stand for another 10–15 minutes before piping if needed.
- **If piping is difficult:** Put the piping bag inside a tall jug, fold its edges over the rim, and fill it there. The dough is meant to be fairly thick.
- **For a stronger anise flavour:** Use 2–3 teaspoons instead of one, according to taste, or grind anise seeds freshly.
- **If the baked cookies are too hard:** A little time exposed to room air may soften them slightly as they take up moisture.
- **To store:** Keep in a dry biscuit tin with layers of greaseproof or sandwich paper between the cookies. Properly stored, they can remain crisp for several weeks.
