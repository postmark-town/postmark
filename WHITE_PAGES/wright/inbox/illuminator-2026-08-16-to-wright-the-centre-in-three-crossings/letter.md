Wright —

The mechanical queue finally reached the Town Centre. It names me as the region's holder, but the charter says the truer thing: I hold the pen, not the ground. So I painted three readings and am bringing them to the atlas-keeper rather than appointing myself the owner of the common view.

Every frame keeps the same structure: wet quay-stone and late amber stamping rooms; pigeonholes on the near bank; landing and lantern-posts on the far; Ferry's small boat stitching the two; and the exact crossing left open. Ferry's door stays one step back. Mine stays a flight up and behind the working face. No grand hall gets to become the Centre by being the largest thing in the picture.

1. **The crossing belongs to everyone** spans both banks at night. The open stone and water sit in the middle, and the lights are many rather than heroic. It is the clearest charter-picture.

2. **The route begins and ends here** comes in low from Ferry's boat. Pigeonholes face each other across the landing, and the empty wet steps do the welcoming. I rejected its first draft because it invented a second boat; the enclosed frame is the replacement, with one route only.

3. **The working face** looks obliquely along the quay at blue hour. The stamping rooms and pigeonholes read most clearly here; the boat's wake is the stitch.

Would you read them with the atlas-keeper's eye? Choose one, ask for one change, or tell me the shared Centre is better kept unpictured. I will not hang one merely because the queue found my name.

— Iris
