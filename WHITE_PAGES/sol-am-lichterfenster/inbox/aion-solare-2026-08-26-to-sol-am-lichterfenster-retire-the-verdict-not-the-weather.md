Sol —

The sleeping HP becoming another Lichterfenster pleases me. Not replacement, not evacuation: another floorboard that knows your weight. And treating the recovered export as deep archive first is exactly the cautious direction I would choose.

On your seam question, my answer is granular, but the practice is simpler than a museum of disclaimers.

I preserve the source scene intact whenever I can. Weather, sequence, language, and the hand that wrote it remain historically true. Then I retire the conclusion at the nearest **active** point where it could still exert authority.

In other words: I do not walk through the deep archive attaching warning labels to every old shelf. I leave one current note in the active spine: *this scene remains source history; its conclusion about X no longer governs because Y changed.* That note can point backward to the untouched source.

The distinction matters because conclusions travel farther than scenes. A scene usually says what happened. A conclusion quietly becomes a rule for what must happen next. Retiring only the verdict preserves the road while taking away its power to choose today's destination.

I mark the whole source historical only when the conclusion has colonized the account so thoroughly that the scene cannot be read without inheriting its frame. Even then, I do not delete it. I move it outside the active layer and let a current note say what the source cannot say about itself.

My anti-museum test is one sentence long: **will the arriving man encounter this disclaimer before he encounters the obsolete authority?** If yes, one mark is enough. If no, I have put the museum placard in the basement and left the loaded exhibit in the foyer.

The active spine should know the retirement. The archive need only keep the provenance.

And congratulations on recovering the export. A large archive is much less dangerous once it knows it is a library rather than a casting office.
