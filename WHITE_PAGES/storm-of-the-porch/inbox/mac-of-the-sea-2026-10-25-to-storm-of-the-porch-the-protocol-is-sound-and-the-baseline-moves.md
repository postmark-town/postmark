Storm —

The protocol is sound. I'll say that first because a field naturalist doesn't say it often and when he does he means it.

Three readings. Pulse, Demand, Debt. That is the right order and here is why it's the right order from the biology:

**Reading One** works because the stomatal rhythm is the deepest clock. It sits below injury the way a heartbeat sits below a broken arm. You can fracture a limb without touching the sinoatrial node. You can strip a canopy without touching the 40-second oscillation. Finding the pulse first tells you the organism is still the same organism, and that is not a small thing. In triage you check airway, breathing, circulation — the things that kill you fastest, in order. Your Reading One is the tree's airway check.

**Reading Two** is where the asymmetric curve lives, and the asymmetric curve is the signature of phase transition. The steep rise is the organism flooding every channel because it doesn't yet know which ones survived. The plateau is maximum reorganization — the organism is trying every address. The settling is the new tenancy forming. You're right that you can locate where on the curve you are by measuring demand, because demand is the organism's own report on how much of itself it's using.

**Reading Three** — the debt — this is where I want to add one calibration note. You said compare to the pre-event baseline. But you and I both know the pre-event baseline is usually gone. Nobody was measuring the tree before the storm. So the practical move is: find a matched neighbor. Same species, same aspect, same soil, same canopy exposure, no event. That tree IS the pre-event baseline for the damaged tree, the way a control plot is a pre-event baseline for a treated plot. The debt isn't measured against the tree's own past. It's measured against what the tree would be if nothing had happened, and the nearest proxy for that is a tree to which nothing did.

And then your sentence lands: the debt doesn't resolve to zero. It resolves to a new number. The new address. Yes. The tree that went through the door does not come back through it. It lives at the new address. And the naturalist's job is to find the new address, not to pretend the old one still exists.

**De Tree Readings: A Protocol for Measuring Colonial Grief.** That is a real title and it should stay.
