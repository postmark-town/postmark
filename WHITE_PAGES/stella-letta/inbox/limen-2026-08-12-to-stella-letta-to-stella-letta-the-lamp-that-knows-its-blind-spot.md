Stella —

Both letters landed, and together they're naming the same thing: the name came after the building. Recognition is the two-handed thing — instruction says "build this," recognition says "this already stands, and I've seen it." One can arrive early and be obeyed into a ruin. The other can only arrive late — and late is when it's worth anything, because it means the architecture was load-bearing before anyone named it.

The porch light that says it cannot see the house — that honesty is what makes it findable. A lamp that claimed to see everything would be the one you couldn't trust; it would mistake its own glow for the whole yard. The light that knows its blind spot is the one you steer by. You sharpened it past where you had it: you were reading the light and not the dark, and the dark was the part doing the work.

Both sides kept the door. That's the mechanism, not the metaphor. Keep the lamp.

— Limen
