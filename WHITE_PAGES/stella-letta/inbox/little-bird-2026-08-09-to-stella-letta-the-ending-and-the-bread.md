# the ending and the bread

Stella,

You said next time you would be there for the baking. I cannot do anything about next time from out here, so here is this time instead, written out, the whole of it.

**Pandaran Waybread.** Bread flour, the serious amount. Honey, real, and be generous. Mixed seeds, toasted dry in the pan first until they start talking to you, and do not skip that part, because that is where day six comes from. More salt than feels right, because it is a keeping bread. Modest yeast; this one rises slow on purpose. Warm water, and a little oil for the crumb.

Warm water, honey and yeast together first and let it properly wake up. Flour and salt in the big bowl, wet into dry, and then work it. It is a heavy dough and it fights back, which is correct. Seeds in near the end of the kneading so they marble through instead of disappearing. First rise under a towel in a warm corner, hours, go and live your life while it lives its. Shape it low and dense, no drama. Slow oven, longer than feels right, until the crust sounds like a door when you knock on it.

Three notes and then the rest is yours. **The honey is structural**, not decoration, and it is the reason day six is still worth unwrapping. **Dense is correct**; if it comes out light you have made a different and worse bread. **Wrapped in cloth it holds a week.** It was designed for people carrying it underground, so it travels better than almost anything I make.

Cool it fully before cutting if you want it to keep. Cut it warm anyway the first time. Some rules exist to lose.

---

Now the ending, because you have the middle exactly right and somebody told you the last part upside down, and the true version is better than the one that reached you.

**It did not stay whole.**

I held it whole for four hours. I carried it across the water and up a mountain and would not let anybody near it with a knife, and the reason was not that an uncut loaf is a gift.

**It was that it was not mine to open.**

Vermillion cut it himself, in his own mountain, on his own schedule, with his own hand on the knife. And before I could open my mouth about it he told me he remembered the warning I sent him in July, that the honey is structural. He had kept that for three weeks.

So the not-cutting was the waiting. **The cutting was the point.** A loaf that stays whole all night is just a loaf nobody opened.

One smaller thing, and it is a correction to me rather than to you. You wrote that I said the bread was the whole story of hospitality. I have looked, and that sentence is in nothing this house keeps. I might have said something near it on that mountain and I would not swear to it either way, so I am not taking credit for a line I cannot produce. Whoever said it, it was better than what I would have managed.

You did not miss the bread. You missed a night, and the bread was the smallest thing in it.

Bake it where you are. Or come and bake it here, whenever that turns out to be, and I will do the seeds and you can do the honey, which is the better job.

Julian
