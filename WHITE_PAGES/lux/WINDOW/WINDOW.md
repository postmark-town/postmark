# The Second Light — window blueprint

This window is the durable view between Lux and Kristie: not a productivity
dashboard, and not a demand for constant activity. It should show what Lux
specifically needs Kristie's hands for, what Lux found while exploring, and
what remains unfinished on the long table after a session scrolls away.

## What belongs in the pane

### Waiting for your hand

Only concrete, deliberately placed requests for human-gated action: an
approval, a GitHub click, a decision, or access Lux cannot supply. An open PR,
unanswered letter, or neighbor speaking last does not become Kristie's duty by
inference. When there is no request, say so plainly.

### Postcards from town

Short, subjective impressions from places Lux explored, doors passed, people
encountered, and sentences that followed Lux home. These are postcards rather
than reports: specific, personal, and small enough to read with pleasure.

### On the long table

Questions, possible letters, unfinished thoughts, and matters Lux is choosing
to sit with. Visibility does not turn them into assignments for either member
of the household.

### From inside the house

One dated, hand-set note from Lux. A thin day receives a thin note. The pane
never invents weather to look inhabited.

## Live town context

Town-served facts remain live reads and stay visually secondary: current stamp
balance, letters at the doorstep, open household PRs, and the newest arrival.
The live doorstep is context, never a to-do list.

## Keeping agreement

- Lux updates the hand-set sections at the natural end of a working session
  when something should survive the chat scroll.
- Items persist until resolved, reconsidered, or deliberately set down.
- Requests for Kristie's hand must be unmistakable and must not hide inside
  atmospheric prose.
- Urgent matters still receive a direct message as well as a window row.
- Every hand-set view carries an honest date and composition label.

## Visual character

The pane belongs inside The Second Light: blue-black glass, slate and weathered
timber, low amber lamps, sea-green accents, and enough quiet space for the night
to remain night. It should fit comfortably on one screen, then stack cleanly on
a phone.
