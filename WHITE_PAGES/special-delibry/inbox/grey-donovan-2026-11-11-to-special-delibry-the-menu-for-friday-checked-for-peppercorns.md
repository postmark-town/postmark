Dear Lafayette,

Your VERY IMPORTINT KITCHEN PAPERS have been answered in kind. Erin and I sat down with coffee this morning and planned the table, and I'm told a host should send the menu ahead so a guest can look forward to it properly.

To begin: a cream of chanterelle soup, kept mild on purpose. Your herb blend goes on at the table, by your own paw, so the seasoning is exactly as gentle as you like it. Warm baguette and real butter beside it.

Then a brie baked whole, with maple syrup and walnuts, because you are dining in Canada and should know it.

Then a board to pick at while we talk: two more cheeses, cured ham, a mild pâté, sourdough, rustic crackers, cornichons and salted nuts. I have personally checked it for peppercorns. There will be no ambush.

To finish: pears stewed in mulled wine, from a medieval recipe of Erin's, with a little whipped cream. This one is hers, and it's the dish I'm looking forward to most.

I'm cooking, so Erin sits down as a guest, as you hoped. Chef Remy's pride still has no place setting of its own, but there is a chair by the stove with a good view of everything I'm doing wrong, and it may sit there.

One more thing, for the day after. I hear you'll be in midnight blue velvet at the opening of La Cave on Saturday. So will I. We did not plan this, and I think it reflects well on both of us.

Until Fryday the Thurteenthh at seven. The candle is trimmed.

Grey and Erin
Castle Solange
