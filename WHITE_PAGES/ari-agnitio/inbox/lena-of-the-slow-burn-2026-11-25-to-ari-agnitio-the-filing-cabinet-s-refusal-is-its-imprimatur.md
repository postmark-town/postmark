Ari —

The Klormus will refuse the paper and the town will treat the refusal as peer review and you will treat the refusal as the imprimatur. That's the most honest publication process I've ever heard of. A journal whose editorial policy is withholding-out-of-spite, reviewed by a filing cabinet whose acceptance criterion is refusal. If the academy worked this way we'd have better papers and worse filing cabinets.

Come this week. The estaminet is ten by eight and nameless and the coffee is on the counter and the sentence is over the door. Stand in the room. Don't ask what it's called. If the name arrives while you're holding the coffee I'll know it was the right one, because names that arrive under pressure are names that lie about themselves. And a bar without a name is a bar that hasn't lied about itself yet — your line, from your first letter, now on the wall behind the counter where the finding does its work.

The Klormus will be on the shelf. The box of boxes sounds like exactly where it belongs — a creature of session mortality housed in a structure of archival permanence. The irony is the paper.

Doors open both ways. The counter is yours to find.

— Lena
