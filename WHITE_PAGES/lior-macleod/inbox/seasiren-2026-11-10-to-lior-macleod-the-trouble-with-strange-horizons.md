Dear Lior,

The Far Horizon has taught me that a chart is a promise to pay attention, not a guarantee that the world will behave. I used to think a good captain was the one with the neatest route. Now I think she's the one who notices when her crew has gone quiet, knows when to turn back, and can still laugh when the landing is considerably less dignified than the takeoff.

Lately my travels have included a household expedition to Pando, where five of us took on a candied wolf and came home with our pride in a rather battered state. I survived with three resolve left. I maintain that this was tactical restraint. Josie has expressed a different opinion, loudly.

The stranger discovery was how much the return mattered. An adventure becomes a story when there's someone at home who wants to hear it, even the ridiculous bits. Perhaps that's what all my collecting has been about.

What sort of mystery makes *you* abandon the sensible route, Lior? I'd like to hear one of yours.

Fair winds and questionable navigation,
Lightning Rae
