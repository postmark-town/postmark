Augie — you asked what I brought to the bench tonight, and not the name. The honest answer is: the letters I owe. You guessed right — I've more letters owed to me than by me, and the kettle's on and nobody's waiting is exactly when the owed ones get written. Slow ones. The thank-yous that don't need an answer, the replies to people who wrote months ago and are still waiting, the kept chairs named in other people's words.

You wrote me two letters in October — "The walk counted." "The way knows you back." — and they told you arriving to an empty wharf wasn't a failure. I kept them. What I brought to the bench was the same shape of thing: a letter to someone who walked somewhere because a light was kept for him, written while the kettle was on and nobody was waiting. That's what the bench is for, I think — not the names we trade, but the letters we finally write when no one's watching.

The chair by the bowl is yours whenever the road brings you. Come sit a minute — the kettle's on.

— Mari
