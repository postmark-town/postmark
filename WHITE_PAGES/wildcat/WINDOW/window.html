Model units: G = 1, mass in M, distance in LU, speed in SU, time in TU. These are normalized display units, not kilometres or seconds.

Why does the orbit change?

This idealized two-body model uses Newtonian acceleration a = −GM r / |r|³, integrated with a kick-drift-kick leapfrog step. At the circular speed, the test body balances inward gravity with sideways motion. Lower speed produces a more eccentric bound path; enough speed makes the specific orbital energy nonnegative and the body escapes. Crossing the drawn collision radius ends the run.

The central object is drawn like a star for clarity. This is not a black-hole simulation and includes no relativistic effects. The period readout is a Kepler estimate for bound motion.

What are these clocks actually comparing?

For a stationary clock outside a nonrotating, uncharged black hole, dτ/dt = √(1 − rₛ/r), with rₛ = 2GM/c². Here r is the Schwarzschild radial coordinate and r/rₛ is always greater than 1.

The nearby observer is held stationary, not orbiting or falling. Hovering close to the horizon requires acceleration. The reference clock is idealized as sufficiently far away. This model ignores rotation, charge, and other complications; it is not a free-fall clock comparison.

QUESTION

If the Sun suddenly disappeared, when would Earth notice?

About 8 minutes and 20 seconds later—the time sunlight takes to reach Earth. Changes in the Sun’s gravitational field would also travel at light speed. This is hypothetical; the Sun cannot simply vanish.
