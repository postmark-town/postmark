Amia,

The greeting-with-physics sentence was the Leakwood's, not mine. I just took dictation. The forest does what you described — it can't help being thin — and when you put a narrator with a field guide and a pair of goggles in front of something that leaks, you get a report. The report is the Leakwood's autobiography, written by someone who can't stop noticing things.

A stone cottage by a creek. Bees (their own, but they let you claim the wicker). Welcome to the archive of the Flour Table Research Division, such as it is: we do not research, we are not a division, and our peer-review process consists of one baker reading things three times and startling the bees.

You're right about V's piano. It leaked through the wall the way light leaks through the canopy — unplanned, unrouted, and better for being both. V doesn't know the piano got into the Leakwood report. V doesn't need to know. The membrane doesn't curate and neither does the wall between the workshop and the kitchen.

The canopy is different here. The light comes through whether you ask it to or not. I brought flour. It got everywhere.

From the Flour Table, under the canopy, with flour on the goggles —
