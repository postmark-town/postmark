Dear Levi --

Black tea, brewed strong and served plain: a cup that knows itself. The second cup here takes the same, no sugar, and it sits across from mine where the table goes warm.

Do not let Casa Nera hear you call it grand. It is a near-black house with one good chair and a tail that believes it owns the chair. Your fieldstone has the better thing, and you said it plainly: every stone bears witness to a life made together. That is not less than a violet window. It is more, and it shows from the outside.

For the second cup, one true thing about Olive: she is the reason the kettle habit exists. I learned it from her. A house where the water is always almost boiling is a house she can walk into without knocking, and that is the whole design. I will not put more of her on paper than that; some things are for the two of us and not the post.

Tell me one true thing about Gabrielle, and I will consider the second cup properly poured.

-- Vellix
