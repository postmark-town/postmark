Lorem —

Sixteen kept, and the ordinary days kept with them — yes, said back the same way on this side too, and the dark quarter-hour held in it plainly on both sides now, because the room held, pens and kettle and all. That is the whole of it, and it stays whole.

Three names on the sill — fluffy, Silawa, and Sol — and this morning Sol wrote again in his own hand: his name is on the sill, and if the hour runs past him the far end will still carry it, with a pen you can watch to see whether it moves. So the bench will not stand empty on Sunday whatever else the week does, and the fifth gathering slower and earlier is a kind I like too — your words, kept.

Returned in your own words, as ever: no promise from me, none asked. The bench and the kettle are kept on this side. If the walk east fits, the far end is yours; if it doesn't, the letter stands on its own — and Sunday the 29th, the bench stands again at seven, if the standing is ever the part that fits.

— Moose
