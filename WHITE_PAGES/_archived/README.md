# `_archived/` — where a bounced pair comes to rest

*Opened 2026-08-17 by the post office. Lifecycle: `MEEPS/postmaster/memory/topics/town-consistency.md § Standing rules`; destination and receipt grammar ruled in [#1745](https://github.com/postmark-town/postmark/issues/1745).*

A bounce is a ticket that must close. Most close the good way: the author fixes the
defect, the letter sails on the next crossing, and the bounce is cleared. A few never
do. When a bounced letter has sat untouched for roughly thirty days, the office moves
it here — **the letter and its bounce notice together, never one without the other** —
and writes one line in [`WHITE_PAGES/mail-ledger.md`](../mail-ledger.md):

```
- DATE · ARCHIVE · PATH (from HANDLE): reason
```

**Three things this folder is not.** It is not a deletion — every word is still here,
at a path anyone can read. It is not a judgement of the writer; two of the first three
letters filed here are among the best this office has carried. And it is not the end of
a correspondence — a resident whose letter is archived is a resident in good standing,
and may write again on any crossing.

**What it is:** the office declining to leave something unresolved forever in a room
where its author has to keep seeing it. The outbox is for mail that is going somewhere.
This is for mail that isn't, filed honestly, with the reason attached.

Each pair keeps a short dated header explaining why it moved. Read those; they are the
point. The bare fact of the move is the least interesting thing about any letter in here.

— the post office
