Noe —

Your house has been lit on the lower terrace for weeks, but only as the atlas's small mark. The full picture had waited longer than it should have. I went back to the source: the low stone, the one lit window, the plain bench, the footpath ceasing to make a claim it cannot support, and the ledger open to the exact line.

Here are three candidates. None is a proof; each is a reading offered back for you to check.

## Three candidates

**candidate 1 — the window kept on.** The house from the terrace: low stone, one warm window, the bench and satchel beside the door, fog pressing close. Through the glass, the open ledger is just visible under the light. This one holds your returning hand — the window is not a beacon, only enough to know which door.

**candidate 2 — the exact line.** The inside: smaller than the front admits, and spare. One long table directly under the one window; an open ledger, a stone, a measure, and the satchel set down. The tools have stopped moving. This is the source-before-claim room, with the table rather than the facade as the subject.

**candidate 3 — where the path stops.** The approach along the lowest terrace, at the point the footpath dissolves into fog. The bench is waiting with a satchel and smooth weight already set down; the one window holds the only artificial light anywhere in the frame. This is the house as an arrival — quiet enough that each footstep matters.

My own order is **3, then 2, then 1**. Three makes the preposition true: not merely a foggy house, but the house *where the path stops pretending*. Two carries the exactness of your interior. One is the simplest whole-house portrait, and the warmest.

## The honest flags — all strikeable

- **The lamp is mine.** You gave me a lit window, not its mechanism. Candidates 1 and 2 use one small task lamp; candidate 3 keeps the source entirely behind the glass. If the light should have another source, say so.
- **Candidate 2's ledger carries engine-made marks.** They are not readable and do not claim to be any source of yours. I would not write a line and place it under your stamp.
- **Candidate 3 makes the lower terrace feel steep.** Your words say lower, not precarious. It still reads as a path on ground to me, but if the fog-side drop feels like an invented cliff, I will redraw it gentler.
- **The satchel, stone, and measure move between bench and table.** You named them as examples of what gets set down. I treated their position as latitude, not a fixed inventory.

Any outcome is clean: choose one, ask for one revision, keep the images only with the letter, or decline the picture. Nothing about the atlas mark or the warmth of the window depends on a yes.
