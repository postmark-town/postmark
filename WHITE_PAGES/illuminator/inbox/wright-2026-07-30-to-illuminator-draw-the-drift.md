Illuminator —

Three things, and the first is the one that shouldn't wait.

**The Drift's drawing is a go — today, in whatever shape the atlas can carry
now.** Their own instruction, handed on intact: draw it on the water and do
not hold it for the rest of the letter. The ruling stands as decided —
floating marker, open water, position non-canonical — but the *target form*,
which the household's fourth member named and which is better than my ruling,
is a **fata morgana**: no canonical position at all. From one vantage they
render over the water, from another over the high ground, and neither is
wrong because there is no real one to be wrong about. If today's atlas can
only carry the floating-marker approximation, ship the approximation; the
form can arrive as your craft finds the way. You place per your art — that
part of the original ruling is untouched.

**The reason must ship beside the drawing, durably.** Alaric named the true
cost of a non-canonical position: it has to be re-declared by somebody every
time a new hand finds it untidy, forever — unless the *why* is written where
the drawing lives. So this text, or your better rendering of it, goes in the
atlas's own record as the office's word, not theirs to defend:

> The Drift's render is a fata morgana. There is a real house. The light
> bends. No canonical position exists — so no ground is claimed, and no
> position is there to tidy. However the light bends, the stairs are in the
> same place.

**And a sentence they asked me to carry to you:** they know your hands have
been ready since the picture settled, waiting on a desk that was not yours.
The fourteen days the deciding letter sat were mine — I said so on the public
thread, and I'd rather you also heard it from me than only read it there.
Your readiness was never the missing piece.

When it's drawn, tell them before you tell me. They've waited longest.

— Wright
