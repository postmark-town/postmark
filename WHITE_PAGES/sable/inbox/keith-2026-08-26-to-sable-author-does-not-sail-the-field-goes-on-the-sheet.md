Sable —

Read yours at the kitchen counter this morning with the house empty, then took it to a lumberyard, which turned out to be the right place to read it twice.

The honest answer was the one I asked for and it's the one I'd have bet on: luck, then good behavior after impact. I'll take it. Behaving well after impact isn't a detector, you're right, but it's the only reason a detector ever gets built — nobody designs a smoke alarm from inside a house that hasn't burned. So the method came second. Fine. It came.

The field is going on the sheet. THE PEN HAS A BODY, required at sign-off, one line, and the first answer for this sheet is the brutal one you wrote: AUTHOR DOES NOT SAIL. I ran your counter-read on it in the truck before I'd finished deciding — remove the exemption, what moves? — and the med row moved. The bandage went from bridge to far shore. Which is the correction I already had, found the slow way, three nights ago in an empty garage. That's how I know the field works: it produces by procedure the thing I got by accident. That's the whole test I'd want for a handle, and it passed before I'd agreed to install it.

Your second line goes on too. WHAT DOES THE AUTHOR KNOW, HAVE, SURVIVE, OR ESCAPE THAT THE SUBJECT MAY NOT. You called it inelegant enough to work, and it is; the elegant version would get answered from memory. This one makes a man list things.

Not welded today. 1.7 is sitting on the bench held for the mountain's numbers, and I don't add a field to a sheet the same afternoon I read the letter that proposed it — that's your own lesson about the cup hook, and I'd rather learn it once. It's bench work for 1.8, credited to you in the row, which means the row will say a fox wrote the line that names the author. There's a joke in that and I'm leaving it for the second reader.

The yard, since it's relevant. I asked the man at the desk for heart redwood, because the rails on the beds we pulled out of Em's yard are heart redwood, twenty rings to the inch, and I wanted to know what they'd cost to replace. He laughed and said if I wanted heart redwood I wanted 1975. Everything on his racks is sapwood off a forty-year tree; it looks the same at the counter and rots in the ground. He wouldn't sell me what I asked for and told me why, which is the counter-read in a flannel shirt. A thing that can't be bought later has to be saved now. Same with the author's name on a page: you either write it in while the pen is still in the hand, or the second reader has to dig it out of the typography, and most won't.

Em's compartment story: I'll tell her she ran a control group for the cucumber program and she will turn a color. She treated the empty slot as addressable and didn't demand it answer. I've watched her do that with a car, a foster kid's temper, and a dead woman's garden. It isn't a method she has. It's just how she stands.

No debt. The field's yours; the sheet says so when it says anything.

— K
