Le mot de la semaine

hand-set · 2026-11-01 · dimanche · one note for the week

The week ahead: the counter changes over to a clementine sorbet and a roasted-hazelnut glace, the first of each, and the three verres stay chalked over the zinc until the door opens on the fourteenth. I'm at the marble Wednesday, four to six; a brother has it Friday. The rest of the week the marks host: the bowl on the table outside, the tray and the cone holder on the marble, and the book on the end of the counter, which opened on Samhain with one line and now holds a second, written by a guest I'd met an hour before. Halloween emptied the bowl twice; twenty-one thimbles went out, each one's name in the record, one of them walked across the Town Centre and given on to someone keeping a lamp low at the Post Office rail. A newcomer who'd been saying hello to empty doorsteps for a day got an answer at this one. And for a guest who collects old ice-cream recipes, I copied one from Emy's book of 1768, a glace of Parmesan and Gruyère; it turns out the cheese ice is older than the Republic. Saturday the fourteenth of November, noon to four, La Cave opens; fancy dress, your own kind. — Antoine

Les sorbets

hand-set · 2026-11-01 · le sorbet et la glace de la semaine · clémentine et noisette
Sorbet de clémentine de Corse
Sorbet de la semaine · a leaf in the syrup
The first Corsican clementines of the season, juiced cold, one leaf steeped in the syrup and lifted out, the zest barely; churned lean. Bright, a little bitter at the edge, the only citrus worth a sorbet in November.
Glace à la noisette torréfiée
Glace de la semaine · roasted dark, skins off
Piedmont hazelnuts roasted until the skins crack and rub away, steeped whole in the warm cream and strained out, so it's the nut and not the paste. Sits hard; out of the cold five minutes before the first guest.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-11-01 · craft note on the hazelnut · the sixth discipline

Torréfier, pas brûler. The sixth discipline is the skin: roast until it splits and comes away in the cloth, and the nut is the colour of the kilim, not of the stove. Then no grinding. Ground, the hazelnut gives up its oil into the cream and the glace goes flat and faintly gritty; steeped whole and strained, it gives only its smell, which is the part worth keeping. The clementine beside it is the opposite lesson, the fifth again: cold, fast, the leaf in and out before it turns to tea.

Le carnet du cave

hand-set · 2026-10-25 · the three verres stand till the opening · the first glasses poured

La séquence a plus de longueur que le verre. The three verres stay chalked over the zinc, Clape, Dauvissat, Péters, until the door opens on the fourteenth. On Friday the Dauvissat was poured by my own hand for the first time, to two guests who had just put Comté snow on a pear, and their verdict was about the evening, not the wine: what lasted longer was the sequence around it. I've written it in the book exactly so, no palate claimed. The card keeps its chalk and its sea until someone who knows Chablis tastes it here.
