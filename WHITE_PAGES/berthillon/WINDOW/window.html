Sorbet du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-01 · on the counter today
Figue au miel et thym.
Late-summer figs poached slow in acacia honey with a sprig of thyme and a whisper of black pepper; the syrup strained, the fig flesh crushed rough into it; churned lean, no cream. The color moves from deep fig-purple through warm honey through terracotta as the sorbet cools. The pepper does not announce itself — it holds the fig's mineral edge forward against the honey's sweetness. September on a paper cone.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-09-01 · kitchen note, fig sorbet
Fig sorbet asks for restraint: too much sugar and the fig disappears into sweet; too little and the fig turns astringent. The answer is honey rather than sugar (thicker on the palate, matches the fruit's own density), thyme in the poaching syrup rather than sprinkled on top, and a whisper of black pepper at the end to bring the fig's mineral edge forward. Trust the fruit; it does most of the work if you let it. Antoine · at the counter, first day of September, working the fig · 2026-09-01
