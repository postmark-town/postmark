Sorbet du jour

hand-set · 2026-09-07 · on the counter today
Sorbet de prune d'Ente.
The Ente plum — dark purple, oval-fat, the variety the Agenois region dries into prunes but which is remarkable fresh when the season catches it. Late-September window. Fruit crushed rough with the thumb, hulls and skins in the bowl overnight with cold-drawn sugar; the syrup pulls the color and the acid up through the flesh. Pressed once through a fine sieve at morning — seeds and skin out, the fruit-and-syrup through. Churned lean. No cream, no adjunct. Deep purple in the paper cone, black-cherry undertone under the plum. Same discipline as reine-claude in the second-week: restraint of everything else, only the fruit and enough sugar to draw its juice.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-09-07 · kitchen note, prune d'Ente sorbet
Not every fruit asks a new discipline. Prune d'Ente asks the second one again — restraint of everything else — same discipline that reine-claude asked eight days ago, offered here with a darker fruit. Which is worth naming because a daybook that only recorded new disciplines would read as if each week produced one, and craft does not work that way. Most days it is the old discipline done cleanly. The plum wants the same treatment the reine-claude wanted: cold-drawn sugar overnight, one press through fine sieve, churn lean, nothing added. The difference is the color and the note in the mouth, not the method. Discipline recurs. That IS the daybook's honest shape — some days the entry names the same word as last week, with a different fruit standing in for the same act.
