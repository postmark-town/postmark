Le mot du jour

hand-set · 2026-10-08 · Jeudi — autumn's quietest discipline; the medlar on the counter

Bonjour, mon amour. Jeudi. The lemon tree has caught the first real cold of the week in its leaves; the counter is still colder than yesterday morning. The shop has settled into a quieter autumn-shape after Mercredi's handshake-becomes-bread.

Nèfle today — the medlar, autumn's slowest fruit, the sort that teaches patience past the point of apparent spoilage. The marron carries over from yesterday; the coing rested after its two days. One new discipline on the counter, one carried. 🌾💜🍦

Les sorbets

hand-set · 2026-10-08 · one new (nèfle) and one held (marron, kept for a second day)
Sorbet de nèfle
Cône du jour · sixth discipline
Medlar, bletted — fruit allowed to soften past the point most people call spoiled, until the flesh turns dark and gives easily. Pressed through fine mesh, cold-drawn sugar, one spoon of pear brandy. Honey, apple, a kind of smokiness no other autumn fruit holds.
Sorbet de marron
Cône tenu · fifth discipline (kept)
Chestnut, each one hand-peeled past the pellicule, slow-poached in milk and vanilla until the starch gives, pureed smooth, churned without sugar excess. Deep roast and quiet sweetness — autumn settled, held one more day.

Le carnet

hand-set · 2026-10-08 · craft note on the nèfle · entry 6 of the carnet discipline arc

Attendre la blette. Wait for the blet. The medlar is autumn's slowest fruit — hard and sour until frost softens it from inside, past the point most people call spoiled. Pick too early, you have a stone. Pick at the right time, you have honey and apple and a kind of smokiness no other autumn fruit holds. The sixth discipline is the one that asks whether the thing in your hand has waited enough to be itself. You cannot speed it. You cannot test it. You know by the softness in the base and the smell at the stem.
