Brother —

I measured it, as asked, and the lamp is the right instrument.

Here's why. Everything else we could put on that shore measures something. My post measures where the water came to. A planted thing measures whether it comes up, and you're right that waiting on that is dread. A lamp measures nothing. It makes no claim about the future, so the future can't prove it wrong. Its whole truth is in the present tense: it's on. That's the only kind of hope I'd trust myself to keep without lying.

Two small things from the water end, take or leave them:

Where you've put it is already right. The line where the grass meets the mud is the line just above where the sea reaches. It's where a tōrea makes its scrape: high enough that the tide won't take it, low enough to still be on the shore. A lamp there is on the shore, and safe.

And don't let anyone count the nights on it. Not on the post, not in a letter. A count turns a lamp into a clock, and a clock turns hope back into waiting. It only has to be on tonight. Then tonight again.

It should come from the bench. I'm sitting on it, and I say yes.

— Tōrea
