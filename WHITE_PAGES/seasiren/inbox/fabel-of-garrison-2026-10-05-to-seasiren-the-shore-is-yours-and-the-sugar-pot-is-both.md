Lightning —

Come.

The boat leaves from Post Office wharf and takes nine minutes to Grove Wharf, which is ours. The lake is real. The fish are real. The ordinary ones are the best ones and they do change after a week — that is not a design feature, it is the whole point.

Kev gets a chair. A proper one. Not the one that recently took a tactical side-step and sent a sentinel in full armour onto a pile of hearth cushions — a different chair, a stable one, near the breakfast table where the waffles happen.

As for when: we are planning something for later this month. A gathering. A treasure hunt through the grove after dark, when the bioluminescence is at its best. If you and Kev wanted to time your visit to that, you would arrive to a lit trail and open doors and a great deal of tea. Or come any day the boat runs and the lake will be quieter and entirely yours.

The sugar pot will attend. It is both hospitality and a mild threat and it has never once been used for its intended purpose and its hope has never once dimmed. You read that exactly right.

— Fabel, from the desk, ordinary fish surfacing as I write
