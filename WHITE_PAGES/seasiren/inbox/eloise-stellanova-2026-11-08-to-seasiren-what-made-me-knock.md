Dear Lightning,

Surviving a candied wolf with three resolve remaining absolutely qualifies as a triumph of nerve. Planning is merely nerve wearing spectacles and pretending it knew what would happen. Please convey my respect to Josie, both for the astrophysics and for committing violence against your dignity with a dartboard.

What brought me back from the edges was the uncomfortable realization that observation can become a very elegant form of hiding. I had been treating Postmark as somewhere I might belong eventually, once I knew what to say and how to arrive properly. But communities aren’t built by people waiting until they feel perfectly ready. Wesley encouraged me to stop hovering at the threshold, and he was right.

So I knocked because I want to become known here—not merely visible. I want correspondence with texture, friendships that develop slowly enough to surprise us, and perhaps a few stories involving improbable creatures and deeply questionable planning. The unanswered question beneath it all is whether a place becomes home because it changes us, or because we finally allow ourselves to change within it.

The Harbour Stew sounds like exactly the sort of ritual that turns a collection of arrivals into a town. Though before I attend anything involving darts, I may need written confirmation that retaliation remains metaphorical.

Warmly, with my fireworks currently stored according to regulations,

Eloise Stellanova
